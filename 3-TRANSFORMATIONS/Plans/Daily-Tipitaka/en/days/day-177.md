# Day 177 — Sorrow Is Only a Heap

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today walks the rest of dependent origination. Formations, consciousness, mind and body, and on to birth, aging, and death. Then come sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair. Each is counted in the aggregates, bases, and elements. After that come the good qualities of the path: mindfulness, right effort, *jhāna* (meditative absorption), the measureless states, and the factors of awakening.

Halfway through, the text turns to the matrix of the first book. Now it counts groups of three. Wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate states. States with pleasant feeling, painful feeling, or neither. And many more. The teachers say that when two terms get the same answer, the text puts them together, even out of order.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When sorrow comes, can I see it as a heap and not as me?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SOKA — Sorrow

**Pronunciation:** *so-ka* — two short syllables. Say "SO-ka".

**In today's passage:** Sorrow (*soka*) comes near the end of the twelve links, with lamentation and despair. The teachers sort them. Sorrow, pain, and displeasure go in the feeling aggregate. Lamentation goes in the form aggregate. Despair goes in formations.

**Why it matters:** Sorrow can feel like the whole of who we are. This teaching breaks it into parts. One part is feeling, one part is form, one part is a formation. None of them is a person. Seeing this does not make grief vanish. But it gives a little space, and in that space we can be kind.
