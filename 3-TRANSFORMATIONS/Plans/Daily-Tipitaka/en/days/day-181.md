# Day 181 — Apart, Yet Counted Together

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The third method turns the second one around. Now we start with things left out by an aggregate (*khandha*, a heap) but still included by a base (*āyatana*) and an element (*dhātu*).

Feeling is the first example. Perception and formations are not feeling, and neither is subtle matter. They sit in other heaps. Yet all of them are counted together in one base, the mind-object base. *Nibbāna* is counted there too, though it belongs to no heap at all. That is why the answers keep saying "leaving the unconstructed out of the aggregate count." The verses run through the truths, the life faculty, other faculties, the links of dependent origination, and the clusters of taints, fetters, and defilements. Things can share one place and still be different kinds.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *What do I lump together in my mind that is really many different things?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### DHAMMĀYATANA — The Mind-Object Base

**Pronunciation:** *dham-mā-ya-ta-na* — five syllables; "ā" is long, as in "father". Say "DHUM-maa-ya-ta-na".

**In today's passage:** In almost every verse today, the answer is "one base, one element". That base is the mind-object base (*dhammāyatana*). It holds feeling, perception, formations, subtle matter, and *nibbāna*. They are counted in one place, yet each stays its own kind.

**Why it matters:** The mind holds many things at once: a worry, a kind thought, a memory. Sharing one place does not make them one thing. When a hard thought comes, it is one object among many. You do not have to take it as all of you.
