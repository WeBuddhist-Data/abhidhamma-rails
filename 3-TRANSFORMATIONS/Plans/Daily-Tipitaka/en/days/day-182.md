# Day 182 — A Cry Is Counted as Sound

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The fourth method is short, only two verses. It asks a puzzle question. Take what is included with a thing. Then take what is included with *that*. Where does it all end up?

The first example is the origin (*samudaya*) of suffering, which is craving (*taṇhā*). Craving belongs to the heap of formations. The other formations share that heap with it. Those formations, in turn, include craving again. So the answer comes back to one aggregate, one base, and one element. The same holds for the path, the faculties, the links of dependent origination, and the roots. With lamentation the answer is sound: one base of sound and one element of sound. In this count, a cry of grief is filed under sound.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When I hear grief, in myself or in another, can I meet it with calm and care?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### PARIDEVA — Lamentation

**Pronunciation:** *pa-ri-de-va* — four syllables. Say "pa-ri-DAY-va".

**In today's passage:** Lamentation (*parideva*) is crying out in grief. Today the text sorts it like everything else. It sits in the heap of material form, in the base of sound and the element of sound. Sorrow is felt inside. Lamentation is the sound it makes.

**Why it matters:** When grief makes us cry out, it can feel like the whole world. The teaching quietly files it as sound. This is not coldness. It gives us a little room to breathe. In that room we can be kind to ourselves, and to others who grieve.
