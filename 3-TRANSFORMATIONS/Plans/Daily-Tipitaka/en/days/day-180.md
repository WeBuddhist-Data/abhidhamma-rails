# Day 180 — One Part, Not the Whole

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today begins the second of the fourteen methods (*naya*). The first method counted where each thing belongs. Now the question turns. Take something counted in one aggregate (*khandha*, a heap). What does it still leave out by base (*āyatana*) and by element (*dhātu*)?

The eye base is the first example. It belongs to the heap of material form. Yet ten other bases in that same heap are not the eye. They are left out. The verses then run through the other sense doors, the seven kinds of consciousness, seven faculties, becoming without perception, lamentation, and things that can be seen or have impact. The teachers say whole heaps do not appear in this method. A whole heap leaves nothing of itself out. Only a part can do that.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When one part of my life hurts, do I treat it as the whole of me?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### EKADESA — A Part

**Pronunciation:** *e-ka-de-sa* — four syllables. Say "AY-ka-DAY-sa".

**In today's passage:** The teachers explain why only some terms appear today. Each one names a part (*ekadesa*) of a heap, not the whole heap. The eye is one part of material form. Eye-consciousness is one part of consciousness. A part can leave the other parts out.

**Why it matters:** We often take one part for the whole. One bad feeling becomes "my whole day". One harsh word becomes "who they are". Today, when one part of life stings, name it as one part. Then look at the parts that are still well.
