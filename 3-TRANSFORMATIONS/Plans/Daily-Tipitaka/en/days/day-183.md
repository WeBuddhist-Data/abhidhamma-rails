# Day 183 — Body and Mind, Counted Apart

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

The fifth method closes the methods of inclusion and exclusion. It asks: what is left out by what is left out?

Start with the heap of material form. By aggregate, it leaves out the four mental heaps and *nibbāna*. But by base and element, only one thing is fully left out: consciousness (*viññāṇa*). And what does consciousness fully leave out? Material form. So the answer keeps pointing back to consciousness: one aggregate, one base, and seven elements. Body and mind are counted in separate places. The verses run through the aggregates, bases, truths, faculties, and links of dependent origination. Then come the groups of three, the pairs, and a closing verse that sums it all up.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When my body is tired or in pain, can I keep my mind kind?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### NĀMARŪPA — Mentality and Material Form

**Pronunciation:** *nā-ma-rū-pa* — four syllables; "ā" and "ū" are long. Say "NAA-ma-ROO-pa".

**In today's passage:** Today the text keeps mind and body apart. Material form (*rūpa*) and consciousness leave each other out by every count: aggregate, base, and element. Together the two sides are called mentality and material form (*nāmarūpa*) — mind and body.

**Why it matters:** We often say "I am tired" when the body is tired, or "I am bad" when one thought is bad. The teaching separates the two sides so we can see each one clearly. Neither side is a self. Seeing this, we can care for the body and guard the mind, one at a time.
