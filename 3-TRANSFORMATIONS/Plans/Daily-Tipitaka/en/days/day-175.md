# Day 175 — Counting the Five Aggregates

## 1. 🧭 Today’s Chanting Guide

Today the first method begins: inclusion and exclusion. The book takes each of the five aggregates (the five heaps that make up a person) and asks three questions. In how many aggregates is it included? In how many bases? In how many elements? Then it asks where it is left out.

Take the aggregate of form. It is included in one aggregate, eleven bases, and eleven elements. Only the mind base and the seven consciousness elements leave it out. The teachers say no form exists outside those eleven elements. Then the questions go on to pairs, threes, fours, and all five together. Next come the twelve bases, and the first of the eighteen elements. One thing is set apart: *nibbāna*. It counts as a base and an element, but never as an aggregate.

Today's question to carry into the chant: *When I look at my body, do I find anything besides form?*

## 5. 🔑 Pāli Word of the Day

### SAṄGAHA — Inclusion

**Pronunciation:** *saṅ-ga-ha* — three syllables; "ṅ" is the "ng" in "sing". Say "SUNG-ga-ha".

**In today's passage:** Inclusion (*saṅgaha*) here means counting. The teachers give an example: the eye base "goes into the count" of the form aggregate. Things of the same kind are counted together. Things of a different kind are left out.

**Why it matters:** When pain or joy comes, count it. "This is feeling. It goes in the feeling aggregate." Counted like this, it is one heap among five, not "me" and not "mine". This small habit loosens our grip on what we feel. With a looser grip, we can act with more care.
