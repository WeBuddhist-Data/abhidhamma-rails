### Analysis of the Bases of Power ^2-9-0

#### Discourse Classification ^2-9-1-0

The four bases of success: here a monk develops the basis of success endowed with concentration through desire and formation of endeavour; he develops the basis of success endowed with concentration through energy and formation of endeavour; he develops the basis of success endowed with concentration through mind and formation of endeavour; he develops the basis of success endowed with concentration through investigation and formation of endeavour. ^2-431

##### Base of Power through Desire ^2-9-1-1-0

How does a monk develop the basis of success endowed with concentration through desire and formation of endeavour? If a monk, having made desire the dominant factor, gains concentration, gains one-pointedness of mind — this is called "concentration through desire". For the non-arising of unarisen bad, unwholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the abandoning of arisen bad, unwholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the arising of unarisen wholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the continuation, non-confusion, increase, expansion, and fulfilment by development of arisen wholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives. These are called "the formations of endeavour". Thus this concentration through desire, and these formations of endeavour — having combined and condensed them together, they are reckoned as "concentration through desire and formation of endeavour". ^2-432

Herein, what is desire? Whatever desire, state of desiring, wish to act, wholesome desire for the teaching: this is called "desire".
Herein, what is concentration? Whatever steadiness of mind, its stability, steadfastness, non-distraction, non-wavering, an undistracted state of mind, calm, the faculty of concentration, the power of concentration, right concentration: this is called "concentration".
Herein, what is the formation of endeavour? Whatever mental arousing of energy, launching out, exertion, endeavoring, striving, zeal, vigor, steadfastness, persistence without slackening, not giving up the desire to act, not putting down the task, taking up the task, energy, the faculty of energy, the power of energy, right effort: this is called "the formation of endeavour". Thus he is endowed with this desire, this concentration, and this formation of endeavour, fully endowed with it, has arrived at it, has fully arrived at it, has attained it, has fully attained it, and possesses it. Therefore it is called "endowed with concentration through desire and formation of endeavour". ^2-433

As to "power": whatever is the power, achievement, succeeding, prospering, obtaining, gaining, attainment, accomplishment, touching, realizing, and full attainment of those states.
As to "the basis of success": for one who has become thus, the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations, and the aggregate of consciousness.
As to "develops the basis of success": he practises these states, develops them, and cultivates them. Therefore it is called "develops the basis of success". ^2-434

##### Triads ^2-9-3-1-0

They are only wholesome. They may be joined with pleasant feeling, or may be joined with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. They are things that produce a result. They are not clung to and not able to be clung to. They are not defiled and not open to defilement. They may have applied and sustained thought, may have only sustained thought, or may have neither. They may be accompanied by joy, may be accompanied by happiness, or may be accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned by neither seeing nor development. They are rooted in neither what is to be abandoned by seeing nor what is to be abandoned by development. They lead to dismantling. They are still in training. They are measureless. They have a measureless object. They are superior. They are fixed in rightness. They do not have the path as object; they have the path as root; they do not have the path as dominant factor. They may be arisen, or may be not arisen; they should not be said to be bound to arise. They may be past, may be future, or may be present. They should not be said to have a past object, a future object, or a present object. They may be internal, may be external, or may be both internal and external. They have an external object. They are invisible and without resistance. ^2-464
