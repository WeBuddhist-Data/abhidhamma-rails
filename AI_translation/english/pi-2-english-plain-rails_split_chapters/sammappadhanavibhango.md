### Analysis of the Right Efforts ^2-8-0

#### Discourse Classification ^2-8-1-0

The four right efforts: here a monk, for the non-arising of unarisen bad, unwholesome states, generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the abandoning of arisen bad, unwholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the arising of unarisen wholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the continuation, non-confusion, increase, expansion, and fulfilment by development of arisen wholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives. ^2-390

How does a monk, for the non-arising of unarisen bad, unwholesome states, generate desire, make an effort, arouse energy, exert the mind, and strive? Herein, what are unarisen bad, unwholesome states? The three unwholesome roots — greed, hatred, delusion — and the defilements bound up with them; the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations, and the aggregate of consciousness joined with them; and the bodily action, verbal action, and mental action that originate from them: these are called "unarisen bad, unwholesome states". Thus, for the non-arising of these unarisen bad, unwholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives. ^2-391

As to "generates desire": herein, what is desire? Whatever desire, state of desiring, wish to act, wholesome desire for the teaching: this is called "desire". He produces this desire, generates it, sets it going, sets it in motion, brings it forth, and brings it fully forth. Therefore it is called "generates desire". ^2-392

As to "makes an effort": herein, what is effort? Whatever mental exertion of energy... right effort: this is called "effort". One who has this effort is endowed with it, fully endowed with it, has arrived at it, has fully arrived at it, has attained it, has fully attained it, and possesses it. Therefore it is called "makes an effort". ^2-393

As to "arouses energy": herein, what is energy? Whatever mental exertion of energy... right effort: this is called "energy". He rouses this energy, thoroughly rouses it, practises it, develops it, and cultivates it. Therefore it is called "arouses energy". ^2-394

As to "exerts the mind": herein, what is mind? Whatever consciousness, mind, mentality... the corresponding element of mind-consciousness: this is called "mind". He exerts this mind, fully exerts it, props it up, and sustains it. Therefore it is called "exerts the mind". ^2-395

As to "strives": herein, what is striving? Whatever mental exertion of energy... right effort: this is called "striving". One who has this striving is endowed with it... has fully attained it, and possesses it. Therefore it is called "strives". ^2-396

#### Questions and Answers ^2-8-3-0

The four right efforts: here a monk, for the non-arising of unarisen bad, unwholesome states, generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the abandoning of arisen bad, unwholesome states... for the arising of unarisen wholesome states... for the continuation, non-confusion, increase, expansion, and fulfilment by development of arisen wholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives. ^2-427

Of the four right efforts, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many neutral... how many conflicted, how many peaceful? ^2-428

##### Triads ^2-8-3-1-0

They are only wholesome. They may be joined with pleasant feeling, or may be joined with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. They are things that produce a result. They are not clung to and not able to be clung to. They are not defiled and not open to defilement. They may have applied and sustained thought, may have only sustained thought, or may have neither. They may be accompanied by joy, may be accompanied by happiness, or may be accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned by neither seeing nor development. They are rooted in neither what is to be abandoned by seeing nor what is to be abandoned by development. They lead to dismantling. They are still in training. They are measureless. They have a measureless object. They are superior. They are fixed in rightness. They do not have the path as object. They have the path as root. They may have the path as dominant factor, or should not be said to have the path as dominant factor. They may be arisen, or may be not arisen; they should not be said to be bound to arise. They may be past, may be future, or may be present. They should not be said to have a past object, a future object, or a present object. They may be internal, may be external, or may be both internal and external. They have an external object. They are invisible and without resistance. ^2-429

##### Pairs ^2-8-3-2-0

They are not a root. They are rooted. They are joined with a root. They should not be said to be both a root and rooted; they are rooted but not themselves a root. They should not be said to be both a root and joined with a root; they are joined with a root but not themselves a root. They are not a root, but are rooted. They have conditions. They are conditioned. They are invisible. They offer no resistance. They are immaterial. They are beyond the world. They are knowable by some kinds of consciousness, not knowable by others. They are not themselves a mental taint. They are free of mental taints. They are dissociated from a mental taint. They should not be said to be both a mental taint and subject to mental taints, or to be subject to mental taints but not itself a mental taint. They should not be said to be both a mental taint and joined with a mental taint, or to be joined with a mental taint but not itself a mental taint. They are dissociated from a mental taint and free of it. They are not themselves a fetter... not themselves a tie... not themselves a flood... not themselves a yoke... not themselves a hindrance... not themselves wrong grasping... They have an object. They are not mind. They are mental factors. They are joined with mind. They are mingled with mind. They originate from mind. They co-arise with mind. They follow mind. They are mingled with mind and originating from mind. They are mingled with mind, originating from mind, and co-arising with mind. They are mingled with mind, originating from mind, and following mind. They are external. They are not derived. They are not clung to. They are not themselves clinging... not themselves a defilement... They are not to be abandoned by seeing. They are not to be abandoned by development. They are not rooted in what is to be abandoned by seeing. They are not rooted in what is to be abandoned by development. They may have applied thought, or may lack applied thought. They may have sustained thought, or may lack sustained thought. They may have joy, or may lack joy. They may be accompanied by joy, or may not be accompanied by joy. They may be accompanied by happiness, or may not be accompanied by happiness. They may be accompanied by equanimity, or may not be accompanied by equanimity. They do not belong to the sense sphere. They do not belong to the fine-material sphere. They do not belong to the immaterial sphere. They are unincluded. They lead to liberation. They are fixed. They are unsurpassed. They are peaceful.
