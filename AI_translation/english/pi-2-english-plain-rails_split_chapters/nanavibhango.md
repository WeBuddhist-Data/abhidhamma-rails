(2) Herein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, by way of circumstance and by way of cause, of the result of undertakings of action, past, future, and present? Herein the Tathāgata understands: "There are certain bad undertakings of action that, being obstructed by an accomplishment of destination, do not ripen. There are certain bad undertakings of action that ripen owing to a failure of time. There are certain bad undertakings of action that ripen owing to a failure of effort.
"There are certain good undertakings of action that, being obstructed by a failure of destination, do not ripen. There are certain good undertakings of action that ripen owing to an accomplishment of effort" – whatever wisdom there is on that occasion – understanding... non-delusion, investigation of principles, right view – this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, by way of circumstance and by way of cause, of the result of undertakings of action, past, future, and present. ^2-810

(3) Herein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the practice that leads to every destination? Herein the Tathāgata understands: "This path, this practice, leads to hell", he understands; "this path, this practice, leads to the animal realm", he understands; "this path, this practice, leads to the realm of ghosts", he understands; "this path, this practice, leads to the human world", he understands; "this path, this practice, leads to the heavenly world", he understands; "this path, this practice, leads to final liberation", he understands – whatever wisdom there is on that occasion – understanding... non-delusion, investigation of principles, right view – this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the practice that leads to every destination. ^2-811

(4) Herein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the world with its many and various elements? Herein the Tathāgata understands the diversity of aggregates, understands the diversity of sense-fields, understands the diversity of elements, understands the diversity of the world with its many and various elements – whatever wisdom there is on that occasion – understanding... non-delusion, investigation of principles, right view – this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the world with its many and various elements. ^2-812

(5) Herein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the various inclinations of beings? Herein the Tathāgata understands: "There are beings of inferior inclination, there are beings of refined inclination. Beings of inferior inclination resort to, associate with, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination. Beings of refined inclination resort to, associate with, and attend upon beings of refined inclination.
"In the past too, beings of inferior inclination resorted to, associated with, and attended upon beings of inferior inclination; beings of refined inclination resorted to, associated with, and attended upon beings of refined inclination.
"In the future too, beings of inferior inclination will resort to, associate with, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination; beings of refined inclination will resort to, associate with, and attend upon beings of refined inclination" – whatever wisdom there is on that occasion – understanding... non-delusion, investigation of principles, right view – this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the various inclinations of beings. ^2-813

(6) Herein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the maturity of the faculties of other beings and other persons? Herein the Tathāgata understands the disposition of beings, understands the underlying tendency, understands the behaviour, understands the inclination; he understands beings of little dust in the eyes and beings of much dust in the eyes, of sharp faculties and of dull faculties, of good character and of bad character, easy to teach and hard to teach, and those capable and incapable [of progress]. ^2-814

What, then, is the disposition of beings? "The world is eternal", or "the world is not eternal", or "the world is finite", or "the world is infinite", or "the soul and the body are the same", or "the soul is one thing, the body another", or "the Tathāgata exists after death", or "the Tathāgata does not exist after death", or "the Tathāgata both exists and does not exist after death", or "the Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after death" – thus beings rely either on a view of existence or a view of non-existence. But without approaching either of these two extremes, one gains a conforming acceptance in regard to the states that arise dependent on this-conditionality, and knowledge as it really is. This is the disposition of beings. ^2-815

And what is the underlying tendency of beings? The underlying tendencies of beings: the underlying tendency to sensual lust, the underlying tendency to aversion, the underlying tendency to conceit, the underlying tendency to views, the underlying tendency to doubt, the underlying tendency to lust for existence, and the underlying tendency to ignorance. Whatever in the world has an agreeable and pleasing nature, there the underlying tendency to lust lies latent in beings. Whatever in the world has a disagreeable and unpleasing nature, there the underlying tendency to aversion lies latent in beings. Thus, in regard to these two states, ignorance has settled in. Along with it are to be seen conceit, views, and doubt, which share the same basis. This is the underlying tendency of beings. ^2-816

And what is the behaviour of beings? Meritorious formation, demeritorious formation, imperturbable formation, whether on a limited plane or a vast plane – this is the behaviour of beings. ^2-817

And what is the inclination of beings? There are beings of inferior inclination, there are beings of refined inclination. Beings of inferior inclination resort to, associate with, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination. Beings of refined inclination resort to, associate with, and attend upon beings of refined inclination.
In the past too, beings of inferior inclination resorted to, associated with, and attended upon beings of inferior inclination. Beings of refined inclination resorted to, associated with, and attended upon beings of refined inclination.
In the future too, beings of inferior inclination will resort to, associate with, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination. Beings of refined inclination will resort to, associate with, and attend upon beings of refined inclination. This is the inclination of beings. ^2-818

Which are those beings of much dust in the eyes? The ten grounds of defilement: greed, hatred, delusion, conceit, views, doubt, sloth, restlessness, lack of shame, and lack of moral dread. Those beings for whom these ten grounds of defilement have been practised, developed, cultivated, and have become abundant – these are the beings of much dust in the eyes. ^2-819

Which are those beings of little dust in the eyes? Those beings for whom these ten grounds of defilement have not been practised, not developed, not cultivated, and have not become abundant – these are the beings of little dust in the eyes. ^2-820

Which are those beings of dull faculties? The five faculties: the faculty of faith, the faculty of energy, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of concentration, and the faculty of wisdom. Those beings for whom these five faculties have not been practised, not developed, not cultivated, and have not become abundant – these are the beings of dull faculties. ^2-821

Which are those beings of sharp faculties? Those beings for whom these five faculties have been practised, developed, cultivated, and have become abundant – these are the beings of sharp faculties. ^2-822

Which are those beings of bad character? Those beings who have a bad disposition, a bad underlying tendency, bad behaviour, a bad inclination, much dust in the eyes, and dull faculties – these are the beings of bad character. ^2-823

Which are those beings of good character? Those beings who have a good disposition, good behaviour, a good inclination, little dust in the eyes, and sharp faculties – these are the beings of good character. ^2-824

Which are those beings hard to teach? Those beings who are of bad character are the very ones hard to teach. Those beings who are of good character are the very ones easy to teach. ^2-825

Which are those beings incapable [of progress]? Those beings possessed of an obstruction of action, possessed of an obstruction of defilement, possessed of an obstruction of result, without faith, without desire, of deficient wisdom, incapable of entering upon the fixed course of rightness among wholesome states – these are the beings incapable [of progress]. ^2-826

Which are those beings capable [of progress]? Those beings not possessed of an obstruction of action, not possessed of an obstruction of defilement, not possessed of an obstruction of result, possessing faith, possessing desire, possessing wisdom, capable of entering upon the fixed course of rightness among wholesome states – these are the beings capable [of progress]. Whatever wisdom there is on that occasion – understanding... non-delusion, investigation of principles, right view – this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the maturity of the faculties of other beings and other persons. ^2-827

(7) Herein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the defilement, purification, and emergence in respect of the jhānas, the liberations, the concentrations, and the attainments? "Meditator". There are four kinds of meditator. There is a certain meditator who, when accomplishment is present, perceives it as failure; there is a certain meditator who, when failure is present, perceives it as accomplishment; there is a certain meditator who, when accomplishment is present, perceives it as accomplishment; there is a certain meditator who, when failure is present, perceives it as failure – these are the four kinds of meditator.
There are also another four kinds of meditator. There is a certain meditator who enters slowly and emerges quickly; there is a certain meditator who enters quickly and emerges slowly; there is a certain meditator who enters slowly and emerges slowly; there is a certain meditator who enters quickly and emerges quickly – these are the four kinds of meditator.
There are also another four kinds of meditator. There is a certain meditator who is skilled in concentration but not skilled in attainment; there is a certain meditator who is skilled in attainment but not skilled in concentration; there is a certain meditator who is skilled both in concentration and in attainment; there is a certain meditator who is skilled neither in concentration nor in attainment – these are the four kinds of meditator.
"Jhāna". There are four jhānas: the first jhāna, the second jhāna, the third jhāna, and the fourth jhāna.
"Liberation". There are eight liberations. One possessing material form sees material forms – this is the first liberation.
Not perceiving material form internally, one sees material forms externally – this is the second liberation.
One is resolved only upon the beautiful – this is the third liberation.
With the complete surmounting of perceptions of material form, with the disappearance of perceptions of resistance, with non-attention to perceptions of diversity, aware that "space is boundless", one enters and dwells in the base of boundless space – this is the fourth liberation.
With the complete surmounting of the base of boundless space, aware that "consciousness is boundless", one enters and dwells in the base of boundless consciousness – this is the fifth liberation.
With the complete surmounting of the base of boundless consciousness, aware that "there is nothing", one enters and dwells in the base of nothingness – this is the sixth liberation.
With the complete surmounting of the base of nothingness, one enters and dwells in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception – this is the seventh liberation.
With the complete surmounting of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, one enters and dwells in the cessation of perception and feeling – this is the eighth liberation.
"Concentration". There are three concentrations: concentration accompanied by applied thought and sustained thought, concentration without applied thought but with only sustained thought, and concentration without applied thought and without sustained thought.
"Attainment". There are nine progressive dwelling-attainments: the attainment of the first jhāna, the attainment of the second jhāna, the attainment of the third jhāna, the attainment of the fourth jhāna, the attainment of the base of boundless space, the attainment of the base of boundless consciousness, the attainment of the base of nothingness, the attainment of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, and the attainment of the cessation of perception and feeling.
"Defilement" means a state that shares in decline. "Purification" means a state that shares in distinction. "Emergence" – both purification and emergence are emergence, and the emergence from any given concentration is also emergence. Whatever wisdom there is on that occasion – understanding... non-delusion, investigation of principles, right view – this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the defilement, purification, and emergence in respect of the jhānas, the liberations, the concentrations, and the attainments. ^2-828

(8) Herein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the recollection of past lives? Whatever wisdom there is on that occasion – understanding... non-delusion, investigation of principles, right view – this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the recollection of past lives. ^2-829

(9) Herein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the passing away and rebirth of beings? Herein the Tathāgata, with the divine eye, purified and surpassing the human, sees beings passing away and being reborn, inferior and superior, fair and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate, and understands beings as faring according to their deeds: "These beings, indeed, who possessed bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, and mental misconduct, who reviled the noble ones, who held wrong view, who undertook deeds based on wrong view – at the breaking up of the body after death, they have been reborn in a state of misery, an unhappy destination, a lower realm, in hell. But these beings, who possessed bodily good conduct, verbal good conduct, and mental good conduct, who did not revile the noble ones, who held right view, who undertook deeds based on right view – at the breaking up of the body after death, they have been reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world." Thus, with the divine eye, purified and surpassing the human, he sees beings passing away and being reborn, inferior and superior, fair and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate, and understands beings as faring according to their deeds. Whatever wisdom there is on that occasion – understanding... non-delusion, investigation of principles, right view – this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the passing away and rebirth of beings. ^2-830
