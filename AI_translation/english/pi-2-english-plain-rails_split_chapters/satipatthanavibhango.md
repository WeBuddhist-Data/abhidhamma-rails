##### Exposition of Contemplation of the Body ^2-7-1-1-0

How does a monk dwell internally contemplating the body in the body? Here a monk reviews this very body, from the soles of the feet up and from the hair of the head down, enclosed by skin, full of many kinds of impurities: "In this body there are head-hair, body-hair, nails, teeth, skin, flesh, sinews, bones, bone marrow, kidneys, heart, liver, membranes, spleen, lungs, intestines, mesentery, undigested food, feces, bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat, tears, grease, saliva, snot, oil of the joints, and urine." He practises that sign, develops it, cultivates it, and establishes it well. Having practised that sign, developed it, cultivated it, and established it well, he directs his mind to the body externally.
How does a monk dwell externally contemplating the body in the body? Here a monk reviews another's body, from the soles of the feet up and from the hair of the head down, enclosed by skin, full of many kinds of impurities: "In his body there are head-hair, body-hair, nails, teeth, skin, flesh, sinews, bones, bone marrow, kidneys, heart, liver, membranes, spleen, lungs, intestines, mesentery, undigested food, feces, bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat, tears, grease, saliva, snot, oil of the joints, and urine." He practises that sign, develops it, cultivates it, and establishes it well. Having practised that sign, developed it, cultivated it, and established it well, he directs his mind to the body both internally and externally.
How does a monk dwell both internally and externally contemplating the body in the body? Here a monk reviews this body, from the soles of the feet up and from the hair of the head down, enclosed by skin, full of many kinds of impurities: "In this body there are head-hair, body-hair, nails, teeth, skin, flesh, sinews, bones, bone marrow, kidneys, heart, liver, membranes, spleen, lungs, intestines, mesentery, undigested food, feces, bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat, tears, grease, saliva, snot, oil of the joints, and urine." In this way a monk dwells both internally and externally contemplating the body in the body, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, having removed covetousness and displeasure in regard to the world. ^2-356

As to "having removed covetousness and displeasure in regard to the world": herein, what is the world? That very body is the world. The five aggregates subject to clinging are the world. ##### Triads ^2-7-3-1-0

They may be wholesome, or may be neutral. They may be joined with pleasant feeling, or may be joined with neutral feeling. They may be a result, or may produce a result. They are not clung to but able to be clung to. They are not defiled but open to defilement. They may have applied and sustained thought, may have only sustained thought, or may have neither. They may be accompanied by joy, may be accompanied by happiness, or may be accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned by neither seeing nor development. They are rooted in neither what is to be abandoned by seeing nor what is to be abandoned by development. They may lead to dismantling, or may lead to neither. They may be still in training, or may be beyond training. They are measureless. They have a measureless object. They are superior. They may be fixed in rightness, or may be not fixed. They do not have the path as object, but may have the path as root, may have the path as dominant factor, or should not be said to have the path as root or as dominant factor. They may be arisen, may be not arisen, or may be bound to arise. They may be past, may be future, or may be present.
