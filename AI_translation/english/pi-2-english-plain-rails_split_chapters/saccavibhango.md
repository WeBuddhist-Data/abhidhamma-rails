### Analysis of the Truths ^2-4-0

#### Discourse Classification ^2-4-1-0

The four noble truths: the noble truth of suffering, the noble truth of the origin of suffering, the noble truth of the cessation of suffering, the noble truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering. ^2-189

##### The Truth of Suffering ^2-4-1-1-0

Herein, what is the noble truth of suffering? Birth is suffering, aging is suffering, death is suffering; sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair are suffering; association with what is unpleasant is suffering, separation from what is pleasant is suffering, not getting what one wants is also suffering; in brief, the five aggregates affected by clinging are suffering. ^2-190

Herein, what is birth? Whatever, in this or that group of beings, is the birth, coming-to-birth, descent into the womb, production, appearance of the aggregates, and obtaining of the sense-fields of those beings: this is called 'birth'. ^2-191

Herein, what is aging? Whatever, in this or that group of beings, is the aging, decrepitude, broken teeth, grey hair, wrinkled skin, decline of vitality, and deterioration of the faculties of those beings: this is called 'aging'. ^2-192

Herein, what is death? Whatever, from this or that group of beings, is the falling away, passing, breaking up, disappearance, dying, death, completion of time, breaking up of the aggregates, laying down of the body, and cutting off of the life faculty of those beings: this is called 'death'. ^2-193

Herein, what is sorrow? For one who is touched by the loss of relatives, or by the loss of wealth, or by the loss through illness, or by the loss of virtue, or by the loss of view, or who is affected by any other kind of loss, or touched by any other kind of painful thing — the sorrow, sorrowing, state of sorrowing, inner sorrow, inner deep sorrow, burning of the heart, grief, and dart of sorrow of such a person: this is called 'sorrow'. ^2-194

Herein, what is lamentation? For one who is touched by the loss of relatives, or by the loss of wealth, or by the loss through illness, or by the loss of virtue, or by the loss of view, or who is affected by any other kind of loss, or touched by any other kind of painful thing — the wailing, lamenting, state of wailing, state of lamenting, weeping, wailing aloud, crying out, moaning, and state of moaning of such a person: this is called 'lamentation'. ^2-195

Herein, what is pain? Whatever bodily discomfort, bodily pain, the unpleasant, painful feeling born of bodily contact, the unpleasant, painful feeling that is felt and is born of bodily contact: this is called 'pain'. ^2-196

Herein, what is grief? Whatever mental discomfort, mental pain, the unpleasant, painful feeling born of mental contact, the unpleasant, painful feeling that is felt and is born of mental contact: this is called 'grief'. ^2-197

Herein, what is despair? For one who is touched by the loss of relatives, or by the loss of wealth, or by the loss through illness, or by the loss of virtue, or by the loss of view, or who is affected by any other kind of loss, or touched by any other kind of painful thing — the despair, deep despair, state of despair, and state of deep despair of such a person: this is called 'despair'. ^2-198

Herein, what is association with what is unpleasant, which is suffering? Here, whatever sights, sounds, smells, tastes, or tangibles are undesirable, unwanted, or displeasing to someone; or whoever wishes them harm, wishes them misfortune, wishes them discomfort, or wishes them no safety from bondage — the meeting, encountering, joining, or coming together with these: this is called 'association with what is unpleasant, which is suffering'. ^2-199

Herein, what is separation from what is pleasant, which is suffering? Here, whatever sights, sounds, smells, tastes, or tangibles are desirable, wanted, or pleasing to someone; or whoever wishes them good, wishes them benefit, wishes them comfort, or wishes them safety from bondage — mother, father, brother, sister, friends, colleagues, or blood relatives — the not meeting, not encountering, not joining, or not coming together with these: this is called 'separation from what is pleasant, which is suffering'. ^2-200

Herein, what is meant by 'not getting what one wants is also suffering'? For beings subject to birth, a wish arises like this: "Oh, that we were not subject to birth! And that birth would not come to us!" But this cannot be attained by wishing. This too is 'not getting what one wants is also suffering'.
For beings subject to aging... for beings subject to sickness... for beings subject to death... for beings subject to sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair, a wish arises like this: "Oh, that we were not subject to sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair! And that sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair would not come to us!" But this cannot be attained by wishing. This too is 'not getting what one wants is also suffering'. ^2-201

Herein, what are, in brief, the five aggregates affected by clinging, which are suffering? They are: the aggregate of material form affected by clinging, the aggregate of feeling affected by clinging, the aggregate of perception affected by clinging, the aggregate of formations affected by clinging, the aggregate of consciousness affected by clinging. These are called, in brief, 'the five aggregates affected by clinging, which are suffering'.
This is called 'the noble truth of suffering'. ^2-202

##### The Truth of the Path ^2-4-1-4-0

Herein, what is the noble truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering? It is just this noble eightfold path — namely, right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration.
Herein, what is right view? Knowledge of suffering, knowledge of the origin of suffering, knowledge of the cessation of suffering, knowledge of the path leading to the cessation of suffering: this is called 'right view'.
Herein, what is right intention? The intention of renunciation, the intention of non-ill-will, the intention of harmlessness: this is called 'right intention'.
Herein, what is right speech? Abstaining from false speech, abstaining from divisive speech, abstaining from harsh speech, abstaining from idle talk: this is called 'right speech'.
Herein, what is right action? Abstaining from killing living beings, abstaining from taking what is not given, abstaining from sexual misconduct: this is called 'right action'.
Herein, what is right livelihood? Here, a noble disciple, having given up wrong livelihood, earns a living by right livelihood: this is called 'right livelihood'.
Herein, what is right effort? Here, a monk generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives for the non-arising of unarisen bad, unwholesome states. He generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives for the abandoning of arisen bad, unwholesome states. He generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives for the arising of unarisen wholesome states. He generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives for the continuation, non-confusion, increase, growth, and fulfillment by development of arisen wholesome states. This is called 'right effort'.
Herein, what is right mindfulness? Here, a monk dwells contemplating the body in the body, ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having removed covetousness and grief regarding the world. He dwells contemplating feelings in feelings... mind in mind... and dwells contemplating mental objects in mental objects, ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having removed covetousness and grief regarding the world. This is called 'right mindfulness'.
Herein, what is right concentration? Here, a monk, quite secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, enters and dwells in the first absorption, which is accompanied by thinking and reflecting, and has joy and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of thinking and reflecting, he enters and dwells in the second absorption, which has inner clarity and unification of mind, is without thinking and without reflecting, and has joy and happiness born of concentration. With the fading away of joy, he dwells equanimous, mindful, and clearly comprehending, and experiences happiness with the body — that state which the noble ones describe as "one who is equanimous and mindful dwells happily" — he enters and dwells in the third absorption. With the abandoning of happiness and the abandoning of pain, with the previous disappearance of gladness and grief, he enters and dwells in the fourth absorption, which has neither pain nor pleasure and is purified by equanimity and mindfulness. This is called 'right concentration'.
This is called 'the noble truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering'.
