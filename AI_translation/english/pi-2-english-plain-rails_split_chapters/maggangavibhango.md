### Analysis of the Path Factors ^2-11-0

#### Discourse Classification ^2-11-1-0

The noble eightfold path, namely: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration. ^2-486

Herein, what is right view? Knowledge of suffering, knowledge of the origin of suffering, knowledge of the cessation of suffering, knowledge of the path leading to the cessation of suffering — this is called "right view".
Herein, what is right intention? The intention of renunciation, the intention of non-ill-will, the intention of harmlessness — this is called "right intention".
Herein, what is right speech? Abstaining from false speech, abstaining from divisive speech, abstaining from harsh speech, abstaining from idle chatter — this is called "right speech".
Herein, what is right action? Abstaining from killing living beings, abstaining from taking what is not given, abstaining from sexual misconduct — this is called "right action".
Herein, what is right livelihood? Here a noble disciple, having abandoned wrong livelihood, makes his living by right livelihood — this is called "right livelihood".
Herein, what is right effort? Here a monk, for the non-arising of unarisen bad, unwholesome states, generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the abandoning of arisen bad, unwholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the arising of unarisen wholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives; for the continuation, non-confusion, increase, expansion, and fulfilment by development of arisen wholesome states, he generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind, and strives — this is called "right effort".
Herein, what is right mindfulness? Here a monk dwells contemplating the body in the body, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, having removed covetousness and displeasure in regard to the world; dwells contemplating feelings in feelings, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, having removed covetousness and displeasure in regard to the world; dwells contemplating the mind in the mind, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, having removed covetousness and displeasure in regard to the world; dwells contemplating mental objects in mental objects, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, having removed covetousness and displeasure in regard to the world — this is called "right mindfulness".
Herein, what is right concentration? Here a monk, quite secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, enters and dwells in the first absorption, accompanied by applied and sustained thought, with joy and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of applied and sustained thought, he enters and dwells in the second absorption, which has internal confidence and concentration of mind, is without applied thought and without sustained thought, and has joy and happiness born of concentration. With the fading away of joy, he dwells with equanimity, mindful and clearly comprehending, and experiences happiness with the body; he enters and dwells in the third absorption, of which the noble ones say: "one of equanimity and mindfulness dwells happily". With the abandoning of happiness and the abandoning of pain, and with the earlier disappearance of gladness and sadness, he enters and dwells in the fourth absorption, which is neither painful nor pleasant, and which has purity of mindfulness through equanimity — this is called "right concentration". ^2-487

The noble eightfold path, namely: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration. ^2-488

Herein, what is right view? Here a monk develops right view that inclines toward seclusion, dispassion, and cessation, and ripens in release; he develops right intention... he develops right speech... he develops right action... he develops right livelihood... he develops right effort... he develops right mindfulness... he develops right concentration that inclines toward seclusion, dispassion, and cessation, and ripens in release.

#### Abhidhamma Classification ^2-11-2-0

The eightfold path: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration. ^2-490

Herein, what is the eightfold path? Here, at the time when a monk develops the world-transcending absorption that leads out of the world and leads to the dismantling of accumulation, for the abandoning of wrong views and for reaching the first stage — quite secluded from sense pleasures... he enters and dwells in the first absorption, a difficult practice with slow direct knowledge — at that time the eightfold path is present: right view... right concentration. ^2-491

Herein, what is right view? Whatever wisdom, understanding... non-delusion, investigation of states, right view, the factor of investigation, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "right view".
Herein, what is right intention? Whatever thinking, thought, intention, fixing, focusing, and directing of the mind, right intention, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "right intention".
Herein, what is right speech? The abstaining, avoiding, refraining, abstention from the four kinds of verbal misconduct — the not doing, not committing, not transgressing the boundary, cutting off the bridge to it — right speech, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "right speech".
Herein, what is right action? The abstaining, avoiding, refraining, abstention from the three kinds of bodily misconduct — the not doing, not committing, not transgressing the boundary, cutting off the bridge to it — right action, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "right action".
Herein, what is right livelihood? The abstaining, avoiding, refraining, abstention from wrong livelihood — the not doing, not committing, not transgressing the boundary, cutting off the bridge to it — right livelihood, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "right livelihood".
Herein, what is right effort? Whatever mental exertion of energy... right effort, the factor of energy, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "right effort".
Herein, what is right mindfulness? Whatever mindfulness, recollection... right mindfulness, the factor of mindfulness, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "right mindfulness".
Herein, what is right concentration? Whatever steadiness of mind... right concentration, the factor of concentration, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "right concentration". This is called "the eightfold path". The remaining states are joined with the eightfold path. ^2-492

#### Questions and Answers ^2-11-3-0

The noble eightfold path, namely: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration. ^2-504

Of the eight path factors, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many neutral... how many conflicted, how many peaceful? ^2-505

##### Triads ^2-11-3-1-0

May be wholesome, may be neutral. Right intention is joined with pleasant feeling; the seven path factors may be joined with pleasant feeling, or may be joined with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. May be things that produce a result, may be things having the nature to produce a result. They are not clung to and not able to be clung to. They are not defiled and not open to defilement. Right intention has only sustained thought; the seven path factors may have applied and sustained thought, may have only sustained thought, or may have neither. Right intention is accompanied by joy, accompanied by happiness, and not accompanied by equanimity; the seven path factors may be accompanied by joy, may be accompanied by happiness, or may be accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned by neither seeing nor development. They are rooted in neither what is to be abandoned by seeing nor what is to be abandoned by development. May lead to dismantling, may neither lead to accumulation nor to dismantling. May be still in training, may be beyond training. They are measureless. They have a measureless object. They are superior. May be fixed in rightness, may be unfixed. They do not have the path as object; may have the path as root, may have the path as dominant factor; it should not be said without qualification that they have the path as root, or that they have the path as dominant factor. May be arisen, may be unarisen, may be bound to arise. May be past, may be future, may be present. It should not be said that they have a past object, a future object, or a present object. May be internal, may be external, or may be both internal and external. They have an external object. They are invisible and without resistance. ^2-506

##### Pairs ^2-11-3-2-0

Right view is a root; the seven path factors are not a root. They are rooted. They are joined with a root. Right view is both a root and rooted; the seven path factors should not be said to be both a root and rooted — they are rooted but not themselves a root. Right view is both a root and joined with a root; the seven path factors should not be said to be both a root and joined with a root — they are joined with a root but not themselves a root. The seven path factors are not a root, but are rooted; right view should not be said to be either "not a root, but rooted" or "not a root and rootless".
They have conditions. They are conditioned. They are invisible. They offer no resistance. They are immaterial. They are beyond the world. They are knowable by some kinds of consciousness, not knowable by others.
They are not themselves a mental taint. They are free of mental taints. They are dissociated from a mental taint. They should not be said to be both a mental taint and subject to mental taints, or to be subject to mental taints but not itself a mental taint. They should not be said to be both a mental taint and joined with a mental taint, or to be joined with a mental taint but not itself a mental taint. They are dissociated from a mental taint and free of it.
They are not themselves a fetter... not themselves a tie... not themselves a flood... not themselves a yoke... not themselves a hindrance... not themselves wrong grasping... They have an object. They are not mind. They are mental factors. They are joined with mind. They are mingled with mind. They originate from mind. They co-arise with mind. They follow mind. They are mingled with mind and originating from mind. They are mingled with mind, originating from mind, and co-arising with mind. They are mingled with mind, originating from mind, and following mind. They are external. They are not derived. They are not clung to.
They are not themselves clinging... not themselves a defilement... They are not to be abandoned by seeing. They are not to be abandoned by development. They are not rooted in what is to be abandoned by seeing. They are not rooted in what is to be abandoned by development. Right intention is without applied thought; the seven path factors may have applied thought, or may lack applied thought. Right intention has sustained thought; the seven path factors may have sustained thought, or may lack sustained thought. Right intention has joy; the seven path factors may have joy, or may lack joy. Right intention is accompanied by joy; the seven path factors may be accompanied by joy, or may not be accompanied by joy. Right intention is accompanied by happiness; the seven path factors may be accompanied by happiness, or may not be accompanied by happiness. Right intention is not accompanied by equanimity; the seven path factors may be accompanied by equanimity, or may not be accompanied by equanimity. They do not belong to the sense sphere. They do not belong to the fine-material sphere. They do not belong to the immaterial sphere. They are unincluded. May lead to liberation, or may not lead to liberation. May be fixed, or may be unfixed. They are unsurpassed. They are peaceful.
