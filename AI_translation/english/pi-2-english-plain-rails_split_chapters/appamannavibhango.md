### Analysis of the Immeasurables ^2-13-0

#### Discourse Classification ^2-13-1-0

There are four immeasurables. Here a monk dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness; so too the second, so too the third, so too the fourth. Thus above, below, across, everywhere, extending to all as to himself, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness that is abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, without ill will. He dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by compassion; so too the second, so too the third, so too the fourth. Thus above, below, across, everywhere, extending to all as to himself, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by compassion that is abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, without ill will. He dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by sympathetic joy; so too the second, so too the third, so too the fourth. Thus above, below, across, everywhere, extending to all as to himself, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by sympathetic joy that is abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, without ill will. He dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by equanimity; so too the second, so too the third, so too the fourth. Thus above, below, across, everywhere, extending to all as to himself, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by equanimity that is abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, without ill will. ^2-642

##### Loving-kindness ^2-13-1-1-0

And how does a monk dwell pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness? Just as, on seeing one person who is dear and pleasing, he would feel loving-kindness for them — in the same way he pervades all beings with loving-kindness.
Herein, what is loving-kindness? Whatever loving-kindness towards beings, feeling of loving-kindness, state of feeling loving-kindness, and liberation of mind through loving-kindness there is: this is called "loving-kindness".
Herein, what is mind? Whatever mind, mind, mental activity, heart, the bright, mind, mind sense-field, mind faculty, consciousness, the aggregate of consciousness, and the corresponding mind-consciousness element there is: this is called "mind". This mind is accompanied by, co-arisen with, conjoined with, and joined with this loving-kindness. Therefore it is called "with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness". ^2-643

As to "one direction": the eastern direction, or the western direction, or the northern direction, or the southern direction, or above, or below, or across, or an intermediate direction. ^2-644

As to "pervading": having pervaded, having resolved upon it. ^2-645

As to "dwells": he behaves, conducts himself, maintains himself, keeps himself going, carries on, lives, dwells. Therefore it is called "dwells". ^2-646

As to "likewise the second": just as toward the first direction, so too toward the second direction, so too toward the third direction, so too toward the fourth direction, so too above, so too below, so too across, so too toward an intermediate direction. ^2-647

#### Questions and Answers ^2-13-3-0

There are four immeasurables. Here a monk dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness, so too the second, so too the third, so too the fourth; thus above, below, across, everywhere, extending to all as to himself, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness that is abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, without ill will. He dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by compassion, so too the second, so too the third, so too the fourth; thus above, below, across, everywhere, extending to all as to himself, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by compassion that is abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, without ill will. He dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by sympathetic joy, so too the second, so too the third, so too the fourth; thus above, below, across, everywhere, extending to all as to himself, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by sympathetic joy that is abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, without ill will. He dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by equanimity, so too the second, so too the third, so too the fourth; thus above, below, across, everywhere, extending to all as to himself, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by equanimity that is abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, without ill will. ^2-699

Of the four immeasurables, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many neutral... how many conflicted, how many peaceful? ^2-700

##### Triads ^2-13-3-1-0

May be wholesome, may be neutral. The three immeasurables are joined with pleasant feeling; equanimity is joined with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. May be resultant, may be things that produce a result, may be things that are neither resultant nor things that produce a result. May be clung to and able to be clung to, may be not clung to but able to be clung to. They are undefiled but open to defilement. The three immeasurables may be accompanied by applied and sustained thought, may be without applied thought but having only sustained thought, may be without applied and sustained thought; equanimity is without applied and sustained thought. The three immeasurables may be accompanied by joy, may be accompanied by happiness, not accompanied by equanimity, or it should not be said that they are accompanied by joy; equanimity is accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned by neither seeing nor development. They are rooted in neither what is to be abandoned by seeing nor what is to be abandoned by development. May lead to accumulation, may neither lead to accumulation nor to dismantling. They are neither in training nor beyond training. They are exalted. It should not be said that they have a limited object, an exalted object, or a measureless object. They are middling, unfixed. It should not be said that they have the path as object, that they have the path as root, or that they have the path as dominant factor. May be arisen, may be unarisen, may be bound to arise. May be past, may be future, may be present. It should not be said that they have a past object, a future object, or a present object. May be internal, may be external, may be both internal and external; they have an external object; they are invisible and without resistance. ^2-701

##### Pairs ^2-13-3-2-0

Loving-kindness is a root; the other three immeasurables are not a root, but are rooted, and joined with a root. Loving-kindness is both a root and rooted; it should not be said of the other three immeasurables that they are both a root and rooted — they are rooted but not a root. Loving-kindness is both a root and joined with a root; it should not be said of the other three immeasurables that they are both a root and joined with a root — they are joined with a root but not a root. The other three immeasurables are not a root, but are rooted; it should not be said of loving-kindness either that it is not a root but rooted, or that it is not a root and rootless.
They have conditions. They are conditioned. They are invisible. They are without resistance. They are immaterial. They are worldly. They are knowable by some kinds of consciousness, not knowable by others. They are not themselves a mental taint, but are subject to mental taints, and dissociated from a mental taint. It should not be said that they are both a mental taint and subject to mental taints — they are subject to mental taints but not themselves a mental taint. It should not be said that they are both a mental taint and joined with a mental taint — they are joined with a mental taint but not themselves a mental taint. They are dissociated from a mental taint yet subject to mental taints.
They are not themselves a fetter... not themselves a tie... not themselves a flood... not themselves a yoke... not themselves a hindrance... not themselves wrong grasping... They have an object. They are not mind. They are mental factors. They are joined with mind. They are mingled with mind. They originate from mind. They co-arise with mind. They follow mind. They are mingled with mind and originating from mind. They are mingled with mind, originating from mind, and co-arising with mind. They are mingled with mind, originating from mind, and following mind. They are external. They are not derived. May be clung to, may be not clung to.
They are not themselves clinging... not themselves a defilement... They are not to be abandoned by seeing. They are not to be abandoned by development. They are not rooted in what is to be abandoned by seeing. They are not rooted in what is to be abandoned by development. The three immeasurables may be accompanied by applied thought, may be without applied thought; equanimity is without applied thought. The three immeasurables may be accompanied by sustained thought, may be without sustained thought; equanimity is without sustained thought. The three immeasurables may have joy, may be without joy; equanimity is without joy. The three immeasurables may be accompanied by joy, may not be accompanied by joy; equanimity is not accompanied by joy. The three immeasurables are accompanied by happiness; equanimity is not accompanied by happiness. Equanimity is accompanied by equanimity; the three immeasurables are not accompanied by equanimity. They do not belong to the sense sphere. They belong to the fine-material sphere. They do not belong to the immaterial sphere. They are included. They do not lead out. They are unfixed. They are surpassable. They are peaceful.
