### Analysis of the Awakening Factors ^2-10-0

#### Discourse Classification ^2-10-1-0

The seven awakening factors: the factor of mindfulness, the factor of investigation, the factor of energy, the factor of joy, the factor of tranquillity, the factor of concentration, the factor of equanimity. ^2-466

Herein, what is the factor of mindfulness? Here a monk is mindful, endowed with the highest mindfulness and alertness, able to recall and remember what was done and said long ago — this is called "the factor of mindfulness".
Dwelling thus mindfully, he investigates that state with wisdom, examines it, and inquires into it thoroughly — this is called "the factor of investigation".
As he investigates, examines, and inquires thoroughly into that state with wisdom, tireless energy arises in him — this is called "the factor of energy".
For one whose energy is aroused, unworldly joy arises — this is called "the factor of joy".
For one who is joyful in mind, the body becomes tranquil, and the mind also becomes tranquil — this is called "the factor of tranquillity".
For one whose body is tranquil and who is happy, the mind becomes concentrated — this is called "the factor of concentration".
Dwelling thus with mind concentrated, he looks on well with equanimity — this is called "the factor of equanimity". ^2-467

The seven awakening factors: the factor of mindfulness, the factor of investigation, the factor of energy, the factor of joy, the factor of tranquillity, the factor of concentration, the factor of equanimity. ^2-468

Herein, what is the factor of mindfulness? There is mindfulness of internal things, and there is mindfulness of external things. Whichever mindfulness of internal things there is, that factor of mindfulness leads to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to Nibbāna. Whichever mindfulness of external things there is, that factor of mindfulness leads to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to Nibbāna.
Herein, what is the factor of investigation? There is examination of internal things, and there is examination of external things. Whichever examination of internal things there is, that factor of investigation leads to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to Nibbāna. Whichever examination of external things there is, that factor of investigation leads to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to Nibbāna.
Herein, what is the factor of energy? There is bodily energy, and there is mental energy. Whichever bodily energy there is, that factor of energy leads to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to Nibbāna. Whichever mental energy there is, that factor of energy leads to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to Nibbāna.

Whichever joy accompanied by applied and sustained thought there is, that factor of joy leads to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to Nibbāna. Whichever joy without applied and sustained thought there is, that factor of joy leads to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to Nibbāna.

Whichever equanimity toward external things there is, that factor of equanimity leads to direct knowledge, to awakening, and to Nibbāna. ^2-469

Here a monk develops the factor of mindfulness that inclines toward seclusion, dispassion, and cessation, and ripens in release; he develops the factor of investigation... he develops the factor of energy... he develops the factor of joy... he develops the factor of tranquillity... he develops the factor of concentration... he develops the factor of equanimity that inclines toward seclusion, dispassion, and cessation, and ripens in release.

#### Abhidhamma Classification ^2-10-2-0

The seven awakening factors: the factor of mindfulness, the factor of investigation, the factor of energy, the factor of joy, the factor of tranquillity, the factor of concentration, the factor of equanimity. ^2-472

Herein, what are the seven awakening factors? Here, at the time when a monk develops the world-transcending absorption that leads out of the world and leads to the dismantling of accumulation, for the abandoning of wrong views and for reaching the first stage — quite secluded from sense pleasures... he enters and dwells in the first absorption, a difficult practice with slow direct knowledge — at that time the seven awakening factors are present: the factor of mindfulness... the factor of equanimity. ^2-473

Herein, what is the factor of mindfulness? Whatever mindfulness, recollection... right mindfulness, the factor of mindfulness, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "the factor of mindfulness".
Herein, what is the factor of investigation? Whatever wisdom, understanding... non-delusion, investigation of states, right view, the factor of investigation, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "the factor of investigation".
Herein, what is the factor of energy? Whatever mental initiation of energy... right effort, the factor of energy, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "the factor of energy".
Herein, what is the factor of joy? Whatever joy, delight, gladness, rejoicing, mirth, merriment, felicity, elation, satisfaction of mind — the factor of joy: this is called "the factor of joy".
Herein, what is the factor of tranquillity? Whatever tranquillity, tranquillisation, calming, thorough calming, and the state of having been thoroughly calmed, of the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations, and the aggregate of consciousness — the factor of tranquillity: this is called "the factor of tranquillity".
Herein, what is the factor of concentration? Whatever steadiness of mind... right concentration, the factor of concentration, a path factor, included in the path: this is called "the factor of concentration".
Herein, what is the factor of equanimity? Whatever equanimity, observing with equanimity, watching with equanimity, and neutrality of mind — the factor of equanimity: this is called "the factor of equanimity". These are called the seven awakening factors. The remaining states are joined with the seven awakening factors. ^2-474

#### Questions and Answers ^2-10-3-0

The seven awakening factors: the factor of mindfulness, the factor of investigation, the factor of energy, the factor of joy, the factor of tranquillity, the factor of concentration, the factor of equanimity. ^2-482

Of the seven awakening factors, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many neutral... how many conflicted, how many peaceful? ^2-483

##### Triads ^2-10-3-1-0

May be wholesome, may be neutral. The factor of joy is joined with pleasant feeling; the six factors may be joined with pleasant feeling, or may be joined with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. May be things that produce a result, may be things having the nature to produce a result. They are not clung to and not able to be clung to. They are not defiled and not open to defilement. May have applied and sustained thought, may have only sustained thought, or may have neither. The factor of joy is not accompanied by joy — it is accompanied by happiness, not accompanied by equanimity; the six factors may be accompanied by joy, may be accompanied by happiness, or may be accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned by neither seeing nor development. They are rooted in neither what is to be abandoned by seeing nor what is to be abandoned by development. May lead to dismantling, may neither lead to accumulation nor to dismantling. May be still in training, may be beyond training. They are measureless. They have a measureless object. They are superior. May be fixed in rightness, may be unfixed. They do not have the path as object; may have the path as root, may have the path as dominant factor; it should not be said without qualification that they have the path as root, or that they have the path as dominant factor. May be arisen, may be unarisen, may be bound to arise. May be past, may be future, may be present. It should not be said that they have a past object, a future object, or a present object. May be internal, may be external, may be both internal and external. They have an external object. They are invisible and without resistance. ^2-484

##### Pairs ^2-10-3-2-0

The factor of investigation is a root; the six factors are joined with a root. The factor of investigation is both a root and rooted; the six factors should not be said to be both a root and rooted — they are rooted but not themselves a root. The factor of investigation is both a root and joined with a root; the six factors should not be said to be both a root and joined with a root — they are joined with a root but not themselves a root. The six factors are not a root, but are rooted; the factor of investigation should not be said to be either "not a root, but rooted" or "not a root and rootless". They have conditions. They are conditioned. They are invisible. They offer no resistance. They are immaterial. They are beyond the world. They are knowable by some kinds of consciousness, not knowable by others. They are not themselves a mental taint. They are free of mental taints. They are dissociated from a mental taint. They should not be said to be both a mental taint and subject to mental taints, or to be subject to mental taints but not itself a mental taint. They should not be said to be both a mental taint and joined with a mental taint, or to be joined with a mental taint but not itself a mental taint. They are dissociated from a mental taint and free of it. They are not themselves a fetter... not themselves a tie... not themselves a flood... not themselves a yoke... not themselves a hindrance... not themselves wrong grasping... They have an object. They are not mind. They are mental factors. They are joined with mind. They are mingled with mind. They originate from mind. They co-arise with mind. They follow mind. They are mingled with mind and originating from mind. They are mingled with mind, originating from mind, and co-arising with mind. They are mingled with mind, originating from mind, and following mind. They are external. They are not derived. They are not clung to. They are not themselves clinging... not themselves a defilement... They are not to be abandoned by seeing. They are not to be abandoned by development. They are not rooted in what is to be abandoned by seeing. They are not rooted in what is to be abandoned by development. They may have applied thought, or may lack applied thought. They may have sustained thought, or may lack sustained thought.
The factor of joy lacks joy; the six factors may have joy, or may lack joy. The factor of joy is not accompanied by joy; the six factors may be accompanied by joy, or may not be accompanied by joy. The factor of joy is accompanied by happiness; the six factors may be accompanied by happiness, or may not be accompanied by happiness. The factor of joy is not accompanied by equanimity; the six factors may be accompanied by equanimity, or may not be accompanied by equanimity. They do not belong to the sense sphere. They do not belong to the fine-material sphere. They do not belong to the immaterial sphere. They are unincluded. They may lead to liberation, or may not lead to liberation. They may be fixed, or may be unfixed. They are unsurpassed. They are peaceful.
