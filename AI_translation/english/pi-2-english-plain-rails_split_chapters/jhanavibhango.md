### Analysis of the Absorptions ^2-12-0

#### Discourse Classification ^2-12-1-0

Here a monk dwells restrained by the restraint of the code of monastic rules, accomplished in proper conduct and resort, seeing danger in the slightest faults, having undertaken the training he trains in the training rules, guarding the doors of the sense faculties, moderate in food, devoted to wakefulness in the first and last watches of the night, persistent, discerning, and devoted to the development of the states that are aids to awakening. He acts with clear comprehension when going forward and returning; he acts with clear comprehension when looking ahead and looking away; he acts with clear comprehension when bending and stretching his limbs; he acts with clear comprehension when wearing his outer robe, bowl, and robes; he acts with clear comprehension when eating, drinking, chewing, and tasting; he acts with clear comprehension when defecating and urinating; he acts with clear comprehension when walking, standing, sitting, sleeping, waking, speaking, and staying silent. He resorts to a secluded dwelling place: the forest, the foot of a tree, a mountain, a ravine, a hillside cave, a charnel ground, a jungle thicket, the open air, a heap of straw — quiet, with little noise, free from crowds, hidden from people, suitable for retreat. Having gone to the forest, to the foot of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, he sits down, folding his legs crosswise, holding his body straight, and establishing mindfulness in front of him. Having abandoned covetousness for the world, he dwells with a mind free from covetousness; he purifies his mind of covetousness. Having abandoned ill will and hatred, he dwells with a mind free from ill will, sympathetic to the welfare of all living beings; he purifies his mind of ill will and hatred. Having abandoned dullness and drowsiness, he dwells free from dullness and drowsiness, perceiving light, mindful and clearly comprehending; he purifies his mind of dullness and drowsiness. Having abandoned restlessness and remorse, he dwells unagitated, with a mind inwardly calmed; he purifies his mind of restlessness and remorse. Having abandoned doubt, he dwells having crossed over doubt, unperplexed about wholesome states; he purifies his mind of doubt. Having abandoned these five hindrances, the corruptions of the mind that weaken wisdom, quite secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, he enters and dwells in the first absorption, accompanied by applied and sustained thought, with joy and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of applied and sustained thought, he enters and dwells in the second absorption, which has internal confidence and concentration of mind, is without applied thought and without sustained thought, and has joy and happiness born of concentration. With the fading away of joy, he dwells with equanimity, mindful and clearly comprehending, and experiences happiness with the body; he enters and dwells in the third absorption, of which the noble ones say: "one of equanimity and mindfulness dwells happily". With the abandoning of happiness and the abandoning of pain, and with the earlier disappearance of gladness and sadness, he enters and dwells in the fourth absorption, which is neither painful nor pleasant, and which has purity of mindfulness through equanimity. With the complete transcending of perceptions of material form, with the disappearance of perceptions of resistance, and with non-attention to perceptions of diversity, aware that "space is infinite", he enters and dwells in the base of infinite space. With the complete transcending of the base of infinite space, aware that "consciousness is infinite", he enters and dwells in the base of infinite consciousness. With the complete transcending of the base of infinite consciousness, aware that "there is nothing", he enters and dwells in the base of nothingness. With the complete transcending of the base of nothingness, he enters and dwells in the base of neither perception nor non-perception. ^2-508

#### Questions and Answers ^2-12-3-0

The four absorptions: here a monk, quite secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, enters and dwells in the first absorption, accompanied by applied and sustained thought, with joy and happiness born of seclusion... With the stilling of applied and sustained thought, he enters and dwells in the second absorption, which has internal confidence and concentration of mind, is without applied thought and without sustained thought, and has joy and happiness born of concentration... With the fading away of joy, he dwells with equanimity, mindful and clearly comprehending, and experiences happiness with the body; he enters and dwells in the third absorption, of which the noble ones say: "one of equanimity and mindfulness dwells happily"... With the abandoning of happiness and the abandoning of pain, and with the earlier disappearance of gladness and sadness, he enters and dwells in the fourth absorption, which is neither painful nor pleasant, and which has purity of mindfulness through equanimity. ^2-638

##### Triads ^2-12-3-1-0

May be wholesome, may be neutral. The first three absorptions, apart from the happy feeling arisen in them, are joined with happy feeling; the fourth absorption, apart from the neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling arisen in it, is joined with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. May be clung to and able to be clung to, may be not clung to but able to be clung to, may be not clung to and not able to be clung to. May be undefiled but open to defilement, may be undefiled and not open to defilement. The first absorption, apart from the applied and sustained thought arisen in it, has applied and sustained thought; the other three absorptions are without applied and sustained thought. The first two absorptions, apart from the joy arisen in them, are accompanied by joy; the first three absorptions, apart from the happiness arisen in them, are accompanied by happiness; the fourth absorption, apart from the equanimity arisen in it, is accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned by neither seeing nor development. They are rooted in neither what is to be abandoned by seeing nor what is to be abandoned by development. May lead to accumulation, may lead to dismantling, may neither lead to accumulation nor to dismantling. May be still in training, may be beyond training, may be neither in training nor beyond it. May be exalted, may be measureless. The first three absorptions should not be said to have a limited object or an exalted object; may have a measureless object, or it should not be said that they have a measureless object; the fourth absorption may have a limited object, may have an exalted object, may have a measureless object, or it should not be said that it has a limited object, an exalted object, or a measureless object. May be middling, may be refined. May be fixed in rightness, may be unfixed. The first three absorptions do not have the path as object; may have the path as root, may have the path as dominant factor; it should not be said without qualification that they have the path as root or that they have the path as dominant factor; the fourth absorption may have the path as object, may have the path as root, may have the path as dominant factor; it should not be said without qualification that it has the path as object, that it has the path as root, or that it has the path as dominant factor. May be arisen, may be unarisen, may be bound to arise. May be past, may be future, may be present.
