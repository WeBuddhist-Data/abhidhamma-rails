#### Description of Threes of Persons ^4-1-3-0

What kind of person is one without hope? Here, someone is immoral, of bad character, impure, of suspect conduct, secretive in their actions, not an ascetic though claiming to be an ascetic, not living the holy life though claiming to live the holy life, rotten inside, oozing, and full of filth. They hear: "It seems that the monk named so-and-so, through the destruction of the taints, has realized by direct knowledge themselves, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and lives having entered it." They do not think: "When, I wonder, shall I too, through the destruction of the taints, realize by direct knowledge myself, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and live having entered it?" This person is called "one without hope". ^4-91

What kind of person is one with hope? Here, someone has good morality and is of good character. They hear: "It seems that the monk named so-and-so, through the destruction of the taints, has realized by direct knowledge themselves, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and lives having entered it." They think: "When, I wonder, shall I too, through the destruction of the taints, realize by direct knowledge myself, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and live having entered it?" This person is called "one with hope". ^4-92

What kind of person is one whose hope is gone? Here, someone, through the destruction of the taints, has realized by direct knowledge themselves, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and lives having entered it. They hear: "It seems that the monk named so-and-so, through the destruction of the taints, has realized by direct knowledge themselves, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and lives having entered it." They do not think: "When, I wonder, shall I too, through the destruction of the taints, realize by direct knowledge myself, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and live having entered it?" Why is that? Because the hope for liberation that they had before, when they were not yet liberated, has died away. This person is called "one whose hope is gone". ^4-93

In this context, what are the three persons compared to sick people? There are three sick people – here, someone who is sick, whether they receive suitable food or do not receive suitable food, whether they receive suitable medicine or do not receive suitable medicine, whether they receive a proper nurse or do not receive a proper nurse, does not recover from that illness. (1)
Then again, someone who is sick, whether they receive suitable food or do not receive suitable food, whether they receive suitable medicine or do not receive suitable medicine, whether they receive a proper nurse or do not receive a proper nurse, recovers from that illness. (2)
Then again, someone who is sick, when they receive suitable food and not when they do not, when they receive suitable medicine and not when they do not, when they receive a proper nurse and not when they do not, recovers from that illness. (3)
Of these, it is on account of this sick person – the one who, when they receive suitable food and not when they do not, when they receive suitable medicine and not when they do not, when they receive a proper nurse and not when they do not, recovers from that illness – that the Blessed One has allowed meals for the sick, allowed medicines for the sick, and allowed nurses for the sick. And on account of this sick person, other sick people too should be nursed.
In the same way [variant: evameva (Sī.)], there are three persons compared to sick people who exist and are found in the world. Which three? Here, someone, whether they get to see the Tathāgata or do not get to see the Tathāgata, whether they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata or do not get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, does not enter the course of rightness in wholesome states. (1)
Then again, someone, whether they get to see the Tathāgata or do not get to see the Tathāgata, whether they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata or do not get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, enters the course of rightness in wholesome states. (2)
Then again, someone, when they get to see the Tathāgata and not when they do not, when they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata and not when they do not, enters the course of rightness in wholesome states. (3)
Of these, it is on account of this person – the one who, when they get to see the Tathāgata and not when they do not, when they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata and not when they do not, enters the course of rightness in wholesome states – that the Blessed One has allowed the Teaching to be taught; and on account of this person, the Teaching should be taught to others too. These are the three persons compared to sick people who exist and are found in the world. ^4-94

What kind of person is a body-witness? Here, someone lives having touched the eight liberations with the body, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "a body-witness". ^4-95

What kind of person is one attained to view? Here, someone really understands, "This is suffering"; really understands, "This is the origin of suffering"; really understands, "This is the cessation of suffering"; really understands, "This is the way leading to the cessation of suffering". And the teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and examined by them with wisdom. And, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one attained to view". ^4-96

What kind of person is one liberated by faith? Here, someone really understands, "This is suffering" … And the teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and examined by them with wisdom. And, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed, but not in the way they are for one attained to view – this person is called "one liberated by faith". ^4-97

What kind of person is one whose speech is like dung? Here, someone is a liar. When they have gone to a council, or to an assembly, or into the midst of their relatives, or into the midst of a guild, or into the midst of the royal court, and are summoned and questioned as a witness – "Come, good man [variant: ehi bho (Syā., Ka.) Ma. Ni. 1.440; A. Ni. 3.28], tell what you know" – then, not knowing, they say, "I know", or, knowing, they say, "I do not know"; not seeing, they say, "I see", or, seeing, they say, "I do not see". Thus, for their own sake, or for the sake of others, or for the sake of some small material gain, they speak deliberate lies – this person is called "one whose speech is like dung". ^4-98

What kind of person is one whose speech is like flowers? Here, someone has abandoned lying and refrains from telling lies. When they have gone to a council, or to an assembly, or into the midst of their relatives, or into the midst of a guild, or into the midst of the royal court, and are summoned and questioned as a witness – "Come, good man, tell what you know" – then, not knowing, they say, "I do not know", or, knowing, they say, "I know"; not seeing, they say, "I do not see", or, seeing, they say, "I see". Thus, neither for their own sake, nor for the sake of others, nor for the sake of some small material gain, do they speak deliberate lies – this person is called "one whose speech is like flowers". ^4-99

What kind of person is one whose speech is like honey? Here, someone speaks words that are gentle, pleasing to the ear, affectionate, going to the heart, courteous, loved by many people, and liked by many people; they speak that kind of speech – this person is called "one whose speech is like honey". ^4-100

What kind of person is one whose mind is like an open sore? Here, someone is angry and easily upset. Even when only a little is said to them, they lose their temper, get irritated, become hostile, become stubborn [variant: patiṭṭhīyati (Syā., Ka.) A. Ni. 3.25], and show irritation, hatred and displeasure. Just as an infected sore, when struck by a stick or a potsherd [variant: kathalāya (Ka.), kathalena (commentary) A. Ni. 3.25], discharges even more [variant: assavanoti (Sī.)], in the same way, here, someone is angry and easily upset; even when only a little is said to them, they lose their temper, get irritated, become hostile, become stubborn, and show irritation, hatred and displeasure – this person is called "one whose mind is like an open sore". ^4-101

What kind of person is one whose mind is like lightning? Here, someone really understands, "This is suffering"; really understands, "This is the origin of suffering"; really understands, "This is the cessation of suffering"; really understands, "This is the way leading to the cessation of suffering". Just as a person with eyes would see shapes in the pitch darkness of the night in a flash of lightning, in the same way, here, someone really understands, "This is suffering"; really understands, "This is the origin of suffering"; really understands, "This is the cessation of suffering"; really understands, "This is the way leading to the cessation of suffering" – this person is called "one whose mind is like lightning". ^4-102

What kind of person is one whose mind is like a diamond? Here, someone, through the destruction of the taints, has realized by direct knowledge themselves, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and lives having entered it. Just as there is nothing, whether gem or stone, that a diamond cannot break, in the same way, here, someone, through the destruction of the taints, has realized by direct knowledge themselves, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and lives having entered it – this person is called "one whose mind is like a diamond". ^4-103

What kind of person is one who is blind? Here, someone does not have the kind of eye with which they could gain wealth not yet gained, or increase wealth already gained; nor do they have the kind of eye with which they could know wholesome and unwholesome states, know blameworthy and blameless states, know inferior and excellent states, know dark and bright states that are opposites of each other – this person is called "one who is blind". ^4-104

What kind of person is one who is one-eyed? Here, someone has the kind of eye with which they could gain wealth not yet gained, or increase wealth already gained; but they do not have the kind of eye with which they could know wholesome and unwholesome states, know blameworthy and blameless states, know inferior and excellent states, know dark and bright states that are opposites of each other – this person is called "one who is one-eyed". ^4-105

What kind of person is one who is two-eyed? Here, someone has the kind of eye with which they could gain wealth not yet gained, or increase wealth already gained; and they also have the kind of eye with which they could know wholesome and unwholesome states, know blameworthy and blameless states, know inferior and excellent states, know dark and bright states that are opposites of each other – this person is called "one who is two-eyed". ^4-106

What kind of person is one whose wisdom is upturned? Here, someone often goes [variant: gato (Sī.), gantvā (Syā.)] to the monastery to hear the Teaching [variant: dhammassavanāya (Syā.)] in the presence of the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with its meaning and its wording; they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. Sitting on that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, pay no attention to its middle, and pay no attention to its end. Even after rising from that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, pay no attention to its middle, and pay no attention to its end. Just as when a pot is turned upside down [variant: nikujjo (Syā.) A. Ni. 3.30], the water poured onto it runs off and does not stay, in the same way, here, someone often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching in the presence of the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with its meaning and its wording; they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. Sitting on that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, pay no attention to its middle, and pay no attention to its end. Even after rising from that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, pay no attention to its middle, and pay no attention to its end – this person is called "one whose wisdom is upturned". ^4-107

What kind of person is one whose wisdom is like a lap? Here, someone often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching in the presence of the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with its meaning and its wording; they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. Sitting on that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, pay attention to its middle, and pay attention to its end. But after rising from that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, pay no attention to its middle, and pay no attention to its end. Just as when a man has various foods piled in his lap – sesame, rice [variant: tilataṇḍulā (Ka.) A. Ni. 3.30], sweets, jujubes – and, getting up from that seat, would spill them through absent-mindedness, in the same way, here, someone often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching in the presence of the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with its meaning and its wording; they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. Sitting on that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, pay attention to its middle, and pay attention to its end. But after rising from that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, pay no attention to its middle, and pay no attention to its end – this person is called "one whose wisdom is like a lap". ^4-108

What kind of person is one whose wisdom is wide? Here, someone often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching in the presence of the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with its meaning and its wording; they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. Sitting on that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, pay attention to its middle, and pay attention to its end. Even after rising from that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, pay attention to its middle, and pay attention to its end. Just as when a pot is set upright, the water poured into it stays and does not run off, in the same way, here, someone often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching in the presence of the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with its meaning and its wording; they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. Sitting on that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, pay attention to its middle, and pay attention to its end. Even after rising from that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, pay attention to its middle, and pay attention to its end – this person is called "one whose wisdom is wide". ^4-109

What kind of person is one who is not free of passion for sensual pleasures or for becoming? Stream-enterers and once-returners – these persons are called "those not free of passion for sensual pleasures or for becoming". ^4-110

What kind of person is one who is free of passion for sensual pleasures but not free of passion for becoming? A non-returner – this person is called "one who is free of passion for sensual pleasures but not free of passion for becoming". ^4-111

What kind of person is one who is free of passion for both sensual pleasures and becoming? An arahant – this person is called "one who is free of passion for both sensual pleasures and becoming". ^4-112

What kind of person is one like a line drawn on rock? Here, someone often gets angry, and that anger of theirs stays with them for a long time. Just as a line drawn on rock does not quickly disappear through wind or water, but lasts a long time, in the same way, here, someone often gets angry, and that anger of theirs stays with them for a long time – this person is called "one like a line drawn on rock". ^4-113

What kind of person is one like a line drawn on earth? Here, someone often gets angry, but that anger of theirs does not stay with them for a long time. Just as a line drawn on earth [variant: paṭhaviyā (Sī., Syā.)] quickly disappears through wind or water, and does not last long, in the same way, here, someone often gets angry, but that anger of theirs does not stay with them for a long time – this person is called "one like a line drawn on earth". ^4-114

What kind of person is one like a line drawn on water? Here, someone, even when spoken to roughly, spoken to harshly, spoken to unpleasantly, simply stays in harmony [variant: … ceva (Syā.) A. Ni. 3.133], makes peace [variant: … ceva (Syā.) A. Ni. 3.133], and is friendly [variant: … ceva (Syā.) A. Ni. 3.133]. Just as a line drawn on water quickly disappears and does not last long, in the same way, here, someone, even when spoken to roughly, spoken to harshly, spoken to unpleasantly, simply stays in harmony, makes peace, and is friendly – this person is called "one like a line drawn on water". ^4-115

In this context, what are the three persons compared to coarse cloth? There are three kinds of coarse cloth – coarse cloth, even when new, is ugly, rough to the touch, and of little value; coarse cloth, even when half-worn, is ugly, rough to the touch, and of little value; coarse cloth, even when old, is ugly, rough to the touch, and of little value. Even old coarse cloth is used for scouring pots, or else people throw it away on a rubbish heap. In the same way, there are these three persons compared to coarse cloth who exist and are found among monks. Which three? If a monk, even one newly ordained, is immoral and of bad character, this is their ugliness. Just as that coarse cloth is ugly, this person is like that. Those who associate with them, keep company with them, attend on them, and follow their example – for them this leads to harm and suffering for a long time. This is their roughness. Just as that coarse cloth is rough to the touch, this person is like that. And for those from whom they receive robes, alms food, lodging, and medicines and supplies for the sick, it does not bring great fruit or great benefit. This is their little value. Just as that coarse cloth is of little value, this person is like that.
If a monk of middle standing … if a monk, even an elder, is immoral and of bad character, this is their ugliness. Just as that coarse cloth is ugly, this person is like that. Those who associate with them, keep company with them, attend on them, and follow their example – for them this leads to harm and suffering for a long time. This is their roughness. Just as that coarse cloth is rough to the touch, this person is like that. And for those from whom they receive robes, alms food, lodging, and medicines and supplies for the sick, it does not bring great fruit or great benefit. This is their little value. Just as that coarse cloth is of little value, this person is like that.
If an elder monk of this kind speaks in the midst of the Sangha, the monks say to them: "What is the use of your speaking, foolish and incompetent as you are? Do you really think you have something to say?" Angry and displeased, they utter the kind of speech for which the Sangha suspends them, like coarse cloth thrown on a rubbish heap. These are the three persons compared to coarse cloth who exist and are found among monks. ^4-116

In this context, what are the three persons compared to fine Kāsi cloth? There are three kinds of fine Kāsi cloth – fine Kāsi cloth, even when new, is beautiful, pleasant to the touch, and of great value; fine Kāsi cloth, even when half-worn, is beautiful, pleasant to the touch, and of great value; fine Kāsi cloth, even when old, is beautiful, pleasant to the touch, and of great value. Even old fine Kāsi cloth is used as a wrap for jewels, or else people keep it in a perfume box.
In the same way, there are these three persons compared to fine Kāsi cloth who exist and are found among monks. Which three? If a monk, even one newly ordained, has good morality and is of good character, this is their beauty. Just as that fine Kāsi cloth is beautiful, this person is like that. Those who associate with them, keep company with them, attend on them, and follow their example – for them this leads to welfare and happiness for a long time. This is their pleasantness. Just as that fine Kāsi cloth is pleasant to the touch, this person is like that. And for those from whom they [variant: yesaṃ kho pana (all editions) A. Ni. 3.100] receive [variant: patigaṇhāti (Sī.); but it agrees with the Rūpasiddhi subcommentary] robes, alms food, lodging, and medicines and supplies for the sick, it brings great fruit and great benefit. This is their great value. Just as that fine Kāsi cloth is of great value, this person is like that.
If a monk of middle standing … if a monk, even an elder, has good morality and is of good character, this is their beauty. Just as that fine Kāsi cloth is beautiful, this person is like that. Those who associate with them, keep company with them, attend on them, and follow their example – for them this leads to welfare and happiness for a long time. This is their pleasantness. Just as that fine Kāsi cloth is pleasant to the touch, this person is like that. And for those from whom they receive robes, alms food, lodging, and medicines and supplies for the sick, it brings great fruit and great benefit. This is their great value. Just as that fine Kāsi cloth is of great value, this person is like that.
If an elder monk of this kind speaks in the midst of the Sangha, the monks say: "Be quiet, venerable ones; the elder monk is speaking on the Teaching and the Discipline." Those words of theirs are taken to heart, like fine Kāsi cloth kept in a perfume box. These are the three persons compared to fine Kāsi cloth who exist and are found among monks. ^4-117

What kind of person is one who is easy to measure? Here, someone is restless, puffed up, fickle, talkative, loose in speech, forgetful, not clearly comprehending, unconcentrated, with a wandering mind, and with unrestrained faculties – this person is called "one who is easy to measure". ^4-118

What kind of person is one who is hard to measure? Here, someone is not restless, not puffed up, not fickle, not talkative, not loose in speech; their mindfulness is established, they are clearly comprehending, concentrated, with a unified mind, and with restrained faculties – this person is called "one who is hard to measure". ^4-119

What kind of person is one who cannot be measured? Here, someone, through the destruction of the taints, has realized by direct knowledge themselves, in this very life, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that is free of taints, and lives having entered it – this person is called "one who cannot be measured". ^4-120

What kind of person is one who should not be associated with, kept company with, or attended on? Here, someone is inferior in moral discipline, concentration and wisdom. A person like this should not be associated with, kept company with, or attended on, except out of kindness, except out of compassion. ^4-121

What kind of person is one who should be associated with, kept company with, and attended on? Here, someone is equal in moral discipline, concentration and wisdom. A person like this should be associated with, kept company with, and attended on. Why is that? "As we are alike in moral discipline, there will be talk about moral discipline among us, we will be at ease with it, and it will flow on for us; as we are alike in concentration, there will be talk about concentration among us, we will be at ease with it, and it will flow on [variant: pavattanī (Sī.) A. Ni. 3.26] for us; as we are alike in wisdom, there will be talk about wisdom among us, we will be at ease with it, and it will flow on for us." Therefore a person like this should be associated with, kept company with, and attended on. ^4-122

What kind of person is one who should be associated with, kept company with, and attended on with honour and respect? Here, someone is superior in moral discipline, concentration and wisdom. A person like this should be associated with, kept company with, and attended on with honour and respect. Why is that? "I shall complete the aggregate of moral discipline that is not yet complete, or I shall support with wisdom, in this respect and that, the aggregate of moral discipline that is complete; I shall complete the aggregate of concentration that is not yet complete, or I shall support with wisdom, in this respect and that, the aggregate of concentration that is complete; I shall complete the aggregate of wisdom that is not yet complete, or I shall support with wisdom, in this respect and that, the aggregate of wisdom that is complete." Therefore a person like this should be associated with, kept company with, and attended on with honour and respect. ^4-123

What kind of person is one who should be shunned, and not associated with, kept company with, or attended on? Here, someone is immoral, of bad character, impure, of suspect conduct, secretive in their actions, not an ascetic though claiming to be an ascetic, not living the holy life though claiming to live the holy life, rotten inside, oozing, and full of filth. A person like this should be shunned, and not associated with, kept company with, or attended on. Why is that? Even though someone does not follow the example of a person like this, a bad reputation still spreads about them: "This individual has bad friends, bad companions, and leans towards the bad." Just as a snake that has fallen into dung, even though it does not bite, still smears one, in the same way, even though someone does not follow the example of a person like this, a bad reputation still spreads about them: "This individual has bad friends, bad companions, and leans towards the bad"! Therefore a person like this should be shunned, and not associated with, kept company with, or attended on. ^4-124

What kind of person is one who should be regarded with detachment, and not associated with, kept company with, or attended on? Here, someone is angry and easily upset. Even when only a little is said to them, they lose their temper, get irritated, become hostile, become stubborn, and show irritation, hatred and displeasure. Just as an infected sore, when struck by a stick or a potsherd, discharges even more, in the same way, here, someone is angry and easily upset; even when only a little is said to them, they lose their temper, get irritated, become hostile, become stubborn, and show irritation, hatred and displeasure. Just as a firebrand of tinduka wood, when struck by a stick or a potsherd, hisses and crackles even more, in the same way, here, someone is angry and easily upset; even when only a little is said to them, they lose their temper, get irritated, become hostile, become stubborn, and show irritation, hatred and displeasure. Just as a cesspit, when struck by a stick or a potsherd, smells even worse, in the same way, here, someone is angry and easily upset; even when only a little is said to them, they lose their temper, get irritated, become hostile, become stubborn, and show irritation, hatred and displeasure; a person like this should be regarded with detachment, and not associated with, kept company with, or attended on. Why is that? "They might insult me, abuse me, or do me harm!" Therefore a person like this should be regarded with detachment, and not associated with, kept company with, or attended on. ^4-125

What kind of person is one who should be associated with, kept company with, and attended on? Here, someone has good morality and is of good character – a person like this should be associated with, kept company with, and attended on. Why is that? Even though someone does not follow the example of a person like this, a good reputation still spreads about them: "This individual has good friends, good companions, and leans towards the good"! Therefore a person like this should be associated with, kept company with, and attended on. ^4-126

What kind of person is one who fulfils morality, but practises concentration only to a limited degree and wisdom only to a limited degree? Stream-enterers and once-returners – these persons are called those who fulfil morality, but practise concentration only to a limited degree and wisdom only to a limited degree. ^4-127

What kind of person is one who fulfils morality and fulfils concentration, but practises wisdom only to a limited degree? A non-returner – this person is called one who fulfils morality and fulfils concentration, but practises wisdom only to a limited degree. ^4-128

What kind of person is one who fulfils morality, fulfils concentration, and fulfils wisdom? An arahant – this person is called one who fulfils morality, fulfils concentration, and fulfils wisdom. ^4-129

In this context, what are the three teachers? Here, a certain teacher teaches [variant: paññāpeti (Sī., Syā.)] the full understanding of sensual pleasures, but does not teach the full understanding of material forms, and does not teach the full understanding of feelings. Then again, a certain teacher teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures, and teaches the full understanding of material forms, but does not teach the full understanding of feelings. Then again, a certain teacher teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures, teaches the full understanding of material forms, and teaches the full understanding of feelings.
Of these, the teacher who teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures, but does not teach the full understanding of material forms, and does not teach the full understanding of feelings, should be understood as a teacher who has gained the meditative attainment of the form sphere. Of these, the teacher who teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures, and teaches the full understanding of material forms, but does not teach the full understanding of feelings, should be understood as a teacher who has gained the meditative attainment of the formless sphere. Of these, the teacher who teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures, teaches the full understanding of material forms, and teaches the full understanding of feelings, should be understood as a teacher who is a Fully Awakened Buddha. These are the three teachers. ^4-130

In this context, what are another three teachers? Here, a certain teacher teaches that the self is real and lasting both in this very life and in the life to come. Then again, a certain teacher teaches that the self is real and lasting in this very life, but does not teach that the self is real and lasting in the life to come. Then again, a certain teacher does not teach that the self is real and lasting in this very life, and does not teach that the self is real and lasting in the life to come.
Of these, the teacher who teaches that the self is real and lasting both in this very life and in the life to come should be understood as a teacher of eternalism. Of these, the teacher who teaches that the self is real and lasting in this very life, but does not teach that the self is real and lasting in the life to come, should be understood as a teacher of annihilationism. Of these, the teacher who does not teach that the self is real and lasting in this very life, and does not teach that the self is real and lasting in the life to come, should be understood as a teacher who is a Fully Awakened Buddha. These are another three teachers.
End of the exposition of threes. ^4-131
