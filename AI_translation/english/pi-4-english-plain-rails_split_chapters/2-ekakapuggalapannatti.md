### Exposition ^4-1-0

#### Description of Single Types of Persons ^4-1-1-0

What kind of person is one liberated at times? Here, someone lives from time to time, on occasion after occasion, having touched [variant: phassitvā (Sī., Pī.)] the eight liberations with the body, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one liberated at times". ^4-1

What kind of person is one liberated regardless of times? Here, someone does not live from time to time, on occasion after occasion, having touched the eight liberations with the body, yet, having seen with wisdom, their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one liberated regardless of times". All noble persons are liberated regardless of times with respect to the noble liberation. ^4-2

What kind of person is one whose attainments can be shaken? Here, someone has gained the meditative attainments connected with form or with the formless. But they have not gained them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they do not enter them and emerge from them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, and for as long as they wish. It is possible that, through heedlessness, those attainments of that person may be shaken – this person is called "one whose attainments can be shaken". ^4-3

What kind of person is one whose attainments cannot be shaken? Here, someone has gained the meditative attainments connected with form or with the formless. And they have gained them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they enter them and emerge from them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, and for as long as they wish. It is impossible, it cannot happen, that, through heedlessness, those attainments of that person should be shaken – this person is called "one whose attainments cannot be shaken". All noble persons have attainments that cannot be shaken with respect to the noble liberation. ^4-4

What kind of person is one liable to fall away? Here, someone has gained the meditative attainments connected with form or with the formless. But they have not gained them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they do not enter them and emerge from them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, and for as long as they wish. It is possible that, through heedlessness, that person may fall away from those attainments – this person is called "one liable to fall away". ^4-5

What kind of person is one not liable to fall away? Here, someone has gained the meditative attainments connected with form or with the formless. And they have gained them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they enter them and emerge from them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, and for as long as they wish. It is impossible, it cannot happen, that, through heedlessness, that person should fall away from those attainments – this person is called "one not liable to fall away". All noble persons are not liable to fall away with respect to the noble liberation. ^4-6

What kind of person is one able to hold on through volition? Here, someone has gained the meditative attainments connected with form or with the formless. But they have not gained them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they do not enter them and emerge from them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, and for as long as they wish. If they keep their intent on them, they do not fall away from those attainments. If they do not keep their intent on them, they fall away from those attainments – this person is called "one able to hold on through volition". ^4-7

What kind of person is one able to hold on through guarding? Here, someone has gained the meditative attainments connected with form or with the formless. But they have not gained them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they do not enter them and emerge from them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, and for as long as they wish. If they guard them, they do not fall away from those attainments. If they do not guard them, they fall away from those attainments – this person is called "one able to hold on through guarding". ^4-8

What kind of person is an ordinary person? The person whose three fetters are not abandoned, and who is not practising to abandon those states – this person is called "an ordinary person". ^4-9

What kind of person is one who enters the lineage? The person who possesses those states immediately after which the noble state comes about – this person is called "one who enters the lineage". ^4-10

What kind of person is one who refrains out of fear? The seven learners, and those ordinary persons who are virtuous, refrain out of fear. An arahant refrains without fear. ^4-11

What kind of person is one unable to progress? Those persons who are hindered by their actions, hindered by defilements, hindered by results, who are without faith, without zeal, lacking wisdom, and dull, unable to enter the course of rightness in wholesome states – these persons are called "those unable to progress". ^4-12

What kind of person is one able to progress? Those persons who are not hindered by their actions, not hindered by defilements, not hindered by results, who have faith, have zeal, are wise [variant: paññavantā (Sī.)], and are not dull, able to enter the course of rightness in wholesome states – these persons are called "those able to progress". ^4-13

What kind of person is one whose destiny is fixed? The five persons who have done deeds with immediate results, those with wrong view whose destiny is fixed, and the eight noble persons are persons whose destiny is fixed. The remaining persons are persons whose destiny is not fixed. ^4-14

What kind of person is one who is practising? The four persons who possess the path are those who are practising; the four persons who possess the fruit are established in the fruit. ^4-15

What kind of person is one whose two ends come at once? The person for whom the ending of the taints and the ending of life occur neither earlier nor later than each other – this person is called "one whose two ends come at once". ^4-16

What kind of person is one for whom the aeon remains? Suppose this person were practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry, and it were the time for the aeon to burn up: the aeon would not burn up until this person had realized the fruit of stream-entry. This person is called "one for whom the aeon remains". All persons who possess the path are ones for whom the aeon remains. ^4-17

What kind of person is a noble one? The eight noble persons are noble. The remaining persons are not noble. ^4-18

What kind of person is a learner? The four persons who possess the path and the three persons who possess the fruit are "learners". An arahant is an adept. The remaining persons are neither learners nor adepts. ^4-19

What kind of person is one with the three knowledges? A person who possesses the three knowledges is "one with the three knowledges". ^4-20

What kind of person is one with the six direct knowledges? A person who possesses the six direct knowledges is "one with the six direct knowledges". ^4-21

What kind of person is a Fully Awakened Buddha? Here, someone, in teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves; and attains there all-knowing wisdom and mastery of the powers – this person is called "a Fully Awakened Buddha". ^4-22

What kind of person is a Solitary Buddha? Here, someone, in teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves; but does not attain there all-knowing wisdom, nor mastery of the powers – this person is called "a Solitary Buddha". ^4-23

What kind of person is one liberated both ways? Here, someone lives having touched the eight liberations with the body; and, having seen with wisdom, their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one liberated both ways". ^4-24

What kind of person is one liberated by wisdom? Here, someone does not live having touched the eight liberations with the body; yet, having seen with wisdom, their taints are destroyed. This person is called "one liberated by wisdom". ^4-25

What kind of person is a body-witness? Here, someone lives having touched the eight liberations with the body; and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed. This person is called "a body-witness". ^4-26

What kind of person is one attained to view? Here, someone really understands, "This is suffering"; really understands, "This is the origin of suffering"; really understands, "This is the cessation of suffering"; really understands, "This is the way leading to the cessation of suffering". And the teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and examined by them with wisdom. And, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one attained to view". ^4-27

What kind of person is one liberated by faith? Here, someone really understands, "This is suffering"; really understands, "This is the origin of suffering"; really understands, "This is the cessation of suffering"; really understands, "This is the way leading to the cessation of suffering". And the teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and examined by them with wisdom. And, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed, but not in the way they are for one attained to view – this person is called "one liberated by faith". ^4-28

What kind of person is a follower by the teaching? The person who is practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry, in whom the wisdom faculty is predominant, and who cultivates the noble path that is carried by wisdom and led by wisdom – this person is called "a follower by the teaching". A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry is a follower by the teaching; once established in the fruit, they are one attained to view. ^4-29

What kind of person is a follower by faith? The person who is practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry, in whom the faith faculty is predominant, and who cultivates the noble path that is carried by faith and led by faith – this person is called "a follower by faith". A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry is a follower by faith; once established in the fruit, they are one liberated by faith. ^4-30

What kind of person is one with at most seven more lives? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of three fetters, is a stream-enterer, no longer liable to fall into the lower realms, fixed in destiny, and headed for awakening [variant: sambodhiparāyaṇo (Sī., Ka.)]. After running on and wandering among gods and humans seven times, they make an end of suffering – this person is called "one with at most seven more lives". ^4-31

What kind of person is one who goes from family to family? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of three fetters, is a stream-enterer, no longer liable to fall into the lower realms, fixed in destiny, and headed for awakening. After running on and wandering through two or three families, they make an end of suffering – this person is called "one who goes from family to family". ^4-32

What kind of person is one with a single seed? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of three fetters, is a stream-enterer, no longer liable to fall into the lower realms, fixed in destiny, and headed for awakening. After producing just one human becoming, they make an end of suffering – this person is called "one with a single seed". ^4-33

What kind of person is a once-returner? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of three fetters and the weakening of lust, hatred and delusion, is a once-returner; coming back to this world only once more, they make an end of suffering – this person is called "a once-returner". ^4-34

What kind of person is a non-returner? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously; they attain final release there, and are not liable to return from that world – this person is called "a non-returner". ^4-35

What kind of person is one who attains final release in the interval? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously; they attain final release there, and are not liable to return from that world. Either immediately after being reborn, or before reaching the middle of their lifespan, they bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters – this person is called "one who attains final release in the interval". ^4-36

What kind of person is one who attains final release on arrival? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously; they attain final release there, and are not liable to return from that world. After passing the middle of their lifespan, or on the arrival of death [variant: kālaṃ kiriyaṃ (Ka.)], they bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters – this person is called "one who attains final release on arrival". ^4-37

What kind of person is one who attains final release without effort? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously; they attain final release there, and are not liable to return from that world. Without effort, they bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters – this person is called "one who attains final release without effort". ^4-38

What kind of person is one who attains final release with effort? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously; they attain final release there, and are not liable to return from that world. With effort, they bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters – this person is called "one who attains final release with effort". ^4-39

What kind of person is one who goes upstream to the Highest heaven? Here, someone, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously; they attain final release there, and are not liable to return from that world. Passing away from the Aviha heaven, they go to the Atappa heaven; passing away from the Atappa heaven, they go to the Sudassa heaven; passing away from the Sudassa heaven, they go to the Sudassī heaven; passing away from the Sudassī heaven, they go to the Highest heaven; in the Highest heaven they bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters – this person is called "one who goes upstream to the Highest heaven". ^4-40

What kind of person is a stream-enterer, and one practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry? A person practising to abandon the three fetters is one practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry. The person whose three fetters are abandoned – this person is called "a stream-enterer". ^4-41

A person practising to weaken sensual lust and ill will is one practising to realize the fruit of once-returning. The person whose sensual lust and ill will are weakened – this person is called "a once-returner". ^4-42

A person practising to abandon sensual lust and ill will without remainder is one practising to realize the fruit of non-returning. The person whose sensual lust and ill will are abandoned without remainder – this person is called "a non-returner". ^4-43

A person practising to abandon without remainder lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness and ignorance is one practising to realize the fruit of arahantship. The person whose lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness and ignorance are abandoned without remainder – this person is called "an arahant".
End of the exposition of single types. ^4-44
