#### Description of Fours of Persons ^4-1-4-0

What kind of person is a bad person? Here, someone kills living beings, takes what is not given, engages in sexual misconduct, is a liar, and indulges in alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which cause heedlessness – this person is called "a bad person". ^4-132

What kind of person is one worse than a bad person? Here, someone kills living beings themselves and encourages others to kill living beings; takes what is not given themselves and encourages others to take what is not given; engages in sexual misconduct themselves and encourages others to engage in sexual misconduct; is a liar themselves and encourages others to tell lies; indulges in alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which cause heedlessness, themselves and encourages others to indulge in alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which cause heedlessness – this person is called "one worse than a bad person". ^4-133

What kind of person is a good person? Here, someone refrains from killing living beings, refrains from taking what is not given, refrains from sexual misconduct, refrains from telling lies, and refrains from alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which cause heedlessness – this person is called "a good person". ^4-134

What kind of person is one better than a good person? Here, someone refrains from killing living beings themselves and encourages others to refrain from killing living beings; refrains from taking what is not given themselves and encourages others to refrain from taking what is not given; refrains from sexual misconduct themselves and encourages others to refrain from sexual misconduct; refrains from telling lies themselves and encourages others to refrain from telling lies; refrains from alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which cause heedlessness, themselves and encourages others to refrain from alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which cause heedlessness – this person is called "one better than a good person". ^4-135

What kind of person is one who is wicked? Here, someone kills living beings, takes what is not given, engages in sexual misconduct, is a liar, speaks divisively [variant: pisuṇāvāco], speaks harshly [variant: pharusāvāco (Sī.) Dī. Ni. 3.115], engages in idle chatter, is covetous, has a mind of ill will, and holds wrong view [variant: micchādiṭṭhī (Ka.)] – this person is called "one who is wicked". ^4-136

What kind of person is one more wicked than the wicked? Here, someone kills living beings themselves and encourages others to kill living beings; takes what is not given themselves and encourages others to take what is not given; engages in sexual misconduct themselves and encourages others to engage in sexual misconduct; is a liar themselves and encourages others to tell lies; speaks divisively themselves and encourages others to divisive speech; speaks harshly themselves and encourages others to harsh speech; engages in idle chatter themselves and encourages others to idle chatter; is covetous themselves and encourages others to covetousness; has a mind of ill will themselves and encourages others to ill will; holds wrong view themselves and encourages others to wrong view – this person is called "one more wicked than the wicked". ^4-137

What kind of person is one who is good? Here, someone refrains from killing living beings, refrains from taking what is not given, refrains from sexual misconduct, refrains from telling lies, refrains from divisive speech, refrains from harsh speech, refrains from idle chatter, is free of covetousness, has a mind free of ill will, and holds right view [variant: sammādiṭṭhī (Ka.)] – this person is called "one who is good". ^4-138

What kind of person is one better than the good? Here, someone refrains from killing living beings themselves and encourages others to refrain from killing living beings; refrains from taking what is not given themselves and encourages others to refrain from taking what is not given; refrains from sexual misconduct themselves and encourages others to refrain from sexual misconduct; refrains from telling lies themselves and encourages others to refrain from telling lies; refrains from divisive speech themselves and encourages others to refrain from divisive speech; refrains from harsh speech themselves and encourages others to refrain from harsh speech; refrains from idle chatter themselves and encourages others to refrain from idle chatter; is free of covetousness themselves and encourages others to be free of covetousness; has a mind free of ill will themselves and encourages others to be free of ill will; holds right view themselves and encourages others to right view – this person is called "one better than the good". ^4-139

What kind of person is one of bad character? Here, someone kills living beings, takes what is not given … holds wrong view – this person is called "one of bad character". ^4-140

What kind of person is one worse than one of bad character? Here, someone kills living beings themselves and encourages others to kill living beings; takes what is not given themselves and encourages others to take what is not given … holds wrong view themselves and encourages others to wrong view – this person is called "one worse than one of bad character". ^4-141

What kind of person is one of good character? Here, someone refrains from killing living beings, refrains from taking what is not given … holds right view – this person is called "one of good character". ^4-142

What kind of person is one better than one of good character? Here, someone refrains from killing living beings themselves and encourages others to refrain from killing living beings … holds right view themselves and encourages others to right view – this person is called "one better than one of good character". ^4-143

What kind of person is one who is blameworthy? Here, someone possesses blameworthy action of body, possesses blameworthy action of speech, and possesses blameworthy action of mind – this person is called "one who is blameworthy". ^4-144

What kind of person is one with much blame? Here, someone possesses action of body that is mostly blameworthy and only a little blameless; possesses action of speech that is mostly blameworthy and only a little blameless; possesses action of mind that is mostly blameworthy and only a little blameless – this person is called "one with much blame". ^4-145

What kind of person is one with little blame? Here, someone possesses action of body that is mostly blameless and only a little blameworthy; possesses action of speech that is mostly blameless and only a little blameworthy; possesses action of mind that is mostly blameless and only a little blameworthy – this person is called "one with little blame". ^4-146

What kind of person is one who is blameless? Here, someone possesses blameless action of body, possesses blameless action of speech, and possesses blameless action of mind – this person is called "one who is blameless". ^4-147

What kind of person is one who understands from a hint? The person for whom the breakthrough to the truth occurs at the very moment it is spoken – this person is called "one who understands from a hint". ^4-148

What kind of person is one who understands after explanation? The person for whom the breakthrough to the truth occurs when the meaning of what was said in brief is explained in detail – this person is called "one who understands after explanation". ^4-149

What kind of person is one who must be guided? The person for whom the breakthrough to the truth occurs gradually, in this way: through learning, through asking, through paying attention wisely, through associating with, keeping company with, and attending on good friends – this person is called "one who must be guided". ^4-150

What kind of person is one who gets no further than the words? The person for whom, though they hear much, speak much, memorize much, and teach much, the breakthrough to the truth does not occur in that birth – this person is called "one who gets no further than the words". ^4-151

What kind of person is one whose ready understanding is apt [variant: yuttapaṭibhāṇo (Syā.) A. Ni. 4.132] but not fluent? Here, someone, when asked a question, answers aptly but not quickly – this person is called "one whose ready understanding is apt but not fluent". ^4-152

What kind of person is one whose ready understanding is fluent but not apt? Here, someone, when asked a question, answers quickly but not aptly – this person is called "one whose ready understanding is fluent but not apt". ^4-153

What kind of person is one whose ready understanding is both apt and fluent? Here, someone, when asked a question, answers both aptly and quickly – this person is called "one whose ready understanding is both apt and fluent". ^4-154

What kind of person is one whose ready understanding is neither apt nor fluent? Here, someone, when asked a question, answers neither aptly nor quickly – this person is called "one whose ready understanding is neither apt nor fluent". ^4-155

In this context, what are the four persons who teach the Teaching? Here, a certain teacher of the Teaching speaks little and incoherently, and their audience is not skilled in telling the coherent from the incoherent. A teacher of the Teaching like this, with an audience like this, is reckoned simply as a teacher of the Teaching.
Then again, a certain teacher of the Teaching speaks little but coherently, and their audience is skilled in telling the coherent from the incoherent. A teacher of the Teaching like this, with an audience like this, is reckoned simply as a teacher of the Teaching.
Then again, a certain teacher of the Teaching speaks much but incoherently, and their audience is not skilled in telling the coherent from the incoherent. A teacher of the Teaching like this, with an audience like this, is reckoned simply as a teacher of the Teaching.
Then again, a certain teacher of the Teaching speaks much and coherently, and their audience is skilled in telling the coherent from the incoherent. A teacher of the Teaching like this, with an audience like this, is reckoned simply as a teacher of the Teaching. These are the four "persons who teach the Teaching". ^4-156

In this context, what are the four persons compared to rain clouds? There are four kinds of rain clouds – one that thunders but does not rain, one that rains but does not thunder, one that both thunders and rains, and one that neither thunders nor rains. In the same way, there are these four persons compared to rain clouds who exist and are found in the world. Which four? One who thunders but does not rain, one who rains but does not thunder, one who both thunders and rains, and one who neither thunders nor rains.
And how is a person one who thunders but does not rain? Here, someone speaks but is not a doer. In this way a person is one who thunders but does not rain. Just as that rain cloud thunders but does not rain, this person is like that.
And how is a person one who rains but does not thunder? Here, someone is a doer but does not speak. In this way a person is one who rains but does not thunder. Just as that rain cloud rains but does not thunder, this person is like that.
And how is a person one who both thunders and rains? Here, someone both speaks and is a doer. In this way a person is one who both thunders and rains. Just as that rain cloud both thunders and rains, this person is like that.
And how is a person one who neither thunders nor rains? Here, someone neither speaks nor is a doer. In this way a person is one who neither thunders nor rains. Just as that rain cloud neither thunders nor rains, this person is like that.
These are the four persons compared to rain clouds who exist and are found in the world. ^4-157

These are the four persons compared to mice who exist and are found in the world. ^4-158

And how does a person, without examining and without looking into it, praise one who deserves blame? In this way a person, without examining and without looking into it, praises one who deserves blame.

In this way a person, without examining and without looking into it, blames one who deserves praise.

What kind of person is one who lives on the fruit of effort, not on the fruit of merit? The person whose livelihood comes about through getting up, striving, and exerting themselves, not through merit – this person is called "one who lives on the fruit of effort, not on the fruit of merit".
What kind of person is one who lives on the fruit of merit, not on the fruit of effort? The gods who wield power over the creations of others [variant: paranimmitavasavattideve (Sī., Syā.)], and the gods above them, live on the fruit of merit, not on the fruit of effort.
What kind of person is one who lives on both the fruit of effort and the fruit of merit? The person whose livelihood comes about through getting up, striving, and exerting themselves, and also through merit – this person is called "one who lives on both the fruit of effort and the fruit of merit".
What kind of person is one who lives neither on the fruit of effort nor on the fruit of merit? Beings in hell live neither on the fruit of effort nor on the fruit of merit. ^4-167

And how is a person in darkness, headed for darkness? Here, someone is reborn in a low family – a family of outcastes, or of hunters, or of bamboo-workers [variant: veṇakule (Sī., Syā.)], or of cart-makers, or of refuse-removers – a poor family [variant: daḷidde (Sī.); see the Aṅguttara Nikāya] with little food and drink, living with difficulty, where food and clothing are hard to get. And they are ugly, unsightly, stunted, often sick, blind or crippled or lame or paralysed, and they do not get food, drink, clothing, vehicles, garlands, perfumes and ointments, beds, dwellings and lamps. They behave badly by body, behave badly by speech, behave badly by mind. Having done wrong by body, having done wrong by speech, having done wrong by mind, on the breaking up of the body, after death, they are reborn in a state of misery, a bad destination, a place of ruin, in hell. In this context, what are the four persons compared to trees? There are four kinds of trees – softwood surrounded by heartwood, heartwood surrounded by softwood, softwood surrounded by softwood, and heartwood surrounded by heartwood. In the same way, there are these four persons compared to trees who exist and are found in the world. Which four? Softwood surrounded by heartwood, heartwood surrounded by softwood, softwood surrounded by softwood, and heartwood surrounded by heartwood.
And how is a person softwood, surrounded by heartwood? Here, someone is immoral and of bad character, but their following has good morality and is of good character. In this way a person is softwood, surrounded by heartwood. Just as that tree is softwood surrounded by heartwood, this person is like that.
And how is a person heartwood, surrounded by softwood? Here, someone has good morality and is of good character, but their following is immoral and of bad character. In this way a person is heartwood, surrounded by softwood. Just as that tree is heartwood surrounded by softwood, this person is like that.
And how is a person softwood, surrounded by softwood? Here, someone is immoral and of bad character, and their following too is immoral and of bad character. In this way a person is softwood, surrounded by softwood. Just as that tree is softwood surrounded by softwood, this person is like that.
And how is a person heartwood, surrounded by heartwood? Here, someone has good morality and is of good character, and their following too has good morality and is of good character. In this way a person is heartwood, surrounded by heartwood. Just as that tree is heartwood surrounded by heartwood, this person is like that. These are the four persons compared to trees who exist and are found in the world. ^4-170

And how is a person one who torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves? Here, someone goes naked, rejecting conventions, licking their hands clean [variant: hatthāvalekhano (Syā.)], not coming when asked to come, not stopping when asked to stop; they do not accept food brought to them, or food specially prepared for them, or an invitation to a meal. They do not receive food from the mouth of a pot, or from the mouth of a bowl [variant: kalopimukhā (Sī., Syā.) Ma. Ni. 2.7]; not across a threshold, not across a stick, not across a pestle; not from two people who are eating, not from a pregnant woman, not from a woman nursing a child, not from a woman who has gone with a man; not from collected food, not from where a dog is waiting, not from where flies are swarming; they take no fish, no meat, and drink no liquor, no wine, no fermented husk-water. They go to only one house and take only one mouthful, or go to two houses and take two mouthfuls … or go to seven houses and take seven mouthfuls; they live on one helping, live on two helpings … live on seven helpings; they take nutriment once a day, take nutriment once every two days [variant: dvāhikampi (Sī.)] … take nutriment once every seven days. Thus they live devoted to the practice of eating meals at set intervals, even up to once every half month. They live on greens, or on millet, or on wild rice, or on hide-parings, or on moss, or on bran, or on rice-scum, or on sesame flour, or on grass, or on cow dung; they keep themselves going on forest roots and fruits, eating fallen fruits. They wear hemp, they wear mixed fibre, they wear shrouds taken from corpses, they wear rags, they wear bark, they wear antelope hide, they wear strips of antelope hide, they wear kusa-grass cloth, they wear fibre cloth, they wear cloth made of wood, they wear a blanket of human hair, they wear a blanket of animal hair, they wear owls' feathers [variant: ulukapakkhampi (Sī., Syā.)]; they pull out their hair and beard, devoted to the practice of pulling out hair and beard; they stand continuously, refusing to sit; they squat on their heels, devoted to the exertion of squatting; they lie on thorns, making their bed on thorns; they live devoted to the practice of going down into water three times a day, the third time in the evening [variant: sāyaṃtatiyakampi (Syā., Ka.) Ma. Ni. 2.7]. Thus they live devoted to the practice of scorching and tormenting the body in many kinds of ways. In this way a person is one who torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves. ^4-174

And how is a person one who torments others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others? Here, someone is a sheep butcher, a pig butcher, a bird-catcher, a deer-trapper, a hunter, a fisherman, a thief, an executioner of thieves, a slaughterer of cattle, a jailer, or anyone else who has a cruel occupation. In this way a person is one who torments others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others. ^4-175

And how is a person one who both torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves, and torments others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others? Here, someone is a head-anointed noble king [variant: muddhābhisitto (Syā., Ka.)], or a wealthy brahmin. They have a new hall [variant: santāgāraṃ (Syā.), yaññāgāraṃ (Sī.)] built to the east of the city, shave off their hair and beard, put on a rough antelope hide [variant: khurājinaṃ (Syā., Ka.)], smear their body with ghee and oil, and, scratching their back with a deer's horn [variant: kaṇḍūyamāno (Sī.)], enter the hall together with their queen and their brahmin high priest. There they lie down on the bare ground, strewn with greenery. The king lives on the milk in one teat of a single cow that has a calf of the same colour; the queen lives on the milk in the second teat; the brahmin high priest lives on the milk in the third teat; with the milk in the fourth teat they make an offering to the fire; and the calf lives on what is left. They say: "Let so many bulls be slaughtered for the sacrifice; let so many bullocks be slaughtered for the sacrifice; let so many heifers be slaughtered for the sacrifice; let so many goats be slaughtered for the sacrifice; let so many rams be slaughtered for the sacrifice; (let so many horses be slaughtered for the sacrifice) [variant: ( ) not in the Sinhalese manuscript. Likewise in the Kandaraka Sutta of the Majjhima Nikāya]; let so many trees be cut down for posts; let so much grass be cut for strewing [variant: parihiṃsatthāyāti (Sī., Syā., Ka.) Ma. Ni. 2.9]." And those who are their slaves, servants or workers, driven by punishment and driven by fear, with tears on their faces, weeping, do the tasks. In this way a person is one who both torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves, and torments others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others. ^4-176

And how is a person one who neither torments themselves nor is devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves, and neither torments others nor is devoted to the practice of tormenting others? Not tormenting themselves and not tormenting others, they live in this very life free from hunger, at peace, cooled, experiencing happiness, having themselves become holy.
Here, a Tathāgata arises in the world, an arahant, a Fully Awakened Buddha, accomplished in true knowing and conduct, well-gone, knower of the world, unsurpassed guide of people to be trained, teacher of gods and humans, the Awakened One, the Blessed One. Having realized by direct knowledge himself this world with its gods, its Māra and its Brahmā, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, its gods and humans, he makes it known. He teaches the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with its meaning and its wording; he reveals the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. A householder, or a householder's son, or someone reborn in some other family hears that Teaching. Having heard that Teaching, they gain faith in the Tathāgata. Possessing that gain of faith, they reflect: "Household life is cramped, a dusty road; going forth is like the open air. It is not easy, while living at home, to lead the holy life entirely complete, entirely pure, polished like a shell. Suppose I were to shave off my hair and beard, put on ochre robes, and go forth from home into homelessness!" Then, on a later occasion, having abandoned a small fortune or a large fortune, having abandoned a small circle of relatives or a large circle of relatives, they shave off their hair and beard, put on ochre robes, and go forth from home into homelessness. ^4-177

Having gone forth in this way, and having entered upon the monks' training and way of life, they abandon killing living beings and refrain from killing living beings; with the stick laid down and the weapon laid down, conscientious and kind, they live with compassion for the welfare of all living beings.
Having abandoned taking what is not given, they refrain from taking what is not given; taking only what is given, expecting only what is given, they live honestly, having themselves become pure.
Having abandoned unchastity, they live the holy life, living apart [variant: anācārī (Ka.)], refraining from sexual intercourse, the way of villagers.
Having abandoned lying, they refrain from telling lies; they tell the truth, keep to the truth, are trustworthy and reliable, and do not deceive the world.
Having abandoned divisive speech, they refrain from divisive speech; what they have heard here they do not repeat elsewhere in order to divide these people from those, and what they have heard elsewhere they do not repeat to these people in order to divide them from those. Thus they reunite those who are divided and support those who are united; they delight in harmony, love harmony, rejoice in harmony, and speak words that create harmony.
Having abandoned harsh speech, they refrain from harsh speech. They speak words that are gentle, pleasing to the ear, affectionate, going to the heart, courteous, loved by many people, and liked by many people; they speak that kind of speech.
Having abandoned idle chatter, they refrain from idle chatter; they speak at the right time, speak what is true, speak what is beneficial, speak on the Teaching, speak on the Discipline; they speak words worth treasuring, at the right time, reasoned, well defined, and connected with what is beneficial. ^4-178

They refrain from damaging seeds and plants; they eat only one meal a day, refraining from eating at night and refraining from eating at the wrong time; they refrain from watching dancing, singing, music and shows; they refrain from wearing garlands, perfumes and ointments, and from adorning and beautifying themselves; they refrain from high beds and luxurious beds; they refrain from accepting gold and silver.
They refrain from accepting raw grain; they refrain from accepting raw meat; they refrain from accepting women and girls; they refrain from accepting female and male slaves; they refrain from accepting goats and sheep; they refrain from accepting chickens and pigs; they refrain from accepting elephants, cattle, horses and mares; they refrain from accepting fields and land; they refrain from running errands and carrying messages; they refrain from buying and selling; they refrain from false weights, false metal and false measures; they refrain from bribery, deception, fraud and crooked dealing [variant: … sāviyogā (Syā., Ka.)]; they refrain from wounding, killing, binding, robbery, plunder and violence. ^4-179

They are content with robes to sustain the body and with alms food to sustain the belly. Wherever they go, they go taking only these with them. Just as a bird, wherever it flies, flies with its wings as its only burden, in the same way a monk is content with robes to sustain the body and with alms food to sustain the belly; wherever they go, they go taking only these with them. Possessing this noble body of moral discipline, they experience within themselves a blameless happiness. ^4-180

On seeing a form with the eye, they do not grasp at its appearance or grasp at its details. Since, if they lived with the eye faculty unrestrained, covetousness and displeasure – bad, unwholesome states – might flow in upon them, they practise to restrain it; they guard the eye faculty; they undertake restraint of the eye faculty. On hearing a sound with the ear … On smelling a smell with the nose … On tasting a taste with the tongue … On feeling a tangible with the body … On knowing a mind-object with the mind, they do not grasp at its appearance or grasp at its details. Since, if they lived with the mind faculty unrestrained, covetousness and displeasure – bad, unwholesome states – might flow in upon them, they practise to restrain it; they guard the mind faculty; they undertake restraint of the mind faculty. Possessing this noble restraint of the faculties, they experience within themselves an untainted happiness. ^4-181

They act with clear awareness when going forward and coming back; they act with clear awareness when looking ahead and looking around; they act with clear awareness when bending and stretching; they act with clear awareness when wearing their outer robe, carrying their bowl, and wearing their robes; they act with clear awareness when eating, drinking, chewing and tasting; they act with clear awareness when defecating and urinating; they act with clear awareness when walking, standing, sitting, asleep, awake, speaking and keeping silent.

After the meal, returning from their almsround, they sit down, fold their legs crosswise, set their body upright, and establish mindfulness in front of them. Having abandoned covetousness for the world, they live with a mind free of covetousness; they cleanse their mind of covetousness. Having abandoned ill will and hatred, they live with a mind free of ill will, with compassion for the welfare of all living beings; they cleanse their mind of ill will and hatred. Having abandoned sloth and drowsiness [variant: thīnamiddhaṃ (Sī., Syā.)], they live free of sloth and drowsiness, perceiving light, mindful and clearly comprehending; they cleanse their mind of sloth and drowsiness. Having abandoned restlessness and remorse, they live without restlessness, with a mind calm within; they cleanse their mind of restlessness and remorse. Having abandoned doubt, they live beyond doubt, without uncertainty about wholesome states; they cleanse their mind of doubt. ^4-182

Having abandoned these five hindrances, impurities of the mind that weaken wisdom, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, they live having entered the first jhāna, which has thinking and holding the thought, with rapture and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of thinking and holding the thought, they live having entered the second jhāna, which has internal confidence and unification of mind, is without thinking and holding the thought, and has rapture and happiness born of concentration. With the fading away of rapture, they live with equanimity, mindful and clearly comprehending, and experience pleasure with the body; they live having entered the third jhāna, of which the noble ones declare: "One with equanimity and mindfulness lives happily." With the abandoning of pleasure and the abandoning of suffering, and with the earlier disappearance of joy and displeasure, they live having entered the fourth jhāna, which is neither suffering nor happiness and has purity of equanimity and mindfulness.
With their mind thus concentrated, purified, cleansed, unblemished, free of impurities, pliable, workable, steady and attained to the unshakable, they direct their mind to the knowledge of recollecting past lives. With the divine eye, which is purified and goes beyond the human, they see beings passing away and being reborn, inferior and excellent, beautiful and ugly, in good and bad destinations; they understand how beings fare according to their actions: "These beings, good sirs, were engaged in bad conduct of body, engaged in bad conduct of speech, engaged in bad conduct of mind; they spoke against the noble ones, held wrong view, and took up actions based on wrong view.
