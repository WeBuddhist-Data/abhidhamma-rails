#### Description of Fives of Persons ^4-1-5-0

Of these, the person who does wrong and feels remorse, and does not really understand that liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom in which those bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder, should be told this: "Venerable one, taints born of wrongdoing [variant: ārabbhajā (Ka.) A. Ni. 5.142] are found in you, and taints born of remorse are growing. It would be good if the venerable one, having abandoned the taints born of wrongdoing and dispelled the taints born of remorse, would cultivate the mind and wisdom. In this way the venerable one will become equal to that fifth person."
Of these, the person who does wrong but does not feel remorse, and does not really understand that liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom in which those bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder, should be told this: "Venerable one, taints born of wrongdoing are found in you, but taints born of remorse are not growing. It would be good if the venerable one, having abandoned the taints born of wrongdoing, would cultivate the mind and wisdom. In this way the venerable one will become equal to that fifth person."
Of these, the person who does no wrong but feels remorse, and does not really understand that liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom in which those bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder, should be told this: "Venerable one, taints born of wrongdoing are not found in you, but taints born of remorse are growing. It would be good if the venerable one, having dispelled the taints born of remorse, would cultivate the mind and wisdom. In this way the venerable one will become equal to that fifth person."
Of these, the person who does no wrong and does not feel remorse, and does not really understand that liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom in which those bad, unwholesome states cease without remainder, should be told this: "Venerable one, taints born of wrongdoing are not found in you, and taints born of remorse are not growing. It would be good if the venerable one would cultivate the mind and wisdom. In this way the venerable one will become equal to that fifth person." These four persons, being advised in this way and instructed in this way by that fifth person, gradually reach the destruction of the taints. ^4-191

And how does a person give and then look down on others? Here, someone gives robes, alms food, lodging, and medicines and supplies for the sick to a person, and they think: "I am the one who gives; this one [variant: ayaṃ pana (Syā., Ka.) A. Ni. 5.141] receives." Having given, they look down on that person. In this way a person gives and then looks down on others.
And how does a person look down on others through living with them? Here, someone lives together with a person for two or three years, and looks down on that person through living with them. In this way a person looks down on others through living with them.
And how does a person believe too readily? Here, when someone else is being praised or criticized, someone quickly believes it. In this way a person believes too readily.
And how is a person fickle? Here, someone has short-lived faith, short-lived devotion, short-lived affection and short-lived trust. In this way a person is fickle.
And how is a person dull and confused? Here, someone does not know wholesome and unwholesome states, does not know blameworthy and blameless states, does not know inferior and excellent states, does not know dark and bright states that are opposites of each other. In this way a person is dull and confused. ^4-192

In this context, what are the five persons compared to warriors? There are five kinds of warriors – here, a certain warrior, on merely seeing the cloud of dust, loses heart, falters, does not stand firm [variant: satthambhati (Sī.) A. Ni. 5.141], and cannot enter the battle. There is a warrior like this. This is the first warrior who exists and is found in the world.
Again, here a certain warrior can bear the cloud of dust, but on merely seeing the tops of the banners loses heart, falters, does not stand firm, and cannot enter the battle. There is a warrior like this. This is the second warrior who exists and is found in the world.
Again, here a certain warrior can bear the cloud of dust and can bear the tops of the banners, but on merely hearing the battle cry [variant: ussādanaṃyeva (Sī.), ussādanaññeva (Syā., Ka.) A. Ni. 5.141] loses heart, falters, does not stand firm, and cannot enter the battle. There is a warrior like this. This is the third warrior who exists and is found in the world.
Again, here a certain warrior can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners and can bear the battle cry, but in the clash is struck and wounded. There is a warrior like this. This is the fourth warrior who exists and is found in the world.
Again, here a certain warrior can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners, can bear the battle cry and can bear the clash. Having won that battle, victorious, they take over that very front line of the battle. There is a warrior like this. This is the fifth warrior who exists and is found in the world. These are the five warriors who exist and are found in the world. ^4-193

In the same way, there are these five persons compared to warriors who exist and are found among monks. Which five? Here, a certain monk, on merely seeing the cloud of dust, loses heart, falters, does not stand firm, and cannot keep up the holy life [variant: santānetuṃ (Sī., Syā.) A. Ni. 5.75]; declaring their weakness in the training [variant: āvīkatvā (Sī.)], they give up the training and return to the lower life. What is the cloud of dust in their case? Here, a monk hears: "In such-and-such a village or town there is a woman or a girl who is beautiful, lovely, graceful, possessing the finest beauty of complexion." Having heard this, they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm, and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. This is the cloud of dust in their case.
Just as that warrior, on merely seeing the cloud of dust, loses heart, falters, does not stand firm, and cannot enter the battle, this person is like that. There is a person like this. This is the first person like a warrior who exists and is found among monks. ^4-194

Again, here a certain monk can bear the cloud of dust, but on merely seeing the tops of the banners loses heart, falters, does not stand firm, and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. What are the tops of the banners in their case? Here, a monk does not hear: "In such-and-such a village or town there is a woman or a girl who is beautiful, lovely, graceful, possessing the finest beauty of complexion," but they themselves [variant: sāmaṃyeva (Sī.)] see a woman or a girl who is beautiful, lovely, graceful, possessing the finest beauty of complexion. Having seen her, they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm, and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. These are the tops of the banners in their case.
Just as that warrior can bear the cloud of dust, but on merely seeing the tops of the banners loses heart, falters, does not stand firm, and cannot enter the battle, this person is like that. There is a person like this. This is the second person like a warrior who exists and is found among monks. ^4-195

Again, here a certain monk can bear the cloud of dust and can bear the tops of the banners, but on merely hearing the battle cry loses heart, falters, does not stand firm, and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. What is the battle cry in their case? Here, when a monk has gone to a forest, to the foot of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, a woman comes up to them and smiles [variant: uhasati (commentary) A. Ni. 5.75], chats, laughs and teases. Being smiled at, chatted to, laughed at and teased by the woman, they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm, and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. This is the battle cry in their case.
Just as that warrior can bear the cloud of dust and can bear the tops of the banners, but on merely hearing the battle cry loses heart, falters, does not stand firm, and cannot enter the battle, this person is like that. There is a person like this. This is the third person like a warrior who exists and is found among monks. ^4-196

Again, here a certain monk can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners and can bear the battle cry, but in the clash is struck and wounded. What is the clash in their case? Here, when a monk has gone to a forest, to the foot of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, a woman comes up to them, sits down beside them, lies down beside them, and throws herself on them. With the woman sitting down beside them, lying down beside them, and throwing herself on them, they indulge in sexual intercourse without giving up the training and without declaring their weakness. This is the clash in their case.
Just as that warrior can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners and can bear the battle cry, but in the clash is struck and wounded, this person is like that. There is a person like this. This is the fourth person like a warrior who exists and is found among monks. ^4-197

Again, here a certain monk can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners, can bear the battle cry and can bear the clash. Having won that battle, victorious, they take over that very front line of the battle. What is victory in battle in their case? Here, when a monk has gone to a forest, to the foot of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, a woman comes up to them, sits down beside them, lies down beside them, and throws herself on them. With the woman sitting down beside them, lying down beside them, and throwing herself on them, they disentangle themselves, free themselves, and go wherever they wish.
They resort to a secluded dwelling: a forest, the foot of a tree, a mountain, a ravine, a hillside cave, a charnel ground, a forest thicket, the open air, a heap of straw. Having gone to a forest, to the foot of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, they sit down, fold their legs crosswise, set their body upright, and establish mindfulness in front of them. Having abandoned covetousness for the world, they live with a mind free of covetousness; they cleanse their mind of covetousness. Having abandoned ill will and hatred, they live with a mind free of ill will, with compassion for the welfare of all living beings; they cleanse their mind of ill will and hatred. Having abandoned sloth and drowsiness, they live free of sloth and drowsiness, perceiving light, mindful and clearly comprehending; they cleanse their mind of sloth and drowsiness. Having abandoned restlessness and remorse, they live without restlessness, with a mind calm within; they cleanse their mind of restlessness and remorse. Having abandoned doubt, they live beyond doubt, without uncertainty about wholesome states; they cleanse their mind of doubt.
Having abandoned these five hindrances, impurities of the mind that weaken wisdom, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, they live having entered the first jhāna, which has thinking and holding the thought, with rapture and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of thinking and holding the thought, the second jhāna … the third jhāna … they live having entered the fourth jhāna.
With their mind thus concentrated, purified, cleansed, unblemished, free of impurities, pliable, workable, steady and attained to the unshakable, they direct their mind to the knowledge of the destruction of the taints. They really understand, "This is suffering"; really understand, "This is the origin of suffering"; really understand, "This is the cessation of suffering"; really understand, "This is the way leading to the cessation of suffering"; really understand, "These are the taints"; really understand, "This is the origin of the taints"; really understand, "This is the ending of the taints"; really understand, "This is the way leading to the ending of the taints".
As they know and see in this way, their mind is liberated from the taint of sensual pleasures, liberated from the taint of becoming, and liberated from the taint of ignorance. When it is liberated, the knowing occurs: "It is liberated." They understand: "Birth is destroyed, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is nothing further for this state of existence." This is victory in battle in their case. Just as that warrior can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners, can bear the battle cry and can bear the clash, and, having won that battle, victorious, takes over that very front line of the battle, this person is like that. There is a person like this. This is the fifth person like a warrior who exists and is found among monks. These are the five persons compared to warriors who exist and are found among monks. ^4-198

In this context, what are the five who eat only alms food? Some eat only alms food out of dullness and confusion; some eat only alms food because they have bad wishes and are driven by desire; some eat only alms food out of madness and a disturbed mind; some eat only alms food thinking, "This is praised by the Buddhas and the disciples of the Buddhas"; and some eat only alms food simply for the sake of having few wishes [variant: appicchaṃyeva (Syā.) A. Ni. 5.181], simply for the sake of contentment, simply for the sake of wearing away defilements, simply for the sake of having just this as their aim [variant: idamaṭṭhitaṃyeva (Sī.)]. Of these, the one who eats only alms food simply for the sake of having few wishes, simply for the sake of contentment, simply for the sake of wearing away defilements, simply for the sake of having just this as their aim, is the foremost, the best, the chief [variant: mokkho (Sī.)], the highest and the finest of these five who eat only alms food.
Just as from a cow comes milk, from milk curd, from curd butter [variant: nonītaṃ (Sī.)], from butter ghee, and from ghee the cream of ghee, and the cream of ghee is said to be the foremost among these; in the same way, the one who eats only alms food simply for the sake of having few wishes, simply for the sake of contentment, simply for the sake of wearing away defilements, simply for the sake of having just this as their aim, is the foremost, the best, the chief, the highest and the finest of these five who eat only alms food. These are the five who eat only alms food. ^4-199
