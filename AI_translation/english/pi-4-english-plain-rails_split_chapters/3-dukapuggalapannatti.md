#### Description of Pairs of Persons ^4-1-2-0

What kind of person is one who is angry? In this context, what is anger? Whatever anger there is, being angry, angriness, hatred, hating, hatefulness [variant: dūsanā dūsitattaṃ (Syā.)], ill will, ill-willed bearing, being ill-willed, hostility, antagonism, fierceness, abusiveness, displeasure of consciousness – this is called anger. The person in whom this anger is not abandoned – this person is called "one who is angry". ^4-45

What kind of person is one who holds grudges? In this context, what is resentment? First there is anger, later there is resentment. Whatever resentment of this kind there is, bearing a grudge, being resentful, not letting go [variant: āṭhapanā (Ka.) Vibha. 891], keeping, fixing, joining, prolonging, strengthening of anger – this is called resentment. The person in whom this resentment is not abandoned – this person is called "one who holds grudges". ^4-46

What kind of person is one who belittles others? In this context, what is belittling? Whatever belittling there is, acting belittlingly, the state of belittling [variant: makkhīyanā makkhīyitattaṃ (Sī.), makkhiyanā makkhiyitattaṃ (Ka.)], callousness, callous behaviour – this is called belittling. The person in whom this belittling is not abandoned – this person is called "one who belittles others". ^4-47

What kind of person is one who domineers? In this context, what is domineering? Whatever domineering there is, acting domineeringly, the state of domineering, a domineering manner, cause of dispute, rivalry, refusing to give up – this is called domineering. The person in whom this domineering is not abandoned – this person is called "one who domineers". ^4-48

What kind of person is one who is envious? In this context, what is envy? Whatever envy there is over the gains, honour, respect, esteem, homage and veneration given to others, envying, enviousness, jealousy, being jealous [variant: ussuyā ussuyanā (Ka.) Vibha. 893], jealousness – this is called envy. The person in whom this envy is not abandoned – this person is called "one who is envious". ^4-49

What kind of person is one who is stingy? In this context, what is stinginess? There are five kinds of stinginess – stinginess about dwellings, stinginess about families, stinginess about gains, stinginess about praise, and stinginess about the teaching. Whatever stinginess of this kind there is, being stingy, a stingy nature, meanness, miserliness, tight-fistedness, the tight hold of consciousness – this is called stinginess. The person in whom this stinginess is not abandoned – this person is called "one who is stingy". ^4-50

What kind of person is one who is crafty? In this context, what is craftiness? Here, someone is crafty, thoroughly crafty. Whatever crafty behaviour there is in that, being crafty, craftiness, scheming, schemingness [variant: kakkhaḷatā kakkhaḷiyaṃ (Syā.); likewise in the exposition of pairs in the Khuddakavibhaṅga], wiliness, guile – this is called craftiness. The person in whom this craftiness is not abandoned – this person is called "one who is crafty". ^4-51

What kind of person is one who is deceitful? In this context, what is deceit? Here, someone, having done wrong by body, having done wrong by speech, having done wrong by mind, forms an evil wish for the sake of hiding it: they wish, "Let no one know about me"; they intend, "Let no one know about me"; they speak, "Let no one know about me"; they act with the body, "Let no one know about me". Whatever deceit of this kind there is, deceitfulness, cunning, trickery, fraud, evasion, side-stepping, hiding, thorough hiding, covering, covering up, not making plain, not bringing to light, concealment, wrongdoing – this is called deceit. The person in whom this deceit is not abandoned – this person is called "one who is deceitful". ^4-52

What kind of person is one without conscience? In this context, what is shamelessness? Not feeling shame at what one should feel shame about, not feeling shame at engaging in bad, unwholesome states – this is called shamelessness. A person who possesses this shamelessness is "one without conscience". ^4-53

What kind of person is one without moral fear? In this context, what is fearlessness of wrongdoing? Not feeling moral fear at what one should fear, not feeling moral fear at engaging in bad, unwholesome states – this is called fearlessness of wrongdoing. A person who possesses this fearlessness of wrongdoing is "one without moral fear". ^4-54

What kind of person is one who is hard to correct? In this context, what is being hard to correct? When being corrected in accordance with the teaching, being difficult, obstinacy, being hard to correct, taking the contrary side, delighting in opposing, disrespect, lack of regard, lack of reverence, lack of deference – this is called being hard to correct. A person who possesses this trait of being hard to correct is "one who is hard to correct". ^4-55

What kind of person is one who has bad friends? In this context, what is having bad friends? There are those persons who are without faith, immoral, of little learning, stingy, and lacking wisdom; associating with them, frequenting them, keeping company with them, going to them, resorting to them, devotion to them, attachment to them, leaning towards them – this is called having bad friends. A person who possesses this trait of having bad friends is "one who has bad friends". ^4-56

What kind of person is one who does not guard the doors of the faculties? In this context, what is failure to guard the doors of the faculties? Here, someone, on seeing a form with the eye, grasps at its appearance and grasps at its details. Since, if they lived with the eye faculty unrestrained, covetousness and displeasure – bad, unwholesome states – might flow in upon them, they do not practise to restrain it; they do not guard the eye faculty; they do not undertake restraint of the eye faculty. On hearing a sound with the ear … On smelling a smell with the nose … On tasting a taste with the tongue … On feeling a tangible with the body … On knowing a mind-object with the mind, they grasp at its appearance and grasp at its details. Since, if they lived with the mind faculty unrestrained, covetousness and displeasure – bad, unwholesome states – might flow in upon them, they do not practise to restrain it; they do not guard the mind faculty; they do not undertake restraint of the mind faculty. Whatever lack of guarding, lack of protecting, lack of watching, and lack of restraint of these six faculties there is – this is called failure to guard the doors of the faculties. A person who possesses this failure to guard the doors of the faculties is "one who does not guard the doors of the faculties". ^4-57

What kind of person is one who lacks moderation in eating? In this context, what is not knowing moderation in eating? Here, someone, without reflecting, unwisely, takes nutriment for fun, for indulgence, for looks, for beautification; whatever lack of contentment, not knowing moderation, and not reflecting in eating there is in that – this is called not knowing moderation in eating. A person who possesses this not knowing moderation in eating is "one who lacks moderation in eating". ^4-58

What kind of person is one who is forgetful? In this context, what is forgetfulness? Whatever lack of mindfulness, lack of recollection, lack of recall, lack of mindfulness, not remembering, not keeping in mind, shallowness, forgetting there is – this is called forgetfulness. A person who possesses this forgetfulness is "one who is forgetful". ^4-59

What kind of person is one who is not clearly comprehending? In this context, what is lack of clear awareness? Whatever not knowing, not seeing, no breakthrough, not understanding, not awakening, not penetrating, not comprehending, not fathoming [variant: asaṃgāhanā apariyogāhanā (Sī., Syā., Ka.)], not considering impartially, not reviewing, not seeing directly, dullness, foolishness, lack of clear awareness, delusion, deep delusion, utter delusion, ignorance, the flood of ignorance, the yoke of ignorance, the underlying tendency to ignorance, the obsession of ignorance, the barrier of ignorance, delusion, the unwholesome root there is – this is called lack of clear awareness. A person who possesses this lack of clear awareness is "one who is not clearly comprehending". ^4-60

What kind of person is one who has failed in moral discipline? In this context, what is failure in morality? Transgression by body, transgression by speech, transgression by body and speech – this is called failure in morality. All lack of morality is failure in morality. A person who possesses this failure in morality is "one who has failed in moral discipline". ^4-61

What kind of person is one who has failed in view? In this context, what is failure in view? "There is nothing given, nothing offered, nothing sacrificed; there is no fruit or result of good and bad [variant: sukaṭadukkaṭānaṃ (Sī.)] actions; this world does not exist, the other world does not exist; there is no mother, there is no father; there are no spontaneously reborn beings; there are no ascetics and brahmins in the world who have gone rightly [variant: samaggatā (Ka.)] and practise rightly, who, having realized this world and the other world by direct knowledge themselves, make them known." Whatever view of this kind there is, resort to views, thicket of views, wilderness of views, distortion of views, wavering of views, fetter of views, grasping, seizing, adherence, misapprehension, wrong path, wrong road, wrongness, sectarian ground, upside-down grasping [variant: vipariyesaggāho (all editions); the correct form of the word needs consideration] – this is called failure in view. All wrong view is failure in view. A person who possesses this failure in view is "one who has failed in view". ^4-62

What kind of person is one with internal fetters? The person in whom the five lower fetters are not abandoned – this person is called "one with internal fetters". ^4-63

What kind of person is one with external fetters? The person in whom the five higher fetters are not abandoned – this person is called "one with external fetters". ^4-64

What kind of person is one who is free of anger? In this context, what is anger? What kind of person is one who has conscience? In this context, what is moral shame? Feeling shame at what one should feel shame about, feeling shame at engaging in bad, unwholesome states – this is called moral shame. A person who possesses this moral shame is "one who has conscience". ^4-73

What kind of person is one who has moral fear? In this context, what is fear of wrongdoing? Feeling moral fear at what one should fear, feeling moral fear at engaging in bad, unwholesome states – this is called fear of wrongdoing. A person who possesses this fear of wrongdoing is "one who has moral fear". ^4-74

What kind of person is one who is easy to correct? In this context, what is being amenable? When being corrected in accordance with the teaching, being easy to speak to, openness to correction, being amenable, not taking the contrary side, not delighting in opposing, respect, regard, reverence, deference – this is called being amenable. A person who possesses this trait of being amenable is "one who is easy to correct". ^4-75

What kind of person is one who has good friends? In this context, what is having good friends? There are those persons who have faith, are virtuous, have much learning, are generous, and are wise; associating with them, frequenting them, keeping company with them, going to them, resorting to them, devotion to them, attachment to them, leaning towards them – this is called having good friends. A person who possesses this trait of having good friends is "one who has good friends". ^4-76

What kind of person is one who guards the doors of the faculties? In this context, what is guarding the doors of the faculties? Here, someone, on seeing a form with the eye, does not grasp at its appearance or grasp at its details. Since, if they lived with the eye faculty unrestrained, covetousness and displeasure – bad, unwholesome states – might flow in upon them, they practise to restrain it; they guard the eye faculty; they undertake restraint of the eye faculty. On hearing a sound with the ear … On smelling a smell with the nose … On tasting a taste with the tongue … On feeling a tangible with the body … On knowing a mind-object with the mind, they do not grasp at its appearance or grasp at its details. Since, if they lived with the mind faculty unrestrained, covetousness and displeasure – bad, unwholesome states – might flow in upon them, they practise to restrain it; they guard the mind faculty; they undertake restraint of the mind faculty. Whatever guarding, protecting, watching, and restraint of these six faculties there is – this is called guarding the doors of the faculties. A person who possesses this guarding of the doors of the faculties is "one who guards the doors of the faculties". ^4-77

What kind of person is one who has moderation in eating? In this context, what is knowing moderation in eating? Here, someone, reflecting wisely, takes nutriment: "Not for fun, not for indulgence, not for looks, not for beautification, but only for the continuance of this body, for sustaining it, for ending discomfort, and for supporting the holy life. In this way I shall put an end to old feeling and not give rise to new feeling, and I shall keep going, and live blamelessly and at ease." Whatever contentment, knowing moderation, and reflecting in eating there is in that – this is called knowing moderation in eating. A person who possesses this knowing moderation in eating is "one who has moderation in eating". ^4-78

What kind of person is one whose mindfulness is established? In this context, what is mindfulness? Whatever mindfulness, recollection, recall, mindfulness, remembering, keeping in mind, non-shallowness, not forgetting, mindfulness, the mindfulness faculty, the power of mindfulness, right mindfulness there is – this is called mindfulness. A person who possesses this mindfulness is "one whose mindfulness is established". ^4-79

What kind of person is one who is clearly comprehending? In this context, what is clear awareness? Whatever wisdom, understanding, investigation, close investigation, investigation of phenomena, discernment, discrimination, close discrimination, learning, skill, subtlety, clarity, reflection, examination, breadth, intelligence, guidance, insight, clear awareness, goad, wisdom, the wisdom faculty, the power of wisdom, the sword of wisdom, the tower of wisdom, the light of wisdom, the radiance of wisdom, the lamp of wisdom, the jewel of wisdom, non-delusion, investigation of phenomena, right view there is – this is called clear awareness. A person who possesses this clear awareness is "one who is clearly comprehending". ^4-80

What kind of person is one accomplished in morality? In this context, what is accomplishment in morality? Non-transgression by body, non-transgression by speech, non-transgression by body and speech – this is called accomplishment in morality. All restraint in moral discipline is accomplishment in morality. A person who possesses this accomplishment in morality is "one accomplished in morality". ^4-81

What kind of person is one accomplished in view? In this context, what is accomplishment in view? "There is what is given, what is offered, what is sacrificed; there is fruit and result of good and bad actions; this world exists, the other world exists; there is a mother, there is a father; there are spontaneously reborn beings; there are ascetics and brahmins in the world who have gone rightly and practise rightly, who, having realized this world and the other world by direct knowledge themselves, make them known." Whatever wisdom of this kind there is, understanding … non-delusion, investigation of phenomena, right view – this is called accomplishment in view. All right view is accomplishment in view. A person who possesses this accomplishment in view is "one accomplished in view". ^4-82

What two kinds of persons are rare in the world? The one who acts first out of kindness, and the one who is grateful and acknowledges a kindness done to them – these two persons are rare in the world. ^4-83

What two kinds of persons are hard to satisfy? The one who puts away whatever they receive, and the one who gives away whatever they receive – these two persons are "hard to satisfy". ^4-84

What two kinds of persons are easy to satisfy? The one who does not put away whatever they receive, and the one who does not give away whatever they receive – these two persons are "easy to satisfy". ^4-85

For what two kinds of persons do the taints grow? The one who feels remorse about what is no cause for remorse, and the one who does not feel remorse about what is a cause for remorse – for these two persons the taints grow. ^4-86

For what two kinds of persons do the taints not grow? The one who does not feel remorse about what is no cause for remorse, and the one who feels remorse about what is a cause for remorse – for these two persons the taints do not grow. ^4-87

What kind of person is one inclined to the inferior? Here, someone is immoral and of bad character; they associate with, keep company with, and attend on another who is immoral and of bad character – this person is called "one inclined to the inferior". ^4-88

What kind of person is one inclined to the excellent? Here, someone has good morality and is of good character; they associate with, keep company with, and attend on another who has good morality and is of good character – this person is called "one inclined to the excellent". ^4-89

What kind of person is one who is satisfied? Solitary Buddhas [variant: paccekabuddho (Sī.)], and those disciples of the Tathāgata who are arahants, are satisfied. A Fully Awakened Buddha is both one who is satisfied and one who satisfies others [variant: tappetā ca, ayaṃ vuccati puggalo titto (Sī.)].
End of the exposition of pairs. ^4-90
