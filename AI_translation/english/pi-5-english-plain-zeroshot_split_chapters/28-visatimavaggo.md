#### (194) 1. Discussion of the Unintentional ^5-28-1-0

Having unintentionally deprived their mother of life, does one become a person who has committed a deed with immediate result? Yes. Having unintentionally killed a living being, does one become one who kills living beings? That should not be said… Having unintentionally deprived their mother of life, does one become a person who has committed a deed with immediate result? Yes. Having unintentionally taken what is not given… having unintentionally spoken falsely, does one become one who speaks falsely? That should not be said…
Having unintentionally killed a living being, does one not become one who kills living beings? Yes. Having unintentionally deprived their mother of life, does one not become a person who has committed a deed with immediate result? That should not be said… Having unintentionally taken what is not given… having unintentionally spoken falsely, does one not become one who speaks falsely? Yes. Having unintentionally deprived their mother of life, does one not become a person who has committed a deed with immediate result? That should not be said… ^5-857

Having unintentionally deprived their mother of life, does one become a person who has committed a deed with immediate result? Yes. "Having unintentionally deprived one's mother of life, one becomes a person who has committed a deed with immediate result" — is there really such a discourse? There is not. "Having intentionally deprived one's mother of life, one becomes a person who has committed a deed with immediate result" — is there really such a discourse? Yes. If there really is the discourse "Having intentionally deprived one's mother of life, one becomes a person who has committed a deed with immediate result," then surely you must not say: "Having unintentionally deprived one's mother of life, one becomes a person who has committed a deed with immediate result." ^5-858

Should it not be said that "one who has killed their mother has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But hasn't the mother been deprived of life? Yes. If the mother has been deprived of life, then surely you must also say: "One who has killed their mother has committed a deed with immediate result."
Should it not be said that "one who has killed their father has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But hasn't the father been deprived of life? Yes. If the father has been deprived of life, then surely you must also say: "One who has killed their father has committed a deed with immediate result."
Should it not be said that "one who has killed a worthy one (arahant) has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But hasn't a worthy one been deprived of life? Yes. If a worthy one has been deprived of life, then surely you must also say: "One who has killed a worthy one has committed a deed with immediate result."
Should it not be said that "one who has shed a Buddha's blood has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But hasn't the blood of the Tathāgata (the Buddha) been shed? Yes. If the blood of the Tathāgata has been shed, then surely you must also say: "One who has shed a Buddha's blood has committed a deed with immediate result." ^5-859

Has one who splits the Community committed a deed with immediate result? Yes. Have all who split the Community committed a deed with immediate result? That should not be said… Have all who split the Community committed a deed with immediate result? Yes. Has one who splits the Community while perceiving it to be in accord with the Dhamma committed a deed with immediate result? That should not be said… ^5-860

Has one who splits the Community while perceiving it to be in accord with the Dhamma committed a deed with immediate result? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Upāli, there is one who splits the Community who is bound for the lower realms, bound for hell, who remains there for an aeon (world-age), who is incurable; Upāli, there is one who splits the Community who is not bound for the lower realms, not bound for hell, does not remain there for an aeon, and is not incurable"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "one who splits the Community while perceiving it to be in accord with the Dhamma has committed a deed with immediate result." ^5-861

Should it not be said that "one who splits the Community while perceiving it to be in accord with the Dhamma has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Bound for the lower realms, bound for hell, remaining there for an aeon, is the one who splits the Community;
delighting in faction, standing on what is not the Dhamma, they fall away from security from bondage;
having split a Community that was united, they are tormented in hell for an aeon" [Cūḷava. 354; A. Ni. 10.39; Itivu. 18]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case one who splits the Community has committed a deed with immediate result.
The Discussion of the Unintentional is finished. ^5-862

##### (195) 2. Discussion of Knowledge ^5-28-2-1-0

Does an ordinary person have no knowledge? Yes. Does an ordinary person have no wisdom, understanding, investigation, close investigation, investigation of states, discernment, discrimination, close discrimination? That should not be said… But doesn't an ordinary person have wisdom, understanding, investigation… close discrimination? Yes. If an ordinary person has wisdom, understanding, investigation… close discrimination, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person has no knowledge." ^5-863

Does an ordinary person have no knowledge? Yes. Could an ordinary person enter the first meditative absorption (jhāna)? Yes. If an ordinary person could enter the first meditative absorption, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person has no knowledge."
Could an ordinary person enter the second meditative absorption… the third meditative absorption… the fourth meditative absorption… enter the base of the infinity of space, enter the base of the infinity of consciousness, the base of nothingness, the base of neither perception nor non-perception; could an ordinary person give a gift… give robes, give almsfood, give lodgings, give medicines and requisites for the sick? Yes. If an ordinary person could give medicines and requisites for the sick, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person has no knowledge." ^5-864

Does an ordinary person have knowledge? Yes. Does an ordinary person, with that knowledge, fully understand suffering, abandon its origin, realize its cessation, develop the path? That should not be said…
The Discussion of Knowledge is finished. ^5-865

##### (196) 3. Discussion of Hell Wardens ^5-28-3-1-0

Are there no hell wardens in the hells? Yes. Are there no punishments in the hells? That should not be said… Are there punishments in the hells? Yes. Are there hell wardens in the hells? That should not be said…
Among human beings, are there punishments, and are there also punishers? Yes. In the hells, are there punishments, and are there also punishers? That should not be said… In the hells, are there punishments, but no punishers? Yes. Among human beings, are there punishments, but no punishers? That should not be said… ^5-866

Are there hell wardens in the hells? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Not Vessabhū, nor the king of the ghosts,
nor Soma, Yama or King Vessavaṇa —
it is their own actions that strike them there, those driven out from here [variant: paṇuṇṇaṃ (Syā., Ka.), panunnaṃ (Sī.)] who have reached the other world"?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there are no hell wardens in the hells. ^5-867

Are there no hell wardens in the hells? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, the hell wardens make that being undergo [variant: karonti (M. Ni. 3.250; A. Ni. 3.36)] the punishment called the fivefold binding: they drive a red-hot iron stake through one hand, they drive a red-hot iron stake through the other hand, they drive a red-hot iron stake through one foot, they drive a red-hot iron stake through the other foot, they drive a red-hot iron stake through the middle of the chest; there that being feels painful, sharp, piercing feelings; and they do not die so long as that evil action has not been used up" [M. Ni. 3.250; A. Ni. 3.36]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there are hell wardens in the hells.
Are there no hell wardens in the hells? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, the hell wardens lay that being down and pare them with axes [variant: kudhārīhi (all editions)]… Monks, the hell wardens set that being feet up and head down and pare them with adzes… Monks, the hell wardens harness that being to a chariot and drive them back and forth over ground that is burning, blazing and glowing [variant: sañjotibhūtāya (Syā.)]… Monks, the hell wardens make that being climb up and down a great mountain of hot embers that is burning, blazing and glowing… Monks, the hell wardens take hold of that being feet up and head down and throw them into a red-hot metal cauldron that is burning, blazing and glowing. There they are cooked in a swirl of froth, and while being cooked in a swirl of froth they go now up, now down, now across. There they feel painful, sharp, piercing feelings, and they do not die so long as that evil action has not been used up. Monks, the hell wardens throw that being into the Great Hell. And that Great Hell, monks —
"has four corners and four doors, and is divided into equal parts;
it is surrounded by an iron wall and roofed over with iron.
"Its floor is made of iron, blazing and full of fire;
it spreads a hundred leagues (yojanas) all around and stays so at all times" [M. Ni. 3.250; A. Ni. 3.36; Pe. Va. 71]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there are hell wardens in the hells.
The Discussion of Hell Wardens is finished. ^5-868

##### (197) 4. Discussion of Animals ^5-28-4-1-0

Are there animals among the gods? Yes. Are there gods among the animals? That should not be said… Are there animals among the gods? Yes. Is the world of the gods the animal realm? That should not be said… Are there animals among the gods? Yes. Are there insects, moths, mosquitoes, flies, snakes, scorpions, centipedes and earthworms [variant: gaṇḍupādāti (Syā., Kaṃ., Pī.)] there? That should not be said… ^5-869

Are there no animals among the gods? Yes. But isn't there the great elephant called Erāvaṇa there, and a divine chariot yoked with a thousand (horses)? Yes. If there is the great elephant called Erāvaṇa there, and a divine chariot yoked with a thousand (horses), then surely you must also say: "There are animals among the gods." ^5-870

Are there animals among the gods? Yes. Are there elephant keepers, horse keepers [variant: hatthibhaṇḍā assabhaṇḍā (?)], fodder-gatherers, trainers and cooks there? That should not be said. In that case there are no animals among the gods.
The Discussion of Animals is finished. ^5-871

##### (198) 5. Discussion of the Path ^5-28-5-1-0

Is the path fivefold? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared the eightfold path, namely: right view… right concentration? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared the eightfold path, namely: right view… right concentration, then surely you must not say: "The path is fivefold."
Is the path fivefold? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Of paths, the eightfold is the best; of truths, the four statements;
freedom from greed is the best of states, and of two-footed beings, the one who has vision" [Dha. Pa. 273, in the Dhammapada]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the path is eightfold. ^5-872

Is right speech a factor of the path, but not the path? Yes. Is right view a factor of the path, but not the path? That should not be said… Is right speech a factor of the path, but not the path? Yes. Is right intention… right effort… right mindfulness… right concentration a factor of the path, but not the path? That should not be said…
Is right action… right livelihood a factor of the path, but not the path? Yes. Is right view… right concentration a factor of the path, but not the path? That should not be said…
Is right view a factor of the path, and is it the path? Yes. Is right speech a factor of the path, and is it the path? That should not be said… Is right view a factor of the path, and is it the path? Yes. Is right action… right livelihood a factor of the path, and is it the path? That should not be said…
Is right intention… right effort… right mindfulness… right concentration a factor of the path, and is it the path? Yes. Is right speech… right action… right livelihood a factor of the path, and is it the path? That should not be said… ^5-873

Is the noble path eightfold? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Their bodily action, verbal action and livelihood have already been well purified beforehand; in this way this noble eightfold path of theirs comes to the fulfilment of its development" [M. Ni. 3.431]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the path is fivefold. ^5-874

Is the path fivefold? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Subhadda, in whatever teaching and discipline the noble eightfold path is not found, no ascetic is found there, no second ascetic is found there, no third ascetic is found there, no fourth ascetic is found there. But in whatever teaching and discipline the noble eightfold path is found, an ascetic is found there, a second… a third… a fourth ascetic is found there. In this teaching and discipline, Subhadda, the noble eightfold path is found. Here alone, Subhadda, is there an ascetic, here a second ascetic, here a third ascetic, here a fourth ascetic. The doctrines of others are empty of other ascetics" [D. Ni. 2.214]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the path is eightfold.
The Discussion of the Path is finished. ^5-875

##### (199) 6. Discussion of Knowledge ^5-28-6-1-0

Is the knowledge with twelve aspects world-transcending? Yes. Are there twelve kinds of world-transcending knowledge? That should not be said… Are there twelve kinds of world-transcending knowledge? Yes. Are there twelve paths of stream-entry? That should not be said… Are there twelve paths of stream-entry? Yes. Are there twelve fruits of stream-entry? That should not be said… Are there twelve paths of once-returning… paths of non-returning… paths of arahantship (the state of a worthy one)? That should not be said… Are there twelve paths of arahantship? Yes. Are there twelve fruits of arahantship? That should not be said… ^5-876

Should it not be said that "the knowledge with twelve aspects is world-transcending"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "‘This is the noble truth of suffering’ — thus, monks, in regard to things not heard before, there arose in me vision, there arose knowledge, there arose wisdom, there arose true knowledge, there arose light. ‘This noble truth of suffering is to be fully understood’ — thus, monks… ‘it has been fully understood’ — thus, monks… ‘this is the noble truth of the origin of suffering [variant: dukkhasamudayo (Syā., Kaṃ., Pī.)]’ — thus, monks… ‘this noble truth of the origin of suffering is to be abandoned’ — thus, monks… ‘it has been abandoned’ — thus, monks… ‘this is the noble truth of the cessation of suffering [variant: dukkhanirodhā (Syā., Kaṃ., Pī.)]’ — thus, monks… ‘this noble truth of the cessation of suffering is to be realized’ — thus, monks… ‘it has been realized’ — thus, monks… ‘this is the noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering’ — thus, monks… ‘this noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering is to be developed’ — thus, monks… ‘it has been developed’ — thus, monks, in regard to things not heard before, there arose in me vision… there arose light" [Mahāva. 15; S. Ni. 5.1081; Paṭi. Ma. 2.30]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the knowledge with twelve aspects is world-transcending.
The Discussion of Knowledge is finished.
