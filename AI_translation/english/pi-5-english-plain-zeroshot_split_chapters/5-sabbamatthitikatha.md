#### Discussion Clarifying the Words ^5-5-9-0

Does the past exist? Yes. Is what exists the past? Of what exists, some may be past and some may be not past.
Acknowledge the refutation. If the past exists, and of what exists some may be past and some may be not past, then the past is not past, and what is not past is past. What you say here — that it should be said that "the past exists; of what exists, some may be past and some may be not past; so the past is not past, and what is not past is past" — is wrong.

#### Proof from the Discourses ^5-5-10-0

Should it not be said that "the past exists, the future exists"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, whatever material form there is, whether past, future or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near — this is called the aggregate of material form. Whatever feeling… whatever perception… whatever mental formations… whatever consciousness there is, whether past, future or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near — this is called the aggregate of consciousness" [S. Ni. 3.48]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the past exists and the future exists.
Does the past exist, and does the future exist? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, there are these three pathways of language, pathways of naming, pathways of designation, which are unmixed, have never been mixed, are not being mixed and will not be mixed, and which are not rejected by wise ascetics and brahmins. Which three? Monks, the material form that is past, ceased, gone, changed — its reckoning is ‘it was’, its name is ‘it was’, its designation is ‘it was’; its reckoning is not ‘it is’, its reckoning is not ‘it will be’. Whatever feeling… whatever perception… whatever mental formations… whatever consciousness is past, ceased, gone, changed — its reckoning is ‘it was’, its name is ‘it was’, its designation is ‘it was’; its reckoning is not ‘it is’, its reckoning is not ‘it will be’.
"Monks, the material form that is unborn, not manifested — its reckoning is ‘it will be’, its name is ‘it will be’, its designation is ‘it will be’; its reckoning is not ‘it is’, its reckoning is not ‘it was’. Whatever feeling… whatever perception… whatever mental formations… whatever consciousness is unborn, not manifested — its reckoning is ‘it will be’, its name is ‘it will be’, its designation is ‘it will be’; its reckoning is not ‘it is’, its reckoning is not ‘it was’.
"Monks, the material form that is born, manifested — its reckoning is ‘it is’, its name is ‘it is’, its designation is ‘it is’; its reckoning is not ‘it was’, its reckoning is not ‘it will be’. Whatever feeling… whatever perception… whatever mental formations… whatever consciousness is born, manifested — its reckoning is ‘it is’, its name is ‘it is’, its designation is ‘it is’; its reckoning is not ‘it was’, its reckoning is not ‘it will be’. These, monks, are the three pathways of language, pathways of naming, pathways of designation, which are unmixed, have never been mixed, are not being mixed and will not be mixed, and which are not rejected by wise ascetics and brahmins.
"Monks, even the people of Ukkalā, Vassa and Bhañña, who taught that there is no cause, taught that action has no effect, and taught that nothing exists, did not think that these three pathways of language, pathways of naming, pathways of designation should be criticized or rejected. Why is that? For fear of blame, opposition and censure" [in the Saṃyutta Nikāya]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the past exists, the future exists."
Does the past exist? Yes. But didn't the venerable Phagguna say this to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, is there that eye by which one, in describing the past Buddhas — who have attained final nibbāna, who have cut off proliferation, cut off the road, ended the cycle, and passed beyond all suffering — could describe them? Venerable sir, is there that tongue… venerable sir, is there that mind by which one, in describing the past Buddhas — who have attained final nibbāna, who have cut off proliferation, cut off the road, ended the cycle, and passed beyond all suffering — could describe them?"
"There is no such eye, Phagguna, by which one, in describing the past Buddhas — who have attained final nibbāna, who have cut off proliferation, cut off the road, ended the cycle, and passed beyond all suffering — could describe them. There is no such tongue, Phagguna… there is no such mind, Phagguna, by which one, in describing the past Buddhas — who have attained final nibbāna, who have cut off proliferation, cut off the road, ended the cycle, and passed beyond all suffering — could describe them" [S. Ni. 4.83]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the past exists."
Does the past exist? Yes. But didn't the venerable Nandaka say this: "Formerly there was greed; that was unwholesome. Now it is not; this is wholesome. Formerly there was hatred… formerly there was delusion; that was unwholesome. Now it is not; this is wholesome" [in the Aṅguttara Nikāya]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the past exists."
Should it not be said that "the future exists"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, if there is greed for the nutriment of edible food, if there is delight, if there is craving, then consciousness becomes established there and grows. Where consciousness is established and grows, there is a descent of name-and-form (mind and body). Where there is a descent of name-and-form, there is growth of mental formations. Where there is growth of mental formations, there is the production of renewed existence in the future. Where there is the production of renewed existence in the future, there is birth, ageing and death in the future. Where there is birth, ageing and death in the future, that, monks, I say, is accompanied by sorrow, by dust [variant: sadaraṃ (S. Ni. 2.64); that reading is more fitting] and by despair.
"Monks, if for the nutriment of contact… for the nutriment of mental intention… for the nutriment of consciousness there is greed, if there is delight… is accompanied by dust and by despair, I say" [S. Ni. 2.64]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the future exists.
Does the future exist? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, if there is no greed for the nutriment of edible food, if there is no delight, if there is no craving, then consciousness does not become established there and does not grow. Where consciousness is not established and does not grow, there is no descent of name-and-form. Where there is no descent of name-and-form, there is no growth of mental formations. Where there is no growth of mental formations, there is no production of renewed existence in the future. Where there is no production of renewed existence in the future, there is no birth, ageing and death in the future. Where there is no birth, ageing and death in the future, that, monks, I say, is without sorrow, without dust and without despair.
"Monks, if for the nutriment of contact… for the nutriment of mental intention… for the nutriment of consciousness there is no greed, if there is no delight… is without dust and without despair, I say" [S. Ni. 2.64]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the future exists."
The Discussion of "Everything Exists" is finished. ^5-296
