But did the Blessed One not say: "Just as, monks, the great ocean slopes gradually, inclines gradually, shelves gradually, and does not drop off abruptly; in the same way, monks, in this Dhamma and discipline there is gradual training, gradual work, a gradual way of practice, and no abrupt penetration to final knowledge" [Cūḷava. 385; A. Ni. 8.20; Udā. 45, also in the Udāna]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the breakthrough is gradual.
Should it not be said that "the breakthrough is gradual"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Gradually, little by little, moment by moment, the wise one
should blow away their own impurities, as a smith does with silver" [Dha. Pa. 239, in the Dhammapada]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the breakthrough is gradual.
Is the breakthrough gradual? Yes. But didn't the venerable elder Gavampati say this to the monks: "Friends, I heard this face to face from the Blessed One, I received it face to face: ‘Monks, whoever sees suffering also sees the origin of suffering, also sees the cessation of suffering, also sees the way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering; whoever sees the origin of suffering also sees suffering, also sees the cessation of suffering, also sees the way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering; whoever sees the cessation of suffering also sees suffering, also sees the origin of suffering, also sees the way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering; whoever sees the way of practice leading to the cessation of suffering also sees suffering, also sees the origin of suffering, also sees the cessation of suffering’" [S. Ni. 5.1100]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the breakthrough is gradual."
Is the breakthrough gradual? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Together with their attainment of vision,
three things are abandoned:
identity view and doubt,
and whatever there may be of rules and rituals.
They are freed from the four lower realms,
and incapable of doing the six grave wrongs"?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the breakthrough is gradual."
Is the breakthrough gradual? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, at the time when the dust-free, stainless eye of the Dhamma arises in a noble disciple — ‘Whatever is subject to arising, all that is subject to cessation’ — then, monks, together with the arising of vision, three fetters are abandoned in the noble disciple: identity view, doubt, and clinging to rules and rituals"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the breakthrough is gradual."
The Discussion of Gradual Breakthrough is finished. ^5-346

#### The Second Chapter ^5-10-11-0

##### (19) 10. Discussion of Speech ^5-10-11-1-0

Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, world-transcending? Yes. Does it strike a world-transcending ear and not a worldly one; do people understand it with world-transcending consciousness and not with worldly consciousness; do disciples understand it and not ordinary people? That should not be said…
But doesn't the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, strike the worldly ear? Yes. If the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, strikes the worldly ear, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is world-transcending."
But don't people understand the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, with worldly consciousness? Yes. If people understand the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, with worldly consciousness, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is world-transcending."
But don't ordinary people understand the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. If ordinary people understand the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is world-transcending." ^5-347

Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, world-transcending? Yes. Is it the path, the fruit, nibbāna (final release), the path of stream-entry, the fruit of stream-entry, the path of once-returning, the fruit of once-returning, the path of non-returning, the fruit of non-returning, the path of arahantship (the state of a worthy one), the fruit of arahantship, a foundation of mindfulness, a right striving, a basis of psychic power, a faculty, a power, a factor of awakening? That should not be said…
Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, world-transcending? Yes. Are there some who hear the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is a world-transcending state cognizable by the ear; does it strike the ear; does it come within range of the ear? That should not be said…
But isn't it the case that a world-transcending state is not cognizable by the ear, does not strike the ear, and does not come within range of the ear? Yes. If a world-transcending state is not cognizable by the ear, does not strike the ear, and does not come within range of the ear, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is world-transcending." ^5-348

Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, world-transcending? Yes. Are there some who might feel greed for the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is a world-transcending state a basis for greed, enticing, desirable, intoxicating, binding, infatuating? That should not be said…
But isn't it the case that a world-transcending state is not a basis for greed, not enticing, not desirable, not intoxicating, not binding, not infatuating? Yes. If a world-transcending state is not a basis for greed, not enticing, not desirable, not intoxicating, not binding, not infatuating, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is world-transcending."
Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, world-transcending? Yes. Are there some who might feel hatred towards the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is a world-transcending state a basis for hatred, a basis for anger, a basis for aversion? That should not be said…
But isn't it the case that a world-transcending state is not a basis for hatred, not a basis for anger, not a basis for aversion? Yes. If a world-transcending state is not a basis for hatred, not a basis for anger, not a basis for aversion, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is world-transcending."
Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, world-transcending? Yes. Are there some who might be deluded about the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is a world-transcending state a basis for delusion, something that causes lack of knowledge, that causes blindness, that brings wisdom to an end, that sides with distress, that does not lead to nibbāna? That should not be said…
But isn't it the case that a world-transcending state is not a basis for delusion, does not cause lack of knowledge, does not cause blindness, increases wisdom, sides with freedom from distress, and leads to nibbāna? Yes. If a world-transcending state is not a basis for delusion, does not cause lack of knowledge, does not cause blindness, increases wisdom, sides with freedom from distress, and leads to nibbāna, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is world-transcending." ^5-349

Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, world-transcending? Yes. Do all who hear the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, develop the path? That should not be said…
Do all who hear the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, develop the path? Yes. Foolish ordinary people hear the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One — do foolish ordinary people develop the path? That should not be said… Does a killer of their mother develop the path… a killer of their father… a killer of a worthy one (arahant)… a shedder of a Buddha's blood… a splitter of the Community hears the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One — does the splitter of the Community develop the path? That should not be said… ^5-350

Is it possible to point out both a heap of grain and a heap of gold with a golden stick? Yes. In just the same way, the Blessed One, with world-transcending speech, speaks of both worldly and world-transcending things.
Is it possible to point out both a heap of grain and a heap of gold with a stick of castor-oil wood? Yes. In just the same way, the Blessed One, with worldly speech, speaks of both worldly and world-transcending things. ^5-351

When the Buddha, the Blessed One, speaks of the worldly, is his speech worldly, and when he speaks of the world-transcending, is it world-transcending? Yes. When he speaks of the worldly, does it strike a worldly ear, and when he speaks of the world-transcending, does it strike a world-transcending ear; when he speaks of the worldly, do people understand it with worldly consciousness, and when he speaks of the world-transcending, do they understand it with world-transcending consciousness; when he speaks of the worldly, do ordinary people understand it, and when he speaks of the world-transcending, do disciples understand it? That should not be said…
Should it not be said that "when the Buddha, the Blessed One, speaks of the worldly, his speech is worldly, and when he speaks of the world-transcending, it is world-transcending"? Yes. But doesn't the Blessed One speak of both worldly and world-transcending things? Yes. If the Blessed One speaks of both worldly and world-transcending things, then surely you must also say: "When the Buddha, the Blessed One, speaks of the worldly, his speech is worldly, and when he speaks of the world-transcending, it is world-transcending."
When the Buddha, the Blessed One, speaks of the worldly, is his speech worldly, and when he speaks of the world-transcending, is it world-transcending? Yes. When he speaks of the path, is it the path; when he speaks of what is not the path, is it not the path; when he speaks of the fruit, is it the fruit; when he speaks of what is not the fruit, is it not the fruit; when he speaks of nibbāna, is it nibbāna; when he speaks of what is not nibbāna, is it not nibbāna; when he speaks of the conditioned, is it conditioned; when he speaks of the unconditioned, is it unconditioned; when he speaks of material form, is it material form; when he speaks of what is not material form, is it not material form; when he speaks of feeling, is it feeling; when he speaks of what is not feeling, is it not feeling; when he speaks of perception, is it perception; when he speaks of what is not perception, is it not perception; when he speaks of mental formations, is it mental formations; when he speaks of what are not mental formations, is it not mental formations; when he speaks of consciousness, is it consciousness; when he speaks of what is not consciousness, is it not consciousness? That should not be said…
The Discussion of Speech is finished. ^5-352

##### (20) 11. Discussion of Cessation ^5-10-12-1-0

Are there two cessations? Yes. Are there two cessations of suffering? That should not be said… Are there two cessations of suffering? Yes. Are there two truths of cessation? That should not be said… Are there two truths of cessation? Yes. Are there two truths of suffering? That should not be said… Are there two truths of cessation? Yes. Are there two truths of the origin? That should not be said… Are there two truths of cessation? Yes. Are there two truths of the path? That should not be said…
Are there two truths of cessation? Yes. Are there two protections… two shelters… two refuges… two final resorts… two imperishables… two deathless states… two nibbānas (final releases)? That should not be said…
Are there two nibbānas? Yes. Is there between the two nibbānas any difference of high and low, of inferior and superior, of better and worse, or any boundary, division, dividing line or gap? That should not be said…
Are there two cessations? Yes. But isn't it the case that people bring to cessation, through reflection, formations that have ceased without reflection? Yes. If people bring to cessation, through reflection, formations that have ceased without reflection, then surely you must not say: "There are two cessations."
Should it not be said that "there are two cessations"? Yes. But aren't formations that have ceased without reflection utterly broken up, and formations that have ceased through reflection also utterly broken up? Yes. If formations that have ceased without reflection are utterly broken up, and formations that have ceased through reflection are also utterly broken up, then surely you must also say: "There are two cessations."
Are there two cessations? Yes. Have formations that ceased through reflection too [variant: paṭisaṅkhāniruddhā (?)] ceased because of the noble path? Yes. Have formations that ceased without reflection ceased because of the noble path? That should not be said…
Are there two cessations? Yes. Do formations that ceased through reflection not arise again? Yes. Do formations that ceased without reflection not arise again? That should not be said… In that case it should not be said that "there are two cessations."
The Discussion of Cessation is finished.
