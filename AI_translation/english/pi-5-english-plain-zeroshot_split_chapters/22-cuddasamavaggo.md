##### (137) 2. Discussion of the Arising of the Six Sense Bases ^5-22-2-1-0

Do the six sense bases take shape in the mother's womb at one and the same time, neither before nor after? Yes. Does one descend into the mother's womb with all one's major and minor limbs, with no faculty lacking? That should not be said…
Does the eye base take shape with the rebirth-seeking mind? Yes. Do the hands take shape with the rebirth-seeking mind, do the feet take shape, does the head take shape, does the ear take shape, does the nose take shape, does the mouth take shape, do the teeth take shape? That should not be said…
Does the ear base… the nose base… the tongue base take shape with the rebirth-seeking mind? Yes. Do the hands take shape with the rebirth-seeking mind, do the feet take shape, does the head take shape, does the ear take shape, does the nose take shape, does the mouth take shape, do the teeth take shape? That should not be said… ^5-691

For one who has entered the mother's womb, does the eye base arise later? Yes. Does one perform action in the mother's womb to obtain an eye? That should not be said… For one who has entered the mother's womb, does the ear base… the nose base… the tongue base arise later? Yes. Does one perform action in the mother's womb to obtain a tongue? That should not be said…
For one who has entered the mother's womb, do head hairs, body hairs, nails, teeth and bones arise later? Yes. Does one perform action in the mother's womb to obtain bones? That should not be said…
Should it not be said that "for one who has entered the mother's womb, head hairs, body hairs, nails, teeth and bones arise later"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"First there is the kalala (a fluid drop); from the kalala comes the abbuda (a froth);
from the abbuda the pesi (a lump of flesh) [variant: pesī (Syā., Kaṃ., Pī.)] is born; from the pesi the ghana (a firm mass) arises [variant: nibbattatī (Sī., Syā., S. Ni. 1.235), nibbattati (Pī., Ka.)];
from the ghana the limbs grow, and the head hairs, body hairs and nails.
"And whatever the mother eats — food, drink and meals —
by that the person who has entered the mother's womb is sustained there" [S. Ni. 1.235]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, for one who has entered the mother's womb, head hairs, body hairs, nails, teeth and bones arise later.
The Discussion of the Arising of the Six Sense Bases is finished. ^5-692

##### (143) 8. Discussion of the Indeterminate ^5-22-8-1-0

Are speculative views indeterminate? Yes. Are they a result that is indeterminate, a functional state that is indeterminate, material form, nibbāna (final release), the eye base… the touch base? That should not be said… Are speculative views indeterminate? Yes. Is contact associated with speculative views indeterminate? That should not be said… Are speculative views indeterminate? Yes. Are speculative views indeterminate? Yes. Are they without fruit and without result? That should not be said… But don't they have fruit and result? Yes. If they have fruit and result, then surely you must not say: "Speculative views are indeterminate."
Are speculative views indeterminate? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared that among faults wrong view is the worst [in the Aṅguttara Nikāya]? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared that among faults wrong view is the worst, then surely you must not say: "Speculative views are indeterminate."
Are speculative views indeterminate? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Wrong view, Vaccha, is unwholesome [variant: akusalaṃ (M. Ni. 2.194)]; right view is wholesome" [variant: kusalaṃ (M. Should it not be said that "speculative views are indeterminate"? Yes.
