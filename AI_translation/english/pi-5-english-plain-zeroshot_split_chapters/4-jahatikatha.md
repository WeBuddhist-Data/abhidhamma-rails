#### Discussion without Citing the Discourses ^5-4-1-0

Does an ordinary person abandon sensual desire and ill will? Yes. Do they abandon them absolutely, abandon them without remainder, abandon them so that they never return, abandon them together with the root, abandon them together with craving, abandon them together with the underlying tendency, abandon them by noble knowledge, abandon them by the noble path, abandon them while penetrating the unshakeable, abandon them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? That should not be said…
Does an ordinary person suppress sensual desire and ill will? Yes. Do they suppress them absolutely, suppress them without remainder, suppress them so that they never return, suppress them together with the root, suppress them together with craving, suppress them together with the underlying tendency, suppress them by noble knowledge, suppress them by the noble path, suppress them while penetrating the unshakeable, suppress them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? That should not be said…
Does a person practising for the realization of the fruit of non-returning abandon sensual desire and ill will, and do they abandon them absolutely, abandon them without remainder… abandon them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? Yes. Does an ordinary person abandon sensual desire and ill will, and do they abandon them absolutely, abandon them without remainder… abandon them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? That should not be said…
Does a person practising for the realization of the fruit of non-returning suppress sensual desire and ill will, and do they suppress them absolutely, suppress them without remainder… suppress them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? Yes. Does an ordinary person suppress sensual desire and ill will, and do they suppress them absolutely, suppress them without remainder… suppress them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? That should not be said…
Does an ordinary person abandon sensual desire and ill will, but not abandon them absolutely, not abandon them without remainder… not abandon them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? Yes. Does a person practising for the realization of the fruit of non-returning abandon sensual desire and ill will, but not abandon them absolutely… not abandon them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? That should not be said…
Does an ordinary person suppress sensual desire and ill will, but not suppress them absolutely, not suppress them without remainder… not suppress them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? Yes. Does a person practising for the realization of the fruit of non-returning suppress sensual desire and ill will, but not suppress them absolutely, not suppress them without remainder… not suppress them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? That should not be said…
Does an ordinary person abandon sensual desire and ill will? Yes. By which path? By a path belonging to the realm of form. Is a path belonging to the realm of form one that leads out, leads to destruction, leads to awakening, leads to dismantling, is free of taints, not subject to the fetters, not subject to the knots, not subject to the floods, not subject to the bonds, not subject to the hindrances, not seized upon, not subject to clinging, not subject to defilement? That should not be said. But isn't a path belonging to the realm of form one that does not lead out, does not lead to destruction, does not lead to awakening, does not lead to dismantling, is accompanied by taints, subject to the fetters… subject to defilement? Yes. If a path belonging to the realm of form is one that does not lead out, does not lead to destruction… is subject to defilement, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person abandons sensual desire and ill will by a path belonging to the realm of form."
Does a person practising for the realization of the fruit of non-returning abandon sensual desire and ill will by the path of non-returning, and is that path one that leads out, leads to destruction, leads to awakening, leads to dismantling, is free of taints… not subject to defilement? Yes. Does an ordinary person abandon sensual desire and ill will by a path belonging to the realm of form, and is that path one that leads out, leads to destruction, leads to awakening, leads to dismantling, is free of taints… not subject to defilement? That should not be said…
Does an ordinary person abandon sensual desire and ill will by a path belonging to the realm of form, and is that path one that does not lead out, does not lead to destruction, does not lead to awakening, does not lead to dismantling, is accompanied by taints… subject to defilement? Yes. Does a person practising for the realization of the fruit of non-returning abandon sensual desire and ill will by the path of non-returning, and is that path one that does not lead out, does not lead to destruction, does not lead to awakening, does not lead to dismantling, is accompanied by taints… subject to defilement? That should not be said… ^5-279

Does an ordinary person who is free from greed for sensual pleasures become established in the fruit of non-returning upon breaking through to the Dhamma? Yes. Do they become established in arahantship (the state of a worthy one)? That should not be said…
Does an ordinary person who is free from greed for sensual pleasures become established in the fruit of non-returning upon breaking through to the Dhamma? Yes. Do they develop three paths at one and the same time, neither before nor after? That should not be said…
Do they develop three paths at one and the same time, neither before nor after? Yes. Do they realize three fruits of the ascetic life at one and the same time, neither before nor after? That should not be said…
Do they realize three fruits of the ascetic life at one and the same time, neither before nor after? Yes. Is there a coming together of three contacts, three feelings, three perceptions, three intentions, three states of mind, three kinds of faith, three kinds of energy, three kinds of mindfulness, three kinds of concentration, three kinds of wisdom? #### Discussion Citing the Discourses ^5-4-2-0

Do they abandon identity view, doubt and clinging to rules and rituals by the path of non-returning? Yes. But didn't the Blessed One declare that the fruit of stream-entry comes from abandoning three fetters? Yes. If the Blessed One declared that the fruit of stream-entry comes from abandoning three fetters, then surely you must not say: "One abandons identity view, doubt and clinging to rules and rituals by the path of non-returning." Do they abandon gross sensual desire and gross ill will by the path of non-returning? That should not be said…
Do they abandon gross sensual desire and gross ill will by the path of non-returning? Yes. But didn't the Blessed One declare that the fruit of once-returning comes from the weakening of sensual desire and ill will? Yes. If the Blessed One declared that the fruit of once-returning comes from the weakening of sensual desire and ill will, then surely you must not say: "One abandons gross sensual desire and gross ill will by the path of non-returning."
Does an ordinary person who is free from greed for sensual pleasures become established in the fruit of non-returning upon breaking through to the Dhamma? Yes. Do all who break through to the Dhamma become established in the fruit of non-returning upon breaking through to the Dhamma? That should not be said…
Should it not be said that "an ordinary person abandons sensual desire and ill will"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"In times past there were [variant: ahiṃsakā (A. Ni. 6.54)] six teachers of great renown,
free of the stench of corruption, intent on compassion [variant: karuṇādhimuttā (Sī., Ka.)], gone beyond the fetter of sensual desire.
"Having made sensual desire fade away, they went to the Brahmā world;
and their disciples, too, were many hundreds.
"Free of the stench of corruption, intent on compassion, gone beyond the fetter of sensual desire,
having made sensual desire fade away, they went to the Brahmā world" [A. Ni. 6.54]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, an ordinary person abandons sensual desire and ill will.
Does an ordinary person abandon sensual desire and ill will? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, that teacher Sunetta, though he lived so long and endured so long, was not freed from birth, ageing and death, from sorrows, lamentations, pains, griefs and despairs; he was not freed from suffering, I say. Why is that? Because he did not understand and did not penetrate four things. Which four? Because he did not understand and did not penetrate noble virtue, noble concentration, noble wisdom and noble liberation. But now, monks, noble virtue has been understood and penetrated, noble concentration has been understood and penetrated, noble wisdom has been understood and penetrated, noble liberation has been understood and penetrated; craving for existence has been cut off, what leads to existence is destroyed, and now there is no renewed existence.
"Virtue, concentration and wisdom, and unsurpassed liberation —
these things have been understood by Gotama, the renowned.
"So the Buddha, having directly known them, taught the Dhamma to the monks:
the teacher, the maker of an end of suffering, the one with vision, has attained final nibbāna (final release)" [A. Ni. 7.66]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "an ordinary person abandons sensual desire and ill will."
The Discussion of Abandoning is finished. ^5-281
