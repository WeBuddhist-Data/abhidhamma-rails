##### (146) 2. Discussion of Mutual Condition ^5-23-2-1-0

Is it only with ignorance as condition that there are formations, and should it not be said that "with formations as condition there is also ignorance"? Yes. But hasn't ignorance arisen together with formations? Yes. If ignorance has arisen together with formations, then surely you must also say: "With ignorance as condition there are formations, and with formations as condition there is also ignorance."
Is it only with craving as condition that there is clinging, and should it not be said that "with clinging as condition there is also craving"? Yes. But hasn't craving arisen together with clinging? Yes. If craving has arisen together with clinging, then surely you must also say: "With craving as condition there is clinging, and with clinging as condition there is also craving." ^5-718

"Monks, with ageing and death as condition there is birth; with birth as condition there is existence" — is there really such a discourse? There is not. In that case it is only with ignorance as condition that there are formations, and it should not be said that "with formations as condition there is also ignorance"; it is only with craving as condition that there is clinging, and it should not be said that "with clinging as condition there is also craving."
"Monks, with consciousness as condition there is name-and-form (mind and body); with name-and-form as condition there is also consciousness" [D. Ni. 2.58, but slightly different] — is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case with ignorance as condition there are formations, and with formations as condition there is also ignorance; with craving as condition there is clinging, and with clinging as condition there is also craving.
The Discussion of Mutual Condition is finished. ^5-719

##### (155) 11. Discussion of the Accumulation of Action ^5-23-11-1-0

Is action one thing and the accumulation of action another? Yes. Is contact one thing and the accumulation of contact another; feeling one thing and the accumulation of feeling another; perception one thing and the accumulation of perception another; intention one thing and the accumulation of intention another; mind one thing and the accumulation of mind another; faith one thing and the accumulation of faith another; energy one thing and the accumulation of energy another; mindfulness one thing and the accumulation of mindfulness another; concentration one thing and the accumulation of concentration another; wisdom one thing and the accumulation of wisdom another; greed one thing and the accumulation of greed another… lack of moral fear one thing and the accumulation of lack of moral fear another? That should not be said… ^5-737

Is action one thing and the accumulation of action another? Yes. Has the accumulation of action arisen together with action? That should not be said…
Has the accumulation of action arisen together with action? Yes. Is the accumulation of action that has arisen together with wholesome action wholesome? That should not be said…
Is the accumulation of action that has arisen together with wholesome action wholesome? Yes. Is the accumulation of action that has arisen together with action associated with pleasant feeling associated with pleasant feeling? That should not be said… with painful feeling… Is the accumulation of action that has arisen together with action associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling? That should not be said… ^5-738

Has the accumulation of action arisen together with action? Yes. Is the accumulation of action that has arisen together with unwholesome action unwholesome? That should not be said…
Is the accumulation of action that has arisen together with unwholesome action unwholesome? Yes. Is the accumulation of action that has arisen together with action associated with pleasant feeling associated with pleasant feeling? That should not be said… with painful feeling… Is the accumulation of action that has arisen together with action associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling? That should not be said… ^5-739

Has action arisen together with the mind, and does action have an object? Yes. Has the accumulation of action arisen together with the mind, and does the accumulation of action have an object? That should not be said… Has the accumulation of action arisen together with the mind, and is the accumulation of action without an object? Yes. Has action arisen together with the mind, and is action without an object? That should not be said…
Has action arisen together with the mind, and when the mind breaks up, does action break up? Yes. Has the accumulation of action arisen together with the mind, and when the mind breaks up, does the accumulation of action break up? That should not be said…
Has the accumulation of action arisen together with the mind, and when the mind breaks up, does the accumulation of action not break up? Yes. Has action arisen together with the mind, and when the mind breaks up, does action not break up? That should not be said… ^5-740

Is the accumulation of action in the action? Yes. Is that very action that accumulation of action? That should not be said…
Is the accumulation of action in the action, and is the result produced from the accumulation of action? Yes. Is that very action that accumulation of action and that result of action? That should not be said…
Is the accumulation of action in the action, is the result produced from the accumulation of action, and does the result have an object? Yes. Does the accumulation of action have an object? That should not be said… Is the accumulation of action without an object? Yes. Is the result without an object? That should not be said… ^5-741

Is action one thing and the accumulation of action another? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Here, Puṇṇa, someone generates a bodily formation that is both hurtful and unhurtful [variant: sabyāpajjhampi abyāpajjhampi (Ka.); see M. Ni. 2.81], generates a verbal formation… a mental formation that is both hurtful and unhurtful. When they have been reborn in a world that is both hurtful and unhurtful, contacts that are both hurtful and unhurtful touch them. Touched by contacts that are both hurtful and unhurtful, they feel a feeling that is both hurtful and unhurtful, a mixture of pleasure and pain, like human beings, some gods and some beings in the lower world. Thus, Puṇṇa, a being is reborn from a being: one is reborn through what one does, and when one has been reborn, contacts touch one. That is why I say, Puṇṇa, that beings are heirs of their actions" [M. Ni. 2.81]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "action is one thing and the accumulation of action another."
The Discussion of the Accumulation of Action is finished.
