#### (177) 1. Discussion of the Human World ^5-26-1-0

Should it not be said that "the Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world"? Yes. But aren't there shrines, parks, monasteries, villages, towns, cities, kingdoms and countries where the Buddha lived? Yes. If there are shrines, parks, monasteries, villages, towns, cities, kingdoms and countries where the Buddha lived, then surely you must also say: "The Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world."
Should it not be said that "the Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world"? Yes. But wasn't the Blessed One born at Lumbinī, fully awakened at the foot of the tree of awakening; didn't the Blessed One set the wheel of the Dhamma rolling at Bārāṇasī, relinquish the formation of life (his remaining life span) at the Cāpāla shrine, and attain final nibbāna (final release) at Kusinārā? Yes. If the Blessed One was born at Lumbinī… attained final nibbāna at Kusinārā, then surely you must also say: "The Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world."
Should it not be said that "the Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, on one occasion I was staying at Ukkaṭṭhā, in the Subhaga Grove, at the foot of a royal sal tree" [D. Ni. 2.91]; "Monks, on one occasion I was staying at Uruvelā, at the goatherds' banyan tree, when I had just become fully awakened" [A. Ni. 4.21]; "Monks, on one occasion I was staying at Rājagaha, in the Bamboo Grove, the squirrels' feeding ground" [D. Ni. 2.180]; "Monks, on one occasion I was staying at Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's park"; "Monks, on one occasion I was staying at Vesālī, in the Great Wood, in the hall with the peaked roof" [D. Ni. 3.11]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world. ^5-802

Did the Buddha, the Blessed One, stay in the human world? Yes. But wasn't the Blessed One born in the world, didn't he grow up in the world, and doesn't he dwell having overcome the world, unstained by the world [A. Ni. 4.36]? Yes. If the Blessed One was born in the world, grew up in the world, and dwells having overcome the world, unstained by the world, then surely you must not say: "The Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world."
The Discussion of the Human World is finished. ^5-803

##### (178) 2. Discussion of the Teaching of the Dhamma ^5-26-2-1-0

Should it not be said that "the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One"? Yes. By whom was it taught? It was taught by a created figure. Is the created figure a conqueror, a teacher, a Fully Awakened Buddha, all-knowing, all-seeing, the lord of the Dhamma, the final authority on the Dhamma? That should not be said…
Should it not be said that "the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One"? Yes. By whom was it taught? It was taught by the venerable Ānanda. Is the venerable Ānanda a conqueror, a teacher, a Fully Awakened Buddha, all-knowing, all-seeing, the lord of the Dhamma, the final authority on the Dhamma? That should not be said… ^5-804

Should it not be said that "the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Sāriputta, I might teach the Dhamma in brief; Sāriputta, I might teach the Dhamma in detail; Sāriputta, I might teach the Dhamma both in brief and in detail; but those who understand are hard to find" [A. Ni. 3.33, in the Sāriputta Discourse]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One. ^5-805

Should it not be said that "the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "‘Monks, I teach the Dhamma through direct knowledge, not without direct knowledge; monks, I teach the Dhamma with a basis, not without a basis; monks, I teach the Dhamma with wonders, not without wonders. Since I [variant: yañcassa (Sī., Pī., Ka.)], monks, teach the Dhamma through direct knowledge and not without direct knowledge, teach the Dhamma with a basis and not without a basis, teach the Dhamma with wonders and not without wonders, it is fitting for me to give instruction, fitting to give guidance. And this is enough, monks, for you to be satisfied, enough to be pleased, enough to be glad: the Blessed One is fully awakened, the Dhamma is well proclaimed, the Community is practising well.’ And while this explanation was being spoken, the ten-thousandfold world system shook" [A. Ni. 3.126]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One.
The Discussion of the Teaching of the Dhamma is finished. ^5-806

##### (179) 3. Discussion of Compassion ^5-26-3-1-0

Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have no compassion? Yes. Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have no loving-kindness? That should not be said… Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have no compassion? Yes. Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have no sympathetic joy… no equanimity? That should not be said…
Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have loving-kindness? Yes. Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have compassion? That should not be said… Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have sympathetic joy… equanimity? Yes. Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have compassion? That should not be said…
Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have no compassion? Yes. Is the Blessed One without compassion? That should not be said… But isn't the Blessed One compassionate, devoted to the welfare of the world, sympathetic to the world, acting for the good of the world? Yes. If the Blessed One is compassionate, devoted to the welfare of the world, sympathetic to the world, acting for the good of the world, then surely you must not say: "The Buddha, the Blessed One, has no compassion."…
Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have no compassion? Yes. But didn't the Blessed One enter the attainment of great compassion? Yes. If the Blessed One entered the attainment of great compassion, then surely you must not say: "The Buddha, the Blessed One, has no compassion." ^5-807

Does the Buddha, the Blessed One, have compassion? Yes. Does the Blessed One have greed? That should not be said. In that case the Buddha, the Blessed One, has no compassion.
The Discussion of Compassion is finished. ^5-808

##### (180) 4. Discussion of Fragrant Substances ^5-26-4-1-0

Do the excrement and urine of the Buddha, the Blessed One, far surpass other fragrant substances? Yes. Does the Blessed One feed on fragrances? That should not be said… But doesn't the Blessed One eat rice and gruel? Yes. If the Blessed One eats rice and gruel, then surely you must not say: "The excrement and urine of the Buddha, the Blessed One, far surpass other fragrant substances."
Do the excrement and urine of the Buddha, the Blessed One, far surpass other fragrant substances? Yes. Are there some who bathe in the excrement and urine of the Buddha, the Blessed One, anoint themselves with it, rub themselves with it [variant: uccārenti (Sī., Pī., Ka.)], store it in a basket, put it away in a box, display it in the market, and use that scent for what a scent is used for? That should not be said…
The Discussion of Fragrant Substances is finished. ^5-809

##### (181) 5. Discussion of a Single Path ^5-26-5-1-0

Does one realize the four fruits of the ascetic life by a single noble path? Yes. Is there a coming together of four contacts… of four perceptions? That should not be said… Does one realize the four fruits of the ascetic life by a single noble path? Yes. By the path of stream-entry? That should not be said… By the path of once-returning… by the path of non-returning? That should not be said…
By which path? By the path of arahantship (the state of a worthy one). Does one abandon identity view, doubt and clinging to rules and rituals by the path of arahantship? That should not be said… Does one abandon identity view, doubt and clinging to rules and rituals by the path of arahantship? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared the abandoning of the three fetters to be the fruit of stream-entry? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared the abandoning of the three fetters to be the fruit of stream-entry, then surely you must not say: "One abandons identity view, doubt and clinging to rules and rituals by the path of arahantship."
Does one abandon gross sensual desire and gross ill will by the path of arahantship? That should not be said… Does one abandon gross sensual desire and gross ill will by the path of arahantship? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared the weakening of sensual desire and ill will to be the fruit of once-returning? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared the weakening of sensual desire and ill will to be the fruit of once-returning, then surely you must not say: "One abandons gross sensual desire and gross ill will by the path of arahantship."
Does one abandon even subtle sensual desire and even subtle ill will by the path of arahantship? That should not be said… Does one abandon even subtle sensual desire and even subtle ill will by the path of arahantship? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared the abandoning without remainder of sensual desire and ill will to be the fruit of non-returning? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared the abandoning without remainder of sensual desire and ill will to be the fruit of non-returning, then surely you must not say: "One abandons even subtle sensual desire and even subtle ill will by the path of arahantship." ^5-810

Should it not be said that "one realizes the four fruits of the ascetic life by a single noble path"? Yes. Has the Blessed One developed the path of stream-entry? Yes. Is the Blessed One a stream-enterer? That should not be said… Has the Blessed One developed the path of once-returning… the path of non-returning? Yes. Is the Blessed One a non-returner? That should not be said… ^5-811

Does the Blessed One realize the four fruits of the ascetic life by a single noble path, while disciples realize the four fruits of the ascetic life by four noble paths? Yes. Do disciples see what the Buddha, the Blessed One, has not seen, reach what he has not reached, realize what he has not realized? That should not be said…
The Discussion of a Single Path is finished. ^5-812

##### (182) 6. Discussion of Passing from One Meditative Absorption to Another ^5-26-6-1-0

Does one pass from one meditative absorption (jhāna) to another? Yes. Does one pass from the first meditative absorption to the third meditative absorption? That should not be said… Does one pass from one meditative absorption to another? Yes. Does one pass from the second meditative absorption to the fourth meditative absorption? That should not be said…
Does one pass from the first meditative absorption to the second meditative absorption? Yes. Is the turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the first meditative absorption the very same turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the second meditative absorption? That should not be said…
One passes from the first meditative absorption to the second meditative absorption — should it not be said that "the turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the first meditative absorption is the very same turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the second meditative absorption"? Yes. Does the second meditative absorption arise for one who does not turn the mind to it… for one who does not aspire? That should not be said… But doesn't the second meditative absorption arise for one who turns the mind to it… for one who aspires? Yes. If the second meditative absorption arises for one who turns the mind to it… for one who aspires, then surely you must not say: "One passes from the first meditative absorption to the second meditative absorption."…
Does one pass from the first meditative absorption to the second meditative absorption? Yes. Does the first meditative absorption arise for one who attends to sensual pleasures as dangerous? Yes. Does the second meditative absorption arise for one who attends to sensual pleasures as dangerous? That should not be said…
Is the first meditative absorption with thinking and examining? Yes. Is the second meditative absorption with thinking and examining? That should not be said… Does one pass from the first meditative absorption to the second meditative absorption? Yes. Is that very first meditative absorption the second meditative absorption? That should not be said… ^5-813

Does one pass from the second meditative absorption to the third meditative absorption? Yes. Is the turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the second meditative absorption the very same turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the third meditative absorption? That should not be said…
One passes from the second meditative absorption to the third meditative absorption — should it not be said that "the turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the second meditative absorption is the very same turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the third meditative absorption"? Yes. Does the third meditative absorption arise for one who does not turn the mind to it… for one who does not aspire? That should not be said… But doesn't the third meditative absorption arise for one who turns the mind to it… for one who aspires? Yes. If the third meditative absorption arises for one who turns the mind to it… for one who aspires, then surely you must not say: "One passes from the second meditative absorption to the third meditative absorption."
Does one pass from the second meditative absorption to the third meditative absorption? Yes. Does the second meditative absorption arise for one who attends to thinking and examining as dangerous? Yes. Does the third meditative absorption arise for one who attends to thinking and examining as dangerous? That should not be said…
Is the second meditative absorption with joy? Yes. Is the third meditative absorption with joy? That should not be said… Does one pass from the second meditative absorption to the third meditative absorption? Yes. Is that very second meditative absorption the third meditative absorption? That should not be said… ^5-814

Does one pass from the third meditative absorption to the fourth meditative absorption? Yes. Is the turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the third meditative absorption the very same turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the fourth meditative absorption? That should not be said…
One passes from the third meditative absorption to the fourth meditative absorption — should it not be said that "the turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the third meditative absorption is the very same turning of the mind… aspiration for the arising of the fourth meditative absorption"? Yes. Does the fourth meditative absorption arise for one who does not turn the mind to it… for one who does not aspire? That should not be said… But doesn't the fourth meditative absorption arise for one who turns the mind to it… for one who aspires? Yes. If the fourth meditative absorption arises for one who turns the mind to it… for one who aspires, then surely you must not say: "One passes from the third meditative absorption to the fourth meditative absorption."
Does one pass from the third meditative absorption to the fourth meditative absorption? Yes. Does the third meditative absorption arise for one who attends to joy as dangerous? Yes. Does the fourth meditative absorption arise for one who attends to joy as dangerous? That should not be said…
Is the third meditative absorption accompanied by happiness? Yes. Is the fourth meditative absorption accompanied by happiness? That should not be said… Does one pass from the third meditative absorption to the fourth meditative absorption? Yes. Is that very third meditative absorption the fourth meditative absorption? That should not be said… ^5-815

Should it not be said that "one passes from one meditative absorption to another"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Here, monks, a monk, quite secluded from sensual pleasures… enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption" [A. Ni. 2.13; 4.163]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case one passes from one meditative absorption to another.

##### (183) 7. Discussion of the Interval between Meditative Absorptions ^5-26-7-1-0

Is there an interval between meditative absorptions (jhānas)? Yes. Is there an interval between contacts… an interval between perceptions? That should not be said…
Is there an interval between meditative absorptions? Yes. Is there an interval between meditative absorptions in between the second and the third meditative absorption? That should not be said…
Is there an interval between meditative absorptions? Yes. Is there an interval between meditative absorptions in between the third and the fourth meditative absorption? That should not be said…
Is there no interval between meditative absorptions in between the second and the third meditative absorption? Yes. If there is no interval between meditative absorptions in between the second and the third meditative absorption, then surely you must not say: "There is an interval between meditative absorptions."
Is there no interval between meditative absorptions in between the third and the fourth meditative absorption? Yes. If there is no interval between meditative absorptions in between the third and the fourth meditative absorption, then surely you must not say: "There is an interval between meditative absorptions." ^5-817

Is there an interval between meditative absorptions in between the first and the second meditative absorption? Yes. Is there an interval between meditative absorptions in between the second and the third meditative absorption? That should not be said…
Is there an interval between meditative absorptions in between the first and the second meditative absorption? Yes. Is there an interval between meditative absorptions in between the third and the fourth meditative absorption? That should not be said…
Is there no interval between meditative absorptions in between the second and the third meditative absorption? Yes. Is there no interval between meditative absorptions in between the first and the second meditative absorption? That should not be said…
Is there no interval between meditative absorptions in between the third and the fourth meditative absorption? Yes. Is there no interval between meditative absorptions in between the first and the second meditative absorption? That should not be said… ^5-818

Is concentration without thinking, with examining only, the interval between meditative absorptions? Yes. Is concentration with thinking and examining the interval between meditative absorptions? That should not be said…
Is concentration without thinking, with examining only, the interval between meditative absorptions? Yes. Is concentration without thinking and without examining the interval between meditative absorptions? That should not be said…
Is concentration with thinking and examining not the interval between meditative absorptions? Yes. Is concentration without thinking, with examining only, not the interval between meditative absorptions? That should not be said…
Is concentration without thinking and without examining not the interval between meditative absorptions? Yes. Is concentration without thinking, with examining only, not the interval between meditative absorptions? That should not be said… ^5-819

Is there concentration without thinking, with examining only, in between two meditative absorptions that are present? Yes. But when concentration without thinking, with examining only, is occurring, hasn't the first meditative absorption ceased and the second meditative absorption become present? Yes. If, when concentration without thinking, with examining only, is occurring, the first meditative absorption has ceased and the second meditative absorption has become present, then surely you must not say: "Concentration without thinking, with examining only, in between two meditative absorptions that are present, is the interval between meditative absorptions." ^5-820

Is concentration without thinking, with examining only, not the interval between meditative absorptions? Yes. Is concentration without thinking, with examining only, the first meditative absorption… the second meditative absorption… the third meditative absorption… the fourth meditative absorption? That should not be said. In that case concentration without thinking, with examining only, is the interval between meditative absorptions. ^5-821

Is concentration without thinking, with examining only, the interval between meditative absorptions? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared three kinds of concentration — concentration with thinking and examining, concentration without thinking, with examining only, and concentration without thinking and without examining [D. Ni. 3.305, 353]? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared three kinds of concentration — concentration with thinking… without examining — then surely you must not say: "Concentration without thinking, with examining only, is the interval between meditative absorptions."
The Discussion of the Interval between Meditative Absorptions is finished. ^5-822

##### (184) 8. Discussion of "One Hears Sound" ^5-26-8-1-0

Does one in a meditative attainment hear sound? Yes. Does one in a meditative attainment see a visible form with the eye… with the ear… with the nose… with the tongue… feel a touch with the body? That should not be said…
Does one in a meditative attainment hear sound? Yes. Is one possessing ear-consciousness in a meditative attainment? That should not be said. But isn't concentration found in one possessing mind-consciousness? Yes. If concentration is found in one possessing mind-consciousness, then surely you must not say: "One in a meditative attainment hears sound."
Is concentration found in one possessing mind-consciousness, while one possessing ear-consciousness hears sound? Yes. If concentration is found in one possessing mind-consciousness, while one possessing ear-consciousness hears sound, then surely you must not say: "One in a meditative attainment hears sound." Is concentration found in one possessing mind-consciousness, while one possessing ear-consciousness hears sound? Yes. Is there a coming together of two contacts… of two states of mind? That should not be said… ^5-823

Should it not be said that "one in a meditative attainment hears sound"? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One said that sound is a thorn to the first meditative absorption (jhāna)? Yes. If the Blessed One has said that sound is a thorn to the first meditative absorption, then surely you must also say: "One in a meditative attainment hears sound." ^5-824

Since the Blessed One has said that sound is a thorn to the first meditative absorption, does one in a meditative attainment hear sound? Yes. The Blessed One has said that thinking and examining are a thorn to the second meditative absorption — does that one have thinking and examining? That should not be said…
Since the Blessed One has said that sound is a thorn to the first meditative absorption, does one in a meditative attainment hear sound? Yes. The Blessed One has said that joy is a thorn to the third meditative absorption… that in-and-out breathing is a thorn to the fourth meditative absorption… that the perception of material form is a thorn for one who has entered the base of the infinity of space… that the perception of the base of the infinity of space is a thorn for one who has entered the base of the infinity of consciousness… that the perception of the base of the infinity of consciousness is a thorn for one who has entered the base of nothingness… that the perception of the base of nothingness is a thorn for one who has entered the base of neither perception nor non-perception… that perception and feeling are a thorn for one who has entered the cessation of perception and feeling — does that one have perception and feeling? That should not be said…
The Discussion of "One Hears Sound" is finished. ^5-825

##### (185) 9. Discussion of "One Sees a Visible Form with the Eye" ^5-26-9-1-0

Does one see a visible form with the eye? Yes. Does one see material form with material form? That should not be said… Does one see material form with material form? Yes. Does one cognize material form with material form? That should not be said… Does one cognize material form with material form? Yes. Is material form mind-consciousness? Does one touch material form with material form? Yes. Does one cognize material form with material form? That should not be said… Does one cognize material form with material form? Yes. Is material form mind-consciousness? That should not be said… Does one feel a touch with the body? Yes. Does the body have turning of the mind… aspiration? That should not be said… But doesn't the body have no turning of the mind… aspiration? Yes. If the body has no turning of the mind… aspiration, then surely you must not say: "One feels a touch with the body."… ^5-826

Should it not be said that "one sees a visible form with the eye"… "one feels a touch with the body"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Here, monks, a monk sees a visible form with the eye… feels a touch with the body" [M. Ni. 1.349; A. Ni. 4.37 (see the commentary)]? The Eighteenth Chapter.
Here is its summary:
The Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world; the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One; the Buddha, the Blessed One, has no compassion; the excrement and urine of the Buddha, the Blessed One, far surpass other fragrant substances; one realizes the four fruits of the ascetic life by a single noble path; one passes from one meditative absorption to another; there is an interval between meditative absorptions; one in a meditative attainment hears sound; one sees a visible form with the eye, feels a touch with the body. ^5-827
