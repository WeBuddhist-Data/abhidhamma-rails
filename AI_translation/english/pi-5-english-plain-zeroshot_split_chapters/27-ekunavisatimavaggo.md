#### (186) 1. Discussion of Abandoning Defilements ^5-27-1-0

Does one abandon past defilements? Yes. Does one make cease what has ceased, make go what has gone, destroy what is destroyed, make vanish what has vanished, make completely vanish what has completely vanished? That should not be said… Does one abandon past defilements? Yes. But hasn't the past ceased? Yes. If the past has ceased, then surely you must not say: "One abandons past defilements." Does one abandon past defilements? Yes. But doesn't the past not exist? Yes. If the past does not exist, then surely you must not say: "One abandons past defilements." ^5-828

Does one abandon future defilements? Yes. Does one make unborn what is unborn, make unarisen what has not arisen, make unproduced what is not produced, make unmanifest what is not manifested? That should not be said… Does one abandon future defilements? Yes. But isn't the future unborn? Yes. If the future is unborn, then surely you must not say: "One abandons future defilements." Does one abandon future defilements? Yes. But doesn't the future not exist? Yes. If the future does not exist, then surely you must not say: "One abandons future defilements." ^5-829

Does one abandon present defilements? Yes. Does one who is impassioned abandon greed, one who is corrupted abandon hatred, one who is deluded abandon delusion, one who is defiled abandon defilements? That should not be said… Does one abandon greed by means of greed, hatred by means of hatred, delusion by means of delusion, defilements by means of defilements? That should not be said…
Is greed associated with the mind, and is the path associated with the mind? Yes. Is there a coming together of two states of mind? That should not be said…
Is greed unwholesome, and is the path wholesome? Yes. Do wholesome and unwholesome, blameworthy and blameless, inferior and superior, dark and bright states with their counterparts come face to face? That should not be said…
Do wholesome and unwholesome, blameworthy and blameless, inferior and superior, dark and bright states with their counterparts come face to face? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, there are these four things that are very far apart. Which four? The sky, monks, and the earth — this is the first thing that is very far apart… therefore the Dhamma of the good is far from the bad"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "wholesome and unwholesome… come face to face." ^5-830

Should it not be said that "one abandons past defilements, abandons future defilements, abandons present defilements"? Yes. Is there no abandoning of defilements? That should not be said… In that case one abandons past defilements, abandons future defilements, abandons present defilements.
The Discussion of Abandoning Defilements is finished. ^5-831

##### (187) 2. Discussion of Emptiness ^5-27-2-1-0

Is emptiness included in the aggregate of mental formations? Yes. Is the signless included in the aggregate of mental formations? That should not be said… Is emptiness included in the aggregate of mental formations? Yes. Is the desireless included in the aggregate of mental formations? That should not be said… Should it not be said of the signless that "it is included in the aggregate of mental formations"? Yes. Should it not be said of emptiness that "it is included in the aggregate of mental formations"? That should not be said… Should it not be said of the desireless that "it is included in the aggregate of mental formations"? Yes. Should it not be said of emptiness that "it is included in the aggregate of mental formations"? That should not be said…
Is emptiness included in the aggregate of mental formations? Yes. Is the aggregate of mental formations not impermanent, not conditioned, not dependently arisen, not subject to destruction, not subject to passing away, not subject to fading, not subject to cessation, not subject to change? That should not be said… But isn't the aggregate of mental formations impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to passing away, subject to fading, subject to cessation, subject to change? Yes. If the aggregate of mental formations is impermanent… subject to change, then surely you must not say: "Emptiness is included in the aggregate of mental formations." ^5-832

Is the emptiness of the aggregate of material form included in the aggregate of mental formations? Yes. Is the emptiness of the aggregate of mental formations included in the aggregate of material form? That should not be said… Is the emptiness of the aggregate of feeling… of the aggregate of perception… of the aggregate of consciousness included in the aggregate of mental formations? Yes. Is the emptiness of the aggregate of mental formations included in the aggregate of consciousness? That should not be said…
Should it not be said of the emptiness of the aggregate of mental formations that "it is included in the aggregate of material form"? Yes. Should it not be said of the emptiness of the aggregate of material form that "it is included in the aggregate of mental formations"? That should not be said… Should it not be said of the emptiness of the aggregate of mental formations that "it is included in the aggregate of feeling… included in the aggregate of perception… included in the aggregate of consciousness"? Yes. Should it not be said of the emptiness of the aggregate of consciousness that "it is included in the aggregate of mental formations"? That should not be said… ^5-833

Should it not be said that "emptiness is included in the aggregate of mental formations"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, formations (conditioned things) are empty of a self or of what belongs to a self" [M. Ni. 3.69, in the Discourse on the Way to the Imperturbable]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case emptiness is included in the aggregate of mental formations.
The Discussion of Emptiness is finished. ^5-834

##### (192) 7. Discussion of the Absolutely Fixed Course ^5-27-7-1-0

Does an ordinary person have an absolutely fixed course? Yes. Is one who has killed their mother one whose course is absolutely fixed; one who has killed their father… a killer of a worthy one (arahant)… a shedder of a Buddha's blood… a splitter of the Community one whose course is absolutely fixed? That should not be said… ^5-847

Does an ordinary person have an absolutely fixed course? Yes. Would doubt arise in a person whose course is absolutely fixed? Yes. If doubt would arise in a person whose course is absolutely fixed, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person has an absolutely fixed course."
Would doubt not arise in a person whose course is absolutely fixed? Yes. Has it been abandoned? That should not be said… Has it been abandoned? Yes. By the path of stream-entry? That should not be said… By the path of once-returning… by the path of non-returning… by the path of arahantship (the state of a worthy one)? That should not be said…
By which path? By an unwholesome path. Is an unwholesome path one that leads out, leads to destruction, leads to awakening, is free of taints… not subject to defilement? That should not be said… But isn't an unwholesome path one that does not lead out… is subject to defilement? Yes. If an unwholesome path does not lead out… is subject to defilement, then surely you must not say: "In a person whose course is absolutely fixed, doubt has been abandoned by an unwholesome path." ^5-848

Would the annihilationist view arise in a person fixed in the eternalist view? Yes. If the annihilationist view would arise in a person fixed in the eternalist view, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person has an absolutely fixed course."
Would the annihilationist view not arise in a person fixed in the eternalist view? Yes. Has it been abandoned? That should not be said… Has it been abandoned? Yes. By the path of stream-entry? That should not be said… By the path of once-returning… by the path of non-returning… by the path of arahantship? That should not be said…
By which path? By an unwholesome path. Is an unwholesome path… then surely you must not say: "In a person fixed in the eternalist view, the annihilationist view has been abandoned by an unwholesome path." ^5-849

Would the eternalist view arise in a person fixed in the annihilationist view? Yes. If the eternalist view would arise in a person fixed in the annihilationist view, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person has an absolutely fixed course."
Would the eternalist view not arise in a person fixed in the annihilationist view? Yes. Has it been abandoned? That should not be said… Has it been abandoned? Yes. By the path of stream-entry? That should not be said… By the path of once-returning… by the path of non-returning… by the path of arahantship? That should not be said…
By which path? By an unwholesome path. Is an unwholesome path… then surely you must not say: "In a person fixed in the annihilationist view, the eternalist view has been abandoned by an unwholesome path." ^5-850

Should it not be said that "an ordinary person has an absolutely fixed course"? Yes. The Nineteenth Chapter.
Here is its summary:
One abandons past defilements, abandons future defilements, abandons present defilements; emptiness is included in the aggregate of mental formations; the fruit of the ascetic life is unconditioned; acquisition is unconditioned; the suchness of all things is unconditioned; the element of nibbāna is wholesome; an ordinary person has an absolutely fixed course; there are no five faculties that are worldly. ^5-856
