### The Twenty-Second Chapter ^5-30-0

#### (208) 1. Discussion of Final Nibbāna ^5-30-1-0

Is there final nibbāna (final release) without abandoning some fetter? Yes. Is there final nibbāna without abandoning identity view… without abandoning lack of moral fear? That should not be said…
Is there final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter? Yes. Is a worthy one (arahant) one who has greed… defilements? That should not be said… But isn't a worthy one free from greed… free from defilements? Yes. If a worthy one is free from greed… free from defilements, then surely you must not say: "There is final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter." ^5-892

Should it not be said that "there is final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter"? Yes. Does a worthy one know the whole domain of a Buddha? That should not be said. In that case there is final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter.
The Discussion of Final Nibbāna is finished. ^5-893

##### (209) 2. Discussion of a Wholesome Mind ^5-30-2-1-0

Does a worthy one (arahant) attain final nibbāna (final release) with a wholesome mind? Yes. Does a worthy one attain final nibbāna while generating a meritorious formation… while generating an imperturbable formation… while performing action that leads to a destination… while performing action that leads to existence… while performing action that leads to sovereignty… while performing action that leads to lordship… while performing action that leads to great wealth… while performing action that leads to a large retinue… while performing action that leads to the splendour of the gods… while performing action that leads to the splendour of human beings? That should not be said…
Does a worthy one attain final nibbāna with a wholesome mind? Yes. Does a worthy one attain final nibbāna while building up, tearing down, abandoning, clinging, scattering, binding together, putting out, kindling? That should not be said… But doesn't a worthy one neither build up nor tear down, but stand having torn down? Yes. If a worthy one neither builds up nor tears down, but stands having torn down, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one attains final nibbāna with a wholesome mind." But doesn't a worthy one neither abandon nor cling, but stand having abandoned; neither scatter nor bind together, but stand having scattered; doesn't a worthy one neither put out nor kindle, but stand having put out? Yes. If a worthy one neither puts out nor kindles, but stands having put out, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one attains final nibbāna with a wholesome mind." ^5-894

Should it not be said that "a worthy one attains final nibbāna with a wholesome mind"? Yes. But doesn't a worthy one attain final nibbāna with mindfulness established, mindful and clearly comprehending? Yes. If a worthy one attains final nibbāna with mindfulness established, mindful and clearly comprehending, then surely you must also say: "A worthy one attains final nibbāna with a wholesome mind."
The Discussion of a Wholesome Mind is finished. ^5-895

##### (210) 3. Discussion of the Imperturbable ^5-30-3-1-0

Does a worthy one (arahant) attain final nibbāna (final release) while established in the imperturbable? Yes. But doesn't a worthy one attain final nibbāna while established in the ordinary mind? Yes. If a worthy one attains final nibbāna while established in the ordinary mind, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable."
Does a worthy one attain final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable? Yes. Does a worthy one attain final nibbāna while established in a functional mind? That should not be said… But doesn't a worthy one attain final nibbāna while established in a mind that is a result? Yes. If a worthy one attains final nibbāna while established in a mind that is a result, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable."
Does a worthy one attain final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable? Yes. Does a worthy one attain final nibbāna while established in a mind that is functional and indeterminate? That should not be said… But doesn't a worthy one attain final nibbāna while established in a mind that is a result and indeterminate? Yes. If a worthy one attains final nibbāna while established in a mind that is a result and indeterminate, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable."
Does a worthy one attain final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable? Yes. But didn't the Blessed One attain final nibbāna immediately after emerging from the fourth meditative absorption (jhāna) [D. Ni. 2.219]? Yes. If the Blessed One attained final nibbāna immediately after emerging from the fourth meditative absorption, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable."
The Discussion of the Imperturbable is finished. ^5-896

##### (211) 4. Discussion of Breaking Through to the Dhamma ^5-30-4-1-0

Is there breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb? Yes. Is there, for one lying in the womb, teaching of the Dhamma, hearing of the Dhamma, discussion of the Dhamma, questioning, undertaking of virtue, guarding the doors of the sense faculties, moderation in eating, devotion to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night? That should not be said… Is there, for one lying in the womb, no teaching of the Dhamma, no hearing of the Dhamma… no devotion to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night? Yes. If, for one lying in the womb, there is no teaching of the Dhamma, no hearing of the Dhamma… no devotion to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night, then surely you must not say: "There is breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb."
Is there breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb? Yes. But aren't there two conditions for the arising of right view — the voice of another, and wise attention? Yes. If there are two conditions for the arising of right view — the voice of another, and wise attention — then surely you must not say: "There is breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb."
Is there breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb? Yes. Is there breakthrough to the Dhamma for one who is asleep, heedless, unmindful and without clear comprehension? That should not be said…
The Discussion of Breaking Through to the Dhamma is finished. ^5-897

Is there attainment of arahantship (the state of a worthy one) for one lying in the womb? Yes. Is there attainment of arahantship for one who is asleep, heedless, unmindful and without clear comprehension? That should not be said… ^5-898

Is there breakthrough to the Dhamma for one who is dreaming? Yes. Is there breakthrough to the Dhamma for one who is asleep, heedless, unmindful and without clear comprehension? That should not be said… ^5-899

Is there attainment of arahantship for one who is dreaming? Yes. Is there attainment of arahantship for one who is asleep, heedless, unmindful and without clear comprehension? That should not be said…

##### (215) 8. Discussion of "Indeterminate" ^5-30-6-1-0

Is every state of mind of one who is dreaming indeterminate? Yes. Could one kill a living being in a dream? Yes. If one could kill a living being in a dream, then surely you must not say: "Every state of mind of one who is dreaming is indeterminate."
Could one, in a dream, take what is not given… speak falsely, speak divisively, speak harshly, engage in idle chatter, break into houses, carry off plunder, rob a single house, lie in ambush on the highway, go to another's wife, destroy a village, destroy a town; could one engage in sexual intercourse in a dream, could impure fluid be emitted by one who is dreaming; could one, in a dream, give a gift, give robes, give almsfood, give lodgings, give medicines and requisites for the sick, give food to chew, give meals, give drink, pay homage to a shrine, offer a garland at a shrine, offer scents, offer ointments… walk around a shrine keeping it on their right? Yes. If one could, in a dream, walk around a shrine keeping it on their right, then surely you must not say: "Every state of mind of one who is dreaming is indeterminate." ^5-901

Should it not be said that "every state of mind of one who is dreaming is indeterminate"? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared that the mind of one who is dreaming is of no account? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared that the mind of one who is dreaming is of no account, then surely you must also say: "Every state of mind of one who is dreaming is indeterminate."
The Discussion of "Indeterminate" is finished. ^5-902

##### (216) 9. Discussion of Repetition Condition ^5-30-7-1-0

Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, killing living beings, when pursued, developed and cultivated, leads to hell, leads to the animal realm, leads to the realm of ghosts; the lightest result of killing living beings, for one who becomes a human being, is that it leads to a short life" [A. Ni. 8.40]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there is some repetition condition.
Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, taking what is not given, when pursued, developed and cultivated, leads to hell, leads to the animal realm, leads to the realm of ghosts; the lightest result of taking what is not given, for one who becomes a human being, is that it leads to loss of wealth… the lightest result of sexual misconduct, for one who becomes a human being, is that it leads to rivalry and hostility… the lightest result of false speech, for one who becomes a human being, is that it leads to being falsely accused… the lightest result of divisive speech, for one who becomes a human being, is that it leads to being divided from one's friends… the lightest result of harsh speech, for one who becomes a human being, is that it leads to disagreeable sounds… the lightest result of idle chatter, for one who becomes a human being, is that it leads to words that no one accepts; drinking liquor and wine, monks, when pursued… the lightest result of drinking liquor and wine, for one who becomes a human being, is that it leads to madness" [A. Ni. 8.40]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there is some repetition condition. ^5-903

Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, wrong view, when pursued, developed and cultivated, leads to hell, leads to the animal realm, leads to the realm of ghosts"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there is some repetition condition.
Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Wrong intention… wrong concentration, monks, when pursued, developed… leads to the realm of ghosts"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there is some repetition condition. ^5-904

Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, right view, when pursued, developed and cultivated, plunges into the deathless, has the deathless as its final resort, has the deathless as its culmination"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there is some repetition condition.
Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, right intention, when pursued, developed and cultivated… monks, right concentration, when pursued, developed and cultivated, plunges into the deathless, has the deathless as its final resort, has the deathless as its culmination"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there is some repetition condition.
The Discussion of Repetition Condition is finished. ^5-905

##### (217) 10. Discussion of the Momentary ^5-30-8-1-0

Do all things last only a single moment of mind? Yes. Does the great earth last for the duration of a mind, does the great ocean last, does Sineru, king of mountains, last, does water last, does fire last, does air last, do grass, wood and trees last for the duration of a mind? That should not be said…
Do all things last only a single moment of mind? Yes. Is the eye base arisen together with eye-consciousness? That should not be said… Is the eye base arisen together with eye-consciousness? Yes. But didn't the venerable Sāriputta say this: "Friends, if the internal eye is intact but external visible forms do not come within its range, and there is no corresponding bringing to mind, then there is no manifestation of the corresponding section of consciousness [variant: viññāṇabhāvassa (many editions)]. Friends, if the internal eye is intact and external visible forms come within its range, but there is no corresponding bringing to mind, then there is no manifestation of the corresponding section of consciousness. But when, friends, the internal eye is intact, and external visible forms come within its range, and there is the corresponding bringing to mind, then there is the manifestation of the corresponding section of consciousness" [M. Ni. 1.306]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the eye base is arisen together with eye-consciousness."
Is the ear base… the nose base… the tongue base… the body base arisen together with body-consciousness? That should not be said… Is the body base arisen together with body-consciousness? Yes. But didn't the venerable Sāriputta say this: "Friends, if the internal body is intact but external touches do not come within its range, and there is no… Friends, if the internal body is intact and external touches come within its range, but there is no… But when, friends, the internal body is intact, and external touches come within its range, and there is the corresponding bringing to mind, then there is the manifestation of the corresponding section of consciousness" [M. Ni. 1.306]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the body base is arisen together with body-consciousness." ^5-906

Should it not be said that "all things last only a single moment of mind"? Yes. Are all things permanent, stable, eternal, not subject to change? That should not be said. In that case all things last only a single moment of mind.
The Discussion of the Momentary is finished.
The Twenty-Second Chapter.
Here is its summary:
There is final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter; a worthy one attains final nibbāna with a wholesome mind; a worthy one attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable; there is breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb; there is attainment of arahantship for one lying in the womb; there is breakthrough to the Dhamma for one who is dreaming; there is attainment of arahantship for one who is dreaming; every state of mind of one who is dreaming is indeterminate; there is no repetition condition at all; all things last only a single moment of mind. ^5-907
