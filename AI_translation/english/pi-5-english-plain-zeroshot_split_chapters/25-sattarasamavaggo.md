#### (166) 1. Discussion of a Worthy One's Accumulation of Merit ^5-25-1-0

Does a worthy one (arahant) have an accumulation of merit? Yes. Does a worthy one have an accumulation of demerit? That should not be said… Does a worthy one have no accumulation of demerit? Yes. Does a worthy one have no accumulation of merit? That should not be said… ^5-776

Does a worthy one have an accumulation of merit? Yes. Does a worthy one generate a meritorious formation, generate an imperturbable formation, perform action that leads to a destination, perform action that leads to existence, perform action that leads to sovereignty, perform action that leads to lordship [variant: ādhipaccasaṃvattanikaṃ (?)], perform action that leads to great wealth, perform action that leads to a large retinue, perform action that leads to the splendour of the gods, perform action that leads to the splendour of human beings? That should not be said… ^5-777

Does a worthy one have an accumulation of merit? Yes. Does a worthy one build up? That should not be said… Does a worthy one tear down? That should not be said… Does a worthy one abandon… Does a worthy one cling… Does a worthy one scatter… Does a worthy one bind together… Does a worthy one put out… Does a worthy one kindle? That should not be said… But doesn't a worthy one neither build up nor tear down, but stand having torn down? Yes. If a worthy one neither builds up nor tears down, but stands having torn down, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one has an accumulation of merit."
But doesn't a worthy one neither abandon nor cling, but stand having abandoned; neither scatter nor bind together, but stand having scattered; neither put out nor kindle, but stand having put out? Yes. If a worthy one neither puts out nor kindles, but stands having put out, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one has an accumulation of merit." ^5-778

Does a worthy one have no accumulation of merit? Yes. Would a worthy one give a gift? Yes. If a worthy one would give a gift, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one has no accumulation of merit."
Would a worthy one give robes… give almsfood… give lodgings… give medicines and requisites for the sick… give food to chew… give meals… give drink… pay homage to a shrine… offer a garland at a shrine… offer scents… offer ointments… walk around a shrine keeping it on their right [variant: padakkhiṇaṃ (?)]? Yes. If a worthy one would walk around a shrine keeping it on their right, then surely you must not say: "A worthy one has no accumulation of merit."
The Discussion of "A Worthy One Has an Accumulation of Merit" is finished. ^5-779

##### (167) 2. Discussion of "There Is No Untimely Death for a Worthy One" ^5-25-2-1-0

Is there no untimely death for a worthy one (arahant)? Yes. Is there no killer of a worthy one? That should not be said… Is there a killer of a worthy one? Yes. Is there untimely death for a worthy one? That should not be said… Is there no untimely death for a worthy one? Yes. When someone deprives a worthy one of life, do they deprive them of life while there is life and a remainder of life, or do they deprive them of life while there is no life and no remainder of life? They deprive them of life while there is life and a remainder of life. If they deprive them of life while there is life and a remainder of life, then surely you must not say: "There is no untimely death for a worthy one." Since they deprive them of life while there is no life and no remainder of life, is there no killer of a worthy one? That should not be said… ^5-780

Is there no untimely death for a worthy one? Yes. Could poison not affect, could a weapon not affect, could fire not affect the body of a worthy one? That should not be said… But couldn't poison affect, couldn't a weapon affect, couldn't fire affect the body of a worthy one? Yes. If poison could affect, a weapon could affect and fire could affect the body of a worthy one, then surely you must not say: "There is no untimely death for a worthy one."
Could poison not affect, could a weapon not affect, could fire not affect the body of a worthy one? Yes. Is there no killer of a worthy one? That should not be said… ^5-781

Is there untimely death for a worthy one? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, I do not say that intentional actions that have been done and accumulated come to an end without their result having been experienced — and that either in this very life, or in the next rebirth, or on some later occasion"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case there is no untimely death for a worthy one.
The Discussion of "There Is No Untimely Death for a Worthy One" is finished. ^5-782

##### (168) 3. Discussion of "All This Is Due to Action" ^5-25-3-1-0

Is all this due to action? Yes. Is action too due to action? That should not be said… Is all this due to action? Yes. Is all this caused by what was done in the past? That should not be said… Is all this due to action? Yes. Is all this due to the result of action? That should not be said… ^5-783

Is all this due to the result of action? Yes. Would one kill a living being as a result of action? Yes. Does killing living beings have fruit? Yes. Does the result of action have fruit? That should not be said… Is the result of action without fruit? Yes. Is killing living beings without fruit? That should not be said…
Would one, as a result of action, take what is not given… speak falsely… speak divisively… speak harshly… engage in idle chatter… break into houses… carry off plunder… rob a single house… lie in ambush on the highway… go to another's wife… destroy a village… destroy a town… would one, as a result of action, give a gift… give robes… give almsfood… give lodgings… give medicines and requisites for the sick? Yes. Do medicines and requisites for the sick have fruit? Yes. Does the result of action have fruit? That should not be said… Is the result of action without fruit? Yes. Are medicines and requisites for the sick without fruit? That should not be said… ^5-784

Should it not be said that "all this is due to action"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"By action the world goes on [variant: vattati (Pī., Ka., M. Ni. 2.460), vattate (?)], by action people go on [variant: vattati (Pī., Ka., M. Ni. 2.460), vattate (?)];
beings are bound by action, as a moving chariot is held by its linchpin [M. Ni. 2.460; Su. Ni. 659].
"By action one gains fame and praise,
by action loss, slaughter and bondage;
knowing that action works in these different ways,
how could one say in the world, ‘There is no action’?"
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case "all this is due to action."
The Discussion of "All This Is Due to Action" is finished. ^5-785

##### (169) 4. Discussion of What Is Bound Up with the Faculties ^5-25-4-1-0

Is only what is bound up with the faculties suffering? Yes. Is only what is bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to passing away, subject to fading, subject to cessation, subject to change? That should not be said… But isn't what is not bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to passing away, subject to fading, subject to cessation, subject to change? Yes. If what is not bound up with the faculties is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to passing away, subject to fading, subject to cessation, subject to change, then surely you must not say: "Only what is bound up with the faculties is suffering."
Is what is not bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen… subject to change, and yet not suffering? Yes. Is what is bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned… subject to change, and yet not suffering? That should not be said…
Is what is bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned… subject to change, and is it suffering [this pair of questions should come before the previous pair]? Yes. Is what is not bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned… subject to change, and is it suffering [this pair of questions should come before the previous pair]? That should not be said… ^5-786

Is only what is bound up with the faculties suffering? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One said that what is impermanent is suffering [S. Ni. 3.15], and isn't "what is not bound up with the faculties impermanent"? Yes. If the Blessed One has said that what is impermanent is suffering [S. Ni. 3.15], and what is not bound up with the faculties is impermanent, then surely you must not say: "Only what is bound up with the faculties is suffering." ^5-787

Should it not be said that "only what is bound up with the faculties is suffering"? Yes. Just as the holy life (the life of spiritual training) is lived under the Blessed One for the full understanding of suffering bound up with the faculties, so too is the holy life lived under the Blessed One for the full understanding of suffering not bound up with the faculties? That should not be said… Just as suffering bound up with the faculties, once fully understood, does not arise again, so too does suffering not bound up with the faculties, once fully understood, not arise again? That should not be said. In that case only what is bound up with the faculties is suffering.
The Discussion of What Is Bound Up with the Faculties is finished. ^5-788

##### (170) 5. Discussion of "Except for the Noble Path" ^5-25-5-1-0

Except for the noble path, are the remaining formations (conditioned things) suffering? Yes. Is the origin of suffering also suffering? That should not be said… Is the origin of suffering also suffering? Yes. Are there only three noble truths? That should not be said… Are there only three noble truths? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared four noble truths — suffering, the origin of suffering, the cessation of suffering, and the way leading to the cessation of suffering? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared four noble truths — suffering, the origin of suffering, the cessation of suffering, and the way leading to the cessation of suffering — then surely you must not say: "There are only three noble truths."
Is the origin of suffering also suffering? Yes. How so? In the sense of being impermanent. Is the noble path impermanent? Yes. Is the noble path suffering? That should not be said…
Is the noble path impermanent, and yet not suffering? Yes. Is the origin of suffering impermanent, and yet not suffering? That should not be said… Is the origin of suffering impermanent, and is it suffering? Yes. Is the noble path impermanent, and is it suffering? That should not be said… ^5-789

Should it not be said that "except for the noble path, the remaining formations are suffering"? Yes. But isn't that the way leading to the cessation of suffering? Yes. If that is the way leading to the cessation of suffering, then surely you must also say: "Except for the noble path, the remaining formations are suffering."
The Discussion of "Except for the Noble Path" is finished. ^5-790

##### (171) 6. Discussion of "It Should Not Be Said That the Community Receives Offerings" ^5-25-6-1-0

Should it not be said that "the Community receives offerings"? Yes. But isn't the Community worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world? Yes. If the Community is worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world, then surely you must also say: "The Community receives offerings."
Should it not be said that "the Community receives offerings"? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared that the four pairs of persons, the eight kinds of individuals, are worthy of offerings? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared that the four pairs of persons, the eight kinds of individuals, are worthy of offerings, then surely you must also say: "The Community receives offerings."
Should it not be said that "the Community receives offerings"? Yes. But aren't there some who give gifts to the Community? Yes. If there are some who give gifts to the Community, then surely you must also say: "The Community receives offerings." But aren't there some who give robes to the Community… give almsfood… give lodgings… give medicines and requisites for the sick… give food to chew… give meals… give drink? Yes. If there are some who give drink to the Community, then surely you must also say: "The Community receives offerings."
Should it not be said that "the Community receives offerings"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"As fire receives the oblation, as the earth receives a great rain cloud,
so the Community, accomplished in concentration, receives offerings"?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case the Community receives offerings. ^5-791

Does the Community receive offerings? Yes. Does the path receive them, does the fruit receive them? That should not be said…
The Discussion of "It Should Not Be Said That the Community Receives Offerings" is finished. ^5-792

Should it not be said that "what is given to the Community is of great fruit"? Yes. But isn't the Community worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world? Yes. If the Community is worthy of gifts… the unsurpassed field of merit for the world, then surely you must also say: "What is given to the Community is of great fruit."
Should it not be said that "what is given to the Community is of great fruit"? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared that the four pairs of persons, the eight kinds of individuals, are worthy of offerings? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared that the four pairs of persons, the eight kinds of individuals, are worthy of offerings, then surely you must also say: "What is given to the Community is of great fruit." ^5-797

Should it not be said that "what is given to the Community is of great fruit"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Give it to the Community, Gotamī; when you give it to the Community, both I and the Community will be honoured" [M. Ni. 3.376]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case what is given to the Community is of great fruit.
Should it not be said that "what is given to the Community is of great fruit"? Yes. But didn't Sakka, lord of the gods, say this to the Blessed One:
"For people who make offerings, for living beings who seek merit,
who make merit bound up with attachments — where is what is given of great fruit?
"The four who are practising and the four established in the fruit —
this Community is upright, possessed of wisdom and virtue.
"For people who make offerings, for living beings who seek merit,
who make merit bound up with attachments, what is given to the Community is of great fruit" [S. Ni. 1.262; Vi. Va. 642, 751].
"For this Community is vast and exalted,
immeasurable like the ocean, the sea;
these are the best, the disciples of the hero among men [variant: narasīhasāvakā (Ka.)];
bringers of light, they proclaim the Dhamma.
"For them it is well given, well offered, well sacrificed,
those who give a gift with the Community in mind;
that offering, gone to the Community, is well established,
of great fruit, praised by the knowers of the world.
"Recollecting such a sacrifice,
those who go about [variant: viharanti (Sī., Ka.)] in the world filled with joy,
having removed the stain of stinginess together with its root,
blameless, go to a heavenly place" [Vi. Va. 645, 754]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case what is given to the Community is of great fruit.

Should it not be said that "what is given to the Buddha is of great fruit"? Yes. But isn't the Blessed One the foremost of two-footed beings, the best of two-footed beings, the chief of two-footed beings, the highest of two-footed beings, the most excellent of two-footed beings, unequalled, equal to the unequalled, without counterpart, without peer, without rival? Should it not be said that "what is given to the Buddha is of great fruit"? Yes. Is there anyone equal to the Buddha in virtue, concentration and wisdom? There is not. If there is no one equal to the Buddha in virtue, concentration and wisdom, then surely you must also say: "What is given to the Buddha is of great fruit."
Should it not be said that "what is given to the Buddha is of great fruit"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Neither in this world nor in the next
is anyone found who is better than or equal to the Buddha,
who has reached the foremost place among those worthy of gifts,
for those who seek merit and look for abundant fruit" [Vi. Va. 1047]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case what is given to the Buddha is of great fruit.

##### (176) 11. Discussion of the Purification of Offerings ^5-25-11-1-0

Is a gift purified only by the giver, not by the recipient? Yes. But aren't there some recipients who are worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world? Yes. If there are some recipients who are worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world, then surely you must not say: "A gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient."
Is a gift purified only by the giver, not by the recipient? Yes. But hasn't the Blessed One declared that the four pairs of persons, the eight kinds of individuals, are worthy of offerings? Yes. If the Blessed One has declared that the four pairs of persons, the eight kinds of individuals, are worthy of offerings, then surely you must not say: "A gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient."
Is a gift purified only by the giver, not by the recipient? Yes. But aren't there some who, having given a gift to a stream-enterer, make their offering succeed? Yes. If there are some who, having given a gift to a stream-enterer, make their offering succeed, then surely you must not say: "A gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient."
But aren't there some who, having given a gift to a once-returner… to a non-returner… to a worthy one (arahant), make their offering succeed? Yes. If there are some who, having given a gift to a worthy one, make their offering succeed, then surely you must not say: "A gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient." ^5-800

Is a gift purified by the recipient? Yes. Is one the doer for another; is pleasure and pain made by another; does one person do and another experience? That should not be said…
Is a gift purified only by the giver, not by the recipient? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Ānanda, there are these four kinds of purification of offerings. Which four? There is, Ānanda, an offering that is purified by the giver, not by the recipient; there is, Ānanda, an offering that is purified by the recipient, not by the giver; there is, Ānanda, an offering that is purified both by the giver and by the recipient; there is, Ānanda, an offering that is purified neither by the giver nor by the recipient. These, Ānanda, are the four kinds of purification of offerings" [M. Ni. 3.381]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "a gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient."
The Discussion of the Purification of Offerings is finished.
The Seventeenth Chapter.
Here is its summary:
A worthy one has an accumulation of merit; there is no untimely death for a worthy one; all this is due to action; only what is bound up with the faculties is suffering; except for the noble path, the remaining formations are suffering; the Community receives offerings; the Community purifies offerings; the Community eats, drinks, chews and tastes; what is given to the Community is of great fruit; there is a gift for purification [variant: visujjhatīti (Syā.)]. ^5-801
