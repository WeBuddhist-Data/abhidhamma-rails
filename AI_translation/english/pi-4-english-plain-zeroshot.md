## The Description of Persons ^4-0

### The Outline ^4-M-0

#### Outline of the Single Types ^4-M-1-0

There are six kinds of description – the description of aggregates, the description of sense bases, the description of elements, the description of truths, the description of faculties, and the description of persons. ^4-M-1

What does the description of the aggregates cover? It covers the five aggregates – the aggregate of material form, the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of mental formations, the aggregate of consciousness; that is what the description of the aggregates covers. ^4-M-2

What does the description of the sense bases cover? It covers the twelve sense bases – the eye base, the visible-form base, the ear base, the sound base, the nose base, the smell base, the tongue base, the taste base, the body base, the touch base, the mind base, the base of mental objects; that is what the description of the sense bases covers. ^4-M-3

What does the description of the elements cover? It covers the eighteen elements – the eye element, the visible-form element, the eye-consciousness element, the ear element, the sound element, the ear-consciousness element, the nose element, the smell element, the nose-consciousness element, the tongue element, the taste element, the tongue-consciousness element, the body element, the touch element, the body-consciousness element, the mind element, the element of mental objects, the mind-consciousness element; that is what the description of the elements covers. ^4-M-4

What does the description of the truths cover? It covers the four truths – the truth of suffering, the truth of the origin of suffering, the truth of the cessation of suffering, the truth of the path; that is what the description of the truths covers. ^4-M-5

What does the description of the faculties cover? It covers the twenty-two faculties – the eye faculty, the ear faculty, the nose faculty, the tongue faculty, the body faculty, the mind faculty, the faculty of femininity, the faculty of masculinity, the life faculty, the faculty of pleasure, the faculty of pain, the faculty of joy, the faculty of sadness, the faculty of equanimity, the faculty of faith, the faculty of energy, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of concentration, the faculty of wisdom, the faculty of "I shall know what I have not yet known", the faculty of final knowledge, the faculty of one who has final knowledge; that is what the description of the faculties covers. ^4-M-6

### The Exposition ^4-1-0

#### Description of Persons by Single Types ^4-1-1-0

What kind of person is one who is liberated at certain times? Here a certain person, from time to time, on certain occasions, lives having touched the eight liberations with the body [variant: phassitvā (Sī., Pī.)], and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one who is liberated at certain times". ^4-1

What kind of person is one who is liberated not only at certain times? Here a certain person does not live from time to time, on certain occasions, having touched the eight liberations with the body, yet, having seen with wisdom, their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one who is liberated not only at certain times". All noble persons are liberated not only at certain times with regard to the noble liberation. ^4-2

What kind of person is one whose attainments can be shaken? Here a certain person gains the meditative attainments, whether those connected with form or those that are formless. But they do not gain them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they cannot enter them and come out of them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, for as long as they wish. So it is possible that, through that person's carelessness, those attainments might be shaken – this person is called "one whose attainments can be shaken". ^4-3

What kind of person is one whose attainments cannot be shaken? Here a certain person gains the meditative attainments, whether those connected with form or those that are formless. And they gain them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they can enter them and come out of them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, for as long as they wish. It is impossible, there is no way, that through that person's carelessness those attainments could be shaken – this person is called "one whose attainments cannot be shaken". All noble persons are ones whose attainments cannot be shaken with regard to the noble liberation. ^4-4

What kind of person is one who is liable to fall away? Here a certain person gains the meditative attainments, whether those connected with form or those that are formless. But they do not gain them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they cannot enter them and come out of them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, for as long as they wish. So it is possible that, through carelessness, that person might fall away from those attainments – this person is called "one who is liable to fall away". ^4-5

What kind of person is one who is not liable to fall away? Here a certain person gains the meditative attainments, whether those connected with form or those that are formless. And they gain them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they can enter them and come out of them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, for as long as they wish. It is impossible, there is no way, that through carelessness that person could fall away from those attainments – this person is called "one who is not liable to fall away". All noble persons are not liable to fall away with regard to the noble liberation. ^4-6

What kind of person is one who can keep what they have gained through intention? Here a certain person gains the meditative attainments, whether those connected with form or those that are formless. But they do not gain them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they cannot enter them and come out of them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, for as long as they wish. If they keep their mind intent on them, they do not fall away from those attainments. If they do not keep their mind intent on them, they fall away from those attainments – this person is called "one who can keep what they have gained through intention". ^4-7

What kind of person is one who can keep what they have gained through guarding it? Here a certain person gains the meditative attainments, whether those connected with form or those that are formless. But they do not gain them at will, without trouble, without difficulty; they cannot enter them and come out of them wherever they wish, whichever they wish, for as long as they wish. If they guard them, they do not fall away from those attainments. If they do not guard them, they fall away from those attainments – this person is called "one who can keep what they have gained through guarding it". ^4-8

What kind of person is an ordinary person? A person in whom the three fetters have not been abandoned, and who is not practising to abandon them – this person is called "an ordinary person". ^4-9

What kind of person is one at the moment of entering the lineage of the noble ones? One who possesses those mental states immediately after which the noble state comes into being – this person is called "one at the moment of entering the lineage of the noble ones". ^4-10

What kind of person is one who refrains from wrong out of fear? The seven learners refrain from wrong out of fear, and so do those ordinary people who are virtuous. A worthy one (arahant) refrains from wrong without fear. ^4-11

What kind of person is one who is unable to make progress? Those persons who are hindered by their actions, hindered by defilements, hindered by the results of actions, who are without faith, without zeal, lacking in wisdom, and dull, unable to enter the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states – these persons are called "ones who are unable to make progress". ^4-12

What kind of person is one who is able to make progress? Those persons who are not hindered by their actions, not hindered by defilements, not hindered by the results of actions, who have faith, zeal and wisdom [variant: paññavantā (Sī.)], and are not dull, able to enter the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states – these persons are called "ones who are able to make progress". ^4-13

What kind of person is one whose course is fixed? The five kinds of person who have committed a deed that brings immediate result, those of wrong view whose course is fixed, and the eight noble persons – these have a fixed course. The remaining persons are ones whose course is not fixed. ^4-14

What kind of person is one who is on the way to a fruit? The four persons who possess a path are on the way to a fruit; the four persons who possess a fruit are established in a fruit. ^4-15

What kind of person is one who reaches both ends at once? A person for whom the ending of the taints and the ending of life happen together, neither one before nor after the other – this person is called "one who reaches both ends at once". ^4-16

What kind of person is one who remains for an aeon? Suppose this person were practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry, and it were the time for the aeon to burn up; the aeon would not burn up until this person had realized the fruit of stream-entry. This person is called "one who remains for an aeon". All persons who possess a path are ones who remain for an aeon. ^4-17

What kind of person is a noble one? The eight noble persons are noble. The remaining persons are not noble. ^4-18

What kind of person is a learner? The four persons who possess a path and the three who possess a fruit are "learners". A worthy one is beyond learning. The remaining persons are neither learners nor beyond learning. ^4-19

What kind of person is one who has the three knowledges? A person who possesses the three knowledges is "one who has the three knowledges". ^4-20

What kind of person is one who has the six higher knowledges? A person who possesses the six higher knowledges is "one who has the six higher knowledges". ^4-21

What kind of person is a Fully Awakened Buddha? Here a certain person, regarding teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves, and there attains all-knowing wisdom and mastery over the powers – this person is called "a Fully Awakened Buddha". ^4-22

What kind of person is a Solitary Buddha? Here a certain person, regarding teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves, but does not there attain all-knowing wisdom or mastery over the powers – this person is called "a Solitary Buddha". ^4-23

What kind of person is one liberated in both ways? Here a certain person lives having touched the eight liberations with the body, and, having seen with wisdom, their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one liberated in both ways". ^4-24

What kind of person is one liberated by wisdom? Here a certain person does not live having touched the eight liberations with the body, yet, having seen with wisdom, their taints are destroyed. This person is called "one liberated by wisdom". ^4-25

What kind of person is a body-witness? Here a certain person lives having touched the eight liberations with the body, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed. This person is called "a body-witness". ^4-26

What kind of person is one who has attained to view? Here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the origin of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the cessation of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering." And the teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata (the Buddha) have been clearly seen and thoroughly examined by them with wisdom. And, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one who has attained to view". ^4-27

What kind of person is one liberated by faith? Here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the origin of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the cessation of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering." And the teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and thoroughly examined by them with wisdom. And, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed, but not in the way they are for one who has attained to view – this person is called "one liberated by faith". ^4-28

What kind of person is a follower of the Teaching? A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry, in whom the faculty of wisdom is predominant, and who develops the noble path carried by wisdom and led by wisdom – this person is called "a follower of the Teaching". A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry is a follower of the Teaching; once established in the fruit, they are one who has attained to view. ^4-29

What kind of person is a follower by faith? A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry, in whom the faculty of faith is predominant, and who develops the noble path carried by faith and led by faith – this person is called "a follower by faith". A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry is a follower by faith; once established in the fruit, they are one liberated by faith. ^4-30

What kind of person is one with at most seven more lives? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the three fetters, is a stream-enterer, no longer liable to fall into the lower realms, fixed in their course, headed for full awakening [variant: sambodhiparāyaṇo (Sī., Ka.)]. After running on and wandering through birth among gods and humans seven times, they make an end of suffering – this person is called "one with at most seven more lives". ^4-31

What kind of person is one who goes from family to family? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the three fetters, is a stream-enterer, no longer liable to fall into the lower realms, fixed in their course, headed for full awakening. After running on and wandering through two or three families, they make an end of suffering – this person is called "one who goes from family to family". ^4-32

What kind of person is one with a single seed? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the three fetters, is a stream-enterer, no longer liable to fall into the lower realms, fixed in their course, headed for full awakening. After producing just one more human existence, they make an end of suffering – this person is called "one with a single seed". ^4-33

What kind of person is a once-returner? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the three fetters and the weakening of lust, hatred and delusion, is a once-returner; coming back to this world only once more, they make an end of suffering – this person is called "a once-returner". ^4-34

What kind of person is a non-returner? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously, attains final release there, and is not liable to return from that world – this person is called "a non-returner". ^4-35

What kind of person is one who attains final release in the interval? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously, attains final release there, and is not liable to return from that world. Either immediately after being reborn there, or before reaching the middle of their lifespan, they bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters – this person is called "one who attains final release in the interval". ^4-36

What kind of person is one who attains final release on arrival? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously, attains final release there, and is not liable to return from that world. After passing the middle of their lifespan, or when close to death [variant: kālaṃ kiriyaṃ (Ka.)], they bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters – this person is called "one who attains final release on arrival". ^4-37

What kind of person is one who attains final release without effort? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously, attains final release there, and is not liable to return from that world. They bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters without effort – this person is called "one who attains final release without effort". ^4-38

What kind of person is one who attains final release with effort? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously, attains final release there, and is not liable to return from that world. They bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters with effort – this person is called "one who attains final release with effort". ^4-39

What kind of person is one who goes upstream to the highest heaven? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously, attains final release there, and is not liable to return from that world. Passing away from the Aviha heaven, they go to the Atappa heaven; passing away from Atappa, they go to the Sudassa heaven; passing away from Sudassa, they go to the Sudassī heaven; passing away from Sudassī, they go to Akaniṭṭha, the highest heaven; in Akaniṭṭha they bring forth the noble path for abandoning the higher fetters – this person is called "one who goes upstream to the highest heaven". ^4-40

What kind of person is a stream-enterer, and what kind is one practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry? A person practising to abandon the three fetters is one practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry. A person in whom the three fetters have been abandoned – this person is called "a stream-enterer". ^4-41

A person practising to weaken sensual lust and ill will is one practising to realize the fruit of once-returning. A person in whom sensual lust and ill will have been weakened – this person is called "a once-returner". ^4-42

A person practising to abandon sensual lust and ill will without remainder is one practising to realize the fruit of non-returning. A person in whom sensual lust and ill will have been abandoned without remainder – this person is called "a non-returner". ^4-43

A person practising to abandon without remainder lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness and ignorance is one practising to realize the fruit of arahantship. A person in whom lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness and ignorance have been abandoned without remainder – this person is called "a worthy one (arahant)".

#### Description of Persons in Pairs ^4-1-2-0

What kind of person is the angry one? And here, what is anger? Anger, being angry, angriness, hatred, hating, hatefulness [variant: dūsanā dūsitattaṃ (Syā.)], ill will, bearing ill will, being ill-willed, hostility, antagonism, fierceness, abusiveness, displeasure of mind – this is called anger. A person in whom this anger has not been abandoned – this person is called "the angry one". ^4-45

What kind of person is the one who bears grudges? And here, what is a grudge? First there is anger, later there is the grudge. Such a grudge, bearing a grudge, holding a grudge, not letting it go [variant: āṭhapanā (Ka.); Vibha. 891], keeping it, fixing it, joining it on, prolonging it, strengthening anger – this is called a grudge. A person in whom this grudge has not been abandoned – this person is called "the one who bears grudges". ^4-46

What kind of person is the one who belittles others? And here, what is belittling? Belittling, being belittling, the state of belittling [variant: makkhīyanā makkhīyitattaṃ (Sī.), makkhiyanā makkhiyitattaṃ (Ka.)], callousness, callous behaviour – this is called belittling. A person in whom this belittling has not been abandoned – this person is called "the one who belittles others". ^4-47

What kind of person is the one who domineers? And here, what is domineering? Domineering, being domineering, the state of domineering, a domineering manner, a cause for dispute, rivalry, refusing to give way – this is called domineering. A person in whom this domineering has not been abandoned – this person is called "the one who domineers". ^4-48

What kind of person is the envious one? And here, what is envy? Envy over the gains, honour, respect, esteem, homage and veneration that others receive; envy, envying, enviousness, jealousy, being jealous [variant: ussuyā ussuyanā (Ka.); Vibha. 893], jealousness – this is called envy. A person in whom this envy has not been abandoned – this person is called "the envious one". ^4-49

What kind of person is the stingy one? And here, what is stinginess? There are five kinds of stinginess – stinginess about one's dwelling, stinginess about families who support one, stinginess about gains, stinginess about praise, stinginess about the Teaching. Such stinginess, being stingy, a stingy nature, meanness, miserliness, tight-fistedness, a mind that holds back – this is called stinginess. A person in whom this stinginess has not been abandoned – this person is called "the stingy one". ^4-50

What kind of person is the crafty one? And here, what is craftiness? Here someone is crafty, thoroughly crafty. The craftiness there, being crafty, craftiness, scheming, schemingness [variant: kakkhaḷatā kakkhaḷiyaṃ (Syā.); likewise in the exposition of pairs in the Khuddakavibhaṅga], wiliness, guile – this is called craftiness. A person in whom this craftiness has not been abandoned – this person is called "the crafty one". ^4-51

What kind of person is the deceitful one? And here, what is deceit? Here someone, having done wrong by body, having done wrong by speech, having done wrong by mind, forms an evil wish in order to hide it – they wish, "May no one know about me"; they think, "May no one know about me"; they speak with the aim, "May no one know about me"; they act with their body with the aim, "May no one know about me." Such deceit, deceitfulness, cunning, trickery, fraud, evasion, side-stepping, hiding, close hiding, covering, covering up, not making it plain, not bringing it to light, concealment, wrongdoing – this is called deceit. A person in whom this deceit has not been abandoned – this person is called "the deceitful one". ^4-52

What kind of person is the one without conscience? And here, what is lack of conscience? Not feeling shame where shame is due, not feeling shame at engaging in bad, unwholesome states – this is called lack of conscience. A person who has this lack of conscience is "the one without conscience". ^4-53

What kind of person is the one without moral fear? And here, what is lack of moral fear? Not feeling fear where fear is due, not feeling fear of engaging in bad, unwholesome states – this is called lack of moral fear. A person who has this lack of moral fear is "the one without moral fear". ^4-54

What kind of person is the one who is hard to correct? And here, what is being hard to correct? When corrected in accordance with the Teaching: being difficult to speak to, being hard to correct, being unruly, taking a contrary stance, delighting in opposing, disrespect, lack of regard, lack of reverence, lack of deference – this is called being hard to correct. A person who has this quality of being hard to correct is "the one who is hard to correct". ^4-55

What kind of person is the one with bad friends? And here, what is having bad friends? There are persons who are without faith, of bad morality, of little learning, stingy, and lacking in wisdom; associating with them, frequenting them, keeping close company with them, going to them, keeping in with them, devotion to them, attachment to them, leaning towards them – this is called having bad friends. A person who has bad friends in this way is "the one with bad friends". ^4-56

What kind of person is the one who does not guard the doors of the senses? And here, what is not guarding the doors of the senses? Here a certain person, on seeing a form with the eye, grasps at its general appearance and grasps at its details. Though bad, unwholesome states of longing and sadness might flow in upon one who lives with the eye faculty unrestrained, they do not practise to restrain it, they do not guard the eye faculty, they do not undertake the restraint of the eye faculty. On hearing a sound with the ear … on smelling a smell with the nose … on tasting a taste with the tongue … on feeling a touch with the body … on knowing a mental object with the mind, they grasp at its general appearance and grasp at its details. Though bad, unwholesome states of longing and sadness might flow in upon one who lives with the mind faculty unrestrained, they do not practise to restrain it, they do not guard the mind faculty, they do not undertake the restraint of the mind faculty. The not guarding, not protecting, not watching over, not restraining of these six faculties – this is called not guarding the doors of the senses. A person who has this failure to guard the doors of the senses is "the one who does not guard the doors of the senses". ^4-57

What kind of person is the one who does not know moderation in eating? And here, what is not knowing moderation in eating? Here a certain person takes food without reflection, unwisely, for fun, for indulgence, for good looks, for beautification; the lack of contentment, the lack of moderation, the lack of reflection in eating there – this is called not knowing moderation in eating. A person who has this lack of moderation in eating is "the one who does not know moderation in eating". ^4-58

What kind of person is the forgetful one? And here, what is forgetfulness? Lack of mindfulness, lack of recollection, lack of recall, lack of mindfulness, not remembering, not keeping in mind, shallowness, forgetting – this is called forgetfulness. A person who has this forgetfulness is "the forgetful one". ^4-59

What kind of person is the one without clear awareness? And here, what is lack of clear awareness? Not knowing, not seeing, not grasping the truth, not understanding, not awakening, not penetrating, not comprehending, not fathoming [variant: asaṃgāhanā apariyogāhanā (Sī., Syā., Ka.)], not considering impartially, not reviewing, not perceiving directly, dullness, foolishness, lack of clear awareness, delusion, deep delusion, utter delusion, ignorance, the flood of ignorance, the bond of ignorance, the underlying tendency to ignorance, the obsession of ignorance, the barrier of ignorance, delusion, the unwholesome root – this is called lack of clear awareness. A person who has this lack of clear awareness is "the one without clear awareness". ^4-60

What kind of person is the one who has failed in morality? And here, what is failure in morality? Transgression by body, transgression by speech, transgression by body and speech – this is called failure in morality. All lack of morality is failure in morality. A person who has this failure in morality is "the one who has failed in morality". ^4-61

What kind of person is the one who has failed in view? And here, what is failure in view? "There is nothing given, nothing offered, nothing sacrificed; there is no fruit or result of good and bad actions [variant: sukaṭadukkaṭānaṃ (Sī.)]; there is no this world, no other world; there is no mother, no father; there are no spontaneously reborn beings; there are no ascetics or brahmins in the world who have gone rightly [variant: samaggatā (Ka.)] and practise rightly, who, having realized this world and the other world by their own direct knowledge, make them known." Such a view, a resort to views, a thicket of views, a wilderness of views, a distortion of views, a wavering of views, a fetter of views, a grasping, a seizing, an adherence, a clinging, a wrong road, a wrong path, wrongness, a breeding ground for sectarian doctrines, a grasping of what is upside down [variant: vipariyesaggāho (all editions); the correct word-form needs consideration] – this is called failure in view. All wrong view is failure in view. A person who has this failure in view is "the one who has failed in view". ^4-62

What kind of person is the one with inner fetters? A person in whom the five lower fetters have not been abandoned – this person is called "the one with inner fetters". ^4-63

What kind of person is the one with outer fetters? A person in whom the five higher fetters have not been abandoned – this person is called "the one with outer fetters". ^4-64

What kind of person is the one free of anger? And here, what is anger? Anger, being angry, angriness, hatred, hating, hatefulness, ill will, bearing ill will, being ill-willed, hostility, antagonism, fierceness, abusiveness, displeasure of mind – this is called anger. A person in whom this anger has been abandoned – this person is called "the one free of anger". ^4-65

What kind of person is the one who bears no grudges? And here, what is a grudge? First there is anger, later there is the grudge. Such a grudge, bearing a grudge, holding a grudge, not letting it go, keeping it, fixing it, joining it on, prolonging it, strengthening anger – this is called a grudge. A person in whom this grudge has been abandoned – this person is called "the one who bears no grudges". ^4-66

What kind of person is the one who does not belittle others? And here, what is belittling? Belittling, being belittling, the state of belittling, callousness, callous behaviour – this is called belittling. A person in whom this belittling has been abandoned – this person is called "the one who does not belittle others". ^4-67

What kind of person is the one who does not domineer? And here, what is domineering? Domineering, being domineering, the state of domineering, a domineering manner, a cause for dispute, rivalry, refusing to give way – this is called domineering. A person in whom this domineering has been abandoned – this person is called "the one who does not domineer". ^4-68

What kind of person is the one free of envy? And here, what is envy? Envy over the gains, honour, respect, esteem, homage and veneration that others receive; envy, envying, enviousness, jealousy, being jealous, jealousness – this is called envy. A person in whom this envy has been abandoned – this person is called "the one free of envy". ^4-69

What kind of person is the one free of stinginess? And here, what is stinginess? There are five kinds of stinginess – stinginess about one's dwelling, stinginess about families who support one, stinginess about gains, stinginess about praise, stinginess about the Teaching. Such stinginess, being stingy, a stingy nature, meanness, miserliness, tight-fistedness, a mind that holds back – this is called stinginess. A person in whom this stinginess has been abandoned – this person is called "the one free of stinginess". ^4-70

What kind of person is the one who is not crafty? And here, what is craftiness? Here someone is crafty, thoroughly crafty. A person in whom this craftiness has been abandoned – this person is called "the one who is not crafty". ^4-71

What kind of person is the one with conscience? And here, what is conscience? Feeling shame where shame is due, feeling shame at engaging in bad, unwholesome states – this is called conscience. A person who has this conscience is "the one with conscience". ^4-73

What kind of person is the one with moral fear? And here, what is moral fear? Feeling fear where fear is due, feeling fear of engaging in bad, unwholesome states – this is called moral fear. A person who has this moral fear is "the one with moral fear". ^4-74

What kind of person is the one who is easy to correct? And here, what is being easy to correct? When corrected in accordance with the Teaching: being easy to speak to, being easy to correct, being amenable, not taking a contrary stance, not delighting in opposing, respect, regard, reverence, deference – this is called being easy to correct. A person who has this quality of being easy to correct is "the one who is easy to correct". ^4-75

What kind of person is the one with good friends? And here, what is having good friends? There are persons who have faith, are of good morality, of much learning, generous, and wise; associating with them, frequenting them, keeping close company with them, going to them, keeping in with them, devotion to them, attachment to them, leaning towards them – this is called having good friends. A person who has good friends in this way is "the one with good friends". ^4-76

What kind of person is the one who guards the doors of the senses? And here, what is guarding the doors of the senses? Here a certain person, on seeing a form with the eye, does not grasp at its general appearance and does not grasp at its details. Since bad, unwholesome states of longing and sadness might flow in upon one who lives with the eye faculty unrestrained, they practise to restrain it, they guard the eye faculty, they undertake the restraint of the eye faculty. On hearing a sound with the ear … on smelling a smell with the nose … on tasting a taste with the tongue … on feeling a touch with the body … on knowing a mental object with the mind, they do not grasp at its general appearance and do not grasp at its details. The guarding, protecting, watching over, restraining of these six faculties – this is called guarding the doors of the senses. A person who guards the doors of the senses in this way is "the one who guards the doors of the senses". ^4-77

What kind of person is the one who knows moderation in eating? And here, what is knowing moderation in eating? Here a certain person, reflecting wisely, takes food: "Not for fun, not for indulgence, not for good looks, not for beautification, but only to support and sustain this body, to end its discomfort, and to help the holy life, thinking: 'In this way I will put an end to old feelings of hunger and not give rise to new feelings, and I will keep going, blamelessly and at ease.'" The contentment, the moderation, the reflection in eating there – this is called knowing moderation in eating. A person who has this moderation in eating is "the one who knows moderation in eating". ^4-78

What kind of person is the one whose mindfulness is established? And here, what is mindfulness? Mindfulness, recollection, recall, mindfulness, remembering, keeping in mind, not being shallow, not forgetting, mindfulness, the faculty of mindfulness, the power of mindfulness, right mindfulness – this is called mindfulness. A person who has this mindfulness is "the one whose mindfulness is established". ^4-79

What kind of person is the one with clear awareness? And here, what is clear awareness? Wisdom, understanding, investigation, close investigation, investigation of things, discernment, discrimination, close discrimination, learning, skill, subtlety, clarity, reflection, examination, breadth, intelligence, guidance, insight, clear awareness, a goad, wisdom, the faculty of wisdom, the power of wisdom, the sword of wisdom, the tower of wisdom, the light of wisdom, the radiance of wisdom, the lamp of wisdom, the jewel of wisdom, non-delusion, investigation of things, right view – this is called clear awareness. A person who has this clear awareness is "the one with clear awareness". ^4-80

What kind of person is the one accomplished in morality? And here, what is accomplishment in morality? Not transgressing by body, not transgressing by speech, not transgressing by body and speech – this is called accomplishment in morality. All moral restraint is accomplishment in morality. A person who has this accomplishment in morality is "the one accomplished in morality". ^4-81

What kind of person is the one accomplished in view? And here, what is accomplishment in view? "There is what is given, there is what is offered, there is what is sacrificed; there is fruit and result of good and bad actions; there is this world, there is another world; there is mother, there is father; there are spontaneously reborn beings; there are ascetics and brahmins in the world who have gone rightly and practise rightly, who, having realized this world and the other world by their own direct knowledge, make them known." Such wisdom, understanding … non-delusion, investigation of things, right view – this is called accomplishment in view. All right view is accomplishment in view. A person who has this accomplishment in view is "the one accomplished in view". ^4-82

Which two persons are rare in the world? The one who is first to do a kindness, and the one who is grateful and acknowledges a kindness done – these two persons are rare in the world. ^4-83

Which two persons are hard to satisfy? The one who keeps putting away whatever they receive, and the one who keeps giving away whatever they receive – these two persons are called "hard to satisfy". ^4-84

Which two persons are easy to satisfy? The one who does not keep putting away whatever they receive, and the one who does not keep giving away whatever they receive – these two persons are called "easy to satisfy". ^4-85

For which two persons do the taints grow? The one who feels remorse over what is no cause for remorse, and the one who feels no remorse over what is a cause for remorse – for these two persons the taints grow. ^4-86

For which two persons do the taints not grow? The one who feels no remorse over what is no cause for remorse, and the one who feels remorse over what is a cause for remorse – for these two persons the taints do not grow. ^4-87

What kind of person is the one inclined to what is low? Here a certain person is immoral, of bad character, and they associate with, keep company with and attend on another who is immoral, of bad character – this person is called "the one inclined to what is low". ^4-88

What kind of person is the one inclined to what is excellent? Here a certain person is of good morality, of good character, and they associate with, keep company with and attend on another who is of good morality, of good character – this person is called "the one inclined to what is excellent". ^4-89

What kind of person is the one who is satisfied? Solitary Buddhas [variant: paccekabuddho (Sī.)] and those disciples of the Tathāgata (the Buddha) who are worthy ones (arahants) are satisfied. A Fully Awakened Buddha is both satisfied and one who satisfies others [variant: tappetā ca, ayaṃ vuccati puggalo titto (Sī.)].

#### Description of Persons in Threes ^4-1-3-0

What kind of person is the one without hope? Here a certain person is immoral, of bad character, impure, of suspect conduct, secretive in their actions; not an ascetic, though claiming to be one; not living the holy life, though claiming to; rotten inside, oozing with desire, a heap of filth. They hear: "It seems that the monk named so-and-so, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for himself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it." It does not occur to them: "When will I too, through the destruction of the taints, realize for myself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and enter upon it and live in it?" This person is called "the one without hope". ^4-91

What kind of person is the one with hope? Here a certain person is of good morality, of good character. They hear: "It seems that the monk named so-and-so, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for himself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it." It occurs to them: "When will I too, through the destruction of the taints, realize for myself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and enter upon it and live in it?" This person is called "the one with hope". ^4-92

What kind of person is the one whose hope has gone? Here a certain person, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for themselves by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it. They hear: "It seems that the monk named so-and-so, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for himself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it." It does not occur to them: "When will I too, through the destruction of the taints, realize for myself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and enter upon it and live in it?" Why is that? Because the hope for liberation that they had earlier, when not yet liberated, has died down. This person is called "the one whose hope has gone". ^4-93

And here, which are the three persons who are like sick people? There are three kinds of sick people – here a certain sick person, whether or not they get suitable food, whether or not they get suitable medicine, whether or not they get a proper nurse, does not recover from that illness. (1)
And here a certain sick person, whether or not they get suitable food, whether or not they get suitable medicine, whether or not they get a proper nurse, recovers from that illness. (2)
And here a certain sick person recovers from that illness if they get suitable food but not if they do not, if they get suitable medicine but not if they do not, if they get a proper nurse but not if they do not. (3)
Now, it is because of the sick person who recovers from that illness if they get suitable food but not if they do not, if they get suitable medicine but not if they do not, if they get a proper nurse but not if they do not, that the Blessed One has allowed food for the sick, medicine for the sick and nurses for the sick. And it is because of this sick person that other sick people too should be nursed.
In the same way [variant: evameva (Sī.)] three persons who are like sick people are to be found in the world. Which three? Here a certain person, whether or not they get to see the Tathāgata (the Buddha), whether or not they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, does not enter the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states. (1)
And here a certain person, whether or not they get to see the Tathāgata, whether or not they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, enters the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states. (2)
And here a certain person enters the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states if they get to see the Tathāgata but not if they do not, if they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata but not if they do not. (3)
Now, it is because of the person who enters the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states if they get to see the Tathāgata but not if they do not, if they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata but not if they do not, that the Blessed One has allowed the teaching of the Teaching; and it is because of this person that the Teaching should be taught to others too. These are the three persons who are like sick people, to be found in the world. ^4-94

What kind of person is a body-witness? Here a certain person lives having touched the eight liberations with the body, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "a body-witness". ^4-95

What kind of person is one who has attained to view? Here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the origin of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the cessation of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering." The teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and thoroughly examined by them with wisdom, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one who has attained to view". ^4-96

What kind of person is one liberated by faith? Here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering" … The teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and thoroughly examined by them with wisdom, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed, but not in the way they are for one who has attained to view – this person is called "one liberated by faith". ^4-97

What kind of person is the one whose speech is like dung? Here a certain person is a liar. When summoned to a council, or to an assembly, or among their relatives, or before a guild, or before the royal court, and questioned as a witness – "Come now [variant: ehi bho (Syā., Ka.); Ma. Ni. 1.440; A. Ni. 3.28], my good man, tell what you know" – not knowing, they say, "I know," or knowing, they say, "I do not know"; not seeing, they say, "I see," or seeing, they say, "I do not see." So, for their own sake, or for the sake of others, or for the sake of some small material gain, they tell deliberate lies – this person is called "the one whose speech is like dung". ^4-98

What kind of person is the one whose speech is like flowers? Here a certain person, having given up lying, refrains from lying. And how does a person torment themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves? Here a certain person goes naked, rejecting ordinary conventions, licking their hands clean [variant: hatthāvalekhano (Syā.)], not coming when asked, not stopping when asked; they do not accept food brought to them, or food prepared specially for them, or an invitation to a meal. They accept nothing from the mouth of a cooking pot, nothing from the mouth of a bowl [variant: kalopimukhā (Sī., Syā.); Ma. Ni. 2.7], nothing handed across a threshold, nothing handed across a stick, nothing handed across a pestle, nothing from two people eating together, nothing from a pregnant woman, nothing from a woman nursing a child, nothing from a woman who is with a man, nothing from food collected in a time of famine, nothing from where a dog is waiting, nothing from where flies are swarming; they accept no fish, no meat; they drink no liquor, no wine, no fermented drink. They go to just one house for alms and take just one mouthful, or go to two houses and take two mouthfuls … or go to seven houses and take seven mouthfuls; they live on one small helping a day, on two small helpings … on seven small helpings; they take food once a day, once every two days [variant: dvāhikampi (Sī.)] … once every seven days. So they live devoted to the practice of eating at fixed intervals, even up to once every half month. They live on greens, or millet, or wild rice, or hide-parings, or moss, or rice bran, or rice scum, or sesame flour, or grass, or cow dung; they keep themselves going on forest roots and fruits, eating only fallen fruit. They wear clothes of hemp, clothes of mixed hemp, shrouds from corpses, rags from the rubbish heap, tree bark, antelope hide, strips of antelope hide, clothes of kusa grass, clothes of bark fibre, clothes of wood shavings, a blanket of human hair, a blanket of animal hair, owls' wings [variant: ulukapakkhampi (Sī., Syā.)]; they pull out their hair and beard, devoted to the practice of pulling out their hair and beard; they stand all the time, refusing to sit; they squat all the time, devoted to striving in the squatting position; they lie on a bed of thorns, making their bed on thorns; they live devoted to the practice of going down into water to bathe three times a day, the third time in the evening [variant: sāyaṃtatiyakampi (Syā., Ka.); Ma. Ni. 2.7]. So they live devoted to the practice of tormenting and torturing the body in many different ways. This is how a person torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves. ^4-174

And how does a person torment others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others? Here a certain person is a butcher of sheep, a butcher of pigs, a bird-catcher, a deer-trapper, a hunter, a fisherman, a thief, an executioner of thieves, a butcher of cattle, a jailer, or follows any other cruel occupation. This is how a person torments others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others. ^4-175

And how does a person torment themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves, and torment others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others? Here a certain person is a head-anointed noble king [variant: muddhābhisitto (Syā., Ka.)] or a wealthy brahmin. Having had a new sacrificial hall [variant: santāgāraṃ (Syā.), yaññāgāraṃ (Sī.)] built to the east of the city, having shaved off their hair and beard, put on a rough antelope hide [variant: khurājinaṃ (Syā., Ka.)], smeared their body with ghee and oil, and while scratching their back with a deer's horn [variant: kaṇḍūyamāno (Sī.)], they enter the sacrificial hall together with the chief queen and the brahmin high priest. There they lie down on the bare ground strewn with green grass. The king lives on the milk in one teat of a cow whose calf is of the same colour as her; the chief queen lives on the milk in the second teat; the brahmin high priest lives on the milk in the third teat; the milk in the fourth teat is offered into the fire; the calf lives on what is left. They say: "Let so many bulls be slaughtered for the sacrifice, let so many bullocks be slaughtered for the sacrifice, let so many heifers be slaughtered for the sacrifice, let so many goats be slaughtered for the sacrifice, let so many rams be slaughtered for the sacrifice, (let so many horses be slaughtered for the sacrifice) [variant: the words in round brackets are not in the Sinhalese manuscript; the same holds in the Kandaraka Sutta of the Majjhima Nikāya], let so many trees be cut down for sacrificial posts, let so much grass be cut to strew around the sacrifice [variant: parihiṃsatthāyāti (Sī., Syā., Ka.); Ma. Ni. 2.9]." And those who are their slaves, servants or workers, driven by the threat of punishment and by fear, do their tasks weeping, with tears on their faces. This is how a person torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves, and torments others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others. ^4-176

And how does a person neither torment themselves nor devote themselves to the practice of tormenting themselves, nor torment others nor devote themselves to the practice of tormenting others? Tormenting neither themselves nor others, they live here and now free of hunger, at peace, cooled, experiencing happiness, with a self that has become holy.
Here a Tathāgata arises in the world, a worthy one (arahant), fully awakened, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, well-gone, knower of the world, unsurpassed guide of those who can be trained, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed. Having realized it by his own direct knowledge, he makes known this world with its gods, with Māra (the Tempter) and with Brahmā, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, its gods and humans. He teaches the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, with the right meaning and the right wording, and he reveals the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. A householder, or a householder's son, or someone born into some other family, hears that Teaching. Having heard the Teaching, they gain faith in the Tathāgata. Possessing that faith, they reflect: "Household life is cramped, a road full of dust; the life of one gone forth is like the open air. It is not easy, while living at home, to lead the holy life utterly complete, utterly pure, polished like a shell. What if I were to shave off my hair and beard, put on ochre robes, and go forth from home into homelessness!" Then, at a later time, giving up a small or a large fortune, giving up a small or a large circle of relatives, they shave off their hair and beard, put on ochre robes, and go forth from home into homelessness. ^4-177

Having gone forth in this way, and having taken up the training and way of life of the monks, they give up killing living beings and refrain from killing living beings; with the stick and the weapon laid aside, conscientious and kind, they live with compassion for the welfare of all living beings.
Giving up taking what is not given, they refrain from taking what is not given; taking only what is given, expecting only what is given, they live honestly, with a self that has become pure.
Giving up unchaste living, they live the holy life, living apart [variant: anācārī (Ka.)], refraining from sexual intercourse, the way of the village.
Giving up lying, they refrain from lying; they speak the truth, hold to the truth, are trustworthy and reliable, and do not deceive the world.
Giving up divisive speech, they refrain from divisive speech; what they have heard here they do not repeat there in order to divide those people from these, and what they have heard there they do not repeat here in order to divide these people from those. So they reunite those who are divided and support those who are united; they delight in harmony, love harmony, rejoice in harmony, and speak words that create harmony.
Giving up harsh speech, they refrain from harsh speech. They speak such words as are gentle, pleasing to the ear, affectionate, going to the heart, courteous, loved by many and liked by many.
Giving up idle chatter, they refrain from idle chatter; they speak at the right time, speak what is true, speak what is beneficial, speak on the Teaching and the Discipline; at the right time they speak words worth treasuring, well reasoned, well defined and beneficial. ^4-178

They refrain from damaging seeds and plants; they eat only one meal a day, not eating at night, refraining from eating at the wrong time; they refrain from dancing, singing, music and watching shows; they refrain from wearing garlands, using scents and ointments, and adorning and beautifying themselves; they refrain from high and luxurious beds; they refrain from accepting gold and silver.
They refrain from accepting raw grain; they refrain from accepting raw meat; they refrain from accepting women and girls; they refrain from accepting female and male slaves; they refrain from accepting goats and sheep; they refrain from accepting chickens and pigs; they refrain from accepting elephants, cattle, horses and mares; they refrain from accepting fields and land; they refrain from running errands and carrying messages; they refrain from buying and selling; they refrain from cheating with false weights, false metal and false measures; they refrain from bribery, deception, fraud and crooked dealing [variant: … sāviyogā (Syā., Ka.)]; they refrain from wounding, killing, binding, highway robbery, plunder and violence. ^4-179

They are content with robes to protect the body and with alms food to sustain the belly. Wherever they go, they go taking only these with them, just as a bird, wherever it flies, flies with its wings as its only burden. In the same way, a monk is content with robes to protect the body and with alms food to sustain the belly. Wherever they go, they go taking only these with them. Possessing this noble body of morality, they experience within themselves a happiness that is blameless. ^4-180

On seeing a form with the eye, they do not grasp at its general appearance and do not grasp at its details. Since bad, unwholesome states of longing and sadness might flow in upon one who lives with the eye faculty unrestrained, they practise to restrain it, they guard the eye faculty, they undertake the restraint of the eye faculty. On hearing a sound with the ear … on smelling a smell with the nose … on tasting a taste with the tongue … on feeling a touch with the body … on knowing a mental object with the mind, they do not grasp at its general appearance and do not grasp at its details. Since bad, unwholesome states of longing and sadness might flow in upon one who lives with the mind faculty unrestrained, they practise to restrain it, they guard the mind faculty, they undertake the restraint of the mind faculty. Possessing this noble restraint of the senses, they experience within themselves an untainted happiness. ^4-181

In going out and coming back, they act with clear awareness; in looking ahead and looking around, they act with clear awareness; in bending and stretching their limbs, they act with clear awareness; in wearing their outer robe, carrying their bowl and wearing their robes, they act with clear awareness; in eating, drinking, chewing and tasting, they act with clear awareness; in defecating and urinating, they act with clear awareness; in walking, standing, sitting, falling asleep, waking up, speaking and keeping silent, they act with clear awareness.
Possessing this noble body of morality, possessing this noble restraint of the senses, possessing this noble mindfulness and clear awareness, and possessing this noble contentment [see Ma. Ni. 2, Kandaraka Sutta; and A. Ni. 1, Attantapa Sutta], they resort to a secluded dwelling: a forest, the foot of a tree, a mountain, a ravine, a hillside cave, a charnel ground, a jungle thicket, the open air, a heap of straw. After their meal, on returning from their almsround, they sit down, fold their legs crosswise, set their body upright, and establish mindfulness in front of them. Giving up longing for the world, they live with a mind free of longing, and they cleanse their mind of longing; giving up ill will and hatred, they live with a mind free of ill will, with compassion for the welfare of all living beings, and they cleanse their mind of ill will and hatred; giving up dullness and drowsiness [variant: thīnamiddhaṃ (Sī., Syā.)], they live free of dullness and drowsiness, perceiving light, mindful and clearly aware, and they cleanse their mind of dullness and drowsiness; giving up restlessness and remorse, they live without agitation, with a mind calm within, and they cleanse their mind of restlessness and remorse; giving up doubt, they live having gone beyond doubt, without uncertainty about wholesome states, and they cleanse their mind of doubt. ^4-182

Having given up these five hindrances – defilements of the mind that weaken wisdom – quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, they enter and remain in the first meditative absorption (jhāna), which has thinking and examining, and delight and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of thinking and examining, they enter and remain in the second meditative absorption, which has inner confidence and unification of mind, is without thinking and examining, and has delight and happiness born of concentration. With the fading away of delight, they live with equanimity, mindful and clearly aware, and they experience happiness with the body; they enter and remain in the third meditative absorption, of which the noble ones declare: "One with equanimity and mindfulness lives happily." With the giving up of pleasure and pain, and with the earlier disappearance of joy and sadness, they enter and remain in the fourth meditative absorption, which is neither painful nor pleasant and has purity of mindfulness due to equanimity.
When their mind is concentrated in this way – purified, cleansed, unblemished, rid of impurities, pliable, workable, steady and unshakable – they direct it to the knowledge of recollecting past lives. They recollect their many kinds of past lives, that is: one birth, two births, three births, four births, five births, ten births, twenty births, thirty births, forty births, fifty births, a hundred births, a thousand births, a hundred thousand births, many aeons of the world contracting, many aeons of the world expanding, many aeons of the world contracting and expanding: "There I had such a name, such a clan, such an appearance, such food, such experience of pleasure and pain, such an end to my life; passing away from there, I arose elsewhere; there too I had such a name, such a clan, such an appearance, such food, such experience of pleasure and pain, such an end to my life; passing away from there, I was reborn here." So they recollect their many kinds of past lives, with their details and particulars. ^4-183

When their mind is concentrated in this way – purified, cleansed, unblemished, rid of impurities, pliable, workable, steady and unshakable – they direct it to the knowledge of the passing away and rebirth of beings. With the divine eye, which is purified and goes beyond the human, they see beings passing away and being reborn, inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in good destinations and bad destinations, and they understand how beings fare according to their actions: "These beings, who engaged in bad conduct of body, speech and mind, who spoke against the noble ones, held wrong view and took up actions based on wrong view – at the breaking up of the body, after death, they have been reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, in hell. But these beings, who engaged in good conduct of body, speech and mind, who did not speak against the noble ones, held right view and took up actions based on right view – at the breaking up of the body, after death, they have been reborn in a good destination, a heavenly world." So, with the divine eye, which is purified and goes beyond the human, they see beings passing away and being reborn, inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in good destinations and bad destinations, and they understand how beings fare according to their actions. ^4-184

When their mind is concentrated in this way – purified, cleansed, unblemished, rid of impurities, pliable, workable, steady and unshakable – they direct it to the knowledge of the destruction of the taints. They understand as it really is: "This is suffering"; they understand as it really is: "This is the origin of suffering"; they understand as it really is: "This is the cessation of suffering"; they understand as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering"; they understand as it really is: "These are the taints"; they understand as it really is: "This is the origin of the taints"; they understand as it really is: "This is the cessation of the taints"; they understand as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of the taints." As they know and see in this way, their mind is liberated from the taint of sensual desire, liberated from the taint of existence, liberated from the taint of ignorance. When it is liberated, there comes the knowledge: "It is liberated." They understand: "Birth is ended, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is nothing more to come after this." This is how a person neither torments themselves nor devotes themselves to the practice of tormenting themselves, nor torments others nor devotes themselves to the practice of tormenting others. Tormenting neither themselves nor others, they live here and now free of hunger, at peace, cooled, experiencing happiness, with a self that has become holy. ^4-185

And here, which are the five persons who are like warriors? There are five kinds of warriors. Some warriors here are like this. This is the first warrior, to be found in the world.

In the same way, these five persons who are like warriors are to be found among the monks. Which five? #### Description of Persons in Sixes ^4-1-6-0

Among them, the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves, and there attains all-knowing wisdom and mastery over the powers – by this a Fully Awakened Buddha is to be understood.
Among them, the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves, but does not there attain all-knowing wisdom or mastery over the powers – by this a Solitary Buddha is to be understood.
Among them, the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, does not awaken to the truths by themselves, but in this very life makes an end of suffering [variant: dukkhassantaṃ karoti (Sī.), and likewise below] and attains the perfection of a disciple – by this Sāriputta and Moggallāna are to be understood.
Among them, the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, does not awaken to the truths by themselves, and in this very life makes an end of suffering, but does not attain the perfection of a disciple – by this the remaining worthy ones (arahants) are to be understood.
Among them, the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, does not awaken to the truths by themselves, and does not in this very life make an end of suffering, but is a non-returner, one who does not come back to this state of being – by this a non-returner is to be understood.
Among them, the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, does not awaken to the truths by themselves, and does not in this very life make an end of suffering, but is one who comes back to this state of being – by this stream-enterers and once-returners are to be understood.

And how does a person sink once and stay sunk? Here a certain person possesses unwholesome states that are entirely black. This is how a person sinks once and stays sunk.

This is how a person comes up and swims across.
And how does a person come up and reach a foothold? Through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, they are reborn spontaneously, attain final release there, and are not liable to return from that world. This is how a person comes up and reaches a foothold.
And how does a person come up, cross over, reach the far shore, and stand on dry land, a true brahmin? Here a certain person comes up, thinking: "Faith in wholesome states is good, conscience in wholesome states is good, moral fear in wholesome states is good, energy in wholesome states is good, wisdom in wholesome states is good." #### Description of Persons in Nines ^4-1-9-0

What kind of person is a Fully Awakened Buddha? Here a certain person, regarding teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves, and there attains all-knowing wisdom and mastery over the powers – this person is called "a Fully Awakened Buddha".
What kind of person is a Solitary Buddha? Here a certain person, regarding teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves, but does not there attain all-knowing wisdom or mastery over the powers – this person is called "a Solitary Buddha".
What kind of person is one liberated in both ways? Here a certain person lives having touched the eight liberations with the body, and, having seen with wisdom, their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one liberated in both ways".
What kind of person is one liberated by wisdom? Here a certain person does not live having touched the eight liberations with the body, yet, having seen with wisdom, their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one liberated by wisdom".
What kind of person is a body-witness? Here a certain person lives having touched the eight liberations with the body, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "a body-witness".
What kind of person is one who has attained to view? Here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering" … understands as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering." And the teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata (the Buddha) have been clearly seen and thoroughly examined by them with wisdom. And, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one who has attained to view".
What kind of person is one liberated by faith? Here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering" … understands as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering." And the teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and thoroughly examined by them with wisdom. And, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed, but not in the way they are for one who has attained to view – this person is called "one liberated by faith".
What kind of person is a follower of the Teaching? A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry, in whom the faculty of wisdom is predominant, and who develops the noble path carried by wisdom and led by wisdom – this person is called "a follower of the Teaching". A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry is a follower of the Teaching; once established in the fruit, they are one who has attained to view.
What kind of person is a follower by faith? A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry, in whom the faculty of faith is predominant, and who develops the noble path carried by faith and led by faith – this person is called "a follower by faith". A person practising to realize the fruit of stream-entry is a follower by faith; once established in the fruit, they are one liberated by faith.
