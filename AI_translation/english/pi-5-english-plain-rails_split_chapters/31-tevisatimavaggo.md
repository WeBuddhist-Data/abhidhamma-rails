### The Twenty-Third Chapter ^5-31-0

#### (218) 1. Discussion of Shared Intention ^5-31-1-0

May sexual intercourse be engaged in with a shared intention? Yes. With a shared intention, does one become a non-ascetic, become a non-monk, become one whose root is cut, become one who has incurred defeat (an offence entailing expulsion)? That should not be said… May sexual intercourse be engaged in with a shared intention? Yes. With a shared intention, may a living being be killed, what is not given be taken, a falsehood be spoken, divisive words be spoken, harsh words be spoken, idle chatter be engaged in, houses be broken into, plunder be carried off, a single house be robbed, an ambush be laid, another's wife be gone to, a village be plundered, a town be plundered? That should not be said…
The Discussion of Shared Intention is complete. ^5-908

##### (220-4) 3-7. Discussion of Acting at Will through Sovereignty and Other Topics ^5-31-3-1-0

Does the bodhisatta go to the lower world by acting at will through his own sovereignty? Yes. Does the bodhisatta, by acting at will through his own sovereignty, go to hell, go to the Sañjīva hell, go to the Kālasutta hell, go to the Tāpana hell, go to the Mahātāpana hell [variant: patāpanaṃ (Sī., Syā., Kaṃ., Pī.)], go to the Saṅghātaka hell, go to the Roruva hell … go to the Avīci hell? That should not be said…
Does the bodhisatta go to the lower world by acting at will through his own sovereignty? Yes. "The bodhisatta goes to the lower world by acting at will through his own sovereignty" — is there really such a discourse? There is not. If there is no such discourse: "The bodhisatta goes to the lower world by acting at will through his own sovereignty," then surely you must not say: "The bodhisatta goes to the lower world by acting at will through his own sovereignty." ^5-910

Does the bodhisatta descend into a womb by acting at will through his own sovereignty? Yes. Could the bodhisatta, by acting at will through his own sovereignty, be reborn in hell, be reborn in the animal realm? That should not be said…
Does the bodhisatta descend into a womb by acting at will through his own sovereignty? Yes. Does the bodhisatta have psychic power? That should not be said… Does the bodhisatta have psychic power? Yes. Has the bodhisatta cultivated the base of psychic power consisting of desire … the base of psychic power consisting of energy … the base of psychic power consisting of mind … the base of psychic power consisting of investigation? That should not be said…
Does the bodhisatta descend into a womb by acting at will through his own sovereignty? Yes. "The bodhisatta descends into a womb by acting at will through his own sovereignty" — is there really such a discourse? There is not. If there is no such discourse: "The bodhisatta descends into a womb by acting at will through his own sovereignty," then surely you must not say: "The bodhisatta descends into a womb by acting at will through his own sovereignty." ^5-911

Did the bodhisatta practise severe austerities by acting at will through his own sovereignty? Yes. Did the bodhisatta, by acting at will through his own sovereignty, adopt the view "the world is eternal," "the world is not eternal" … "the world is finite" … "the world is infinite" … "the soul is the same as the body" … "the soul is one thing, the body another" … "the Tathāgata (the Buddha) exists after death" … "the Tathāgata does not exist after death" … "the Tathāgata both exists and does not exist after death" … adopt the view "the Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after death"? That should not be said…
Did the bodhisatta practise severe austerities by acting at will through his own sovereignty? Yes. "The bodhisatta practised severe austerities by acting at will through his own sovereignty" — is there really such a discourse? There is not. If there is no such discourse: "The bodhisatta practised severe austerities by acting at will through his own sovereignty," then surely you must not say: "The bodhisatta practised severe austerities by acting at will through his own sovereignty." ^5-912

Did the bodhisatta, by acting at will through his own sovereignty, practise another kind of austerity [variant: amaraṃ tapaṃ (Saṃ. Ni. 1.137)] and acknowledge another teacher? Yes. Did the bodhisatta, by acting at will through his own sovereignty, adopt the view "the world is eternal" … adopt the view "the Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after death"? That should not be said… ^5-913

Did the bodhisatta acknowledge another teacher by acting at will through his own sovereignty? Yes. "The bodhisatta acknowledged another teacher by acting at will through his own sovereignty" — is there really such a discourse? There is not. If there is no such discourse: "The bodhisatta acknowledged another teacher by acting at will through his own sovereignty," then surely you must not say: "The bodhisatta acknowledged another teacher by acting at will through his own sovereignty."
The Discussion of Acting at Will through Sovereignty is complete. ^5-914

#### The Twenty-Third Chapter ^5-31-5-0

##### (226) 9. Discussion of the Not Concretely Produced ^5-31-5-1-0

Is material form not concretely produced? Yes. Is material form not impermanent, not conditioned, not dependently arisen, not subject to destruction, not subject to passing, not subject to fading, not subject to cessation, not subject to change? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that material form is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to passing, subject to fading, subject to cessation, subject to change? Yes. If material form is impermanent, conditioned … subject to change, then surely you must not say: "Material form is not concretely produced."
Is only suffering concretely produced? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One declared that what is impermanent is suffering [Saṃ. Ni. 3.15], and declared: "Material form is impermanent"? Yes. If the Blessed One declared that what is impermanent is suffering, and declared: "Material form is impermanent," then surely you must not say: "Only suffering is concretely produced." … ^5-917

Is feeling … perception … are formations … is consciousness … the eye base … the mind-object base … the eye element … the element of mental objects … the eye faculty … the faculty of one who has fully known not concretely produced? Yes. Is the faculty of one who has fully known not impermanent … not subject to change? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that the faculty of one who has fully known is impermanent, conditioned … subject to change? Yes. If the faculty of one who has fully known is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to passing, subject to fading, subject to cessation, subject to change, then surely you must not say: "The faculty of one who has fully known is not concretely produced."
Is only suffering concretely produced? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One declared that what is impermanent is suffering, and declared: "The faculty of one who has fully known is impermanent"? Yes. If the Blessed One declared that what is impermanent is suffering, and declared: "The faculty of one who has fully known is impermanent," then surely you must not say: "Only suffering is concretely produced."
The Discussion of the Not Concretely Produced is complete.
