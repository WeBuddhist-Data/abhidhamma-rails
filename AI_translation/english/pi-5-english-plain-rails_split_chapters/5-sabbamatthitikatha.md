#### Discussion Clarifying the Terms ^5-5-9-0

Does the past exist? Yes. Is what exists the past? What exists may be the past, or may not be the past.
Acknowledge the refutation. If the past exists, and what exists may be the past or may not be the past, then the past is not the past, and what is not the past is the past. What you say here — that it should be said that "the past exists; what exists may be the past or may not be the past; therefore the past is not the past, and what is not the past is the past" — is wrong.

#### Proof from the Discourses ^5-5-10-0

Should it not be said that "the past exists, the future exists"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, whatever material form there is, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near — this is called the aggregate of material form. Whatever feeling… whatever perception… whatever formations… whatever consciousness there is, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near — this is called the aggregate of consciousness"? [Saṃ. Ni. 3.48] Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the past exists and the future exists.
Does the past exist, does the future exist? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, there are these three pathways of language, pathways of naming, pathways of designation, which are unmixed, have never been mixed, are not being mixed, will not be mixed, and are not rejected by wise ascetics and brahmins. Which three? Monks, whatever material form is past, has ceased, gone, changed — its reckoning is ‘it was’, its label is ‘it was’, its designation is ‘it was’; its reckoning is not ‘it is’, its reckoning is not ‘it will be’. Whatever feeling… whatever perception… whatever formations… whatever consciousness is past, has ceased, gone, changed — its reckoning is ‘it was’, its label is ‘it was’, its designation is ‘it was’; its reckoning is not ‘it is’, its reckoning is not ‘it will be’.
"Monks, whatever material form is unborn, not manifested — its reckoning is ‘it will be’, its label is ‘it will be’, its designation is ‘it will be’; its reckoning is not ‘it is’, its reckoning is not ‘it was’. Whatever feeling… whatever perception… whatever formations… whatever consciousness is unborn, not manifested — its reckoning is ‘it will be’, its label is ‘it will be’, its designation is ‘it will be’; its reckoning is not ‘it is’, its reckoning is not ‘it was’.
"Monks, whatever material form is born, manifested — its reckoning is ‘it is’, its label is ‘it is’, its designation is ‘it is’; its reckoning is not ‘it was’, its reckoning is not ‘it will be’. Whatever feeling… whatever perception… whatever formations… whatever consciousness is born, manifested — its reckoning is ‘it is’, its label is ‘it is’, its designation is ‘it is’; its reckoning is not ‘it was’, its reckoning is not ‘it will be’. These, monks, are the three pathways of language, pathways of naming, pathways of designation, which are unmixed, have never been mixed, are not being mixed, will not be mixed, and are not rejected by wise ascetics and brahmins.
"Monks, even Vassa and Bhañña of Ukkalā, who taught that there is no cause, that actions have no effect, and that good and bad deeds bear no fruit, did not think that these three pathways of language, pathways of naming, pathways of designation should be criticized or rejected. Why is that? Out of fear of blame, attack, and censure"? [Saṃyuttanikāya] Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the past exists, the future exists."
Does the past exist? Yes. But isn't it the case that the venerable Phagguna said this to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, is there that eye by which eye one, describing the past Buddhas who have attained final nibbāna, who have cut through proliferation, cut through the track, ended the cycle, gone beyond all suffering, could describe them? Venerable sir, is there that tongue… venerable sir, is there that mind by which mind one, describing the past Buddhas who have attained final nibbāna, who have cut through proliferation, cut through the track, ended the cycle, gone beyond all suffering, could describe them?"
"Phagguna, there is no eye by which eye one, describing the past Buddhas who have attained final nibbāna, who have cut through proliferation, cut through the track, ended the cycle, gone beyond all suffering, could describe them. Phagguna, there is no tongue… Phagguna, there is no mind by which mind one, describing the past Buddhas who have attained final nibbāna, who have cut through proliferation, cut through the track, ended the cycle, gone beyond all suffering, could describe them"? [Saṃ. Ni. 4.83] Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the past exists."
Does the past exist? Yes. But isn't it the case that the venerable Nandaka said this: "Formerly there was greed; that was unwholesome. Now it does not exist; so this is wholesome. Formerly there was hatred… formerly there was delusion; that was unwholesome. Now it does not exist; so this is wholesome"? [Aṅguttaranikāya] Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the past exists."
Should it not be said that "the future exists"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, if there is lust for the nutriment of material food, if there is delight, if there is craving, consciousness becomes established there and grows. Where consciousness is established and grows, there is the descent of mentality and material form. Where there is the descent of mentality and material form, there is the growth of formations. Where there is the growth of formations, there is the production of renewed existence in the future. Where there is the production of renewed existence in the future, there is birth, aging, and death in the future. Where there is birth, aging, and death in the future, that, monks, I declare to be accompanied by sorrow, by dust [variant: sadaraṃ (Saṃ. Ni. 2.64), which is more fitting], and by despair.
"Monks, if for the nutriment of contact… monks, if for the nutriment of mental volition… monks, if for the nutriment of consciousness there is lust, if there is delight… that I declare to be accompanied by dust and by despair"? [Saṃ. Ni. 2.64] Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the future exists.
Does the future exist? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, if there is no lust for the nutriment of material food, no delight, no craving, consciousness does not become established there and does not grow. Where consciousness is not established and does not grow, there is no descent of mentality and material form. Where there is no descent of mentality and material form, there is no growth of formations. Where there is no growth of formations, there is no production of renewed existence in the future. Where there is no production of renewed existence in the future, there is no birth, aging, and death in the future. Where there is no birth, aging, and death in the future, that, monks, I declare to be without sorrow, without dust, and without despair.
"Monks, if for the nutriment of contact… monks, if for the nutriment of mental volition… monks, if for the nutriment of consciousness there is no lust, no delight… that I declare to be without dust and without despair"? [Saṃ. Ni. 2.64] Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the future exists."
The Discussion of "Everything Exists" is complete. ^5-296
