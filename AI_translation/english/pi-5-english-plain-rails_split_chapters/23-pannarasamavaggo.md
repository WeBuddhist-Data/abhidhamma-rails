##### (146) 2. Discussion of Mutual Conditions ^5-23-2-1-0

Formations are conditioned only by ignorance; should it not be said that "ignorance is also conditioned by formations"? Yes. But isn't it the case that ignorance has arisen together with formations? Yes. If ignorance has arisen together with formations, then surely you must also say: "Formations are conditioned by ignorance, and ignorance is also conditioned by formations."
Clinging is conditioned only by craving; should it not be said that "craving is also conditioned by clinging"? Yes. But isn't it the case that craving has arisen together with clinging? Yes. If craving has arisen together with clinging, then surely you must also say: "Clinging is conditioned by craving, and craving is also conditioned by clinging." ^5-718

"Monks, birth is conditioned by aging and death, and becoming is conditioned by birth" — is there really such a discourse? There is not. In that case, formations are conditioned only by ignorance; it should not be said that "ignorance is also conditioned by formations." Clinging is conditioned only by craving; it should not be said that "craving is also conditioned by clinging."
"Monks, mentality and material form are conditioned by consciousness, and consciousness is also conditioned by mentality and material form" [Dī. Ni. 2.58, though slightly different] — is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, formations are conditioned by ignorance, and ignorance is also conditioned by formations; clinging is conditioned by craving, and craving is also conditioned by clinging.
The Discussion of Mutual Conditions is complete. ^5-719

##### (155) 11. Discussion of the Accumulation of Action ^5-23-11-1-0

Is action one thing and the accumulation of action another? Yes. Is contact one thing and the accumulation of contact another; feeling one thing and the accumulation of feeling another; perception one thing and the accumulation of perception another; volition one thing and the accumulation of volition another; consciousness one thing and the accumulation of consciousness another; faith one thing and the accumulation of faith another; energy one thing and the accumulation of energy another; mindfulness one thing and the accumulation of mindfulness another; concentration one thing and the accumulation of concentration another; wisdom one thing and the accumulation of wisdom another; lust one thing and the accumulation of lust another… fearlessness of wrongdoing one thing and the accumulation of fearlessness of wrongdoing another? That should not be said… ^5-737

Is action one thing and the accumulation of action another? Yes. Does the accumulation of action arise together with the action? That should not be said…
Does the accumulation of action arise together with the action? Yes. Is the accumulation of action that arises together with wholesome action wholesome? That should not be said…
Is the accumulation of action that arises together with wholesome action wholesome? Yes. Is the accumulation of action that arises together with action associated with pleasant feeling associated with pleasant feeling? That should not be said… with painful feeling… is the accumulation of action that arises together with action associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling? That should not be said… ^5-738

Does the accumulation of action arise together with the action? Yes. Is the accumulation of action that arises together with unwholesome action unwholesome? That should not be said…
Is the accumulation of action that arises together with unwholesome action unwholesome? Yes. Is the accumulation of action that arises together with action associated with pleasant feeling associated with pleasant feeling? That should not be said… with painful feeling… is the accumulation of action that arises together with action associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling? That should not be said… ^5-739

Does action arise together with consciousness, and does action have an object? Yes. Does the accumulation of action arise together with consciousness, and does the accumulation of action have an object? That should not be said… does the accumulation of action arise together with consciousness, and does the accumulation of action have no object? Yes. Does action arise together with consciousness, and does action have no object? That should not be said…
Does action arise together with consciousness, and when consciousness breaks up, does action break up? Yes. Does the accumulation of action arise together with consciousness, and when consciousness breaks up, does the accumulation of action break up? That should not be said…
Does the accumulation of action arise together with consciousness, and when consciousness breaks up, does the accumulation of action not break up? Yes. Does action arise together with consciousness, and when consciousness breaks up, does action not break up? That should not be said… ^5-740

Is the accumulation of action in the action? Yes. Is that very same action that accumulation of action? That should not be said…
Is the accumulation of action in the action, and is the result produced from the accumulation of action? Yes. Is that very same action that accumulation of action, and that result of action? That should not be said…
Is the accumulation of action in the action, is the result produced from the accumulation of action, and does the result have an object? Yes. Does the accumulation of action have an object? That should not be said… does the accumulation of action have no object? Yes. Does the result have no object? That should not be said… ^5-741

Is action one thing and the accumulation of action another? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Here, Puṇṇa, someone generates a bodily formation that is both afflictive and unafflictive [variant: sabyāpajjhampi abyāpajjhampi (Ka.); see Ma. Ni. 2.81], generates a verbal formation… a mental formation that is both afflictive and unafflictive. Having generated a bodily formation that is both afflictive and unafflictive, having generated a verbal formation… a mental formation that is both afflictive and unafflictive, they are reborn in a world that is both afflictive and unafflictive. When they have been reborn in a world that is both afflictive and unafflictive, contacts that are both afflictive and unafflictive touch them. Touched by contacts that are both afflictive and unafflictive, they feel a feeling that is both afflictive and unafflictive, a mixture of pleasure and pain, like human beings, some gods and some beings in the lower world. Thus, Puṇṇa, the rebirth of a being comes from what has come to be: by what one does, one is reborn; when one has been reborn, contacts touch one. In this way too, Puṇṇa, I declare: ‘Beings are heirs of their actions’" [Ma. Ni. 2.81]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "action is one thing and the accumulation of action another."
The Discussion of the Accumulation of Action is complete.
