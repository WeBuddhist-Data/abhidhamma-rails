#### (208) 1. Discussion of Final Nibbāna ^5-30-1-0

Is there final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter? Yes. Is there final nibbāna without abandoning some identity view … without abandoning fearlessness of wrongdoing? That should not be said…
Is there final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter? Yes. Is an arahant with lust … with defilements? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that an arahant is free from lust … free from defilements? Yes. If an arahant is free from lust … free from defilements, then surely you must not say: "There is final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter." ^5-892

Should it not be said that "there is final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter"? Yes. Does an arahant know everything within a Buddha's range? That should not be said. In that case, there is final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter.
The Discussion of Final Nibbāna is complete. ^5-893

##### (209) 2. Discussion of Wholesome Consciousness ^5-30-2-1-0

Does an arahant attain final nibbāna with wholesome consciousness? Yes. Does an arahant attain final nibbāna while generating a meritorious formation … while generating an imperturbable formation … while performing action that leads to a destiny … while performing action that leads to existence … while performing action that leads to sovereignty … while performing action that leads to authority … while performing action that leads to great wealth … while performing action that leads to a great following … while performing action that leads to good fortune among the gods … while performing action that leads to good fortune among humans? That should not be said…
Does an arahant attain final nibbāna with wholesome consciousness? Yes. Does an arahant attain final nibbāna while building up, tearing down, abandoning, clinging, scattering, binding, putting out, kindling? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that an arahant neither builds up nor tears down, but is established having torn down? Yes. If an arahant neither builds up nor tears down, but is established having torn down, then surely you must not say: "An arahant attains final nibbāna with wholesome consciousness." But isn't it the case that an arahant neither abandons nor clings, but is established having abandoned; neither scatters nor binds, but is established having scattered; isn't it the case that an arahant neither puts out nor kindles, but is established having put out? Yes. If an arahant neither puts out nor kindles, but is established having put out, then surely you must not say: "An arahant attains final nibbāna with wholesome consciousness." ^5-894

Should it not be said that "an arahant attains final nibbāna with wholesome consciousness"? Yes. But isn't it the case that an arahant attains final nibbāna with mindfulness established, mindful and clearly comprehending? Yes. If an arahant attains final nibbāna with mindfulness established, mindful and clearly comprehending, then surely you must also say: "An arahant attains final nibbāna with wholesome consciousness."
The Discussion of Wholesome Consciousness is complete. ^5-895

##### (210) 3. Discussion of the Imperturbable ^5-30-3-1-0

Does an arahant attain final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable? Yes. But isn't it the case that an arahant attains final nibbāna while established in the ordinary mind? Yes. If an arahant attains final nibbāna while established in the ordinary mind, then surely you must not say: "An arahant attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable."
Does an arahant attain final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable? Yes. Does an arahant attain final nibbāna while established in functional consciousness? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that an arahant attains final nibbāna while established in consciousness that is a result? Yes. If an arahant attains final nibbāna while established in consciousness that is a result, then surely you must not say: "An arahant attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable."
Does an arahant attain final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable? Yes. Does an arahant attain final nibbāna while established in consciousness that is functional and indeterminate? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that an arahant attains final nibbāna while established in consciousness that is a result and indeterminate? Yes. If an arahant attains final nibbāna while established in consciousness that is a result and indeterminate, then surely you must not say: "An arahant attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable."
Does an arahant attain final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One, after emerging from the fourth jhāna, attained final nibbāna immediately afterwards [Dī. Ni. 2.219]? Yes. If the Blessed One, after emerging from the fourth jhāna, attained final nibbāna immediately afterwards, then surely you must not say: "An arahant attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable."
The Discussion of the Imperturbable is complete. ^5-896

##### (211) 4. Discussion of the Breakthrough to the Dhamma ^5-30-4-1-0

Is there a breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb? Yes. For one lying in the womb, is there teaching of the Dhamma, listening to the Dhamma, discussion of the Dhamma, questioning, undertaking of virtue, guarding the doors of the senses, moderation in eating, and devotion to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night? That should not be said… For one lying in the womb, is there no teaching of the Dhamma, no listening to the Dhamma … no devotion to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night? Yes. If, for one lying in the womb, there is no teaching of the Dhamma, no listening to the Dhamma … no devotion to wakefulness in the first and last parts of the night, then surely you must not say: "There is a breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb."
Is there a breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb? Yes. But isn't it the case that there are two conditions for the arising of right view: the voice of another, and wise attention? Yes. If there are two conditions for the arising of right view: the voice of another, and wise attention, then surely you must not say: "There is a breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb."
Is there a breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb? Yes. Is there a breakthrough to the Dhamma for one who is asleep, heedless, unmindful and not clearly comprehending? That should not be said…
The Discussion of the Breakthrough to the Dhamma is complete. ^5-897

##### (215) 8. Discussion of the Indeterminate ^5-30-6-1-0

Is all the consciousness of one who is dreaming indeterminate? Yes. Could one kill a living being in a dream? Yes. If one could kill a living being in a dream, then surely you must not say: "All the consciousness of one who is dreaming is indeterminate."
Could one in a dream take what is not given … speak falsely, speak divisively, speak harshly, chatter idly, break into houses, carry off plunder, rob a single house, lie in ambush, go to another's wife, plunder a village, plunder a town; could one in a dream engage in sexual intercourse; could impure fluid be emitted from one who is dreaming; could one in a dream give a gift, give a robe, give almsfood, give lodging, give medicine and requisites for the sick, give food, give meals, give drink, pay homage to a shrine, offer garlands at a shrine, offer scents, offer ointment … walk around a shrine keeping it on the right? Yes. If one could in a dream walk around a shrine keeping it on the right, then surely you must not say: "All the consciousness of one who is dreaming is indeterminate." ^5-901

Should it not be said that "all the consciousness of one who is dreaming is indeterminate"? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One declared the consciousness of one who is dreaming to be of no account? Yes. If the Blessed One declared the consciousness of one who is dreaming to be of no account, then surely you must also say: "All the consciousness of one who is dreaming is indeterminate."
The Discussion of the Indeterminate is complete. ^5-902

##### (216) 9. Discussion of the Repetition Condition ^5-30-7-1-0

Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, killing living beings, when pursued, developed and cultivated, leads to hell, leads to the animal realm, leads to the realm of ghosts; the lightest result of killing living beings, for one who becomes a human, leads to a short life" [A. Ni. 8.40]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there is a repetition condition.
Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, taking what is not given, when pursued, developed and cultivated, leads to hell, leads to the animal realm, leads to the realm of ghosts; the lightest result of taking what is not given, for one who becomes a human, leads to loss of wealth … the lightest result of sexual misconduct, for one who becomes a human, leads to rivalry and enmity … the lightest result of false speech, for one who becomes a human, leads to being falsely accused … the lightest result of divisive speech, for one who becomes a human, leads to being split from friends … the lightest result of harsh speech, for one who becomes a human, leads to hearing unpleasant sounds … the lightest result of idle chatter, for one who becomes a human, leads to speech that is not accepted; monks, drinking liquor and wine, when pursued … the lightest result of drinking liquor and wine, for one who becomes a human, leads to madness" [A. Ni. 8.40]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there is a repetition condition. ^5-903

Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, wrong view, when pursued, developed and cultivated, leads to hell, leads to the animal realm, leads to the realm of ghosts"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there is a repetition condition.
Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, wrong intention … wrong concentration, when pursued, developed … leads to the realm of ghosts"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there is a repetition condition. ^5-904

Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, right view, when pursued, developed and cultivated, merges into the deathless, has the deathless as its final resort, has the deathless as its culmination"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there is a repetition condition.
Is there no repetition condition at all? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, right intention, when pursued, developed and cultivated … monks, right concentration, when pursued, developed and cultivated, merges into the deathless, has the deathless as its final resort, has the deathless as its culmination"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there is a repetition condition.
The Discussion of the Repetition Condition is complete. ^5-905

##### (217) 10. Discussion of the Momentary ^5-30-8-1-0

Do all phenomena last for a single moment of consciousness? Yes. Does the great earth last for just one moment of consciousness, does the great ocean stay, does Sineru, the king of mountains, stay, does water stay, does fire stay, does air stay, do grass, wood and forest trees last for just one moment of consciousness? That should not be said…
Do all phenomena last for a single moment of consciousness? Yes. Is the eye base co-arising with eye-consciousness? That should not be said… Is the eye base co-arising with eye-consciousness? Yes. But did venerable Sāriputta not say this: "Friends, if the internal eye is intact but external visible forms do not come into its range, and there is no corresponding bringing to mind, then there is no manifestation of the corresponding section of consciousness [variant: viññāṇabhāvassa (many editions)]. Friends, if the internal eye is intact and external visible forms come into its range, but there is no corresponding bringing to mind, then there is no manifestation of the corresponding section of consciousness. But when, friends, the internal eye is intact, external visible forms come into its range, and there is the corresponding bringing to mind, then there is the manifestation of the corresponding section of consciousness" [Ma. Ni. 1.306]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the eye base is co-arising with eye-consciousness."
Is the ear base … the nose base … the tongue base … the body base co-arising with body-consciousness? That should not be said… Is the body base co-arising with body-consciousness? Yes. But did venerable Sāriputta not say this: "Friends, if the internal body is intact but external tangibles do not come into its range, and there is no … Friends, if the internal body is intact and external tangibles come into its range, but there is no … But when, friends, the internal body is intact, external tangibles come into its range, and there is the corresponding bringing to mind, then there is the manifestation of the corresponding section of consciousness" [Ma. Ni. 1.306]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "the body base is co-arising with body-consciousness." ^5-906

Should it not be said that "all phenomena last for a single moment of consciousness"? Yes. Are all phenomena permanent, stable, eternal, not subject to change? That should not be said. In that case, all phenomena last for a single moment of consciousness.
The Discussion of the Momentary is complete.
The Twenty-Second Chapter.
Its summary:
There is final nibbāna without abandoning some fetter; an arahant attains final nibbāna with wholesome consciousness; an arahant attains final nibbāna while established in the imperturbable; there is a breakthrough to the Dhamma for one lying in the womb; there is attainment of arahantship for one lying in the womb; there is a breakthrough to the Dhamma for one who is dreaming; there is attainment of arahantship for one who is dreaming; all the consciousness of one who is dreaming is indeterminate; there is no repetition condition at all; all phenomena last for a single moment of consciousness. ^5-907
