#### (177) 1. Discussion of the Human World ^5-26-1-0

Should it not be said that "the Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world"? Yes. Should it not be said that "the Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world"? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One was born at Lumbinī, became fully awakened at the foot of the tree of awakening, set the wheel of the Dhamma rolling at Bārāṇasī, relinquished the life force at the Cāpāla Shrine, and attained final nibbāna at Kusinārā? Yes. If the Blessed One was born at Lumbinī … and attained final nibbāna at Kusinārā, then surely you must also say: "The Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world."
Should it not be said that "the Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, on one occasion I was staying at Ukkaṭṭhā, in the Subhaga Grove, at the foot of a royal sal tree" [Dī. Ni. 3.11]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the Buddha, the Blessed One, stayed in the human world. ^5-802

Did the Buddha, the Blessed One, stay in the human world? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One was born in the world, grew up in the world, and lives having overcome the world, unstained by the world [A. Ni. 4.36]? Yes. ##### (178) 2. Discussion of the Teaching of the Dhamma ^5-26-2-1-0

Should it not be said that "the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One"? Yes. By whom was it taught? It was taught by a created double. Is the created double the conqueror, the teacher, the Fully Awakened Buddha, the all-knowing, the all-seeing, the master of the Dhamma, the final authority on the Dhamma? That should not be said…
Should it not be said that "the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One"? Yes. By whom was it taught? It was taught by venerable Ānanda. Is venerable Ānanda the conqueror, the teacher, the Fully Awakened Buddha, the all-knowing, the all-seeing, the master of the Dhamma, the final authority on the Dhamma? That should not be said… ^5-804

Should it not be said that "the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Sāriputta, I might teach the Dhamma in brief; Sāriputta, I might teach the Dhamma in detail; Sāriputta, I might teach the Dhamma both in brief and in detail; but those who understand are hard to find" [A. Ni. 3.33, in the Discourse to Sāriputta]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One. ^5-805

Should it not be said that "the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "‘Monks, I teach the Dhamma through direct knowledge, not without direct knowledge; monks, I teach the Dhamma with a sound basis, not without a basis; monks, I teach the Dhamma convincingly, not unconvincingly. Since I [variant: yañcassa (Sī., Pī., Ka.)], monks, teach the Dhamma through direct knowledge and not without direct knowledge, teach the Dhamma with a sound basis and not without a basis, teach the Dhamma convincingly and not unconvincingly, it is fitting that I give instruction, fitting that I give guidance. And indeed, monks, it is enough for you to be satisfied, enough to be pleased, enough to feel joy: the Blessed One is the Fully Awakened Buddha, the Dhamma is well proclaimed, the Community is practising well.’ And while this explanation was being spoken, the ten-thousandfold world system shook" [A. Ni. 3.126]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the Dhamma was taught by the Buddha, the Blessed One.
The Discussion of the Teaching of the Dhamma is complete. ^5-806

##### (179) 3. Discussion of Compassion ^5-26-3-1-0

Is there no compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is there no loving-kindness in the Buddha, the Blessed One? That should not be said… Is there no compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is there no sympathetic joy … no equanimity in the Buddha, the Blessed One? That should not be said…
Is there loving-kindness in the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is there compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One? That should not be said… Is there sympathetic joy … equanimity in the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is there compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One? That should not be said…
Is there no compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is the Blessed One uncompassionate? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that the Blessed One is compassionate, works for the welfare of the world, has sympathy for the world, and acts for the good of the world? Yes. If the Blessed One is compassionate, works for the welfare of the world, has sympathy for the world, and acts for the good of the world, then surely you must not say: "There is no compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One." …
Is there no compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One entered the attainment of great compassion? Yes. If the Blessed One entered the attainment of great compassion, then surely you must not say: "There is no compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One." ^5-807

Is there compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Does the Blessed One have lust? That should not be said. In that case, there is no compassion in the Buddha, the Blessed One.
The Discussion of Compassion is complete. ^5-808

##### (181) 5. Discussion of the Single Path ^5-26-5-1-0

Does one realize the four fruits of the ascetic life by a single noble path? Yes. Do four contacts … four perceptions come together? That should not be said… Does one realize the four fruits of the ascetic life by a single noble path? Yes. By the path of stream-entry? That should not be said… by the path of once-returning … by the path of non-returning? That should not be said…
By which path? By the path of arahantship. Does one abandon identity view, doubt, and clinging to rites and rituals by the path of arahantship? That should not be said… Does one abandon identity view, doubt, and clinging to rites and rituals by the path of arahantship? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One declared the abandoning of the three fetters to be the fruit of stream-entry? Yes. If the Blessed One declared the abandoning of the three fetters to be the fruit of stream-entry, then surely you must not say: "One abandons identity view, doubt, and clinging to rites and rituals by the path of arahantship."
Does one abandon gross sensual lust and gross ill will by the path of arahantship? That should not be said… Does one abandon gross sensual lust and gross ill will by the path of arahantship? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One declared the weakening of sensual lust and ill will to be the fruit of once-returning? Yes. If the Blessed One declared the weakening of sensual lust and ill will to be the fruit of once-returning, then surely you must not say: "One abandons gross sensual lust and gross ill will by the path of arahantship."
Does one abandon subtle sensual lust and subtle ill will by the path of arahantship? That should not be said… Does one abandon subtle sensual lust and subtle ill will by the path of arahantship? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One declared the abandoning of sensual lust and ill will without remainder to be the fruit of non-returning? Yes. If the Blessed One declared the abandoning of sensual lust and ill will without remainder to be the fruit of non-returning, then surely you must not say: "One abandons subtle sensual lust and subtle ill will by the path of arahantship." ^5-810

Should it not be said that "one realizes the four fruits of the ascetic life by a single noble path"? Yes. Did the Blessed One develop the path of stream-entry? Yes. Is the Blessed One a stream-enterer? That should not be said… Did the Blessed One develop the path of once-returning … the path of non-returning? Yes. Is the Blessed One a non-returner? That should not be said… ^5-811

Does the Blessed One realize the four fruits of the ascetic life by a single noble path, while disciples realize the four fruits of the ascetic life by four noble paths? Yes. Do disciples see what the Buddha, the Blessed One, has not seen, reach what he has not reached, and realize what he has not realized? That should not be said…
The Discussion of the Single Path is complete. ^5-812

Does one pass from the first jhāna to the second jhāna? Yes. Is the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the first jhāna the very same as the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the second jhāna? That should not be said…
One passes from the first jhāna to the second jhāna, but should it not be said that "the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the first jhāna is the very same as the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the second jhāna"? Yes. Does the second jhāna arise for one who does not turn the mind … for one who does not aspire? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that the second jhāna arises for one who turns the mind … for one who aspires? Yes. If the second jhāna arises for one who turns the mind … for one who aspires, then surely you must not say: "One passes from the first jhāna to the second jhāna." …
Does one pass from the first jhāna to the second jhāna? Yes. Does the first jhāna arise for one who attends to sensual pleasures as dangerous? Yes. Does the second jhāna arise for one who attends to sensual pleasures as dangerous? That should not be said…
Is the first jhāna accompanied by thinking and by holding the thought? Yes. Is the second jhāna accompanied by thinking and by holding the thought? That should not be said… Does one pass from the first jhāna to the second jhāna? Yes. Is the first jhāna the very same thing as the second jhāna? That should not be said… ^5-813

Does one pass from the second jhāna to the third jhāna? Yes. Is the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the second jhāna the very same as the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the third jhāna? That should not be said…
One passes from the second jhāna to the third jhāna, but should it not be said that "the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the second jhāna is the very same as the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the third jhāna"? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that the third jhāna arises for one who turns the mind … for one who aspires? Yes. If the third jhāna arises for one who turns the mind … for one who aspires, then surely you must not say: "One passes from the second jhāna to the third jhāna."
Does one pass from the second jhāna to the third jhāna? Yes. Does the second jhāna arise for one who attends to thinking and holding the thought as dangerous? Yes. Does the third jhāna arise for one who attends to thinking and holding the thought as dangerous? That should not be said…
Is the second jhāna accompanied by rapture? Yes. Is the third jhāna accompanied by rapture? That should not be said… Does one pass from the second jhāna to the third jhāna? Yes. Is the second jhāna the very same thing as the third jhāna? That should not be said… ^5-814

Does one pass from the third jhāna to the fourth jhāna? Yes. Is the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the third jhāna the very same as the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the fourth jhāna? That should not be said…
One passes from the third jhāna to the fourth jhāna, but should it not be said that "the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the third jhāna is the very same as the turning of the mind … the aspiration for the arising of the fourth jhāna"? Yes. Does the fourth jhāna arise for one who does not turn the mind … for one who does not aspire? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that the fourth jhāna arises for one who turns the mind … for one who aspires? Yes. Is the third jhāna accompanied by happiness? Yes. Is the fourth jhāna accompanied by happiness? That should not be said… Does one pass from the third jhāna to the fourth jhāna? Yes. Is the third jhāna the very same thing as the fourth jhāna? That should not be said… ^5-815

##### (183) 7. Discussion of the Interval between Jhānas ^5-26-7-1-0

Is there an interval between jhānas? Yes. Is there an interval between contacts … an interval between perceptions? That should not be said…

Is there concentration without thinking, with only holding the thought, in between two jhānas as they arise? Yes. Is concentration without thinking, with only holding the thought, not the interval between jhānas? Yes. Is concentration without thinking, with only holding the thought, the interval between jhānas? Yes.
