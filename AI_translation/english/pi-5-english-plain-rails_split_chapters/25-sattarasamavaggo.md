#### (166) 1. Discussion of an Arahant's Accumulation of Merit ^5-25-1-0

Does an arahant have an accumulation of merit? Yes. Does an arahant have an accumulation of demerit? That should not be said… does an arahant have no accumulation of demerit? Yes. Does an arahant have no accumulation of merit? That should not be said… ^5-776

Does an arahant have an accumulation of merit? Yes. Does an arahant generate a meritorious formation, generate an imperturbable formation, perform action that leads to a destination, perform action that leads to existence, perform action that leads to sovereignty, perform action that leads to dominion [variant: ādhipaccasaṃvattanikaṃ (?)], perform action that leads to great wealth, perform action that leads to a great following, perform action that leads to heavenly good fortune, perform action that leads to human good fortune? That should not be said… ^5-777

Does an arahant have an accumulation of merit? Yes. Does an arahant build up? That should not be said… does an arahant tear down? That should not be said… does an arahant abandon… does an arahant cling… does an arahant scatter… does an arahant bind… does an arahant put out… does an arahant kindle? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that an arahant neither builds up nor tears down, but remains established, having torn down? Yes. If an arahant neither builds up nor tears down, but remains established, having torn down, then surely you must not say: "An arahant has an accumulation of merit."
But isn't it the case that an arahant neither abandons nor clings, but remains established, having abandoned; neither scatters nor binds, but remains established, having scattered; neither puts out nor kindles, but remains established, having put out? Yes. If an arahant neither puts out nor kindles, but remains established, having put out, then surely you must not say: "An arahant has an accumulation of merit." ^5-778

Does an arahant have no accumulation of merit? Yes. Would an arahant give a gift? Yes. If an arahant would give a gift, then surely you must not say: "An arahant has no accumulation of merit."
Would an arahant give a robe… give almsfood… give lodging… give medicines and requisites for the sick… give food… give meals… give drink… pay homage to a shrine… place garlands on a shrine… place scents… place ointment… pass around a shrine keeping it on their right [variant: padakkhiṇaṃ (?)]? Yes. If an arahant would pass around a shrine keeping it on their right, then surely you must not say: "An arahant has no accumulation of merit."

##### (167) 2. Discussion of There Being No Untimely Death for an Arahant ^5-25-2-1-0

Is there no untimely death for an arahant? Yes. Is there no one who kills an arahant? That should not be said… is there someone who kills an arahant? Yes. Is there untimely death for an arahant? That should not be said… is there no untimely death for an arahant? Yes. When someone deprives an arahant of life, do they deprive them of life while there is life, while a remainder of life is left, or do they deprive them of life when there is no life, no remainder of life left? They deprive them of life while there is life, while a remainder of life is left. If they deprive them of life while there is life, while a remainder of life is left, then surely you must not say: "There is no untimely death for an arahant." If they deprive them of life when there is no life, no remainder of life left, is there then no one who kills an arahant? That should not be said… ^5-780

Is there no untimely death for an arahant? Yes. Would poison not affect an arahant's body, would a weapon not affect it, would fire not affect it? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that poison would affect an arahant's body, a weapon would affect it, fire would affect it? Yes. If poison would affect an arahant's body, a weapon would affect it, and fire would affect it, then surely you must not say: "There is no untimely death for an arahant."
Would poison not affect an arahant's body, would a weapon not affect it, would fire not affect it? Yes. Is there no one who kills an arahant? That should not be said… ^5-781

Is there untimely death for an arahant? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, I do not declare that intentional actions that have been done and accumulated come to an end without their results being experienced — and that either in this very life, or in the next rebirth, or on some later occasion"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there is no untimely death for an arahant.
The Discussion of There Being No Untimely Death for an Arahant is complete. ^5-782

##### (168) 3. Discussion of All This Being Due to Action ^5-25-3-1-0

Is all this due to action? Yes. Is action too due to action? That should not be said… is all this due to action? Yes. Is all this caused by what was done in the past? That should not be said… is all this due to action? Yes. Is all this due to the result of action? That should not be said… ^5-783

Is all this due to the result of action? Yes. Would one kill a living being through the result of action? Yes. Does killing living beings bear fruit? Yes. Does the result of action bear fruit? That should not be said… does the result of action bear no fruit? Yes. Does killing living beings bear no fruit? That should not be said…
Through the result of action would one take what is not given… speak falsely… speak divisively… speak harshly… chatter idly… break into houses… carry off plunder… rob a single house… lie in ambush on the road… go to another's wife… destroy a village… destroy a town… through the result of action would one give a gift… give a robe… give almsfood… give lodging… give medicines and requisites for the sick? Yes. Do medicines and requisites for the sick bear fruit? Yes. Does the result of action bear fruit? That should not be said… does the result of action bear no fruit? Yes. Do medicines and requisites for the sick bear no fruit? That should not be said… ^5-784

Should it not be said that "all this is due to action"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"The world goes on by action [variant: vattati (Pī., Ka., Ma. Ni. 2.460), vattate (?)], people go on by action [variant: vattati (Pī., Ka., Ma. Ni. 2.460), vattate (?)];
beings are bound by action, like the linchpin of a moving chariot [Ma. Ni. 2.460; Su. Ni. 659].
"By action one gains fame and praise,
by action loss, being killed, and bondage;
knowing this difference made by action,
why would one say there is no action in the world?"
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, "all this is due to action."
The Discussion of All This Being Due to Action is complete. ^5-785

##### (169) 4. Discussion of What Is Bound Up with the Faculties ^5-25-4-1-0

Is only what is bound up with the faculties suffering? Yes. Is only what is bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to passing, subject to fading, subject to cessation, subject to change? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that what is not bound up with the faculties is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to passing, subject to fading, subject to cessation, subject to change? Yes. If what is not bound up with the faculties is impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to passing, subject to fading, subject to cessation, subject to change, then surely you must not say: "Only what is bound up with the faculties is suffering."
Is what is not bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen… subject to change, and yet not suffering? Yes. Is what is bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned… subject to change, and yet not suffering? That should not be said…
Is what is bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned… subject to change, and also suffering [this pair of questions should come before the previous pair]? Yes. Is what is not bound up with the faculties impermanent, conditioned… subject to change, and also suffering [this pair of questions should come before the previous pair]? That should not be said… ^5-786

Is only what is bound up with the faculties suffering? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One declared, "What is impermanent is suffering" [Saṃ. Ni. 3.15], and that "what is not bound up with the faculties is impermanent"? Yes. If the Blessed One declared, "What is impermanent is suffering" [Saṃ. Ni. 3.15], and what is not bound up with the faculties is impermanent, then surely you must not say: "Only what is bound up with the faculties is suffering." ^5-787

Should it not be said that "only what is bound up with the faculties is suffering"? Yes. Just as the holy life is lived under the Blessed One for the full understanding of the suffering bound up with the faculties, is the holy life likewise lived under the Blessed One for the full understanding of the suffering not bound up with the faculties? That should not be said… just as suffering bound up with the faculties, once fully understood, does not arise again, does suffering not bound up with the faculties, once fully understood, likewise not arise again? That should not be said. In that case, only what is bound up with the faculties is suffering.
The Discussion of What Is Bound Up with the Faculties is complete. ^5-788

Is the origin of suffering also suffering? Yes. In what sense? In the sense of impermanence. Is the noble path impermanent? Yes. Is the noble path suffering? That should not be said…
Is the noble path impermanent, and yet not suffering? Yes. Is the origin of suffering impermanent, and yet not suffering? That should not be said… is the origin of suffering impermanent, and also suffering? Yes. Is the noble path impermanent, and also suffering? That should not be said… ^5-789

##### (176) 11. Discussion of the Purification of Offerings ^5-25-11-1-0

Is a gift purified only by the giver, not by the recipient? Yes. But aren't there some recipients who are worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, an unsurpassed field of merit for the world? Yes. If there are some recipients who are worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, an unsurpassed field of merit for the world, then surely you must not say: "A gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient."
Is a gift purified only by the giver, not by the recipient? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One declared the four pairs of persons, the eight individuals, to be worthy of offerings? Yes. If the Blessed One declared the four pairs of persons, the eight individuals, to be worthy of offerings, then surely you must not say: "A gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient."
Is a gift purified only by the giver, not by the recipient? Yes. But aren't there some who, having given a gift to a stream-enterer, make their offering succeed? Yes. If there are some who, having given a gift to a stream-enterer, make their offering succeed, then surely you must not say: "A gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient."
But aren't there some who, having given a gift to a once-returner… to a non-returner… to an arahant, make their offering succeed? Yes. If there are some who, having given a gift to an arahant, make their offering succeed, then surely you must not say: "A gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient." ^5-800

Is a gift purified by the recipient? Yes. Is one person the doer for another — is pleasure and pain made by another, with one person doing and another experiencing it? That should not be said…
Is a gift purified only by the giver, not by the recipient? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Ānanda, there are these four purifications of offerings! Which four? There is, Ānanda, an offering that is purified by the giver, not by the recipient; there is, Ānanda, an offering that is purified by the recipient, not by the giver; there is, Ānanda, an offering that is purified both by the giver and by the recipient; there is, Ānanda, an offering that is purified neither by the giver nor by the recipient. These, Ānanda, are the four purifications of offerings" [Ma. Ni. 3.381]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "a gift is purified only by the giver, not by the recipient."
The Discussion of the Purification of Offerings is complete.
