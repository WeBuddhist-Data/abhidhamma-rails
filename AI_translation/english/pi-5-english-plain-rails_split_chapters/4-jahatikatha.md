Does an ordinary person abandon sensual lust and ill will? Yes. Do they abandon them absolutely, abandon them without remainder, abandon them so that they never return, abandon them together with the root, abandon them together with craving, abandon them together with the underlying tendency, abandon them by noble knowledge, abandon them by the noble path, abandon them while penetrating the unshakeable, abandon them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? Yes. Does an ordinary person abandon sensual lust and ill will, and do they abandon them absolutely, abandon them without remainder… abandon them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? That should not be said…
Does an ordinary person abandon sensual lust and ill will, but not abandon them absolutely, not abandon them without remainder… not abandon them while realizing the fruit of non-returning? Yes. Does an ordinary person abandon sensual lust and ill will? Yes. By which path? By a path of the fine-material sphere. Is a path of the fine-material sphere one that leads out, leads to destruction, leads to awakening, leads to dismantling, is free of taints, not subject to fetters, not subject to ties, not subject to floods, not subject to yokes, not subject to hindrances, not wrongly grasped, not subject to clinging, not subject to defilement? That should not be said. But isn't it the case that a path of the fine-material sphere does not lead out, does not lead to destruction, does not lead to awakening, does not lead to dismantling, is subject to taints, subject to fetters… subject to defilement? Yes. If a path of the fine-material sphere does not lead out, does not lead to destruction… is subject to defilement, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person abandons sensual lust and ill will by a path of the fine-material sphere."
Does the person practising for the realization of the fruit of non-returning abandon sensual lust and ill will by the path of non-returning, and is that path one that leads out, leads to destruction, leads to awakening, leads to dismantling, is free of taints… not subject to defilement? Yes. Does an ordinary person abandon sensual lust and ill will by a path of the fine-material sphere, and is that path one that leads out, leads to destruction, leads to awakening, leads to dismantling, is free of taints… not subject to defilement? That should not be said…

Yes. #### Discussion Citing the Discourses ^5-4-2-0

Does one abandon identity view, doubt, and clinging to rites and rituals by the path of non-returning? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One spoke of the fruit of stream-entry as coming from the abandoning of three fetters? Yes. If the Blessed One spoke of the fruit of stream-entry as coming from the abandoning of three fetters, then surely you must not say: "One abandons identity view, doubt, and clinging to rites and rituals by the path of non-returning." Does one abandon gross sensual lust and gross ill will by the path of non-returning? That should not be said…
Does one abandon gross sensual lust and gross ill will by the path of non-returning? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One spoke of the fruit of once-returning as coming from the weakening of sensual lust and ill will? Yes. If the Blessed One spoke of the fruit of once-returning as coming from the weakening of sensual lust and ill will, then surely you must not say: "One abandons gross sensual lust and gross ill will by the path of non-returning."
Does an ordinary person who is free of lust for sensual pleasures come to stay in the fruit of non-returning together with the breakthrough to the Dhamma? Yes. Do all those who break through to the Dhamma come to stay in the fruit of non-returning together with the breakthrough to the Dhamma? That should not be said…
Should it not be said that "an ordinary person abandons sensual lust and ill will"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"In times past there were [variant: ahiṃsakā (A. Ni. 6.54)] six teachers of renown,
free of the stench of corruption, intent on compassion [variant: karuṇādhimuttā (Sī., Ka.)], gone beyond the fetter of sensual pleasures.
"Having made sensual lust fade away, they went to the Brahma world;
and their disciples too were many hundreds.
"Free of the stench of corruption, intent on compassion, gone beyond the fetter of sensual pleasures,
having made sensual lust fade away, they went to the Brahma world"? [A. Ni. 6.54]
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, an ordinary person abandons sensual lust and ill will.
Does an ordinary person abandon sensual lust and ill will? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, even the teacher named Sunetta, though he lived so long and endured so long, was not freed from birth, aging, and death, from sorrow, lamentation, suffering, displeasure, and despair; he was not freed from suffering, I declare. Why is that? Because of not understanding and not penetrating four things. Which four? Because of not understanding and not penetrating noble moral discipline, noble concentration, noble wisdom, and noble liberation. But now, monks, noble moral discipline has been understood and penetrated, noble concentration has been understood and penetrated, noble wisdom has been understood and penetrated, noble liberation has been understood and penetrated; craving for continued existence has been cut off, the conduit to existence has been destroyed, and now there is no renewed existence.
"Moral discipline, concentration, and wisdom, and unsurpassed liberation:
these things have been understood by Gotama, the renowned one.
"Thus the Buddha, having known them directly, taught the Dhamma to the monks;
the teacher, the one who makes an end of suffering, the one with vision, has attained final nibbāna"? [A. Ni. 7.66]
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "an ordinary person abandons sensual lust and ill will."
The Discussion of Abandoning is complete. ^5-281
