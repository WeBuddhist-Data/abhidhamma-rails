But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, just as the great ocean slopes gradually, inclines gradually, shelves gradually, and does not drop off abruptly; likewise, monks, in this teaching and discipline there is gradual training, gradual work, gradual practice, and no abrupt penetration to final knowledge" [Cūḷava. 385; A. Ni. 8.20; Udā. 45, also in the Udāna]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, breakthrough is gradual.
Should it not be said that "breakthrough is gradual"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Gradually, little by little, moment by moment, a wise person
should blow away their own stains, as a smith does those of silver"? [Dha. Pa. 239, in the Dhammapada]
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, breakthrough is gradual.
Is breakthrough gradual? Yes. But isn't it the case that the venerable elder Gavampati said this to the monks: "Friends, I heard this face to face from the Blessed One, I received it face to face: ‘Monks, whoever sees suffering also sees the origin of suffering, also sees the cessation of suffering, also sees the way leading to the cessation of suffering; whoever sees the origin of suffering also sees suffering, also sees the cessation of suffering, also sees the way leading to the cessation of suffering; whoever sees the cessation of suffering also sees suffering, also sees the origin of suffering, also sees the way leading to the cessation of suffering; whoever sees the way leading to the cessation of suffering also sees suffering, also sees the origin of suffering, also sees the cessation of suffering’" [Saṃ. Ni. 5.1100]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "breakthrough is gradual."
Is breakthrough gradual? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Together with their attainment of vision,
three things are abandoned:
identity view and doubt,
and rites and rituals, whatever there may be;
they are freed from the four states of loss,
and incapable of committing the six grave wrongs"?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "breakthrough is gradual."
Is breakthrough gradual? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, at the time when the dust-free, stainless eye of the teaching arises in a noble disciple — ‘whatever is subject to origin is all subject to cessation’ — together with the arising of vision, monks, three fetters are abandoned in the noble disciple: identity view, doubt, and clinging to rites and rituals"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "breakthrough is gradual."
The Discussion of Gradual Breakthrough is complete. ^5-346

##### (19) 10. Discussion of Speech ^5-10-11-1-0

Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, supramundane? Yes. Does it strike a supramundane ear and not a mundane one; do people understand it with supramundane consciousness and not with mundane consciousness; do disciples understand it and not ordinary people? That should not be said. …
But isn't it the case that the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, strikes a mundane ear? Yes. If the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, strikes a mundane ear, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is supramundane."
But isn't it the case that people understand the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, with mundane consciousness? Yes. If people understand the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, with mundane consciousness, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is supramundane."
But isn't it the case that ordinary people understand the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. If ordinary people understand the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is supramundane." ^5-347

Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, supramundane? Yes. Is it the path, the fruit, nibbāna, the path of stream-entry, the fruit of stream-entry, the path of once-returning, the fruit of once-returning, the path of non-returning, the fruit of non-returning, the path of arahantship, the fruit of arahantship, a foundation of mindfulness, a right effort, a basis of spiritual power, a faculty, a power, an awakening factor? That should not be said. …
Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, supramundane? Yes. Are there any who hear the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is a supramundane state cognizable by the ear, does it strike the ear, does it come within range of the ear? That should not be said. …
But isn't it the case that a supramundane state is not cognizable by the ear, does not strike the ear, does not come within range of the ear? Yes. If a supramundane state is not cognizable by the ear, does not strike the ear, does not come within range of the ear, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is supramundane." ^5-348

Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, supramundane? Yes. Are there any who might feel lust for the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is a supramundane state a basis for lust, enticing, desirable, intoxicating, binding, infatuating? That should not be said. …
But isn't it the case that a supramundane state is not a basis for lust, not enticing, not desirable, not intoxicating, not binding, not infatuating? Yes. If a supramundane state is not a basis for lust, not enticing, not desirable, not intoxicating, not binding, not infatuating, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is supramundane."
Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, supramundane? Yes. Are there any who might feel hatred toward the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is a supramundane state a basis for hatred, a basis for anger, a basis for resentment? That should not be said. …
But isn't it the case that a supramundane state is not a basis for hatred, not a basis for anger, not a basis for resentment? Yes. If a supramundane state is not a basis for hatred, not a basis for anger, not a basis for resentment, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is supramundane."
Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, supramundane? Yes. Are there any who might be deluded by the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One? Yes. Is a supramundane state a basis for delusion, causing lack of knowledge, causing blindness, obstructing wisdom, siding with distress, not leading to nibbāna? That should not be said. …
But isn't it the case that a supramundane state is not a basis for delusion, does not cause lack of knowledge, does not cause blindness, but increases wisdom, sides against distress, and leads to nibbāna? Yes. If a supramundane state is not a basis for delusion, does not cause lack of knowledge, does not cause blindness, but increases wisdom, sides against distress, and leads to nibbāna, then surely you must not say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is supramundane." ^5-349

Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, supramundane? Yes. Do all who hear the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, develop the path? That should not be said. …
Do all who hear the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, develop the path? Yes. Foolish ordinary people hear the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One — do foolish ordinary people develop the path? That should not be said … Does one who has killed their mother develop the path … one who has killed their father … one who has killed an arahant … one who has shed a Buddha's blood … one who splits the Community hears the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One — does one who splits the Community develop the path? That should not be said. … ^5-350

Can one point out both a heap of grain and a heap of gold with a golden stick? Yes. Likewise, the Blessed One, with supramundane speech, speaks of both mundane and supramundane phenomena.
Can one point out both a heap of grain and a heap of gold with a castor-oil stick? Yes. Likewise, the Blessed One, with mundane speech, speaks of both mundane and supramundane phenomena. ^5-351

Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, mundane when he speaks of the mundane, and supramundane when he speaks of the supramundane? Yes. When he speaks of the mundane, does it strike a mundane ear, and when he speaks of the supramundane, does it strike a supramundane ear; when he speaks of the mundane, do people understand it with mundane consciousness, and when he speaks of the supramundane, do they understand it with supramundane consciousness; when he speaks of the mundane, do ordinary people understand it, and when he speaks of the supramundane, do disciples understand it? That should not be said. …
Should it not be said that "the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is mundane when he speaks of the mundane, and supramundane when he speaks of the supramundane"? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One speaks of both mundane and supramundane phenomena? Yes. If the Blessed One speaks of both mundane and supramundane phenomena, then surely you must also say: "The speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, is mundane when he speaks of the mundane, and supramundane when he speaks of the supramundane."
Is the speech of the Buddha, the Blessed One, mundane when he speaks of the mundane, and supramundane when he speaks of the supramundane? Yes. When he speaks of the path, is it the path; when he speaks of what is not the path, is it not the path; when he speaks of the fruit, is it the fruit; when he speaks of what is not the fruit, is it not the fruit; when he speaks of nibbāna, is it nibbāna; when he speaks of what is not nibbāna, is it not nibbāna; when he speaks of the conditioned, is it conditioned; when he speaks of the unconditioned, is it unconditioned; when he speaks of material form, is it material form; when he speaks of what is not material form, is it not material form; when he speaks of feeling, is it feeling; when he speaks of what is not feeling, is it not feeling; when he speaks of perception, is it perception; when he speaks of what is not perception, is it not perception; when he speaks of formations, is it formations; when he speaks of what are not formations, is it not formations; when he speaks of consciousness, is it consciousness; when he speaks of what is not consciousness, is it not consciousness? That should not be said. …

##### (20) 11. Discussion of Cessation ^5-10-12-1-0

Are there two cessations? Yes. Are there two cessations of suffering? That should not be said … Are there two cessations of suffering? Yes. Are there two truths of cessation? That should not be said … Are there two truths of cessation? Yes. Are there two truths of suffering? That should not be said … Are there two truths of cessation? Yes. Are there two truths of origin? That should not be said … Are there two truths of cessation? Yes. Are there two truths of the path? That should not be said. …
Are there two truths of cessation? Yes. Are there two protections … two shelters … two refuges … two resorts … two imperishables … two deathless states … two nibbānas? That should not be said. …
Are there two nibbānas? Yes. Is there between the two nibbānas any higher and lower, inferior and superior, better and worse, or any boundary, break, dividing line or interval? That should not be said. …
Are there two cessations? Yes. But isn't it the case that people, through reflection, bring to an end formations that have ended without reflection? Yes. If people, through reflection, bring to an end formations that have ended without reflection, then surely you must not say: "There are two cessations."
Should it not be said that "there are two cessations"? Yes. But isn't it the case that formations ended without reflection are utterly broken up, and formations ended through reflection are also utterly broken up? Yes. If formations ended without reflection are utterly broken up, and formations ended through reflection are also utterly broken up, then surely you must also say: "There are two cessations."
Are there two cessations? Yes. Have formations ended through reflection [variant: paṭisaṅkhāniruddhā (?)] ended by means of the noble path? Yes. Have formations ended without reflection ended by means of the noble path? That should not be said. …
Are there two cessations? Yes. Do formations ended through reflection not arise again? Yes. Do formations ended without reflection not arise again? That should not be said … In that case it should not be said that "there are two cessations."
