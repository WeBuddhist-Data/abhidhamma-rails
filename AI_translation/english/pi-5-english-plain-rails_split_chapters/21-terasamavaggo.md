#### (126) 1. Discussion of One Who Lasts for an Eon ^5-21-1-0

Would one who lasts for an eon last for the eon? Yes. Does the eon stay in being and does a Buddha arise in the world? That should not be said… Would one who lasts for an eon last for the eon? Yes. Does the eon stay in being and does the Community break up? That should not be said… would one who lasts for an eon last for the eon? Yes. Does the eon stay in being and does one who lasts for an eon perform an action that makes one last for an eon? That should not be said… would one who lasts for an eon last for the eon? Yes. Does the eon stay in being and does a person who lasts for an eon die? That should not be said… ^5-654

Would one who lasts for an eon last for the eon? Yes. Would they last for a past eon, would they last for a future eon? That should not be said… would one who lasts for an eon last for the eon? Yes. Would they last for two eons… last for three eons… last for four eons? That should not be said… ^5-655

Would one who lasts for an eon last for the eon? Yes. Where does one who lasts for an eon go when the eon is burning? They go to another world-system. Do they go when dead, or do they go through the air? They go when dead. Does the action that makes one last for an eon ripen on some later occasion? That should not be said… [‘do they go through the air?’ (?): for this is just the other school's assertion, as in the ninth line of Kathā. 621–623] do they go through the air? Yes [‘do they go through the air?’ (?): for this is just the other school's assertion, as in the ninth line of Kathā. 621–623]. Does one who lasts for an eon have psychic power? That should not be said… does one who lasts for an eon have psychic power? Yes. Has one who lasts for an eon cultivated the base of psychic power consisting in desire, cultivated the base of psychic power consisting in energy, cultivated the base of psychic power consisting in mind, cultivated the base of psychic power consisting in investigation? That should not be said… ^5-656

Should it not be said that "one who lasts for an eon would last for the eon"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Bound for the lower realms, destined for hell, lasting for an eon is the one who splits the Community;
delighting in faction, standing in what is contrary to the teaching, they fall away from security from bondage;
having split [variant: bhinditvā (Sī., Ka.)] a Community in harmony, they are tormented in hell for an eon" [Cūḷava. 354; A. Ni. 10.39; Itivu. 18]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, one who lasts for an eon would last for the eon.
The Discussion of One Who Lasts for an Eon is complete. ^5-657

##### (127) 2. Discussion of Obtaining a Wholesome Mind ^5-21-2-1-0

Would one who lasts for an eon not obtain a wholesome mind? Yes. Would one who lasts for an eon give a gift? Yes. If one who lasts for an eon would give a gift, then surely you must not say: "One who lasts for an eon would not obtain a wholesome mind."
Would one who lasts for an eon not obtain a wholesome mind? Yes. Would one who lasts for an eon give a robe… give almsfood… give lodging… give medicines and requisites for the sick… give food… give meals… give drink… pay homage to a shrine… place garlands on a shrine… place scents… place ointment… pass around a shrine keeping it on their right [variant: padakkhiṇaṃ (Pī.)]? Yes. If one who lasts for an eon would pass around a shrine keeping it on their right, then surely you must not say: "One who lasts for an eon would not obtain a wholesome mind."… ^5-658

Would one who lasts for an eon obtain a wholesome mind? Yes. Would they obtain a wholesome mind of emergence from that state? Yes. Would they obtain a wholesome mind of the fine-material sphere… of the formless sphere… a wholesome and supramundane mind? That should not be said…
The Discussion of Obtaining a Wholesome Mind is complete. ^5-659

##### (128) 3. Discussion of One Engaged in a Deed with Immediate Result ^5-21-3-1-0

Would a person engaged in a deed with immediate result enter the fixed course of rightness? Yes. Would they enter both the fixed course of wrongness and the fixed course of rightness? That should not be said… would a person engaged in a deed with immediate result enter the fixed course of rightness? Yes. But isn't it the case that that action has been undertaken, remorse has been aroused, and regret has been produced? Yes. If that action has been undertaken, remorse has been aroused, and regret has been produced, then surely you must not say: "A person engaged in a deed with immediate result would enter the fixed course of rightness." ^5-660

Is a person engaged in a deed with immediate result incapable of entering the fixed course of rightness? Yes. Have they deprived their mother of life… deprived their father of life… deprived an arahant of life… with a corrupt mind shed the blood of a Tathāgata (the Buddha)… split the Community? That should not be said…
Is a person engaged in a deed with immediate result, having withdrawn from that action, dispelled remorse, and removed regret [variant: paṭivinodetvā (Sī., Pī., Ka.)], incapable of entering the fixed course of rightness? Yes. Have they deprived their mother of life… deprived their father of life… split the Community? That should not be said…
Is a person engaged in a deed with immediate result, having withdrawn from that action, dispelled remorse, and removed regret, incapable of entering the fixed course of rightness? Yes. But isn't it the case that that action has been withdrawn from, remorse has been dispelled, and regret has been removed? Yes. If that action has been withdrawn from, remorse has been dispelled, and regret has been removed, then surely you must not say: "A person engaged in a deed with immediate result, having withdrawn from that action, dispelled remorse, and removed regret, is incapable of entering the fixed course of rightness." ^5-661

Would a person engaged in a deed with immediate result enter the fixed course of rightness? Yes. But isn't it the case that they were engaged in that action? Yes. If they were engaged in that action, then surely you must not say: "A person engaged in a deed with immediate result would enter the fixed course of rightness."
The Discussion of One Engaged in a Deed with Immediate Result is complete. ^5-662

##### (129) 4. Discussion of the Fixed Entering the Fixed Course ^5-21-4-1-0

Does one who is fixed enter the fixed course? Yes. Does one fixed in wrongness enter the fixed course of rightness, and does one fixed in rightness enter the fixed course of wrongness? That should not be said…
Does one who is fixed enter the fixed course? Yes. Having formerly developed the path, do they afterwards enter the fixed course? That should not be said… having formerly developed the path of stream-entry, do they afterwards enter the fixed course of stream-entry? That should not be said… having formerly developed the path of once-returning… of non-returning… the path of arahantship, do they afterwards enter the fixed course of arahantship? That should not be said…
Having formerly developed a foundation of mindfulness… a right exertion… a base of psychic power… a faculty… a power… an awakening factor, do they afterwards enter the fixed course? That should not be said… ^5-663

Should it not be said that "one who is fixed enters the fixed course"? Yes. Is a bodhisatta (a Buddha-to-be) capable of not breaking through to the Dhamma in that birth? That should not be said. In that case, one who is fixed enters the fixed course.
The Discussion of the Fixed Entering the Fixed Course is complete. ^5-664

##### (130) 5. Discussion of the Hindered ^5-21-5-1-0

Does one who is hindered abandon a hindrance? Yes. Does one who is lustful abandon lust, does one who is full of hatred abandon hatred, does one who is deluded abandon delusion, does one who is defiled abandon defilements? That should not be said… does one abandon lust by lust, abandon hatred by hatred, abandon delusion by delusion, abandon defilements by defilements? That should not be said…
Is lust associated with consciousness, and is the path associated with consciousness? Yes. Is there a coming together of two contacts… of two consciousnesses? That should not be said… is lust unwholesome, and is the path wholesome? Yes. Do wholesome and unwholesome, blameworthy and blameless, inferior and superior, dark and bright phenomena that are counterparts come face to face? That should not be said… ^5-665

Do wholesome and unwholesome, blameworthy and blameless, inferior and superior, dark and bright phenomena that are counterparts come face to face? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, these four things are very far apart from each other! Which four? The sky, monks, and the earth — this is the first pair very far apart… therefore the teaching of the good is far from the bad"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "wholesome and unwholesome… phenomena come face to face."
Does one who is hindered abandon a hindrance? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "With their consciousness thus concentrated, purified, cleansed, without blemish, free from impurities, pliable, workable, steady and imperturbable, they direct their consciousness to the knowledge of the destruction of the taints"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "one who is hindered abandons a hindrance."… ^5-666

Should it not be said that "one who is hindered abandons a hindrance"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "For that person, knowing thus and seeing thus, the mind becomes liberated from the taint of sensual desire… the mind becomes liberated from the taint of ignorance"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, one who is hindered abandons a hindrance.
The Discussion of the Hindered is complete. ^5-667

Does one in meditative attainment relish it, and does longing for jhāna have jhāna as its object? Yes. Is that jhāna the object of that same jhāna? ##### (133) 8. Discussion of Lust for the Unpleasant ^5-21-8-1-0

Is there lust for the unpleasant? Yes. Do beings delight in suffering; are there some who wish for, yearn for, seek, search for and look for suffering, and remain clinging to suffering? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that beings delight in happiness, that there are some who wish for, yearn for, seek, search for and look for happiness, and remain clinging to happiness? Yes. If beings delight in happiness, and there are some who wish for, yearn for, seek, search for and look for happiness, and remain clinging to happiness, then surely you must not say: "There is lust for the unpleasant."
Is there lust for the unpleasant? Yes. Does the tendency to lust lie latent in painful feeling, and does the tendency to aversion lie latent in pleasant feeling? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that the tendency to lust lies latent in pleasant feeling, and the tendency to aversion lies latent in painful feeling? Yes. If the tendency to lust lies latent in pleasant feeling, and the tendency to aversion lies latent in painful feeling, then surely you must not say: "There is lust for the unpleasant." ^5-674

Should it not be said that "there is lust for the unpleasant"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Caught up thus in favouring and opposing, whatever feeling they feel — pleasant, painful, or neither-painful-nor-pleasant — they delight in that feeling, welcome it, and remain clinging to it" [Ma. Ni. 3.409, in the Mahātaṇhāsaṅkhaya]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there is lust for the unpleasant.
The Discussion of Lust for the Unpleasant is complete. ^5-675

##### (134) 9. Discussion of Craving for Mental Objects as Indeterminate ^5-21-9-1-0

Is craving for mental objects indeterminate? Yes. Is it resultant indeterminate, functional indeterminate, material form, nibbāna, the eye base… the tangible base? That should not be said…

Should it not be said that "craving for mental objects is indeterminate"? Yes. But isn't it the case that it is craving for mental objects? Yes. If it is craving for mental objects, then surely you must also say: "Craving for mental objects is indeterminate."

One who lasts for an eon would last for the eon; one who lasts for an eon would not obtain a wholesome mind; a person engaged in a deed with immediate result would enter the fixed course of rightness; one who is fixed enters the fixed course; one who is hindered abandons a hindrance; one who is face to face abandons a fetter; longing for jhāna; lust for the unpleasant; craving for mental objects is indeterminate; craving for mental objects is not the origin of suffering. ^5-685
