#### (194) 1. Discussion of the Unintentional ^5-28-1-0

Does one who unintentionally deprives their mother of life commit a deed with immediate result? Yes. Does one who unintentionally kills a living being become a killer of living beings? That should not be said… Does one who unintentionally deprives their mother of life commit a deed with immediate result? Yes. Does one who unintentionally takes what is not given … who unintentionally speaks falsely become a liar? That should not be said…
Does one who unintentionally kills a living being not become a killer of living beings? Yes. Does one who unintentionally deprives their mother of life not commit a deed with immediate result? That should not be said… Does one who unintentionally takes what is not given … who unintentionally speaks falsely not become a liar? Yes. Does one who unintentionally deprives their mother of life not commit a deed with immediate result? That should not be said… ^5-857

Does one who unintentionally deprives their mother of life commit a deed with immediate result? Yes. "One who unintentionally deprives their mother of life commits a deed with immediate result" — is there really such a discourse? There is not. "One who intentionally deprives their mother of life commits a deed with immediate result" — is there really such a discourse? Yes. If there really is such a discourse: "One who intentionally deprives their mother of life commits a deed with immediate result," then surely you must not say: "One who unintentionally deprives their mother of life commits a deed with immediate result." ^5-858

Should it not be said that "one who kills their mother has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But isn't it the case that the mother was deprived of life? Yes. If the mother was deprived of life, then surely you must also say: "One who kills their mother has committed a deed with immediate result."
Should it not be said that "one who kills their father has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But isn't it the case that the father was deprived of life? Yes. If the father was deprived of life, then surely you must also say: "One who kills their father has committed a deed with immediate result."
Should it not be said that "one who kills an arahant has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But isn't it the case that the arahant was deprived of life? Yes. If the arahant was deprived of life, then surely you must also say: "One who kills an arahant has committed a deed with immediate result."
Should it not be said that "one who sheds a Buddha's blood has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Tathāgata's blood was shed? Yes. If the Tathāgata's blood was shed, then surely you must also say: "One who sheds a Buddha's blood has committed a deed with immediate result." ^5-859

Has one who splits the Community committed a deed with immediate result? Yes. Have all who split the Community committed a deed with immediate result? That should not be said… Have all who split the Community committed a deed with immediate result? Yes. Has one who splits the Community while believing that this accords with the Dhamma committed a deed with immediate result? That should not be said… ^5-860

Has one who splits the Community while believing that this accords with the Dhamma committed a deed with immediate result? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Upāli, there is one who splits the Community who is bound for the lower realms, bound for hell, stays there for an eon, and is incurable; Upāli, there is one who splits the Community who is not bound for the lower realms, not bound for hell, does not stay there for an eon, and is not incurable"? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case it should not be said that "one who splits the Community while believing that this accords with the Dhamma has committed a deed with immediate result." ^5-861

Should it not be said that "one who splits the Community while believing that this accords with the Dhamma has committed a deed with immediate result"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"One who splits the Community is bound for the lower realms, bound for hell, staying there for an eon;
delighting in factions, standing in what is not Dhamma, they fall away from safety from bondage;
having split a Community in harmony, they are tormented in hell for an eon" [Cūḷava. 354; A. Ni. 10.39; Itivu. 18]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, one who splits the Community has committed a deed with immediate result.
The Discussion of the Unintentional is complete. ^5-862

##### (195) 2. Discussion of Knowledge ^5-28-2-1-0

Does an ordinary person have no knowledge? Yes. Does an ordinary person have no wisdom, understanding, investigation, close investigation, investigation of phenomena, discernment, discrimination, careful discrimination? That should not be said… But isn't it the case that an ordinary person has wisdom, understanding, investigation … careful discrimination? Yes. If an ordinary person has wisdom, understanding, investigation … careful discrimination, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person has no knowledge." ^5-863

Does an ordinary person have no knowledge? Yes. Could an ordinary person enter the first jhāna? Yes. If an ordinary person could enter the first jhāna, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person has no knowledge."
Could an ordinary person enter the second jhāna … the third jhāna … the fourth jhāna … the base of infinite space, could they enter the base of infinite consciousness, the base of nothingness, the base of neither perception nor non-perception; could an ordinary person give a gift … give a robe, give almsfood, give lodging, give medicine and requisites for the sick? Yes. If an ordinary person could give medicine and requisites for the sick, then surely you must not say: "An ordinary person has no knowledge." ^5-864

Does an ordinary person have knowledge? Yes. With that knowledge, does an ordinary person fully understand suffering, abandon its origin, realize its cessation, and cultivate the path? That should not be said…
The Discussion of Knowledge is complete. ^5-865

##### (196) 3. Discussion of Hell Wardens ^5-28-3-1-0

Are there no hell wardens in the hells? Yes. Are there no punishments in the hells? That should not be said… Are there punishments in the hells? Yes. Are there hell wardens in the hells? That should not be said…
Among humans, are there punishments, and are there also punishers? Yes. In the hells, are there punishments, and are there also punishers? That should not be said… In the hells, are there punishments, but no punishers? Yes. Among humans, are there punishments, but no punishers? That should not be said… ^5-866

Are there hell wardens in the hells? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Not Vessabhū, nor the king of the departed,
nor Soma, Yama, or King Vessavaṇa;
it is their own actions that strike them there, those driven from here [variant: paṇuṇṇaṃ (Syā., Ka.), panunnaṃ (Sī.)] who have reached the next world"?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there are no hell wardens in the hells. ^5-867

Are there no hell wardens in the hells? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, the hell wardens make them undergo the punishment called the fivefold binding [variant: karonti (Ma. Ni. 3.250; A. Ni. 3.36)]: they drive a red-hot iron stake through one hand, they drive a red-hot iron stake through the other hand, they drive a red-hot iron stake through one foot, they drive a red-hot iron stake through the other foot, they drive a red-hot iron stake through the middle of the chest; there they feel painful, sharp, piercing feelings; and they do not die as long as that evil action has not been used up" [Ma. Ni. 3.250; A. Ni. 3.36]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there are hell wardens in the hells.
Are there no hell wardens in the hells? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Monks, the hell wardens lay them down and pare them with axes [variant: kudhārīhi (all editions)] … Monks, the hell wardens hold them feet up and head down and pare them with adzes … Monks, the hell wardens harness them to a chariot and drive them back and forth over ground that is burning, blazing and glowing [variant: sañjotibhūtāya (Syā.)] … Monks, the hell wardens make them climb up and down a great mountain of embers that is burning, blazing and glowing … Monks, the hell wardens take them feet up and head down and throw them into a red-hot metal cauldron that is burning, blazing and glowing. There they are tormented in a swirl of foam; being tormented there in a swirl of foam, now they go up, now they go down, now they go across. There they feel painful, sharp, piercing feelings, and they do not die as long as that evil action has not been used up. Monks, the hell wardens throw them into the Great Hell! And that Great Hell, monks —
‘has four corners and four gates, and is divided into equal parts;
it is surrounded by an iron wall, and roofed over with iron.
‘Its earth is made of iron, blazing and joined with fire;
it spreads a hundred leagues all around and stays so at all times’" [Ma. Ni. 3.250; A. Ni. 3.36; Pe. Va. 71]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, there are hell wardens in the hells.
The Discussion of Hell Wardens is complete. ^5-868

##### (197) 4. Discussion of Animals ^5-28-4-1-0

Are there animals among the gods? Yes. Are there gods among the animals? That should not be said… Are there animals among the gods? Yes. Is the world of the gods the animal realm? That should not be said… Are there animals among the gods? Yes. Are there insects, moths, mosquitoes, flies, snakes, scorpions, centipedes and earthworms there [variant: gaṇḍupādāti (Syā., Kaṃ., Pī.)]? That should not be said… ^5-869

Are there no animals among the gods? Yes. But isn't it the case that there is an elephant there named Erāvaṇa, and a heavenly vehicle drawn by a thousand? Yes. If there is an elephant there named Erāvaṇa, and a heavenly vehicle drawn by a thousand, then surely you must also say: "There are animals among the gods." ^5-870

Are there animals among the gods? Yes. Are there elephant keepers, horse keepers [variant: hatthibhaṇḍā assabhaṇḍā (?)], fodder-gatherers, trainers and cooks there? That should not be said. In that case, there are no animals among the gods.
The Discussion of Animals is complete. ^5-871

##### (198) 5. Discussion of the Path ^5-28-5-1-0

Is the path fivefold? Yes. But isn't it the case that the Blessed One declared the eightfold path, namely right view … right concentration? Yes. If the Blessed One declared the eightfold path, namely right view … right concentration, then surely you must not say: "The path is fivefold."
Is the path fivefold? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say:
"Of paths, the eightfold is the best; of truths, the four statements;
of phenomena, freedom from passion is the best; and of two-footed beings, the One with Vision" [Dha. Pa. 273, in the Dhammapada]?
Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the path is eightfold. ^5-872

Is right speech a factor of the path, but not itself the path? Yes. Is right view a factor of the path, but not itself the path? That should not be said… Is right speech a factor of the path, but not itself the path? Yes. Is right intention … right effort … right mindfulness … right concentration a factor of the path, but not itself the path? That should not be said…
Is right action … right livelihood a factor of the path, but not itself the path? Yes. Is right view … right concentration a factor of the path, but not itself the path? That should not be said…
Is right view a factor of the path, and also the path itself? Yes. Is right speech a factor of the path, and also the path itself? That should not be said… Is right view a factor of the path, and also the path itself? Yes. Is right action … right livelihood a factor of the path, and also the path itself? That should not be said…
Is right intention … right effort … right mindfulness … right concentration a factor of the path, and also the path itself? Yes. Is right speech … right action … right livelihood a factor of the path, and also the path itself? That should not be said… ^5-873

Is the noble path eightfold? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Their bodily action, verbal action and livelihood have already been well purified beforehand; in this way this noble eightfold path of theirs comes to the fulfilment of its development" [Ma. Ni. 3.431]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the path is fivefold. ^5-874

Is the path fivefold? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "Subhadda, in whatever Dhamma and discipline the noble eightfold path is not found, no ascetic is found there, no second ascetic is found there, no third ascetic is found there, no fourth ascetic is found there. And in whatever Dhamma and discipline the noble eightfold path is found, an ascetic is found there, a second … a third … a fourth ascetic is found there. In this Dhamma and discipline, Subhadda, the noble eightfold path is found. Here alone, Subhadda, is there an ascetic, here a second ascetic, here a third ascetic, here a fourth ascetic. The doctrines of others are empty of ascetics" [Dī. Ni. 2.214]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, the path is eightfold.
The Discussion of the Path is complete. ^5-875

##### (199) 6. Discussion of Knowledge ^5-28-6-1-0

Is knowledge with twelve aspects supramundane? Yes. Are there twelve kinds of supramundane knowledge? That should not be said… Are there twelve kinds of supramundane knowledge? Yes. Are there twelve paths of stream-entry? That should not be said… Are there twelve paths of stream-entry? Yes. Are there twelve fruits of stream-entry? That should not be said… Are there twelve paths of once-returning … paths of non-returning … paths of arahantship? That should not be said… Are there twelve paths of arahantship? Yes. Are there twelve fruits of arahantship? That should not be said… ^5-876

Should it not be said that "knowledge with twelve aspects is supramundane"? Yes. But did the Blessed One not say: "‘This is the noble truth of suffering’: in me, monks, regarding things not heard before, there arose vision, there arose knowledge, there arose wisdom, there arose true knowing, there arose light. ‘This noble truth of suffering is to be fully understood’: in me, monks … ‘It has been fully understood’: in me, monks … ‘This is the noble truth of the origin of suffering [variant: dukkhasamudayo (Syā., Kaṃ., Pī.)]’: in me, monks … ‘This noble truth of the origin of suffering is to be abandoned’: in me, monks … ‘It has been abandoned’: in me, monks … ‘This is the noble truth of the cessation of suffering [variant: dukkhanirodhā (Syā., Kaṃ., Pī.)]’: in me, monks … ‘This noble truth of the cessation of suffering is to be realized’: in me, monks … ‘It has been realized’: in me, monks … ‘This is the noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering’: in me, monks … ‘This noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering is to be cultivated’: in me, monks … ‘It has been cultivated’: in me, monks, regarding things not heard before, there arose vision … there arose light" [Mahāva. 15; Saṃ. Ni. 5.1081; Paṭi. Ma. 2.30]? Is there really such a discourse? Yes. In that case, knowledge with twelve aspects is supramundane.
The Discussion of Knowledge is complete.
