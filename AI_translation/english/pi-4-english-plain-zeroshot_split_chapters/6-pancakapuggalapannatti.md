#### Description of Persons in Fives ^4-1-5-0

Among them, the person who does wrong and feels remorse, and does not understand as it really is that liberation of mind, that liberation by wisdom, in which the bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder, should be told this: "Venerable one, the taints born of wrongdoing [variant: ārabbhajā (Ka.); A. Ni. 5.142] are found in you, and the taints born of remorse are growing. It would be good if the venerable one, having abandoned the taints born of wrongdoing and dispelled the taints born of remorse, were to develop the mind and wisdom. In this way the venerable one will become equal to that fifth person."
Among them, the person who does wrong and does not feel remorse, and does not understand as it really is that liberation of mind, that liberation by wisdom, in which the bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder, should be told this: "Venerable one, the taints born of wrongdoing are found in you, but the taints born of remorse are not growing. It would be good if the venerable one, having abandoned the taints born of wrongdoing, were to develop the mind and wisdom. In this way the venerable one will become equal to that fifth person."
Among them, the person who does no wrong yet feels remorse, and does not understand as it really is that liberation of mind, that liberation by wisdom, in which the bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder, should be told this: "Venerable one, the taints born of wrongdoing are not found in you, but the taints born of remorse are growing. It would be good if the venerable one, having dispelled the taints born of remorse, were to develop the mind and wisdom. In this way the venerable one will become equal to that fifth person."
Among them, the person who does no wrong and does not feel remorse, and does not understand as it really is that liberation of mind, that liberation by wisdom, in which those bad, unwholesome states of theirs cease without remainder, should be told this: "Venerable one, the taints born of wrongdoing are not found in you, and the taints born of remorse are not growing. It would be good if the venerable one were to develop the mind and wisdom. In this way the venerable one will become equal to that fifth person." These four persons, being advised in this way and instructed in this way by that fifth person, gradually reach the destruction of the taints. ^4-191

And how does a person give and then look down on the receiver? Here a certain person gives robes, alms food, lodging, and medicines and supplies for the sick to another person, and thinks: "I am the one who gives; this one [variant: ayaṃ pana (Syā., Ka.); A. Ni. 5.141] is the one who receives." Having given to them, they look down on them. This is how a person gives and then looks down on the receiver.
And how does a person look down on others through living with them? Here a certain person lives together with another person for two or three years, and through living with them they come to look down on them. This is how a person looks down on others through living with them.
And how is a person one who believes whatever they are told? Here a certain person, when someone else is being praised or criticized, is quick to believe it. This is how a person is one who believes whatever they are told.
And how is a person fickle? Here a certain person's faith is short-lived, their devotion is short-lived, their affection is short-lived, their trust is short-lived. This is how a person is fickle.
And how is a person dull and confused? Here a certain person does not know wholesome and unwholesome states, does not know blameworthy and blameless states, does not know inferior and superior states, does not know states that are dark and bright along with their counterparts. This is how a person is dull and confused. ^4-192

And here, which are the five persons who are like warriors? There are five kinds of warriors. Here a certain warrior, on merely seeing the cloud of dust, loses heart, falters, does not stand firm [variant: satthambhati (Sī.); A. Ni. 5.141] and cannot go into battle. Some warriors here are like this. This is the first warrior, to be found in the world.
Again, here a certain warrior can bear the cloud of dust, but on merely seeing the tops of the banners they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm and cannot go into battle. Some warriors here are like this. This is the second warrior, to be found in the world.
Again, here a certain warrior can bear the cloud of dust and can bear the tops of the banners, but on merely hearing the battle cry [variant: ussādanaṃyeva (Sī.), ussādanaññeva (Syā., Ka.); A. Ni. 5.141] they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm and cannot go into battle. Some warriors here are like this. This is the third warrior, to be found in the world.
Again, here a certain warrior can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners and can bear the battle cry, but in the clash of weapons they are struck down and wounded. Some warriors here are like this. This is the fourth warrior, to be found in the world.
Again, here a certain warrior can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners, can bear the battle cry and can bear the clash of weapons. Having won that battle, victorious, they take possession of that very front line of the battle. Some warriors here are like this. This is the fifth warrior, to be found in the world. These are the five warriors, to be found in the world. ^4-193

In the same way, these five persons who are like warriors are to be found among the monks. Which five? Here a certain monk, on merely seeing the cloud of dust, loses heart, falters, does not stand firm and cannot keep up the holy life [variant: santānetuṃ (Sī., Syā.); A. Ni. 5.75]; declaring [variant: āvīkatvā (Sī.)] their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life of a layperson. What is the cloud of dust for them? Here a monk hears: "In such-and-such a village or town there is a woman or a girl who is beautiful, lovely to look at, graceful, and blessed with the finest complexion." Having heard this, they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. This is the cloud of dust for them.
Just as that warrior, on merely seeing the cloud of dust, loses heart, falters, does not stand firm and cannot go into battle, this person is like that warrior. Some persons here are like this. This is the first person like a warrior, to be found among the monks. ^4-194

Again, here a certain monk can bear the cloud of dust, but on merely seeing the tops of the banners they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. What are the tops of the banners for them? Here a monk does not hear: "In such-and-such a village or town there is a woman or a girl who is beautiful, lovely to look at, graceful, and blessed with the finest complexion," but they see for themselves [variant: sāmaṃyeva (Sī.)] a woman or a girl who is beautiful, lovely to look at, graceful, and blessed with the finest complexion. Having seen her, they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. These are the tops of the banners for them.
Just as that warrior can bear the cloud of dust, but on merely seeing the tops of the banners loses heart, falters, does not stand firm and cannot go into battle, this person is like that warrior. Some persons here are like this. This is the second person like a warrior, to be found among the monks. ^4-195

Again, here a certain monk can bear the cloud of dust and can bear the tops of the banners, but on merely hearing the battle cry they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. What is the battle cry for them? Here, when a monk has gone to the forest, or to the foot of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, a woman comes up to them and smiles at them [variant: uhasati (commentary); A. Ni. 5.75], chats to them, laughs at them and teases them. Being smiled at, chatted to, laughed at and teased by the woman, they lose heart, falter, do not stand firm and cannot keep up the holy life; declaring their weakness in the training, they give up the training and return to the lower life. This is the battle cry for them.
Just as that warrior can bear the cloud of dust and can bear the tops of the banners, but on merely hearing the battle cry loses heart, falters, does not stand firm and cannot go into battle, this person is like that warrior. Some persons here are like this. This is the third person like a warrior, to be found among the monks. ^4-196

Again, here a certain monk can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners and can bear the battle cry, but in the clash of weapons they are struck down and wounded. What is the clash of weapons for them? Here, when a monk has gone to the forest, or to the foot of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, a woman comes up to them, sits down right beside them, lies down right beside them and throws herself on them. As the woman sits down beside them, lies down beside them and throws herself on them, without giving up the training and without declaring their weakness, they engage in sexual intercourse. This is the clash of weapons for them.
Just as that warrior can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners and can bear the battle cry, but in the clash of weapons is struck down and wounded, this person is like that warrior. Some persons here are like this. This is the fourth person like a warrior, to be found among the monks. ^4-197

Again, here a certain monk can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners, can bear the battle cry and can bear the clash of weapons. Having won that battle, victorious, they take possession of that very front line of the battle. What is the victory in battle for them? Here, when a monk has gone to the forest, or to the foot of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, a woman comes up to them, sits down right beside them, lies down right beside them and throws herself on them. As the woman sits down beside them, lies down beside them and throws herself on them, they disentangle themselves, free themselves, and go off wherever they wish.
They resort to a secluded dwelling: a forest, the foot of a tree, a mountain, a ravine, a hillside cave, a charnel ground, a jungle thicket, the open air, a heap of straw. Gone to the forest, or to the foot of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, they sit down, fold their legs crosswise, set their body upright, and establish mindfulness in front of them. Giving up longing for the world, they live with a mind free of longing, and they cleanse their mind of longing; giving up ill will and hatred, they live with a mind free of ill will, with compassion for the welfare of all living beings, and they cleanse their mind of ill will and hatred; giving up dullness and drowsiness, they live free of dullness and drowsiness, perceiving light, mindful and clearly aware, and they cleanse their mind of dullness and drowsiness; giving up restlessness and remorse, they live without agitation, with a mind calm within, and they cleanse their mind of restlessness and remorse; giving up doubt, they live having gone beyond doubt, without uncertainty about wholesome states, and they cleanse their mind of doubt.
Having given up these five hindrances – defilements of the mind that weaken wisdom – quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, they enter and remain in the first meditative absorption (jhāna), which has thinking and examining, and delight and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of thinking and examining, they enter and remain in the second meditative absorption … the third meditative absorption … the fourth meditative absorption.
When their mind is concentrated in this way – purified, cleansed, unblemished, rid of impurities, pliable, workable, steady and unshakable – they direct it to the knowledge of the destruction of the taints. They understand as it really is: "This is suffering"; they understand as it really is: "This is the origin of suffering"; they understand as it really is: "This is the cessation of suffering"; they understand as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering"; they understand as it really is: "These are the taints"; they understand as it really is: "This is the origin of the taints"; they understand as it really is: "This is the cessation of the taints"; they understand as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of the taints."
As they know and see in this way, their mind is liberated from the taint of sensual desire, liberated from the taint of existence, liberated from the taint of ignorance. When it is liberated, there comes the knowledge: "It is liberated." They understand: "Birth is ended, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is nothing more to come after this." This is the victory in battle for them. Just as that warrior can bear the cloud of dust, can bear the tops of the banners, can bear the battle cry and can bear the clash of weapons, and, having won that battle, victorious, takes possession of that very front line of the battle, this person is like that warrior. Some persons here are like this. This is the fifth person like a warrior, to be found among the monks. These are the five persons who are like warriors, to be found among the monks. ^4-198

And here, which are the five who live only on alms food? One lives only on alms food out of dullness and confusion; one lives only on alms food because they have evil wishes and are driven by desire; one lives only on alms food out of madness and a disturbed mind; one lives only on alms food thinking, "This is praised by the Buddhas and the Buddhas' disciples"; and one lives only on alms food simply for the sake of having few wishes [variant: appicchaṃyeva (Syā.); A. Ni. 5.181], simply for the sake of contentment, simply for the sake of wearing away the defilements, simply because they need nothing more than this [variant: idamaṭṭhitaṃyeva (Sī.)]. Among them, the one who lives only on alms food simply for the sake of having few wishes, simply for the sake of contentment, simply for the sake of wearing away the defilements, simply because they need nothing more than this, is the foremost, the best, the chief [variant: mokkho (Sī.)], the highest and the finest of these five who live only on alms food.
Just as from a cow comes milk, from milk curd, from curd fresh butter [variant: nonītaṃ (Sī.)], from fresh butter ghee, from ghee the cream of ghee, and the cream of ghee is declared the best of them; in the same way, the one who lives only on alms food simply for the sake of having few wishes, simply for the sake of contentment, simply for the sake of wearing away the defilements, simply because they need nothing more than this, is the foremost, the best, the chief, the highest and the finest of these five who live only on alms food. These are the five who live only on alms food. ^4-199

And here, which are the five who refuse food offered after the meal … the five who eat at one sitting … the five who wear robes made of rags … the five who use only three robes … the five who live in the forest … the five who live at the foot of a tree … the five who live in the open air … the five who do not lie down … the five who accept whatever place to sleep is given …. ^4-200
