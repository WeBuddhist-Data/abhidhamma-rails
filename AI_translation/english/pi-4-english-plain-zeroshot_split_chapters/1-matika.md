### The Outline ^4-M-0

#### Outline of the Single Types ^4-M-1-0

There are six kinds of description – the description of aggregates, the description of sense bases, the description of elements, the description of truths, the description of faculties, and the description of persons. ^4-M-1

What does the description of the aggregates cover? It covers the five aggregates – the aggregate of material form, the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of mental formations, the aggregate of consciousness; that is what the description of the aggregates covers. ^4-M-2

What does the description of the sense bases cover? It covers the twelve sense bases – the eye base, the visible-form base, the ear base, the sound base, the nose base, the smell base, the tongue base, the taste base, the body base, the touch base, the mind base, the base of mental objects; that is what the description of the sense bases covers. ^4-M-3

What does the description of the elements cover? It covers the eighteen elements – the eye element, the visible-form element, the eye-consciousness element, the ear element, the sound element, the ear-consciousness element, the nose element, the smell element, the nose-consciousness element, the tongue element, the taste element, the tongue-consciousness element, the body element, the touch element, the body-consciousness element, the mind element, the element of mental objects, the mind-consciousness element; that is what the description of the elements covers. ^4-M-4

What does the description of the truths cover? It covers the four truths – the truth of suffering, the truth of the origin of suffering, the truth of the cessation of suffering, the truth of the path; that is what the description of the truths covers. ^4-M-5

What does the description of the faculties cover? It covers the twenty-two faculties – the eye faculty, the ear faculty, the nose faculty, the tongue faculty, the body faculty, the mind faculty, the faculty of femininity, the faculty of masculinity, the life faculty, the faculty of pleasure, the faculty of pain, the faculty of joy, the faculty of sadness, the faculty of equanimity, the faculty of faith, the faculty of energy, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of concentration, the faculty of wisdom, the faculty of "I shall know what I have not yet known", the faculty of final knowledge, the faculty of one who has final knowledge; that is what the description of the faculties covers. ^4-M-6

#### Outline of the Threes ^4-M-3-0

Three persons –
(1) The one without hope, the one with hope, the one whose hope has gone.
(2) Three persons who are like sick people.
(3) The body-witness, the one who has attained to view, the one liberated by faith.
(4) The one whose speech is like dung, the one whose speech is like flowers, the one whose speech is like honey.
(5) The person whose mind is like an open sore, the person whose mind is like lightning, the person whose mind is like a diamond.
(6) The blind one, the one-eyed one, the two-eyed one.
(7) The person whose wisdom is like an upturned pot, the person whose wisdom is like a lap [variant: uccaṅgupañño (Syā.)], the person whose wisdom is wide.
(8) There is the person who is not free of passion for sensual pleasures or for existence; there is the person who is free of passion for sensual pleasures but not for existence; there is the person who is free of passion for both sensual pleasures and existence.
(9) The person like a line carved in rock, the person like a line drawn in earth, the person like a line drawn on water.
(10) Three persons who are like cloth.
(11) Three persons who are like fine Kāsi cloth.
(12) The one who is easy to measure, the one who is hard to measure, the one who cannot be measured.
(13) There is the person who should not be associated with, kept company with or attended on; there is the person who should be associated with, kept company with and attended on; there is the person who should be associated with, kept company with and attended on with honour and respect [variant: garukatvā (Sī.)].
(14) There is the person who should be shunned in disgust, and not associated with, kept company with or attended on; there is the person who should be regarded with detachment, and not associated with, kept company with or attended on; there is the person who should be associated with, kept company with and attended on.
(15) There is the person who fulfils morality [variant: paripūrīkārī (Syā.)] but practises concentration and wisdom only in part; there is the person who fulfils morality and concentration but practises wisdom only in part; there is the person who fulfils morality, concentration and wisdom.
(16) Three teachers.
(17) Three further teachers.

#### Outline of the Fours ^4-M-4-0

Four persons –
(1) The bad person, the one worse than a bad person, the good person, the one better than a good person.
(2) The wicked one, the one more wicked than the wicked, the good one, the one better than the good.
(3) The one of bad character, the one of worse character than the bad, the one of good character, the one of better character than the good.
(4) The one who is blameworthy, the one with much to blame, the one with little to blame [variant: appasāvajjo (Syā., Ka.); A. Ni. 4.135], the one who is blameless.
(5) The one who understands at a hint, the one who understands after explanation [variant: vipacitaññū (Sī.); A. Ni. 4.133], the one who needs to be guided, the one for whom the words remain only words.
(6) The one whose replies are apt but not fluent, the one whose replies are fluent but not apt, the one whose replies are both apt and fluent, the one whose replies are neither apt nor fluent.
(7) Four persons who teach the Teaching.
(8) Four persons who are like rain clouds.
(9) Four persons who are like mice.
(10) Four persons who are like mangoes.
(11) Four persons who are like pots.
(12) Four persons who are like lakes.
(13) Four persons who are like bulls [variant: balibaddūpamā (Sī.)].
(14) Four persons who are like snakes.
(15) There is the person who, without examining or looking into it, praises one who deserves blame; there is the person who, without examining or looking into it, blames one who deserves praise; there is the person who, without examining or looking into it, shows trust where trust is not due; there is the person who, without examining or looking into it, shows distrust where trust is due.
(16) There is the person who, after examining and looking into it, blames one who deserves blame; there is the person who, after examining and looking into it, praises one who deserves praise; there is the person who, after examining and looking into it, shows distrust where trust is not due; there is the person who, after examining and looking into it, shows trust where trust is due.
(17) There is the person who blames one who deserves blame, truthfully, accurately and at the right time, but does not praise one who deserves praise, truthfully, accurately and at the right time; there is the person who praises one who deserves praise, truthfully, accurately and at the right time, but does not blame one who deserves blame, truthfully, accurately and at the right time; there is the person who both blames one who deserves blame, truthfully, accurately and at the right time; and praises one who deserves praise, truthfully, accurately and at the right time, there is the person who neither blames one who deserves blame, nor praises one who deserves praise, truthfully, accurately and at the right time.
(18) The one who lives on the fruit of effort, not on the fruit of merit; the one who lives on the fruit of merit, not on the fruit of effort; the one who lives on the fruit of both effort and merit; the one who lives on the fruit of neither effort nor merit.
(19) In darkness and heading for darkness, in darkness and heading for light, in light and heading for darkness, in light and heading for light.
(20) Low and sinking lower, low but rising, high but sinking, high and rising higher.
(21) Four persons who are like trees.
(22) The one who judges by appearance and is won over by appearance, the one who judges by voice and is won over by voice.
(23) The one who judges by plainness and is won over by plainness, the one who judges by the Teaching and is won over by the Teaching.
(24) There is the person who works for their own good, not for the good of others; there is the person who works for the good of others, not for their own good; there is the person who works both for their own good and for the good of others; there is the person who works neither for their own good nor for the good of others.
(25) There is the person who torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves; there is the person who torments others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others; there is the person who torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves, and torments others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others; there is the person who neither torments themselves, nor is devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves, nor torments others, nor is devoted to the practice of tormenting others. Tormenting neither themselves nor others, they live here and now free of hunger, at peace, cooled [variant: sītibhūto (Sī., Ka.)], experiencing happiness, with a self that has become holy.
(26) The one with lust, the one with hatred, the one with delusion, the one with conceit.
(27) There is the person who has gained inner calm of mind but not insight into things through higher wisdom; there is the person who has gained insight into things through higher wisdom but not inner calm of mind; there is the person who has gained both inner calm of mind and insight into things through higher wisdom; there is the person who has gained neither inner calm of mind nor insight into things through higher wisdom.
(28) The person who goes with the stream, the person who goes against the stream, the person who stands firm, and the one who has crossed over and reached the far shore [variant: pāragato (Sī., Syā.)], who stands on dry land, a true brahmin.
(29) One who has learned little and is not endowed with learning, one who has learned little but is endowed with learning, one who has learned much but is not endowed with learning, one who has learned much and is endowed with learning.
(30) The unshakable ascetic, the red-lotus ascetic, the white-lotus ascetic, the most refined ascetic among ascetics.
The fours. ^4-M-10

#### Outline of the Fives ^4-M-5-0

Five persons –
(1) There is the person who does wrong [variant: ārambhati (Sī., Syā.)] and feels remorse, and does not understand as it really is that liberation of mind, that liberation by wisdom, in which the bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder. There is the person who does wrong and does not feel remorse, and does not understand as it really is that liberation of mind, that liberation by wisdom, in which the bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder. There is the person who does no wrong yet feels remorse, and does not understand as it really is that liberation of mind, that liberation by wisdom, in which the bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder. There is the person who does no wrong and does not feel remorse, and does not understand as it really is that liberation of mind, that liberation by wisdom, in which the bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder. There is the person who does no wrong and does not feel remorse, and understands as it really is that liberation of mind, that liberation by wisdom, in which the bad, unwholesome states that have arisen in them cease without remainder.
(2) The one who gives and then looks down on the receiver, the one who looks down on others through living with them, the one who believes whatever they are told, the one who is fickle, the one who is dull and confused.
(3) Five persons who are like warriors.
(4) Five who live only on alms food.
(5) Five who refuse food offered after the meal.
(6) Five who eat at one sitting.
(7) Five who wear robes made of rags.
(8) Five who use only three robes.
(9) Five who live in the forest.
(10) Five who live at the foot of a tree.
(11) Five who live in the open air.
(12) Five who do not lie down.
(13) Five who accept whatever place to sleep is given.
(14) Five who live in charnel grounds.
The fives. ^4-M-11

#### Outline of the Sixes ^4-M-6-0

Six persons –
(1) There is the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves, and there attains all-knowing wisdom and mastery over the powers [variant: phalesu (Pī.)]. There is the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, awakens to the truths by themselves, but does not there attain all-knowing wisdom or mastery over the powers. There is the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, does not awaken to the truths by themselves, but in this very life makes an end of suffering and attains the perfection of a disciple. There is the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, does not awaken to the truths by themselves, and in this very life makes an end of suffering, but does not attain the perfection of a disciple. There is the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, does not awaken to the truths by themselves, and does not in this very life make an end of suffering, but is a non-returner, one who does not come back [variant: anāgantvā (Syā., Ka.); A. Ni. 4.171] to this state of being. There is the person who, regarding teachings not heard before, does not awaken to the truths by themselves, and does not in this very life make an end of suffering, but is a returner [variant: sotāpannasakadāgāmī (Syā., Ka.)], one who comes back to this state of being.
The sixes. ^4-M-12

(2) One liberated in both ways, one liberated by wisdom, a body-witness, one who has attained to view, one liberated by faith, a follower of the Teaching, a follower by faith.
