#### Description of Persons in Fours ^4-1-4-0

What kind of person is the bad person? Here a certain person kills living beings, takes what is not given, engages in sexual misconduct, tells lies, and indulges in alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which are a basis for carelessness – this person is called "the bad person". ^4-132

What kind of person is the one worse than a bad person? Here a certain person kills living beings themselves and encourages others to kill living beings; takes what is not given themselves and encourages others to take what is not given; engages in sexual misconduct themselves and encourages others to engage in sexual misconduct; tells lies themselves and encourages others to tell lies; indulges in alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which are a basis for carelessness, themselves and encourages others to indulge in them – this person is called "the one worse than a bad person". ^4-133

What kind of person is the good person? Here a certain person refrains from killing living beings, refrains from taking what is not given, refrains from sexual misconduct, refrains from telling lies, and refrains from alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which are a basis for carelessness – this person is called "the good person". ^4-134

What kind of person is the one better than a good person? Here a certain person refrains from killing living beings themselves and encourages others to refrain from killing living beings; refrains from taking what is not given themselves and encourages others to refrain from taking what is not given; refrains from sexual misconduct themselves and encourages others to refrain from sexual misconduct; refrains from telling lies themselves and encourages others to refrain from telling lies; refrains from alcohol and intoxicating drinks, which are a basis for carelessness, themselves and encourages others to refrain from them – this person is called "the one better than a good person". ^4-135

What kind of person is the wicked one? Here a certain person kills living beings, takes what is not given, engages in sexual misconduct, tells lies, speaks divisively [variant: pisuṇāvāco], speaks harshly [variant: pharusāvāco (Sī.); Dī. Ni. 3.115], indulges in idle chatter, is covetous, has a mind full of ill will, and holds wrong view [variant: micchādiṭṭhī (Ka.)] – this person is called "the wicked one". ^4-136

What kind of person is the one more wicked than the wicked? Here a certain person kills living beings themselves and encourages others to kill living beings; takes what is not given themselves and encourages others to take what is not given; engages in sexual misconduct themselves and encourages others to engage in sexual misconduct; tells lies themselves and encourages others to tell lies; speaks divisively themselves and encourages others to speak divisively; speaks harshly themselves and encourages others to speak harshly; indulges in idle chatter themselves and encourages others to indulge in idle chatter; is covetous themselves and encourages others to covetousness; has a mind full of ill will themselves and encourages others to ill will; holds wrong view themselves and encourages others to wrong view – this person is called "the one more wicked than the wicked". ^4-137

What kind of person is the good one? Here a certain person refrains from killing living beings, refrains from taking what is not given, refrains from sexual misconduct, refrains from telling lies, refrains from divisive speech, refrains from harsh speech, refrains from idle chatter, is not covetous, has a mind free of ill will, and holds right view [variant: sammādiṭṭhī (Ka.)] – this person is called "the good one". ^4-138

What kind of person is the one better than the good? Here a certain person refrains from killing living beings themselves and encourages others to refrain from killing living beings; refrains from taking what is not given themselves and encourages others to refrain from taking what is not given; refrains from sexual misconduct themselves and encourages others to refrain from sexual misconduct; refrains from telling lies themselves and encourages others to refrain from telling lies; refrains from divisive speech themselves and encourages others to refrain from divisive speech; refrains from harsh speech themselves and encourages others to refrain from harsh speech; refrains from idle chatter themselves and encourages others to refrain from idle chatter; is not covetous themselves and encourages others to be free of covetousness; has a mind free of ill will themselves and encourages others to be free of ill will; holds right view themselves and encourages others to right view – this person is called "the one better than the good". ^4-139

What kind of person is the one of bad character? Here a certain person kills living beings, takes what is not given … holds wrong view – this person is called "the one of bad character". ^4-140

What kind of person is the one of worse character than the bad? Here a certain person kills living beings themselves and encourages others to kill living beings; takes what is not given themselves and encourages others to take what is not given … holds wrong view themselves and encourages others to wrong view – this person is called "the one of worse character than the bad". ^4-141

What kind of person is the one of good character? Here a certain person refrains from killing living beings, refrains from taking what is not given … holds right view – this person is called "the one of good character". ^4-142

What kind of person is the one of better character than the good? Here a certain person refrains from killing living beings themselves and encourages others to refrain from killing living beings … holds right view themselves and encourages others to right view – this person is called "the one of better character than the good". ^4-143

What kind of person is the one who is blameworthy? Here a certain person's bodily actions are blameworthy, their verbal actions are blameworthy, their mental actions are blameworthy – this person is called "the one who is blameworthy". ^4-144

What kind of person is the one with much to blame? Here a certain person's bodily actions are mostly blameworthy and only a little blameless, their verbal actions are mostly blameworthy and only a little blameless, their mental actions are mostly blameworthy and only a little blameless – this person is called "the one with much to blame". ^4-145

What kind of person is the one with little to blame? Here a certain person's bodily actions are mostly blameless and only a little blameworthy, their verbal actions are mostly blameless and only a little blameworthy, their mental actions are mostly blameless and only a little blameworthy – this person is called "the one with little to blame". ^4-146

What kind of person is the one who is blameless? Here a certain person's bodily actions are blameless, their verbal actions are blameless, their mental actions are blameless – this person is called "the one who is blameless". ^4-147

What kind of person is the one who understands at a hint? A person who breaks through to the truth at the very moment it is spoken – this person is called "the one who understands at a hint". ^4-148

What kind of person is the one who understands after explanation? A person who breaks through to the truth when the meaning of what has been said in brief is explained in detail – this person is called "the one who understands after explanation". ^4-149

What kind of person is the one who needs to be guided? A person who breaks through to the truth gradually, in this way: through learning it, through asking questions about it, through paying wise attention to it, through associating with, keeping company with and attending on good friends – this person is called "the one who needs to be guided". ^4-150

What kind of person is the one for whom the words remain only words? A person who, though they hear much, say much, memorize much and teach much, does not break through to the truth in this lifetime – this person is called "the one for whom the words remain only words". ^4-151

What kind of person is the one whose replies are apt [variant: yuttapaṭibhāṇo (Syā.); A. Ni. 4.132] but not fluent? Here a certain person, when asked a question, answers aptly but not quickly – this person is called "the one whose replies are apt but not fluent". ^4-152

What kind of person is the one whose replies are fluent but not apt? Here a certain person, when asked a question, answers quickly but not aptly – this person is called "the one whose replies are fluent but not apt". ^4-153

What kind of person is the one whose replies are both apt and fluent? Here a certain person, when asked a question, answers both aptly and quickly – this person is called "the one whose replies are both apt and fluent". ^4-154

What kind of person is the one whose replies are neither apt nor fluent? Here a certain person, when asked a question, answers neither aptly nor quickly – this person is called "the one whose replies are neither apt nor fluent". ^4-155

And here, which are the four persons who are like rain clouds? There are four kinds of rain clouds – one that thunders but does not rain, one that rains but does not thunder, one that both thunders and rains, one that neither thunders nor rains. In the same way, these four persons who are like rain clouds are to be found in the world. Which four? One who thunders but does not rain, one who rains but does not thunder, one who both thunders and rains, one who neither thunders nor rains.
And how does a person thunder but not rain? Here a certain person is a talker, not a doer. This is how a person thunders but does not rain. Just as that cloud thunders but does not rain, this person is like it.
And how does a person rain but not thunder? Here a certain person is a doer, not a talker. This is how a person rains but does not thunder. Just as that cloud rains but does not thunder, this person is like it.
And how does a person both thunder and rain? Here a certain person is both a talker and a doer. This is how a person both thunders and rains. Just as that cloud both thunders and rains, this person is like it.
And how does a person neither thunder nor rain? Here a certain person is neither a talker nor a doer. This is how a person neither thunders nor rains. Just as that cloud neither thunders nor rains, this person is like it.
These are the four persons who are like rain clouds, to be found in the world. ^4-157

What kind of person is the one who lives on the fruit of effort, not on the fruit of merit? A person whose livelihood comes about through getting up, striving and exerting themselves, not through merit – this person is called "the one who lives on the fruit of effort, not on the fruit of merit".
What kind of person is the one who lives on the fruit of merit, not on the fruit of effort? The gods from the Paranimmitavasavattī gods ("those who wield power over what others create") [variant: paranimmitavasavattideve (Sī., Syā.)] upwards live on the fruit of merit, not on the fruit of effort.

And how does a person torment themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves? Here a certain person goes naked, rejecting ordinary conventions, licking their hands clean [variant: hatthāvalekhano (Syā.)], not coming when asked, not stopping when asked; they do not accept food brought to them, or food prepared specially for them, or an invitation to a meal. They accept nothing from the mouth of a cooking pot, nothing from the mouth of a bowl [variant: kalopimukhā (Sī., Syā.); Ma. Ni. 2.7], nothing handed across a threshold, nothing handed across a stick, nothing handed across a pestle, nothing from two people eating together, nothing from a pregnant woman, nothing from a woman nursing a child, nothing from a woman who is with a man, nothing from food collected in a time of famine, nothing from where a dog is waiting, nothing from where flies are swarming; they accept no fish, no meat; they drink no liquor, no wine, no fermented drink. They go to just one house for alms and take just one mouthful, or go to two houses and take two mouthfuls … or go to seven houses and take seven mouthfuls; they live on one small helping a day, on two small helpings … on seven small helpings; they take food once a day, once every two days [variant: dvāhikampi (Sī.)] … once every seven days. So they live devoted to the practice of eating at fixed intervals, even up to once every half month. They live on greens, or millet, or wild rice, or hide-parings, or moss, or rice bran, or rice scum, or sesame flour, or grass, or cow dung; they keep themselves going on forest roots and fruits, eating only fallen fruit. They wear clothes of hemp, clothes of mixed hemp, shrouds from corpses, rags from the rubbish heap, tree bark, antelope hide, strips of antelope hide, clothes of kusa grass, clothes of bark fibre, clothes of wood shavings, a blanket of human hair, a blanket of animal hair, owls' wings [variant: ulukapakkhampi (Sī., Syā.)]; they pull out their hair and beard, devoted to the practice of pulling out their hair and beard; they stand all the time, refusing to sit; they squat all the time, devoted to striving in the squatting position; they lie on a bed of thorns, making their bed on thorns; they live devoted to the practice of going down into water to bathe three times a day, the third time in the evening [variant: sāyaṃtatiyakampi (Syā., Ka.); Ma. Ni. 2.7]. So they live devoted to the practice of tormenting and torturing the body in many different ways. This is how a person torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves. ^4-174

And how does a person torment others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others? And how does a person torment themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves, and torment others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others? Here a certain person is a head-anointed noble king [variant: muddhābhisitto (Syā., Ka.)] or a wealthy brahmin. Having had a new sacrificial hall [variant: santāgāraṃ (Syā.), yaññāgāraṃ (Sī.)] built to the east of the city, having shaved off their hair and beard, put on a rough antelope hide [variant: khurājinaṃ (Syā., Ka.)], smeared their body with ghee and oil, and while scratching their back with a deer's horn [variant: kaṇḍūyamāno (Sī.)], they enter the sacrificial hall together with the chief queen and the brahmin high priest. There they lie down on the bare ground strewn with green grass. The king lives on the milk in one teat of a cow whose calf is of the same colour as her; the chief queen lives on the milk in the second teat; the brahmin high priest lives on the milk in the third teat; the milk in the fourth teat is offered into the fire; the calf lives on what is left. They say: "Let so many bulls be slaughtered for the sacrifice, let so many bullocks be slaughtered for the sacrifice, let so many heifers be slaughtered for the sacrifice, let so many goats be slaughtered for the sacrifice, let so many rams be slaughtered for the sacrifice, (let so many horses be slaughtered for the sacrifice) [variant: the words in round brackets are not in the Sinhalese manuscript; the same holds in the Kandaraka Sutta of the Majjhima Nikāya], let so many trees be cut down for sacrificial posts, let so much grass be cut to strew around the sacrifice [variant: parihiṃsatthāyāti (Sī., Syā., Ka.); Ma. Ni. 2.9]." And those who are their slaves, servants or workers, driven by the threat of punishment and by fear, do their tasks weeping, with tears on their faces. This is how a person torments themselves, devoted to the practice of tormenting themselves, and torments others, devoted to the practice of tormenting others. ^4-176

And how does a person neither torment themselves nor devote themselves to the practice of tormenting themselves, nor torment others nor devote themselves to the practice of tormenting others? Tormenting neither themselves nor others, they live here and now free of hunger, at peace, cooled, experiencing happiness, with a self that has become holy.
Here a Tathāgata arises in the world, a worthy one (arahant), fully awakened, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, well-gone, knower of the world, unsurpassed guide of those who can be trained, teacher of gods and humans, awakened, blessed. Having realized it by his own direct knowledge, he makes known this world with its gods, with Māra (the Tempter) and with Brahmā, this generation with its ascetics and brahmins, its gods and humans. He teaches the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, with the right meaning and the right wording, and he reveals the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. A householder, or a householder's son, or someone born into some other family, hears that Teaching. Having heard the Teaching, they gain faith in the Tathāgata. Possessing that faith, they reflect: "Household life is cramped, a road full of dust; the life of one gone forth is like the open air. It is not easy, while living at home, to lead the holy life utterly complete, utterly pure, polished like a shell. What if I were to shave off my hair and beard, put on ochre robes, and go forth from home into homelessness!" Then, at a later time, giving up a small or a large fortune, giving up a small or a large circle of relatives, they shave off their hair and beard, put on ochre robes, and go forth from home into homelessness. ^4-177

Having gone forth in this way, and having taken up the training and way of life of the monks, they give up killing living beings and refrain from killing living beings; with the stick and the weapon laid aside, conscientious and kind, they live with compassion for the welfare of all living beings.
Giving up taking what is not given, they refrain from taking what is not given; taking only what is given, expecting only what is given, they live honestly, with a self that has become pure.
Giving up unchaste living, they live the holy life, living apart [variant: anācārī (Ka.)], refraining from sexual intercourse, the way of the village.
Giving up lying, they refrain from lying; they speak the truth, hold to the truth, are trustworthy and reliable, and do not deceive the world.
Giving up divisive speech, they refrain from divisive speech; what they have heard here they do not repeat there in order to divide those people from these, and what they have heard there they do not repeat here in order to divide these people from those. So they reunite those who are divided and support those who are united; they delight in harmony, love harmony, rejoice in harmony, and speak words that create harmony.
Giving up harsh speech, they refrain from harsh speech. They speak such words as are gentle, pleasing to the ear, affectionate, going to the heart, courteous, loved by many and liked by many.
Giving up idle chatter, they refrain from idle chatter; they speak at the right time, speak what is true, speak what is beneficial, speak on the Teaching and the Discipline; at the right time they speak words worth treasuring, well reasoned, well defined and beneficial. ^4-178

They refrain from damaging seeds and plants; they eat only one meal a day, not eating at night, refraining from eating at the wrong time; they refrain from dancing, singing, music and watching shows; they refrain from wearing garlands, using scents and ointments, and adorning and beautifying themselves; they refrain from high and luxurious beds; they refrain from accepting gold and silver.
They refrain from accepting raw grain; they refrain from accepting raw meat; they refrain from accepting women and girls; they refrain from accepting female and male slaves; they refrain from accepting goats and sheep; they refrain from accepting chickens and pigs; they refrain from accepting elephants, cattle, horses and mares; they refrain from accepting fields and land; they refrain from running errands and carrying messages; they refrain from buying and selling; they refrain from cheating with false weights, false metal and false measures; they refrain from bribery, deception, fraud and crooked dealing [variant: … sāviyogā (Syā., Ka.)]; they refrain from wounding, killing, binding, highway robbery, plunder and violence. ^4-179

They are content with robes to protect the body and with alms food to sustain the belly. Wherever they go, they go taking only these with them, just as a bird, wherever it flies, flies with its wings as its only burden. In the same way, a monk is content with robes to protect the body and with alms food to sustain the belly. Wherever they go, they go taking only these with them. Possessing this noble body of morality, they experience within themselves a happiness that is blameless. ^4-180

On seeing a form with the eye, they do not grasp at its general appearance and do not grasp at its details. Since bad, unwholesome states of longing and sadness might flow in upon one who lives with the eye faculty unrestrained, they practise to restrain it, they guard the eye faculty, they undertake the restraint of the eye faculty. On hearing a sound with the ear … on smelling a smell with the nose … on tasting a taste with the tongue … on feeling a touch with the body … on knowing a mental object with the mind, they do not grasp at its general appearance and do not grasp at its details. Since bad, unwholesome states of longing and sadness might flow in upon one who lives with the mind faculty unrestrained, they practise to restrain it, they guard the mind faculty, they undertake the restraint of the mind faculty. Possessing this noble restraint of the senses, they experience within themselves an untainted happiness. ^4-181

In going out and coming back, they act with clear awareness; in looking ahead and looking around, they act with clear awareness; in bending and stretching their limbs, they act with clear awareness; in wearing their outer robe, carrying their bowl and wearing their robes, they act with clear awareness; in eating, drinking, chewing and tasting, they act with clear awareness; in defecating and urinating, they act with clear awareness; in walking, standing, sitting, falling asleep, waking up, speaking and keeping silent, they act with clear awareness.
Possessing this noble body of morality, possessing this noble restraint of the senses, possessing this noble mindfulness and clear awareness, and possessing this noble contentment [see Ma. Ni. 2, Kandaraka Sutta; and A. Ni. 1, Attantapa Sutta], they resort to a secluded dwelling: a forest, the foot of a tree, a mountain, a ravine, a hillside cave, a charnel ground, a jungle thicket, the open air, a heap of straw. After their meal, on returning from their almsround, they sit down, fold their legs crosswise, set their body upright, and establish mindfulness in front of them. Giving up longing for the world, they live with a mind free of longing, and they cleanse their mind of longing; giving up ill will and hatred, they live with a mind free of ill will, with compassion for the welfare of all living beings, and they cleanse their mind of ill will and hatred; giving up dullness and drowsiness [variant: thīnamiddhaṃ (Sī., Syā.)], they live free of dullness and drowsiness, perceiving light, mindful and clearly aware, and they cleanse their mind of dullness and drowsiness; giving up restlessness and remorse, they live without agitation, with a mind calm within, and they cleanse their mind of restlessness and remorse; giving up doubt, they live having gone beyond doubt, without uncertainty about wholesome states, and they cleanse their mind of doubt. ^4-182

Having given up these five hindrances – defilements of the mind that weaken wisdom – quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, they enter and remain in the first meditative absorption (jhāna), which has thinking and examining, and delight and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of thinking and examining, they enter and remain in the second meditative absorption, which has inner confidence and unification of mind, is without thinking and examining, and has delight and happiness born of concentration. With the fading away of delight, they live with equanimity, mindful and clearly aware, and they experience happiness with the body; they enter and remain in the third meditative absorption, of which the noble ones declare: "One with equanimity and mindfulness lives happily." With the giving up of pleasure and pain, and with the earlier disappearance of joy and sadness, they enter and remain in the fourth meditative absorption, which is neither painful nor pleasant and has purity of mindfulness due to equanimity.
When their mind is concentrated in this way – purified, cleansed, unblemished, rid of impurities, pliable, workable, steady and unshakable – they direct it to the knowledge of recollecting past lives. They recollect their many kinds of past lives, that is: one birth, two births, three births, four births, five births, ten births, twenty births, thirty births, forty births, fifty births, a hundred births, a thousand births, a hundred thousand births, many aeons of the world contracting, many aeons of the world expanding, many aeons of the world contracting and expanding: "There I had such a name, such a clan, such an appearance, such food, such experience of pleasure and pain, such an end to my life; passing away from there, I arose elsewhere; there too I had such a name, such a clan, such an appearance, such food, such experience of pleasure and pain, such an end to my life; passing away from there, I was reborn here." So they recollect their many kinds of past lives, with their details and particulars. ^4-183

When their mind is concentrated in this way – purified, cleansed, unblemished, rid of impurities, pliable, workable, steady and unshakable – they direct it to the knowledge of the passing away and rebirth of beings. With the divine eye, which is purified and goes beyond the human, they see beings passing away and being reborn, inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in good destinations and bad destinations, and they understand how beings fare according to their actions: "These beings, who engaged in bad conduct of body, speech and mind, who spoke against the noble ones, held wrong view and took up actions based on wrong view – at the breaking up of the body, after death, they have been reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, in hell. But these beings, who engaged in good conduct of body, speech and mind, who did not speak against the noble ones, held right view and took up actions based on right view – at the breaking up of the body, after death, they have been reborn in a good destination, a heavenly world." So, with the divine eye, which is purified and goes beyond the human, they see beings passing away and being reborn, inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in good destinations and bad destinations, and they understand how beings fare according to their actions. ^4-184

When it is liberated, there comes the knowledge: "It is liberated." They understand: "Birth is ended, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is nothing more to come after this." What kind of person is the white-lotus ascetic? Here a certain person, through the complete destruction of the five lower fetters, is reborn spontaneously, attains final release there, and is not liable to return from that world. This person is called "the white-lotus ascetic".
What kind of person is the most refined ascetic among ascetics? Here a certain person, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for themselves by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it. This person is called "the most refined ascetic among ascetics".
