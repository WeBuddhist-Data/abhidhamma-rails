#### Description of Persons in Threes ^4-1-3-0

What kind of person is the one without hope? Here a certain person is immoral, of bad character, impure, of suspect conduct, secretive in their actions; not an ascetic, though claiming to be one; not living the holy life, though claiming to; rotten inside, oozing with desire, a heap of filth. They hear: "It seems that the monk named so-and-so, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for himself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it." It does not occur to them: "When will I too, through the destruction of the taints, realize for myself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and enter upon it and live in it?" This person is called "the one without hope". ^4-91

What kind of person is the one with hope? Here a certain person is of good morality, of good character. They hear: "It seems that the monk named so-and-so, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for himself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it." It occurs to them: "When will I too, through the destruction of the taints, realize for myself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and enter upon it and live in it?" This person is called "the one with hope". ^4-92

What kind of person is the one whose hope has gone? Here a certain person, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for themselves by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it. They hear: "It seems that the monk named so-and-so, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for himself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it." It does not occur to them: "When will I too, through the destruction of the taints, realize for myself by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and enter upon it and live in it?" Why is that? Because the hope for liberation that they had earlier, when not yet liberated, has died down. This person is called "the one whose hope has gone". ^4-93

And here, which are the three persons who are like sick people? There are three kinds of sick people – here a certain sick person, whether or not they get suitable food, whether or not they get suitable medicine, whether or not they get a proper nurse, does not recover from that illness. (1)
And here a certain sick person, whether or not they get suitable food, whether or not they get suitable medicine, whether or not they get a proper nurse, recovers from that illness. (2)
And here a certain sick person recovers from that illness if they get suitable food but not if they do not, if they get suitable medicine but not if they do not, if they get a proper nurse but not if they do not. (3)
Now, it is because of the sick person who recovers from that illness if they get suitable food but not if they do not, if they get suitable medicine but not if they do not, if they get a proper nurse but not if they do not, that the Blessed One has allowed food for the sick, medicine for the sick and nurses for the sick. And it is because of this sick person that other sick people too should be nursed.
In the same way [variant: evameva (Sī.)] three persons who are like sick people are to be found in the world. Which three? Here a certain person, whether or not they get to see the Tathāgata (the Buddha), whether or not they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, does not enter the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states. (1)
And here a certain person, whether or not they get to see the Tathāgata, whether or not they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, enters the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states. (2)
And here a certain person enters the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states if they get to see the Tathāgata but not if they do not, if they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata but not if they do not. (3)
Now, it is because of the person who enters the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states if they get to see the Tathāgata but not if they do not, if they get to hear the Teaching and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata but not if they do not, that the Blessed One has allowed the teaching of the Teaching; and it is because of this person that the Teaching should be taught to others too. These are the three persons who are like sick people, to be found in the world. ^4-94

What kind of person is a body-witness? Here a certain person lives having touched the eight liberations with the body, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "a body-witness". ^4-95

What kind of person is one who has attained to view? Here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the origin of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the cessation of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering." The teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and thoroughly examined by them with wisdom, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed – this person is called "one who has attained to view". ^4-96

What kind of person is one liberated by faith? Here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering" … The teachings proclaimed by the Tathāgata have been clearly seen and thoroughly examined by them with wisdom, and, having seen with wisdom, some of their taints are destroyed, but not in the way they are for one who has attained to view – this person is called "one liberated by faith". ^4-97

What kind of person is the one whose speech is like dung? Here a certain person is a liar. When summoned to a council, or to an assembly, or among their relatives, or before a guild, or before the royal court, and questioned as a witness – "Come now [variant: ehi bho (Syā., Ka.); Ma. Ni. 1.440; A. Ni. 3.28], my good man, tell what you know" – not knowing, they say, "I know," or knowing, they say, "I do not know"; not seeing, they say, "I see," or seeing, they say, "I do not see." So, for their own sake, or for the sake of others, or for the sake of some small material gain, they tell deliberate lies – this person is called "the one whose speech is like dung". ^4-98

What kind of person is the one whose speech is like flowers? Here a certain person, having given up lying, refrains from lying. When summoned to a council, or to an assembly, or among their relatives, or before a guild, or before the royal court, and questioned as a witness – "Come now, my good man, tell what you know" – not knowing, they say, "I do not know," or knowing, they say, "I know"; not seeing, they say, "I do not see," or seeing, they say, "I see." So, neither for their own sake, nor for the sake of others, nor for the sake of some small material gain, do they tell deliberate lies – this person is called "the one whose speech is like flowers". ^4-99

What kind of person is the one whose speech is like honey? Here a certain person speaks such words as are gentle, pleasing to the ear, affectionate, going to the heart, courteous, loved by many and liked by many – this person is called "the one whose speech is like honey". ^4-100

What kind of person is the one whose mind is like an open sore? Here a certain person is angry and easily upset. Even when a little is said to them, they lose their temper, become irritated, hostile and stubborn [variant: patiṭṭhīyati (Syā., Ka.); A. Ni. 3.25], and they show irritation, hatred and displeasure. Just as an infected sore, when struck by a stick or a potsherd [variant: kathalāya (Ka.), kathalena (commentary); A. Ni. 3.25], discharges all the more [variant: assavanoti (Sī.)], in the same way here a certain person is angry and easily upset; even when a little is said to them, they lose their temper, become irritated, hostile and stubborn, and they show irritation, hatred and displeasure – this person is called "the person whose mind is like an open sore". ^4-101

What kind of person is the one whose mind is like lightning? Here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the origin of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the cessation of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering." Just as a person with good eyes might see shapes in a flash of lightning in the pitch darkness of night, in the same way here a certain person understands as it really is: "This is suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the origin of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the cessation of suffering"; understands as it really is: "This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering" – this person is called "the person whose mind is like lightning". ^4-102

What kind of person is the one whose mind is like a diamond? Here a certain person, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for themselves by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it. Just as there is nothing a diamond cannot cut, whether gem or stone, in the same way here a certain person, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for themselves by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it – this person is called "the person whose mind is like a diamond". ^4-103

What kind of person is the blind one? Here a certain person does not have the kind of eye with which they could gain wealth not yet gained or increase wealth already gained; nor do they have the kind of eye with which they could know wholesome and unwholesome states, blameworthy and blameless states, low and excellent states, dark and bright states with their opposites – this person is called "the blind one". ^4-104

What kind of person is the one-eyed one? Here a certain person has the kind of eye with which they could gain wealth not yet gained or increase wealth already gained; but they do not have the kind of eye with which they could know wholesome and unwholesome states, blameworthy and blameless states, low and excellent states, dark and bright states with their opposites – this person is called "the one-eyed one". ^4-105

What kind of person is the two-eyed one? Here a certain person has the kind of eye with which they could gain wealth not yet gained or increase wealth already gained; and they also have the kind of eye with which they could know wholesome and unwholesome states, blameworthy and blameless states, low and excellent states, dark and bright states with their opposites – this person is called "the two-eyed one". ^4-106

What kind of person is the one whose wisdom is like an upturned pot? Here a certain person often goes [variant: gato (Sī.), gantvā (Syā.)] to the monastery to hear the Teaching [variant: dhammassavanāya (Syā.)] from the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, with the right meaning and the right wording, and they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. While sitting in that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, no attention to the middle, no attention to the end. And after rising from that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, no attention to the middle, no attention to the end. Just as, when water is poured onto a pot turned upside down [variant: nikujjo (Syā.); A. Ni. 3.30], it runs off and does not stay there, in the same way here a certain person often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching from the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, with the right meaning and the right wording, and they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. While sitting in that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, no attention to the middle, no attention to the end. And after rising from that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, no attention to the middle, no attention to the end – this person is called "the person whose wisdom is like an upturned pot". ^4-107

What kind of person is the one whose wisdom is like a lap? Here a certain person often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching from the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, with the right meaning and the right wording, and they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. While sitting in that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, to the middle and to the end. But after rising from that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, no attention to the middle, no attention to the end. Just as various foods are piled in a man's lap – sesame seeds, rice grains [variant: tilataṇḍulā (Ka.); A. Ni. 3.30], sweets, jujube fruits – and when he gets up from that seat he spills them through absent-mindedness, in the same way here a certain person often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching from the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, with the right meaning and the right wording, and they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. While sitting in that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, to the middle and to the end. But after rising from that seat, they pay no attention to the beginning of that talk, no attention to the middle, no attention to the end – this person is called "the person whose wisdom is like a lap". ^4-108

What kind of person is the one whose wisdom is wide? Here a certain person often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching from the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, with the right meaning and the right wording, and they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. While sitting in that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, to the middle and to the end. And after rising from that seat too, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, to the middle and to the end. Just as, when water is poured into a pot standing upright, it stays there and does not run off, in the same way here a certain person often goes to the monastery to hear the Teaching from the monks. The monks teach them the Teaching that is good in the beginning, good in the middle and good in the end, with the right meaning and the right wording, and they reveal the holy life that is entirely complete and pure. While sitting in that seat, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, to the middle and to the end. And after rising from that seat too, they pay attention to the beginning of that talk, to the middle and to the end – this person is called "the person whose wisdom is wide". ^4-109

What kind of person is the one who is not free of passion for sensual pleasures or for existence? Stream-enterers and once-returners – these persons are called "not free of passion for sensual pleasures or for existence". ^4-110

What kind of person is the one who is free of passion for sensual pleasures but not for existence? A non-returner – this person is called "free of passion for sensual pleasures but not for existence". ^4-111

What kind of person is the one who is free of passion for both sensual pleasures and existence? A worthy one (arahant) – this person is called "free of passion for both sensual pleasures and existence". ^4-112

What kind of person is the one like a line carved in rock? Here a certain person often gets angry, and that anger of theirs stays with them for a long time. Just as a line carved in rock is not quickly worn away by wind or water but lasts a long time, in the same way here a certain person often gets angry, and that anger of theirs stays with them for a long time – this person is called "the person like a line carved in rock". ^4-113

What kind of person is the one like a line drawn in earth? Here a certain person often gets angry, but that anger of theirs does not stay with them for long. Just as a line drawn in earth [variant: paṭhaviyā (Sī., Syā.)] is quickly worn away by wind or water and does not last long, in the same way here a certain person often gets angry, but that anger of theirs does not stay with them for long – this person is called "the person like a line drawn in earth". ^4-114

What kind of person is the one like a line drawn on water? Here a certain person, even when spoken to sharply, spoken to harshly, spoken to unpleasantly, still stays on good terms [variant: … ceva (Syā.); A. Ni. 3.133], still keeps the peace [variant: … ceva (Syā.); A. Ni. 3.133], still remains friendly [variant: … ceva (Syā.); A. Ni. 3.133]. Just as a line drawn on water quickly disappears and does not last long, in the same way here a certain person, even when spoken to sharply, spoken to harshly, spoken to unpleasantly, still stays on good terms, still keeps the peace, still remains friendly – this person is called "the person like a line drawn on water". ^4-115

And here, which are the three persons who are like cloth? There are three kinds of coarse cloth – coarse cloth, even when new, is ugly, rough to the touch and of little value; coarse cloth that is half-worn is also ugly, rough to the touch and of little value; coarse cloth that is old is also ugly, rough to the touch and of little value. People use even old coarse cloth for scouring pots, or throw it away on a rubbish heap. In the same way, these three persons who are like coarse cloth are to be found among the monks. Which three? If a monk, even a newly ordained one, is immoral and of bad character, this is their ugliness. Just as that coarse cloth is ugly, this person is like it. And for those who associate with them, keep company with them, attend on them and follow their example, this leads to harm and suffering for a long time. This is their roughness to the touch. Just as that coarse cloth is rough to the touch, this person is like it. And for those from whom they accept robes, alms food, lodgings, and medicines and supplies for the sick, this brings no great fruit, no great benefit. This is their low value. Just as that coarse cloth is of little value, this person is like it.
If a monk of middle standing … if a monk who is an elder is immoral and of bad character, this is their ugliness. Just as that coarse cloth is ugly, this person is like it. And for those who associate with them, keep company with them, attend on them and follow their example, this leads to harm and suffering for a long time. This is their roughness to the touch. Just as that coarse cloth is rough to the touch, this person is like it. And for those from whom they accept robes, alms food, lodgings, and medicines and supplies for the sick, this brings no great fruit, no great benefit. This is their low value. Just as that coarse cloth is of little value, this person is like it.
If such an elder monk speaks in the midst of the Sangha (the community of monks), the monks say to him: "What is the use of anything said by a foolish, incompetent person like you? Do you really think you have something worth saying?" Angry and displeased, he utters the kind of words for which the Sangha suspends him, just as coarse cloth is thrown on a rubbish heap. These are the three persons who are like coarse cloth, to be found among the monks. ^4-116

And here, which are the three persons who are like fine Kāsi cloth? There are three kinds of Kāsi cloth – Kāsi cloth, even when new, is beautiful, pleasant to the touch and of great value; Kāsi cloth that is half-worn is also beautiful, pleasant to the touch and of great value; Kāsi cloth that is old is also beautiful, pleasant to the touch and of great value. People use even old Kāsi cloth to wrap jewels, or keep it in a perfume box.
In the same way, these three persons who are like Kāsi cloth are to be found among the monks. Which three? If a monk, even a newly ordained one, is of good morality and of good character, this is their beauty. Just as that Kāsi cloth is beautiful, this person is like it. And for those who associate with them, keep company with them, attend on them and follow their example, this leads to welfare and happiness for a long time. This is their pleasantness to the touch. Just as that Kāsi cloth is pleasant to the touch, this person is like it. And for those from whom they [variant: yesaṃ kho pana (all editions); A. Ni. 3.100] accept [variant: patigaṇhāti (Sī.); this agrees with the Rūpasiddhi subcommentary] robes, alms food, lodgings, and medicines and supplies for the sick, this brings great fruit and great benefit. This is their great value. Just as that Kāsi cloth is of great value, this person is like it.
If a monk of middle standing … if a monk who is an elder is of good morality and of good character, this is their beauty. Just as that Kāsi cloth is beautiful, this person is like it. And for those who associate with them, keep company with them, attend on them and follow their example, this leads to welfare and happiness for a long time. This is their pleasantness to the touch. Just as that Kāsi cloth is pleasant to the touch, this person is like it. And for those from whom they accept robes, alms food, lodgings, and medicines and supplies for the sick, this brings great fruit and great benefit. This is their great value. Just as that Kāsi cloth is of great value, this person is like it.
If such an elder monk speaks in the midst of the Sangha, the monks say: "Be quiet, venerable ones; the elder monk is speaking on the Teaching and the Discipline." His words are taken to heart, just as Kāsi cloth is kept in a perfume box. These are the three persons who are like Kāsi cloth, to be found among the monks. ^4-117

What kind of person is the one who is easy to measure? Here a certain person is restless, puffed up, fickle, talkative, loose in speech, forgetful, without clear awareness, unconcentrated, with a wandering mind and uncontrolled senses – this person is called "the one who is easy to measure". ^4-118

What kind of person is the one who is hard to measure? Here a certain person is not restless, not puffed up, not fickle, not talkative, not loose in speech, with mindfulness established, clearly aware, concentrated, with a unified mind and restrained senses – this person is called "the one who is hard to measure". ^4-119

What kind of person is the one who cannot be measured? Here a certain person, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for themselves by direct knowledge, here and now, the liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom that are free of taints, and has entered upon it and lives in it – this person is called "the one who cannot be measured". ^4-120

What kind of person should not be associated with, kept company with or attended on? Here a certain person is inferior to oneself in morality, concentration and wisdom. Such a person should not be associated with, kept company with or attended on, except out of kindness, except out of compassion. ^4-121

What kind of person should be associated with, kept company with and attended on? Here a certain person is one's equal in morality, concentration and wisdom. Such a person should be associated with, kept company with and attended on. Why is that? "Being equal in morality, we will have talk about morality together, it will be comfortable for us, and it will flow on for us; being equal in concentration, we will have talk about concentration together, it will be comfortable for us, and it will flow on [variant: pavattanī (Sī.); A. Ni. 3.26] for us; being equal in wisdom, we will have talk about wisdom together, it will be comfortable for us, and it will flow on for us." That is why such a person should be associated with, kept company with and attended on. ^4-122

What kind of person should be associated with, kept company with and attended on with honour and respect? Here a certain person is superior to oneself in morality, concentration and wisdom. Such a person should be associated with, kept company with and attended on with honour and respect. Why is that? "If my morality is incomplete, I will complete it; if it is complete, I will support it with wisdom in this or that respect. If my concentration is incomplete, I will complete it; if it is complete, I will support it with wisdom in this or that respect. If my wisdom is incomplete, I will complete it; if it is complete, I will support it with wisdom in this or that respect." That is why such a person should be associated with, kept company with and attended on with honour and respect. ^4-123

What kind of person should be shunned in disgust, and not associated with, kept company with or attended on? Here a certain person is immoral, of bad character, impure, of suspect conduct, secretive in their actions; not an ascetic, though claiming to be one; not living the holy life, though claiming to; rotten inside, oozing with desire, a heap of filth. Such a person should be shunned in disgust, and not associated with, kept company with or attended on. Why is that? Even if one does not follow the example of such a person, a bad reputation still spreads about one: "This individual has bad friends, bad companions, and leans towards bad people." Just as a snake that has fallen into dung, even if it does not bite, still smears one with filth, in the same way, even if one does not follow the example of such a person, a bad reputation still spreads about one: "This individual has bad friends, bad companions, and leans towards bad people!" That is why such a person should be shunned in disgust, and not associated with, kept company with or attended on. ^4-124

What kind of person should be regarded with detachment, and not associated with, kept company with or attended on? Here a certain person is angry and easily upset. Even when a little is said to them, they lose their temper, become irritated, hostile and stubborn, and they show irritation, hatred and displeasure. Just as an infected sore, when struck by a stick or a potsherd, discharges all the more, in the same way here a certain person is angry and easily upset; even when a little is said to them, they lose their temper, become irritated, hostile and stubborn, and they show irritation, hatred and displeasure. Just as a burning brand of tinduka wood, when struck by a stick or a potsherd, hisses and crackles all the more, in the same way here a certain person is angry and easily upset; even when a little is said to them, they lose their temper, become irritated, hostile and stubborn, and they show irritation, hatred and displeasure. Just as a cesspit, when stirred with a stick or a potsherd, smells all the worse, in the same way here a certain person is angry and easily upset; even when a little is said to them, they lose their temper, become irritated, hostile and stubborn, and they show irritation, hatred and displeasure; such a person should be regarded with detachment, and not associated with, kept company with or attended on. Why is that? "They might insult me, abuse me, or do me harm!" That is why such a person should be regarded with detachment, and not associated with, kept company with or attended on. ^4-125

What kind of person should be associated with, kept company with and attended on? Here a certain person is of good morality, of good character – such a person should be associated with, kept company with and attended on. Why is that? Even if one does not follow the example of such a person, a good reputation still spreads about one: "This individual has good friends, good companions, and leans towards good people!" That is why such a person should be associated with, kept company with and attended on. ^4-126

What kind of person fulfils morality but practises concentration and wisdom only in part? Stream-enterers and once-returners – these persons are called ones who fulfil morality but practise concentration and wisdom only in part. ^4-127

What kind of person fulfils morality and concentration but practises wisdom only in part? A non-returner – this person is called one who fulfils morality and concentration but practises wisdom only in part. ^4-128

What kind of person fulfils morality, concentration and wisdom? A worthy one – this person is called one who fulfils morality, concentration and wisdom. ^4-129

And here, which are the three teachers? Here a certain teacher teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures [variant: paññāpeti (Sī., Syā.)], but does not teach the full understanding of material forms, nor the full understanding of feelings. And here a certain teacher teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures and the full understanding of material forms, but does not teach the full understanding of feelings. And here a certain teacher teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures, the full understanding of material forms and the full understanding of feelings.
Now, the teacher who teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures, but not the full understanding of material forms nor of feelings, should be seen as a teacher who has gained the meditative attainments of the form sphere. The teacher who teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures and of material forms, but not the full understanding of feelings, should be seen as a teacher who has gained the meditative attainments of the formless sphere. The teacher who teaches the full understanding of sensual pleasures, of material forms and of feelings should be seen as a Fully Awakened Buddha. These are the three teachers. ^4-130

And here, which are three further teachers? Here a certain teacher declares that the self is real and lasting both in this present life and in the life to come. And here a certain teacher declares that the self is real and lasting in this present life, but does not declare that the self is real and lasting in the life to come. And here a certain teacher does not declare that the self is real and lasting in this present life, nor does he declare that the self is real and lasting in the life to come.
Now, the teacher who declares that the self is real and lasting both in this present life and in the life to come should be seen as a teacher of eternalism (the belief that the self lasts forever). The teacher who declares that the self is real and lasting in this present life, but does not declare that it is real and lasting in the life to come, should be seen as a teacher of annihilationism (the belief that the self is destroyed at death). The teacher who does not declare that the self is real and lasting in this present life, nor in the life to come, should be seen as a Fully Awakened Buddha. These are three further teachers.
