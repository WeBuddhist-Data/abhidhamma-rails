#### The Section of Questions and Answers ^2-14-2-0

There are five training rules: the training rule of abstaining from killing living beings, the training rule of abstaining from taking what is not given, the training rule of abstaining from sexual misconduct, the training rule of abstaining from false speech, the training rule of abstaining from wine, spirits, and intoxicants that cause heedlessness. ^2-714

Of the five training rules, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many neutral… how many involve conflict, how many are free from conflict? ^2-715

##### The Triads ^2-14-2-1-0

They are wholesome only. They may be associated with pleasant feeling; they may be associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. They are things subject to result. They are not taken up at birth but able to be clung to. They are undefiled but connected with defilement. They are with thought and with examination. They may be accompanied by joy; they may be accompanied by happiness; they may be accompanied by equanimity.
They are to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by meditative development. Their causes are to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by meditative development. They are leading to accumulation. They belong to one neither in training nor beyond training. They are limited. They have limited objects. They are middling. They are unfixed. They are not describable as having the path as their object, as having the path as their cause, or as having the path as their dominant factor. They may be arisen; they may be not arisen; they are not describable as due to arise. They may be past; they may be future; they may be present. They have present objects. They may be internal; they may be external; they may be both internal and external. They have external objects. They are invisible and offer no resistance. ^2-716

##### The Pairs ^2-14-2-2-0

They are not roots; they have roots; they are associated with roots. They are not describable as being "both roots and having roots"; they have roots but are not roots. They are not describable as being "both roots and associated with roots"; they are associated with roots but are not roots. They are not roots yet have roots. They have conditions. They are conditioned. They are invisible. They offer no resistance. They are immaterial. They are worldly. They can be known by one means; they cannot be known by another.
They are not mental taints; they are subject to mental taints; they are dissociated from mental taints. They are not describable as being "both mental taints and subject to mental taints"; they are subject to mental taints but not mental taints. They are not describable either as being "mental taints and associated with mental taints", or as being "associated with mental taints but not mental taints". They are dissociated from mental taints but subject to mental taints. They are not fetters… they are not ties… they are not floods… they are not yokes… they are not hindrances… they are not wrong grasping… they have objects. They are not minds. They are mental factors. They are associated with mind. They are conjoined with mind. They are originated by mind. They arise together with mind. They follow along with mind. They are conjoined with and originated by mind. They are conjoined with, originated by, and arise together with mind. They are conjoined with, originated by, and follow along with mind. They are external. They are not derived. They are not taken up at birth. They are not clinging… they are not defilements.
They are not to be abandoned by seeing. They are not to be abandoned by meditative development. Their causes are not to be abandoned by seeing. Their causes are not to be abandoned by meditative development. They are with thought. They are with examination. They may be with joy; they may be without joy. They may be accompanied by joy; they may be not accompanied by joy. They may be accompanied by happiness; they may be not accompanied by happiness. They may be accompanied by equanimity; they may be not accompanied by equanimity. They belong to the sense sphere. They do not belong to the fine-material sphere. They do not belong to the immaterial sphere. They are included in a sphere. They are not leading out. They are unfixed. They are surpassable. They are free from conflict.
