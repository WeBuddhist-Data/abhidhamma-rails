(1) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the possible as possible and the impossible as impossible? Here the Tathāgata knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should take any formation as permanent — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should take some formation as permanent — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should take any formation as pleasant — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should take some formation as pleasant — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should take any thing as self — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should take some thing as self — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should deprive his mother of life — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should deprive his mother of life — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a person accomplished in view should deprive his father of life… should deprive an arahant of life… should with a mind of hatred shed the blood of a Tathāgata… should split the Order… should acknowledge another teacher… should produce an eighth existence — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an ordinary person should produce an eighth existence — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that two arahants who are fully awakened Buddhas should arise in one world system neither before nor after each other — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one arahant who is a fully awakened Buddha should arise in one world system — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that two wheel-turning kings should arise in one world system neither before nor after each other — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one wheel-turning king should arise in one world system — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a woman should be an arahant who is a fully awakened Buddha — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a man should be an arahant who is a fully awakened Buddha — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a woman should be a wheel-turning king — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a man should be a wheel-turning king — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a woman should exercise the rulership of Sakka, the rulership of Māra, the rulership of Brahmā — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a man should exercise the rulership of Sakka, the rulership of Māra, the rulership of Brahmā — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a wished-for, desired, agreeable result should come from bodily misconduct — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an unwished-for, undesired, disagreeable result should come from bodily misconduct — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that a wished-for, desired, agreeable result should come from verbal misconduct… from mental misconduct — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that an unwished-for, undesired, disagreeable result should come from verbal misconduct… from mental misconduct — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that an unwished-for, undesired, disagreeable result should come from good bodily conduct — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a wished-for, desired, agreeable result should come from good bodily conduct — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that an unwished-for, undesired, disagreeable result should come from good verbal conduct… from good mental conduct — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that a wished-for, desired, agreeable result should come from good verbal conduct… from good mental conduct — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to bodily misconduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to bodily misconduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to verbal misconduct… one given to mental misconduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to verbal misconduct… one given to mental misconduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is possible."
He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to good bodily conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to good bodily conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is possible." He knows: "It is impossible, it cannot happen, that one given to good verbal conduct… one given to good mental conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell — this is not possible." He knows: "But it is possible that one given to good verbal conduct… one given to good mental conduct should, on that account and because of that, with the breakup of the body after death, be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world — this is possible." "Whatever things are causes and conditions of whatever things — that, accordingly, is the possible; whatever things are not causes and not conditions of whatever things — that, accordingly, is the impossible." The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the possible as possible and the impossible as impossible. ^2-809

(2) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the result of past, future, and present undertakings of action, according to circumstance and according to cause? Here the Tathāgata knows: "There are some evil undertakings of action which, blocked by a fortunate destination, do not ripen. There are some evil undertakings of action which, blocked by a fortunate body, do not ripen. There are some evil undertakings of action which, blocked by fortunate time, do not ripen. There are some evil undertakings of action which, blocked by fortunate effort, do not ripen.
"There are some evil undertakings of action which ripen through an unfortunate destination. There are some evil undertakings of action which ripen through an unfortunate body. There are some evil undertakings of action which ripen through unfortunate time. There are some evil undertakings of action which ripen through unfortunate effort.
"There are some good undertakings of action which, blocked by an unfortunate destination, do not ripen. There are some good undertakings of action which, blocked by an unfortunate body, do not ripen. There are some good undertakings of action which, blocked by unfortunate time, do not ripen. There are some good undertakings of action which, blocked by unfortunate effort, do not ripen.
"There are some good undertakings of action which ripen through a fortunate destination. There are some good undertakings of action which ripen through a fortunate body. There are some good undertakings of action which ripen through fortunate time. There are some good undertakings of action which ripen through fortunate effort." The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the result of past, future, and present undertakings of action, according to circumstance and according to cause. ^2-810

(3) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the way that leads everywhere? Here the Tathāgata knows: "This is the path, this is the way that leads to hell"; he knows: "This is the path, this is the way that leads to the animal womb"; he knows: "This is the path, this is the way that leads to the realm of ghosts"; he knows: "This is the path, this is the way that leads to the human world"; he knows: "This is the path, this is the way that leads to the world of the gods"; he knows: "This is the path, this is the way that leads to Nibbāna." The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the way that leads everywhere. ^2-811

(4) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the world with its many and different elements? Here the Tathāgata knows the diversity of the aggregates, knows the diversity of the sense-fields, knows the diversity of the elements, knows the diversity of the world with its many and different elements. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the world with its many and different elements. ^2-812

(5) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the different inclinations of beings? Here the Tathāgata knows: "There are beings of inferior inclination; there are beings of refined inclination. Beings of inferior inclination resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination. Beings of refined inclination resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of refined inclination.
"In the past too, beings of inferior inclination resorted to, frequented, and attended upon beings of inferior inclination; beings of refined inclination resorted to, frequented, and attended upon beings of refined inclination.
"In the future too, beings of inferior inclination will resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination; beings of refined inclination will resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of refined inclination." The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the different inclinations of beings. ^2-813

(6) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the higher and lower state of the faculties of other beings, of other persons? Here the Tathāgata knows the disposition of beings, knows their underlying tendencies, knows their conduct, knows their inclination; he knows beings with little dust in their eyes and much dust in their eyes, with sharp faculties and dull faculties, of good character and bad character, easy to instruct and hard to instruct, capable and incapable. ^2-814

And what is the disposition of beings? "The world is eternal", or "the world is not eternal", or "the world is finite", or "the world is infinite", or "the soul and the body are the same", or "the soul is one thing and the body another", or "a Tathāgata exists after death", or "a Tathāgata does not exist after death", or "a Tathāgata both exists and does not exist after death", or "a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after death" — thus beings rely either on the view of existence or on the view of non-existence. Or, avoiding both these extremes, they have gained the conforming acceptance with regard to things that are dependently arisen through specific conditionality, and the knowledge of them as they really are. This is the disposition of beings. ^2-815

And what are the underlying tendencies of beings? There are seven underlying tendencies: the underlying tendency to sensual lust, the underlying tendency to aversion, the underlying tendency to conceit, the underlying tendency to views, the underlying tendency to doubt, the underlying tendency to lust for existence, the underlying tendency to ignorance. Whatever in the world is a dear and agreeable form — there the underlying tendency of beings to lust lies latent. Whatever in the world is a disagreeable and unpleasant form — there the underlying tendency of beings to aversion lies latent. So ignorance lies fallen upon these two things; and conceit, views, and doubt, standing together with it, should be seen there too. This is the underlying tendency of beings. ^2-816

And what is the conduct of beings? The meritorious formation, the demeritorious formation, the imperturbable formation, whether on a limited plane or on a great plane — this is the conduct of beings. ^2-817

And what is the inclination of beings? There are beings of inferior inclination; there are beings of refined inclination. Beings of inferior inclination resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination. Beings of refined inclination resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of refined inclination.
In the past too, beings of inferior inclination resorted to, frequented, and attended upon beings of inferior inclination; beings of refined inclination resorted to, frequented, and attended upon beings of refined inclination.
In the future too, beings of inferior inclination will resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of inferior inclination; beings of refined inclination will resort to, frequent, and attend upon beings of refined inclination. This is the inclination of beings. ^2-818

Which beings have much dust in their eyes? The ten grounds of defilement: greed, hate, delusion, conceit, views, doubt, dullness, restlessness, shamelessness, lack of moral dread. Those beings by whom these ten grounds of defilement have been practised, developed, cultivated, and heaped up — these are the beings with much dust in their eyes. ^2-819

Which beings have little dust in their eyes? Those beings by whom these ten grounds of defilement have not been practised, not developed, not cultivated, and not heaped up — these are the beings with little dust in their eyes. ^2-820

Which beings have dull faculties? The five faculties: the faculty of confidence, the faculty of energy, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of concentration, the faculty of wisdom. Those beings by whom these five faculties have not been practised, not developed, not cultivated, and not heaped up — these are the beings with dull faculties. ^2-821

Which beings have sharp faculties? Those beings by whom these five faculties have been practised, developed, cultivated, and heaped up — these are the beings with sharp faculties. ^2-822

Which beings are of bad character? Those beings who have evil dispositions, evil underlying tendencies, evil conduct, and evil inclinations, with much dust in their eyes and dull faculties — these are the beings of bad character. ^2-823

Which beings are of good character? Those beings who have good dispositions, good conduct, and good inclinations, with little dust in their eyes and sharp faculties — these are the beings of good character. ^2-824

Which beings are hard to instruct? Those beings who are of bad character are the very beings hard to instruct. And those beings who are of good character are the very beings easy to instruct. ^2-825

Which beings are incapable? Those beings who possess the obstruction of action, possess the obstruction of defilement, possess the obstruction of result, who are without confidence, without desire, and without wisdom, and are incapable of entering the fixed course of rightness in wholesome things — these are the incapable beings. ^2-826

Which beings are capable? Those beings who do not possess the obstruction of action, do not possess the obstruction of defilement, do not possess the obstruction of result, who have confidence, desire, and wisdom, and are capable of entering the fixed course of rightness in wholesome things — these are the capable beings. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the higher and lower state of the faculties of other beings, of other persons. ^2-827

(7) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the defilement, the cleansing, and the emergence in the meditative absorptions, liberations, concentrations, and attainments? "A meditator": there are four meditators. There is one meditator who takes what is truly success to be failure; there is one meditator who takes what is truly failure to be success; there is one meditator who takes what is truly success to be success; there is one meditator who takes what is truly failure to be failure — these are the four meditators.
There are another four meditators. There is one meditator who enters slowly and emerges quickly; there is one meditator who enters quickly and emerges slowly; there is one meditator who enters slowly and emerges slowly; there is one meditator who enters quickly and emerges quickly — these are the four meditators.
There are another four meditators. There is one meditator who is skilled in the concentration within concentration but not skilled in the attainment within concentration; there is one meditator who is skilled in the attainment within concentration but not skilled in the concentration within concentration; there is one meditator who is skilled both in the concentration within concentration and in the attainment within concentration; there is one meditator who is skilled neither in the concentration within concentration nor in the attainment within concentration — these are the four meditators.
"Meditative absorption": there are four meditative absorptions — the first meditative absorption, the second meditative absorption, the third meditative absorption, the fourth meditative absorption.
"Liberation": there are eight liberations. Possessing material form, one sees material forms — this is the first liberation.
Not perceiving material form internally, one sees material forms externally — this is the second liberation.
One is resolved only upon the beautiful — this is the third liberation.
Completely passing beyond perceptions of material form, with the disappearance of perceptions of resistance, not attending to perceptions of diversity, aware that "space is infinite", one enters and dwells in the field of infinite space — this is the fourth liberation.
Completely passing beyond the field of infinite space, aware that "consciousness is infinite", one enters and dwells in the field of infinite consciousness — this is the fifth liberation.
Completely passing beyond the field of infinite consciousness, aware that "there is nothing", one enters and dwells in the field of nothingness — this is the sixth liberation.
Completely passing beyond the field of nothingness, one enters and dwells in the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception — this is the seventh liberation.
Completely passing beyond the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, one enters and dwells in the cessation of perception and feeling — this is the eighth liberation.
"Concentration": there are three concentrations — concentration with thought and with examination, concentration without thought but with examination only, concentration without thought and without examination.
"Attainment": there are nine successive dwelling attainments — the attainment of the first meditative absorption, the attainment of the second meditative absorption, the attainment of the third meditative absorption, the attainment of the fourth meditative absorption, the attainment of the field of infinite space, the attainment of the field of infinite consciousness, the attainment of the field of nothingness, the attainment of the field of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, the attainment of the cessation of perception and feeling.
"Defilement": the state that shares in decline. "Cleansing": the state that shares in distinction. "Emergence": cleansing is also emergence; emerging from this or that concentration is also emergence. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the defilement, the cleansing, and the emergence in the meditative absorptions, liberations, concentrations, and attainments. ^2-828

(8) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the recollection of past lives? Here the Tathāgata recollects his manifold past lives, that is: one birth, two births, three births, four births, five births, ten births, twenty births, thirty births, forty births, fifty births, a hundred births, a thousand births, a hundred thousand births, many eons of world-contraction, many eons of world-expansion, many eons of world-contraction and expansion: "There I was so named, of such a clan, of such appearance, with such food, experiencing such happiness and pain, with such a life span; passing away from there, I arose elsewhere; and there too I was so named, of such a clan, of such appearance, with such food, experiencing such happiness and pain, with such a life span; passing away from there, I was reborn here." Thus with its aspects and details he recollects his manifold past lives. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the recollection of past lives. ^2-829

(9) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the passing away and rebirth of beings? Here the Tathāgata, with the divine eye, purified and surpassing the human, sees beings passing away and being reborn — inferior and refined, beautiful and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate — and he knows beings faring according to their actions: "These beings, indeed, endowed with bodily misconduct, endowed with verbal misconduct, endowed with mental misconduct, revilers of the noble ones, of wrong view, undertaking action based on wrong view — with the breakup of the body after death they have been reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, a place of ruin, hell. But these beings, endowed with good bodily conduct, endowed with good verbal conduct, endowed with good mental conduct, not revilers of the noble ones, of right view, undertaking action based on right view — with the breakup of the body after death they have been reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world." Thus with the divine eye, purified and surpassing the human, he sees beings passing away and being reborn — inferior and refined, beautiful and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate — and he knows beings faring according to their actions. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the passing away and rebirth of beings. ^2-830

(10) Therein, what is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the destruction of the mental taints? Here the Tathāgata, with the destruction of the mental taints, having realised for himself with direct knowledge, in this very life, the taintless liberation of mind and liberation by wisdom, enters and dwells in it. The wisdom, understanding… non-delusion, investigation of things, right view therein — this is the Tathāgata's knowledge, as it really is, of the destruction of the mental taints.
The Tens.
