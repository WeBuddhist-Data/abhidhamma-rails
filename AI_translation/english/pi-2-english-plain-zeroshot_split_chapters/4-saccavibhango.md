### Analysis of the Truths ^2-4-0

#### Classification According to the Discourses ^2-4-1-0

There are four noble truths – the noble truth of suffering, the noble truth of the origin of suffering, the noble truth of the ending of suffering, and the noble truth of the way leading to the ending of suffering. ^2-189

##### The Truth of Suffering ^2-4-1-1-0

Therein, what is the noble truth of suffering? Birth is suffering, aging is suffering, death is suffering; sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and despair are suffering; association with what is disliked is suffering; separation from what is loved is suffering; not getting what one wants is also suffering; in brief, the five aggregates that are clung to are suffering. ^2-190

Therein, what is birth? The birth of this or that being into this or that order of beings – the being born, the descent, the coming forth, the appearance of the aggregates, the acquiring of the sense-fields – this is called "birth". ^2-191

Therein, what is aging? The aging of this or that being in this or that order of beings – the growing old, brokenness of teeth, greyness of hair, wrinkling of skin, the decline of the life span, the ripening of the faculties – this is called "aging". ^2-192

Therein, what is death? The passing of this or that being from this or that order of beings – the passing away, the breaking up, the disappearance, mortality, death, the completion of one's time, the breaking up of the aggregates, the laying down of the body, the cutting off of the life faculty – this is called "death". ^2-193

Therein, what is sorrow? When someone is touched by loss of relatives, loss of wealth, loss through illness, loss of virtue, or loss of view, when someone meets with one misfortune or another, is touched by one painful thing or another – the sorrow, the sorrowing, the state of sorrow, inner sorrow, deep inner sorrow, the burning of the mind, sadness, the dart of sorrow – this is called "sorrow". ^2-194

Therein, what is lamentation? When someone is touched by loss of relatives, loss of wealth, loss through illness, loss of virtue, or loss of view, when someone meets with one misfortune or another, is touched by one painful thing or another – the crying out, the lamenting, the act of crying out, the act of lamenting, the state of crying out, the state of lamenting; the talk, the raving, the wailing; the moaning, the act of moaning, the state of moaning – this is called "lamentation". ^2-195

Therein, what is pain? Bodily discomfort, bodily pain; the uncomfortable, painful experience born of body-contact; the uncomfortable, painful feeling born of body-contact – this is called "pain". ^2-196

Therein, what is sadness? Mental discomfort, mental pain; the uncomfortable, painful experience born of mind-contact; the uncomfortable, painful feeling born of mind-contact – this is called "sadness". ^2-197

Therein, what is despair? When someone is touched by loss of relatives, loss of wealth, loss through illness, loss of virtue, or loss of view, when someone meets with one misfortune or another, is touched by one painful thing or another – the distress, the despair, the state of distress, the state of despair – this is called "despair". ^2-198

Therein, what is the suffering of association with what is disliked? Here, whatever sights, sounds, smells, tastes, and things touched are unwanted, unattractive, and disagreeable to someone, or whoever wishes them ill, wishes them harm, wishes them discomfort, wishes them no security – meeting, coming together, joining, and mingling with these – this is called "the suffering of association with what is disliked". ^2-199

Therein, what is the suffering of separation from what is loved? Here, whatever sights, sounds, smells, tastes, and things touched are wanted, attractive, and agreeable to someone, or whoever wishes them well, wishes them good, wishes them comfort, wishes them security – mother or father, brother or sister, friends or companions, relatives or kin – not meeting, not coming together, not joining, and not mingling with these – this is called "the suffering of separation from what is loved". ^2-200

Therein, what is "not getting what one wants is also suffering"? In beings subject to birth such a wish arises: "Oh, that we were not subject to birth! Oh, that birth would not come to us!" But this cannot be gained by wishing. This too is "not getting what one wants is also suffering".
In beings subject to aging… in beings subject to illness… in beings subject to death… in beings subject to sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and despair such a wish arises: "Oh, that we were not subject to sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and despair! Oh, that sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and despair would not come to us!" But this cannot be gained by wishing. This too is "not getting what one wants is also suffering". ^2-201

Therein, what is "in brief, the five aggregates that are clung to are suffering"? Namely: the aggregate of material form that is clung to, the aggregate of feeling that is clung to, the aggregate of perception that is clung to, the aggregate of mental formations that is clung to, and the aggregate of consciousness that is clung to. These are called "in brief, the five aggregates that are clung to are suffering".
This is called "the noble truth of suffering". ^2-202

##### The Truth of the Path ^2-4-1-4-0

Therein, what is the noble truth of the way leading to the ending of suffering? It is just this noble eightfold path, namely: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration.
Therein, what is right view? Knowledge of suffering, knowledge of the origin of suffering, knowledge of the ending of suffering, knowledge of the way leading to the ending of suffering – this is called "right view".
Therein, what is right intention? The intention of renunciation, the intention of goodwill, the intention of harmlessness – this is called "right intention".
Therein, what is right speech? Refraining from false speech, refraining from divisive speech, refraining from harsh speech, refraining from idle chatter – this is called "right speech".
Therein, what is right action? Refraining from killing living beings, refraining from taking what is not given, refraining from sexual misconduct – this is called "right action".
Therein, what is right livelihood? Here a noble disciple, having abandoned wrong livelihood, makes a living by right livelihood – this is called "right livelihood".
Therein, what is right effort? Here a monk generates desire, strives, arouses energy, applies the mind, and endeavours for the non-arising of bad, unwholesome states that have not yet arisen. He generates desire, strives, arouses energy, applies the mind, and endeavours for the abandoning of bad, unwholesome states that have arisen. He generates desire, strives, arouses energy, applies the mind, and endeavours for the arising of wholesome states that have not yet arisen. He generates desire, strives, arouses energy, applies the mind, and endeavours for the maintaining, non-confusion, increase, expansion, development, and fulfilment of wholesome states that have arisen. This is called "right effort".
Therein, what is right mindfulness? Here a monk dwells contemplating the body in the body – ardent, clearly understanding, and mindful, having put away covetousness and sadness towards the world. He dwells contemplating feelings in feelings… the mind in the mind… mental objects in mental objects – ardent, clearly understanding, and mindful, having put away covetousness and sadness towards the world. This is called "right mindfulness".
Therein, what is right concentration? Here a monk, secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption, which has thought and examination and the joy and happiness born of seclusion. With the stilling of thought and examination, he enters and dwells in the second meditative absorption, which has inner confidence and unification of mind, is without thought and examination, and has the joy and happiness born of concentration. With the fading away of joy, he dwells with equanimity, mindful and clearly understanding, and experiences happiness with the body; he enters and dwells in the third meditative absorption, of which the noble ones declare: "Equanimous and mindful, one dwells happily." With the abandoning of pleasure and the abandoning of pain, and with the earlier disappearance of gladness and sadness, he enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption, which is neither painful nor pleasant and has purity of mindfulness through equanimity. This is called "right concentration".
This is called "the noble truth of the way leading to the ending of suffering".
The Classification According to the Discourses is finished. ^2-205
