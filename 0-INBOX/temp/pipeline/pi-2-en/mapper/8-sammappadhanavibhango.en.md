### Analysis of the Right Efforts ^2-8-0

#### Classification According to the Discourses ^2-8-1-0

390. There are four right efforts. Here a monk generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the non-arising of evil, unwholesome things that have not yet arisen; he generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the abandoning of evil, unwholesome things that have arisen; he generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the arising of wholesome things that have not yet arisen; he generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the persistence of wholesome things that have arisen, for their non-confusion, their increase, their expansion, their development, their perfection. ^2-390

391. And how does a monk generate desire, strive, initiate energy, exert his mind, and endeavour for the non-arising of evil, unwholesome things that have not yet arisen? Therein, what are the evil, unwholesome things that have not yet arisen? The three unwholesome roots — greed, hate, delusion — and the defilements that stand together with them; the aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of mental formations, and the aggregate of consciousness associated with them; and the bodily action, verbal action, and mental action originated by them — these are called "the evil, unwholesome things that have not yet arisen". So he generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the non-arising of these evil, unwholesome things that have not yet arisen. ^2-391

392. "Generates desire": therein, what is desire? The desire, wishing, wish to act, wholesome desire for the Dhamma — this is called "desire". He generates this desire, brings it forth, raises it, rouses it, produces it, brings it into being. Therefore it is said, "generates desire". ^2-392

393. "Strives": therein, what is striving? The mental initiation of energy… right effort — this is called "striving". He is endowed with this striving, fully endowed, having reached it, fully reached it, possessed of it, complete in it, furnished with it. Therefore it is said, "strives". ^2-393

394. "Initiates energy": therein, what is energy? The mental initiation of energy… right effort — this is called "energy". He initiates this energy, undertakes it, practises it, develops it, cultivates it. Therefore it is said, "initiates energy". ^2-394

#### The Section of Questions and Answers ^2-8-3-0

427. There are four right efforts. Here a monk generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours for the non-arising of evil, unwholesome things that have not yet arisen; for the abandoning of evil, unwholesome things that have arisen… for the arising of wholesome things that have not yet arisen… for the persistence of wholesome things that have arisen, for their non-confusion, their increase, their expansion, their development, their perfection he generates desire, strives, initiates energy, exerts his mind, and endeavours. ^2-427

428. Of the four right efforts, how many are wholesome, how many unwholesome, how many neutral… how many involve conflict, how many are free from conflict? ^2-428

##### The Triads ^2-8-3-1-0

429. They are wholesome only. They may be associated with pleasant feeling; they may be associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. They are things subject to result. They are not taken up at birth and not able to be clung to. They are undefiled and not connected with defilement. They may be with thought and with examination; they may be without thought but with examination only; they may be without thought and without examination. They may be accompanied by joy; they may be accompanied by happiness; they may be accompanied by equanimity. They are to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by meditative development. Their causes are to be abandoned neither by seeing nor by meditative development. They are leading to dismantling. They belong to one in training. They are measureless. They have measureless objects. They are refined. They are fixed in rightness. They do not have the path as their object. They have the path as their cause. They may have the path as their dominant factor; they may not be describable as having the path as their dominant factor. They may be arisen; they may be not arisen; they are not describable as due to arise. They may be past; they may be future; they may be present. They are not describable as having a past object, a future object, or a present object. They may be internal; they may be external; they may be both internal and external. They have external objects. They are invisible and offer no resistance. ^2-429

##### The Pairs ^2-8-3-2-0

430. They are not roots. They have roots. They are associated with roots. They are not describable as being both roots and having roots; they have roots but are not roots. They are not describable as being both roots and associated with roots; they are associated with roots but are not roots. They are not roots yet have roots. They have conditions. They are conditioned. They are invisible. They offer no resistance. They are immaterial. They are beyond the world. They can be known by one means; they cannot be known by another. They are not mental taints. They are without mental taints. They are dissociated from mental taints. They are not describable either as being mental taints and subject to mental taints, or as being subject to mental taints but not mental taints. They are not describable either as being mental taints and associated with mental taints, or as being associated with mental taints but not mental taints. They are dissociated from mental taints. They are without mental taints. They are not fetters… they are not ties… they are not floods… they are not yokes… they are not hindrances… they are not wrong grasping… they have objects. They are not minds. They are mental factors. They are associated with mind. They are conjoined with mind. They are originated by mind. They arise together with mind. They follow along with mind. They are conjoined with and originated by mind. They are conjoined with, originated by, and arise together with mind. They are conjoined with, originated by, and follow along with mind. They are external. They are not derived. They are not taken up at birth. They are not clinging… they are not defilements… they are not to be abandoned by seeing. They are not to be abandoned by meditative development. Their causes are not to be abandoned by seeing. Their causes are not to be abandoned by meditative development. They may be with thought; they may be without thought. They may be with examination; they may be without examination. They may be with joy; they may be without joy. They may be accompanied by joy; they may be not accompanied by joy. They may be accompanied by happiness; they may be not accompanied by happiness. They may be accompanied by equanimity; they may be not accompanied by equanimity. They do not belong to the sense sphere. They do not belong to the fine-material sphere. They do not belong to the immaterial sphere. They are not included in any sphere. They are leading out. They are fixed. They are unsurpassed. They are free from conflict.
