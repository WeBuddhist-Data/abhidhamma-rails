##### (15) The five doctrines of Nibbāna in this very life ^2-17-6-15-0

943. Therein, what are the five doctrines of Nibbāna in this very life?
(a) Here some ascetic or brahmin speaks thus and holds this view: "When this self, sir, furnished and provided with the five strands of sense pleasure, amuses itself, to that extent, sir, this self has reached the highest Nibbāna in this very life." Thus some declare the highest Nibbāna in this very life of an existing being.
(b) Another says to him: "There is, sir, that self of which you speak; I do not say it does not exist. But this self, sir, has not to that extent reached the highest Nibbāna in this very life. Why is that? Because, sir, sense pleasures are impermanent, painful, and subject to change; with their change and alteration arise sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair. When this self, sir, secluded from sense pleasures… enters and dwells in the first meditative absorption, to that extent, sir, this self has reached the highest Nibbāna in this very life." Thus some declare the highest Nibbāna in this very life of an existing being.
(c) Another says to him: "There is, sir, that self of which you speak; I do not say it does not exist. But this self, sir, has not to that extent reached the highest Nibbāna in this very life. Why is that? Because whatever there is of thought and examination there, by that it is reckoned gross. When this self, sir, with the stilling of thought and examination… enters and dwells in the second meditative absorption, to that extent, sir, this self has reached the highest Nibbāna in this very life." Thus some declare the highest Nibbāna in this very life of an existing being.
(d) Another says to him: "There is, sir, that self of which you speak; I do not say it does not exist. But this self, sir, has not to that extent reached the highest Nibbāna in this very life. Why is that? Because whatever there is of joy and mental elation there, by that it is reckoned gross. When this self, sir, with the fading away of joy… enters and dwells in the third meditative absorption, to that extent, sir, this self has reached the highest Nibbāna in this very life." Thus some declare the highest Nibbāna in this very life of an existing being.
(e) Another says to him: "There is, sir, that self of which you speak; I do not say it does not exist. But this self, sir, has not to that extent reached the highest Nibbāna in this very life. Why is that? Because whatever there is of the mind's engagement with happiness and joy there, by that it is reckoned gross. When this self, sir, with the abandoning of happiness… enters and dwells in the fourth meditative absorption, to that extent, sir, this self has reached the highest Nibbāna in this very life." Thus some declare the highest Nibbāna in this very life of an existing being. These are the five doctrines of Nibbāna in this very life.

953. Therein, what are the eight grounds of laziness?

#### The Exposition of the Wanderings of Craving ^2-17-12-0

##### (1) Concerning what is internal ^2-17-12-1-0

973. Therein, what are the eighteen wanderings of craving concerning what is internal? There is "I am"; there is "I am thus"; there is "I am just so"; there is "I am otherwise"; there is "I shall be"; there is "I shall be thus"; there is "I shall be just so"; there is "I shall be otherwise"; there is "I am lasting"; there is "I am perishing"; there is "may I be"; there is "may I be thus"; there is "may I be just so"; there is "may I be otherwise"; there is "would that I might be"; there is "would that I might be thus"; there is "would that I might be just so"; there is "would that I might be otherwise". ^2-973

974. And how is there "I am"? Not having discarded some thing — material form… feeling… perception… formations… consciousness — one obtains the desire "I am", one obtains the conceit "I am", one obtains the view "I am". When that exists, there are these proliferations: "I am thus", or "I am just so", or "I am otherwise".
(2) And how is there "I am thus"? "I am a noble", or "I am a brahmin", or "I am a merchant", or "I am a worker", or "I am a householder", or "I am a renunciant", or "I am a god", or "I am a human being", or "I have material form", or "I am immaterial", or "I am percipient", or "I am non-percipient", or "I am neither percipient nor non-percipient" — thus there is "I am thus".
(3) And how is there "I am just so"? Comparing oneself with another person: "As he is a noble, so I am a noble", or "as he is a brahmin, so I am a brahmin", or "as he is a merchant, so I am a merchant", or "as he is a worker, so I am a worker", or "as he is a householder, so I am a householder", or "as he is a renunciant, so I am a renunciant", or "as he is a god, so I am a god", or "as he is a human being, so I am a human being", or "as he has material form, so I have material form", or "as he is immaterial, so I am immaterial", or "as he is percipient, so I am percipient", or "as he is non-percipient, so I am non-percipient", or "as he is neither percipient nor non-percipient, so I am neither percipient nor non-percipient" — thus there is "I am just so".
